# Research: Waymo 68% Safer — The Denominator Problem

## News Hook
IIHS study (July 23, 2026): Waymo driverless vehicles have 68% fewer crashes than human drivers per VMT over 50 million driverless miles (2021-2024).
Source: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people

## Key IIHS Data Points
- Waymo crash rate: 1.28 police-reportable crashes per million VMT
- Human driver crash rate: 4.06 per million VMT (in same cities)
- 76% lower in Phoenix, 35% lower in SF, 71% lower in LA, 4% HIGHER in Austin
- 85% fewer single-vehicle crashes, 81% fewer injury crashes
- Sample: 50M Waymo driverless miles vs 222B human miles
- 89 police-reportable crashes for Waymo (64 in driverless mode)

## FARS Cross-Reference Data
- 490,736 drivers in fatal crashes (2014-2023)
- Fleet average impairment: 20.0% (alcohol 15.1%, drugs 8.7%)
- Vehicle death rate range: 0.12 (Mazda CX-5) to 8.54 (Hyundai Veloster) — 65x gap
- National fatality rate Q1 2026: 0.99 per 100M VMT (historic low)

## Original Analysis: Decomposing "68% Safer"
The IIHS compares Waymo against ALL humans in ALL vehicles. But "all humans" includes:
1. The 20% who are impaired — if impaired drivers crash at ~3x the sober rate, removing them alone drops the human rate from 4.06 to ~2.90 (estimated sober baseline)
2. The fleet includes vehicles with 8.54 death rates and 85.7% crash lethality (Cavalier, Neon) alongside 0.12 (CX-5)
3. Waymo operates exclusively modern, sensor-laden vehicles (~$150K+), not 15-year-old fleet-average cars

Controlling for sobriety alone narrows the gap from 68% to ~56%.
Controlling for sobriety AND modern-vehicle-class would narrow further.

## The Paradox
Waymo IS genuinely safer. But the comparison flatters it because "average human driver" is doing a LOT of heavy lifting. The 20% impairment rate + oldest/cheapest vehicle fleet members inflate the human baseline.

A sober driver in a 2024 RAV4 (rate 0.19 per 100M VMT) is already operating at a fatality rate so low it's barely measurable. Waymo isn't competing against THAT driver — it's competing against the pool that includes her AND the guy doing 90 in a 2003 Cobalt after four beers.
