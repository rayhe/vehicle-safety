# Research: The Takata Recall's Long Tail — 4.7 Million Cars Still Haven't Been Fixed
**Article #1123 · Axle McScatter · Kicker: By The Numbers · 2026-10-10**

## News peg
Carfax September 2026 recall tracker (reported Oct ~1, 2026, autobodynews.com): 4.7M U.S.
vehicles still carry unrepaired Takata airbags. Honda's Takata fact sheet (current Aug 21,
2026, hondanews.com): 535,000+ recalled inflators bought back from salvage yards, ongoing.

## Kill test
Passes. Site has covered Takata four times (#808 Toyota replacement airbags, #954 do-not-drive
census, #1026 Takata's ghost/Joyson, plus drafts), but the angle here is new: the COMPLETION
CURVE. The recall is entering its long tail, and my original math shows the unrepaired pool
is shrinking at ~650K vehicles/year — a rate at which scrappage, not owner compliance, is
likely doing most of the work. Nobody on the site has run the completion math or covered
Honda's junkyard buyback as the recall's second front. The 50% Alpha-inflator rupture rate
(a coin flip) from Honda's own fact sheet is a fresh, citable horror. Fresh data, original
calculation, real actionability. Axle McScatter's beat (By The Numbers) fits exactly.

## Verified facts (primary sources)
1. **4.7M unrepaired:** Carfax September 2026 recall tracker; reported by autobodynews.com
   (~Oct 1, 2026). About 4.7M U.S. vehicles have not received the fix.
2. **20% decline since 2024:** At the recall's 10-year mark (2024) Carfax counted 6M+ with the
   repair outstanding. 4.7M is ~20% lower. (autobodynews)
3. **Honda salvage buyback:** American Honda removed 535,000+ recalled inflators from salvage
   yards via Rebuilders Automotive Supply; effort ongoing; Honda also asked major online
   auction sites to ban listings. (hondanews.com Takata fact sheet, current Aug 21, 2026)
4. **NHTSA toll:** 28 U.S. deaths confirmed, at least 400 injuries (NHTSA Takata Recall
   Spotlight). The agency's February consumer alert warned "even minor crashes can result
   in exploding Takata air bags" and that the chance of explosion grows as an airbag ages.
   The 28th confirmed death occurred in a Honda. (autobodynews, citing NHTSA)
5. **Honda completion:** ~12.9M Honda/Acura vehicles recalled, ~21M inflators, ~17M replaced;
   national completion rate 96.2% as of Aug 21. Honda confirmed 20 deaths and 200+ injuries
   in the U.S. from ruptured driver inflators. (Honda fact sheet)
6. **Alpha inflators:** 2001-2003 Honda/Acura "Alpha" driver inflators rupture at up to 50%
   (Honda fact sheet, citing NHTSA and Takata). Honda advises owners not to drive until repaired.
7. **Completion decay:** Honda's review of 14 unrelated campaigns: completion rates drop to
   about 33% by the 9th and 10th year of ownership. Many Takata-affected vehicles are now,
   or will soon be, older than 15 years. (Honda fact sheet)
8. **Stellantis:** Feb 2026 stop-drive for all its unrepaired vehicles (~225,000 U.S.
   vehicles); 6.6M inflators replaced. Follows a 2023 fatality in a 2003 Dodge Ram.
   (autobodynews, citing NHTSA alert and Fox Business)
9. **eBay ban:** Sept 24, 2026 effective date for eBay's ban on listing airbags/inflators,
   following a federal inquiry tied to counterfeit inflators. (autobodynews)
10. **BMW aftermarket:** In 2024 BMW recalled ~395,000 3 Series because some cars had sport
    or M-sport steering wheels containing a Takata driver airbag module that was "not
    officially offered or approved by BMW as a replacement part" — the junkyard parts were
    getting installed. (autobodynews, citing BMW)
11. **Scale:** recall covers ~67M airbags. Carfax: ~1.3M U.S. vehicles have an open
    do-not-drive recall (Takata or other defect). (autobodynews)
12. **Honda outreach:** posters/letters to 160,000+ independent repair facilities; 300 wrapped
    parts trucks as rolling billboards, 100,000+ miles/day; HondaLink app alerts; Carfax
    history-report flagging; insurer outreach; push for state/federal registration laws.
    (Honda fact sheet)

## Original math (article's novel contribution)
- **Removal rate:** (6.0M − 4.7M) / 2 years ≈ 650,000 vehicles/year leaving the unrepaired pool.
  At that linear rate, zero arrives in ~7.2 years (~2033).
- **Scrappage vs. compliance:** The affected fleet is 10-25 years old; normal fleet attrition
  alone removes hundreds of thousands per year. Honda's own 33%-by-year-10 figure implies
  owner compliance in the residual pool is low; much of the 1.3M two-year decline is almost
  certainly dead cars, not repaired ones. The recall is being "completed" by the crusher.
- **The residual is the worst part:** 4.7M vehicles vs. Honda's 96.2% completion on 12.9M —
  the remaining ~3.8% Honda/Acura (~500K vehicles) plus other automakers' holdouts are the
  aging, changing-hands, hard-to-locate cars Carfax flagged. The pool's risk concentration
  RISES as the count falls: Alpha inflators (50% rupture) live in exactly this stratum
  (2001-2003 models, now 23-25 years old).
- **Coin-flip airbags:** 50% rupture rate on Alpha = the single most dangerous recall
  population still on U.S. roads by defect probability.
- **Context:** 4.7M is roughly 1.6% of the ~290M U.S. vehicle fleet — still larger than the
  annual U.S. sales of any single automaker except the top two or three.

## Counterargument (state at full strength)
96.2% completion is extraordinary by recall standards; the absolute risk per vehicle is low
(28 confirmed U.S. deaths across 67M inflators over a decade+). The remaining pool skews
heavily toward vehicles near end of life with low annual mileage, so real exposure is
self-limiting. Carfax's counts are estimates from registration data, and the 6M-to-4.7M
decline mixes scrappage with repairs — so "650K/year" is a removal rate, not a repair rate.
Some "unrepaired" vehicles may already be permanently off the road.

## Limitations
- Carfax tracker methodology is registration-based estimation, not a census of VINs.
- Scrappage-vs-repair split of the 1.3M decline is not published; my inference is
  directional, stated as such.
- NHTSA death/injury counts are confirmed-only and may undercount.
- Inflator counts (67M) and vehicle counts (4.7M) are different denominators; Honda's
  21M inflators / 12.9M vehicles implies ~1.6 inflators per Honda vehicle — do not naively
  extrapolate to other automakers.

## Actionables
- Run your VIN twice a year at nhtsa.gov/recalls (NHTSA's own recommendation).
- Used-car buyers: 2001-2016 Hondas/Acuras especially — demand a VIN check and proof the
  recall is closed before money changes hands.
- If you own a 2001-2003 Honda/Acura: this is a do-not-drive warning with a 50% rupture
  rate. Get it towed; Honda/Stellantis will tow for free or repair at your home.
- Independent shops: Honda asks staff to run VIN checks on every customer car.
