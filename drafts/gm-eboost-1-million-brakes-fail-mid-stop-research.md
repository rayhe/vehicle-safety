# Research: GM eBoost Brake System Investigation — 1.16M Vehicles

## Summary
NHTSA opened Engineering Analysis EA26006 on Aug 21, 2026, expanding from a preliminary evaluation of just the 2023 Cadillac Lyriq to ALL GM vehicles equipped with the eBoost electronic brake system. 1.16 million vehicles potentially affected.

## Core Paradox / Angle
GM replaced the mechanical vacuum brake booster — a reliable technology unchanged in concept for 100+ years — with an electronic system called eBoost. When it fails, the brake pedal goes hard and drivers lose power brake assist. GM claims failure is graceful (ABS/stability remain until vehicle stops). NHTSA says driver reports contradict this: drivers report IMMEDIATE loss of brake assist DURING braking, not after. That distinction is the difference between "inconvenient" and "you crash through a storefront."

### 3. Incident Details (from NHTSA complaints / reporting)
- One 2025 Lyriq driver lost brakes pulling into parking space, crashed through storefront, came to rest "mid-way in the store"
- One 2024 Blazer EV driver deliberately steered into concrete curb to avoid "catastrophic intersection collision"
- Drivers report "hard brake pedal" followed by "Brake System Failure" message at start-up or while driving

### 4. GM vs NHTSA Disagreement
- GM says: spindle fracture causes failure, but ABS/stability control/traction control remain until vehicle fully stops
- NHTSA says: complaints allege IMMEDIATE loss of brake assist during braking, inconsistent with GM's description
- This matters: sudden loss of brake assist during braking = extended stopping distance = crash risk

### 5. What eBoost Is
- Electronic brake boost system, introduced ~2019
- Replaces traditional mechanical vacuum-assisted brake booster
- Uses electronic motor to provide brake pedal assist
- Allows GM to adjust brake feel across driving modes
- When it fails, driver must provide all braking force manually (3-4x harder pedal effort)
- Supplied by Continental Automotive

### 6. Engineering Analysis = Pre-Recall Step
- Engineering Analysis is NHTSA's highest investigation level before deciding whether to seek a compelled recall
- This is the step before NHTSA can order GM to recall if GM won't do so voluntarily
