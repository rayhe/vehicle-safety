## The angle
A guardrail's blunt end is a spear aimed at oncoming traffic. Engineers solved this with "end terminals" that accordion, ribbon, or telescope on impact. Twice in the last decade, the terminals themselves became the killing mechanism: Trinity's secretly-modified ET-Plus, and Lindsay's X-LITE. The body count is small in absolute terms and enormous in irony: hardware installed at taxpayer expense to absorb crashes, impaling drivers instead. The ET-Plus story adds a fraud arc — a $2-per-unit cost cut, a $663M verdict, overturned on appeal, product quietly retired anyway.

## Key facts (all sourced)

1. **Guardrail death toll, national:** Insurance Information Institute table archive (NHTSA-sourced FARS data) lists "guard rail" as the fixed object struck in roughly 850–1,036 fatal crashes per year across recent years (e.g., 977 in one year, 1,036 in another, 932, 851), ~2.5–2.8% of all fatal crashes. One severity table: 909 fatal / 28,000 injury / 75,000 property-damage-only guardrail crashes in a year.
   - https://www.iii.org/table-archive/21904

2. **Barrier deaths, broader:** Virginia Tech study (VTechWorks) of 18 FARS case years: average 375 barrier-tripped rollover fatalities + 421 most-harmful-event=barrier fatalities per year, 1994–2011 — ~800 barrier-related deaths annually, "in crashes with hardware designed to prevent harm."
   - https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0913ee25-5099-401c-ae75-1cb5a2899372/content

3. **End terminals are the lethal part:** Plaintiff-side analysis (Fibich, Leebron, Copeland & Briggs) of FARS 2009–2013: 947 fatal guardrail crashes in five years; claims end-terminal impacts have a fatality rate ~3x that of guardrail-face impacts. ATTRIBUTION NOTE: plaintiff law firm figure, not peer-reviewed — state as such.
   - https://fibichlaw.com/blog/why-947-fatal-guardrail-crashes-demand-houstons-top-legal-representation/

4. **Old FHWA end-crash study** (NTL repository): guardrail-end accidents — of exposed-end crashes where the vehicle then struck a fixed object, 23% involved death or incapacitating injury; turned-down ends 38%. (1988–1991 data; dated, use for historical texture only.)
   - https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/89198/dot_89198_DS1.pdf

5. **Trinity ET-Plus — the $2 change:** In 2005 Trinity Industries changed the ET-Plus end terminal's guide-channel width from 5 inches to 4 inches without telling FHWA. Internal documents: saved ~$2 per unit manufactured. The change allegedly made the terminal lock up and spear through vehicles instead of ribboning away.
   - https://www.cochranfirm.com/washington-dc/practice-areas/trinity-guardrail-injury-lawsuit/

6. **The whistleblower case:** Joshua Harman (False Claims Act qui tam). Oct 20, 2014: federal jury found Trinity defrauded the government — $175M damages, trebled to $525M. June 2015: Judge Rodney Gilstrap entered final judgment ~$663M ($525M trebled damages + $138.4M civil penalties; AASHTO Journal reported $682.4M). Largest whistleblower verdict won without DOJ assistance at the time.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Harman
   - https://www.tsp2.org/2015/06/15/court-trinity-must-pay-682m-in-guardrail-fraud-case-company-will-contest-it/

7. **Overturned:** Sept 29, 2017, Fifth Circuit (Harmon v. Trinity, No. 15-41172) threw out the judgment — even if the change was undisclosed, the omission was not "material" to the government's payment decisions. ET-Plus discontinued anyway; lost federal-aid reimbursement eligibility Dec 31, 2017 after failing AASHTO MASH crash-test criteria.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Harman
   - https://radiolawtalk.com/blog/highway-guardrail-fraud-scheme-debunked/

8. **ET-Plus casualty allegations:** Lawsuits alleged 5 deaths in at least 14 accidents; an NFL player (49ers) killed April 2014 after his vehicle struck an ET-Plus end terminal and flipped. Trinity said "several hundred thousand" ET-Plus units were installed nationwide.
   - https://damorelaw.com/whistleblowers-lawsuit-guardrail-company-nets-525-million/
   - https://news303.blogspot.com/2014/10/whistle-blower-says-1-billion-isnt_21.html

9. **X-LITE — the second spear:** Lindsay Transportation Solutions' X-LITE end terminal linked to 7 deaths in Tennessee, Missouri, Virginia since 2016. Nov 1, 2016: Hannah Eimers, 17, driving a Volvo S80 on I-75 near Niota, TN, hit an X-LITE end terminal; it penetrated the car and killed her. Six days earlier (Oct 26, 2016), TDOT had removed X-LITE from its qualified products list over performance concerns — but left installed units in place under a replace-only-after-hit policy.
   - https://www.cohenmilstein.com/wp-content/uploads/site/Life is a Highway_Navigating Roadway-Derived Litigation_ABA Journal.pdf (ABA TortSource, Spring 2020)

10. **Aftermath:** TDOT pledged millions to replace all 1,800 X-LITE terminals statewide; Missouri did the same. X-LITE installed in 19–21 states; only TN and MO removed them. The Eimers family was billed ~$3,000 by TDOT for the guardrail that killed Hannah (TDOT called it a processing error). Lindsay sued for "unreasonably dangerous and defective" design. NY Senate Bill 5427A sought a ban. Steve Eimers: "Russian roulette"; "the foxes are guarding the henhouse."

## Original contribution (the $2 arithmetic)
- Trinity's own statement: "several hundred thousand" ET-Plus units installed. Take the low reading: 300,000 units × $2/unit saved = **$600,000** total savings from the 2005 change.
- Final judgment: **$663,000,000**. Ratio: 663,000,000 / 600,000 ≈ **1,105×**. Even at 500,000 units ($1M saved), the verdict was 663× the savings.
- Framing: "Trinity saved about two dollars a guardrail. A federal jury valued the lie at $663 million — more than a thousand times what the inch of steel was worth."
- Second original framing: the six-day gap — TDOT deemed X-LITE unfit for its approved list on Oct 26, 2016, then left the installed units standing; Hannah Eimers hit one on Nov 1. A state can know a device is suspect and still leave it aimed at traffic.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
- After the verdict, FHWA ordered the ET-Plus crash-tested. It passed every test. The jury ruled on fraud — an undisclosed paperwork change — not on whether the terminal was actually defective. The Fifth Circuit agreed the omission wasn't material to the government's buying decision.
- Joshua Harman, the whistleblower, co-founded SPIG Industry, a competing guardrail manufacturer. Trinity called him a "professional plaintiff" with a financial motive. He stood to gain ~$200M from the verdict.
- Guardrails as a category are overwhelmingly net lifesavers: trees and poles kill 2,500+ people a year in fixed-object crashes; barriers stand between run-off-road drivers and those objects. ~800 annual barrier deaths occur in a system that prevents far worse outcomes. The VT study authors treat barrier crashes as the price of redirecting vehicles away from unsurvivable hazards.

## Limitations (dedicated accounting)
- FARS "guard rail" fixed-object codes do not distinguish face impacts from end-terminal impacts, so the national end-terminal death count cannot be read directly from FARS; the ~1,000/year figure is all guardrail impacts.
- The "3× fatality rate" for end vs. face impacts comes from a plaintiff-side law firm's analysis of 2009–2013 FARS data, not a peer-reviewed study. Treat as an allegation-grade estimate.
- Trinity defect causation was never adjudicated: the qui tam case was about false claims to the government. Whether the 4-inch channel actually caused specific deaths was never decided by a court.
- X-LITE death counts (7) come from press reports and lawsuit allegations; TDOT acknowledged telescoping failures in some crashes but the full causal picture is litigated, not settled science.
- The old FHWA end-crash severity study uses 1988–1991 data — pre-modern terminals; useful for the physics (ends are the hard part), not current rates.

## Actionable insights
- A bent, knocked-askew, or soil-buried end terminal is the dangerous configuration — terminals only perform to spec when installed exactly right (Tennessee's own commissioner letter blamed unclear X-LITE install instructions for field failures). If you see a damaged end terminal, report it to the state DOT; most states have a maintenance-report line or web form. A terminal hit once and left leaning is a loaded spear.
- For buyers of nothing in particular: this one isn't about your car. It's about the road. The actionable unit is civic, not consumer — and that's the point of the kicker.
