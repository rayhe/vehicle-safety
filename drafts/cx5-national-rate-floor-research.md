# Research: The CX-5 Floor — What the National Rate Would Be If Every Car Were This Safe

## Angle
Q1 2026 fatality rate hit 0.99/100M VMT (lowest quarterly figure since 2014). NHTSA preliminary data July 2026. The Mazda CX-5 has a FARS death rate of 0.12/100M VMT — one-eighth the national average. If the entire fleet performed like the CX-5, annual traffic deaths would drop from ~38,000 to roughly 4,600. The article explores the "safety floor" — the best-performing vehicles in the FARS dataset — and what it means that we already own the technology to cut road deaths by 88% but choose not to deploy it uniformly.

## Kill Test
**Is this genuinely surprising after 650+ articles?** Yes. We've done sedan-vs-SUV switches and individual vehicle spotlights, but never computed the *national death rate that would result from fleet-wide adoption of the safest vehicles*. This is a counterfactual calculation that quantifies the gap between what we have and what's already achievable.

### The Counterfactual
- National 2025 rate: 1.10/100M VMT
- CX-5 rate: 0.12/100M VMT
- If entire fleet performed at CX-5 level: ~89% reduction
- 2025 deaths: ~38,000 (est). At CX-5 rate: ~4,100
- Gap: ~33,900 lives per year
- That's not future technology. That's a vehicle for sale today at $29,990.

### Why CX-5 is so safe
- IIHS Top Safety Pick+ (2026)
- Good ratings across all crash tests
- Small/nimble enough for good visibility
- Unibody construction with modern crumple zones
- Standard AEB with pedestrian detection
- No massive blind spots from oversized hoods
- Lower hood height = less likely to kill pedestrians
- Mazda named safest brand by Consumer Reports/IIHS metrics

### Limitations
- FARS estimated_rate uses VMT estimates, not odometer readings (±15% uncertainty for low-volume models)
- CX-5's low rate partly reflects its driver demographics: suburban families, moderate income, likely to be sober
- Fleet-wide CX-5 adoption is a thought experiment, not a policy proposal
- Pedestrian/cyclist safety also depends on road design, speed limits, enforcement
- Vehicle age in fleet differs (CX-5 is newer on average than Impala)

### Counterargument
The CX-5's low death rate partially reflects WHO buys it, not just the vehicle's engineering. Middle-income suburban families who buy crossover SUVs drive differently than someone buying a Mustang or Cobalt. Controlling for driver behavior would narrow the gap — but it wouldn't close it. The IIHS crash tests, which don't involve driver behavior at all, confirm the CX-5's structural superiority.
