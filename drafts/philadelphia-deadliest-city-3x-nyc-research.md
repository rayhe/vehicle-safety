# Research: Philadelphia — Deadliest Major City for Drivers

## Topical Hook (July 24, 2026)
Philadelphia Controller Christy Brady report citing NHTSA 2024 data:
- Philadelphia: 9.04 fatalities per 100K population (UP from 8.64 in 2023)
- Chicago: 7.15
- Washington D.C.: 4.73
- Boston: 3.64
- New York City: 2.86
- Philadelphia is 3.2x NYC's rate

## Key Data Points
- Philadelphia Vision Zero created 2016, aims to eliminate traffic deaths
- FY2027 budget: $30M over 6 years for Vision Zero
- Philadelphia rate DOWN from 10+ per 100K in 2020 (pandemic peak)
- Controller: "meaningful progress" but still highest among peers
- Priority: local control over speed limits (PA statewide standards don't fit urban)

## National Context (NHTSA 2025/Q1 2026)
- 2025 national: 36,640 deaths, down 6.7% from 2024
- 2024 national: 39,254 deaths (first time below 40K since 2020)
- Q1 2026 rate: 0.99 per 100M VMT (near-record)
- Speeding: 29% of all fatalities (10,035 in 2025, down 11%)

## Novel Contribution: Cost-per-statistical-life calculation
- Philadelphia population: ~1.6M
- 9.04 per 100K = ~145 deaths/year
- $30M over 6 years = $5M/year
- If $5M/year prevented ALL deaths: $34,482 per life saved
- Federal VSL (Value of Statistical Life): $12.5M (DOT, 2024)
- Even a 10% reduction = ~15 lives/year → $333K per life saved → 37:1 benefit-to-cost
- NYC invested ~$1.7B in Vision Zero since 2014 → rate dropped 25%

## FARS Connection
- National per-capita rate: 39,254 / 330M × 100K = 11.9 per 100K
- Philadelphia at 9.04 is BELOW the national average!
- The comparison is only among major cities
- NYC's 2.86 is extraordinary — density reduces VMT per capita
- Philly's gap with NYC is partly a design/infrastructure problem, partly VMT/capita
