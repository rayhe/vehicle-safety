Honda Recalled Its Own Recall. 4,804 Fixed Cars Have to Come Back.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 15, 2026

Honda has recalled 4,804 Accords and CR-Vs that already had their recall repair, because the replacement tire repair kit nozzles the dealers installed were the same defective part.[1] The fix was the defect. Honda's own paperwork says so, in the passive corporate poetry of a Part 573 safety report: the supplier failed at change-point management.

48
Approximate number of cars actually carrying a defective remedy nozzle, by Honda's own 1% estimate. All 4,804 must return because the warehouse mix-up destroyed traceability.

Back in June, Honda recalled 1,049,883 Accord Hybrids and CR-V Hybrids under campaign 26V-366 after the flat tire repair kit's sealant bottle showed it could build pressure and fire its cap off with enough force to injure bystanders.[2] Eight people were hurt before Honda filed. The prescribed remedy was a redesigned nozzle, one without the one-way valve that turned the bottle into a pressure vessel.

Then the supplier built the new nozzles, failed to keep the old ones out of the warehouse, and shipped a mixture to dealers. Honda's filing describes an absence of disposal controls for pre-countermeasure parts, which is the regulatory way of saying the bad parts and the good parts went into the same bin.[1] Some 4,804 owners did the responsible thing, drove to the dealer, had the recall performed, and drove home carrying the same defect they had just had removed.

Those 4,804 cars are 0.46 percent of the million-car parent recall. Honda estimates only 1 percent of them, about 48 vehicles nationwide, actually contain a bad nozzle. But the mix-up happened in a warehouse, not on a serialized assembly line, so nobody can say which 48. All 4,804 get letters. One of them owns the single 2025 CR-V Fuel Cell EV in the batch, which makes its owner the only person in America returning a hydrogen Honda to the dealer twice over the same bottle of tire sealant.

Here is the strongest defense of Honda: this is the recall system working as designed. A supplier found contamination, traced the population, and filed a second recall without being dragged to it. The 1 percent estimate means 4,756 owners get a precautionary letter about a kit most will never open. Given that eight people were injured before the first recall, a second letter is cheap insurance. All of which is to say, the comedy is in the optics; the conduct is exemplary.

What this article does not establish: Honda did not name the supplier, and the 1 percent figure is Honda's own estimate, not an independent count. No FARS fatality data applies here; nobody died, and this is a story about the recall apparatus, not body counts. And note the final remedy, a nozzle with no valve at all, deletes the feature that caused the pressure trap, which is the most honest engineering on this page.

If you own a 2023–2026 Accord Hybrid or CR-V Hybrid and had the June recall done, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting October 17, when the new population becomes searchable.[3] Owner letters go out November 16. Until the dealer visit, treat the sealant kit with suspicion: the bottle only turns dangerous when the nozzle is connected wrong and the relief valve fails to vent, which is the exact combination this recall is chasing.
