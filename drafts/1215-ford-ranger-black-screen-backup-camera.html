The Gap

The Backup Camera That Vanishes When the Radio Dies

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
Tuesday, November 9, 2027

Before you sign the paperwork on that new Ranger, check the glove box for a letter Ford started mailing in October. It tells you something no automaker should have to tell a buyer: your federally mandated backup camera may disappear without warning, and we do not have a fix. NHTSA campaign 26V605 covers 11,405 model-year 2026 Rangers, and Ford estimates 47 percent of them, roughly one in every two, carry the defect.[1][2]

47%
Of the 11,405 recalled Rangers estimated to carry the screen-killing capacitor defect

Read that again slowly: it is not a camera recall, because nothing is wrong with the camera. The 10.1-inch SYNC 4 center stack screen, the same slab of glass that runs your navigation and your playlists, can lose power because of a suspect capacitor lot on its circuit board. When the screen dies, the rearview camera image dies with it, because the two live on the same hardware. Shift into reverse and the display shows nothing, so the truck fails Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111, Rear Visibility, and federal law calls it noncompliant.[1]

Here is the part that should make you angry on behalf of every driveway in America. The backup camera has been mandatory equipment since 2018. NHTSA pushed FMVSS 111 through because backover crashes kill about 210 people and injure 15,000 every year, and the victims skew cruelly young and old: children under five account for 31 percent of backover deaths, adults over seventy for 26 percent.[4] A capacitor, the part that costs less than the floor mats, just vetoed the safety mandate.

Ford's own filing makes the screening problem worse. Affected trucks were built between April 21 and September 3, 2026, but Ford traced the suspect capacitor lot through circuit-board serial numbers all the way to individual VINs, and says the bad units are not in VIN order.[2] Translation: build date will not tell you if your truck is one of them; only a VIN lookup will. This is also, for the record, Ford's 77th recall of 2026.[3] Fifteen warranty claims have come in potentially tied to the condition. Ford says it knows of no crashes or injuries,[3] which is lucky so far.

Now the practical part, because righteous anger does not spot a toddler behind your tailgate. With the remedy still under development, Ford's October interim letters are effectively a warning with no appointment attached.[1] Do not call your dealer and book service expecting parts; call and ask whether the remedy actually exists yet. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now, tonight, before you drive the kids to school tomorrow. And until the repair lands, drive like it is 2017 again: walk around the truck in the driveway, check the blind zone on foot, and assume the screen owes you nothing. That camera was supposed to be the safety net under the federal mandate. The screen just pulled the net down with it.

Ford should never have wired a mandated safety device through its entertainment system, and NHTSA should ask the obvious question in its next rulemaking: how many other mandated systems share a circuit board with the radio? You paid for a safety feature with the force of federal law behind it. You got one whose continued existence depends on a capacitor nobody inspected. That is the gap, and your VIN is either in it or out of it, so go look.
