A Suspected Counterfeit Capacitor Is Recalling 11,405 Rangers. Ford Has No Fix Yet.

Mia Crumplezone • Safety Engineering Editor
September 27, 2026

Ford's recall report contains a sentence you do not usually see in federal paperwork. Inside the 2026 Ranger's infotainment screen sits a multi-layer ceramic capacitor, the one that can crack and short and take out the backup camera, and according to Ford it "contains materials suspected to be counterfeit and fails to meet engineering specifications." That is Ford, in an official NHTSA filing, telling you a fake part may be wired into the safety equipment of 11,405 brand-new trucks. Campaign 26V605 covers Rangers built from April 21 through September 3, 2026.[1][2]

11,405
2026 Ford Rangers recalled under NHTSA 26V605. Every one carries the suspect capacitor. A remedy does not exist yet.

Run the failure chain, because the chain is the whole story. It sits on the printed circuit board that powers the 10.1-inch SYNC 4 center stack. Crack it and you get a short. The short pops the display fuse; the screen goes black, and with it goes the rear-view camera image, which is the only reason federal law cares about this screen at all. These trucks now fail FMVSS 111, "Rear Visibility." One fake component, maybe two cents at volume, defeats a mandated safety system that the rest of the truck does everything right to support.[2]

Designers made a choice here and it deserves the judgment. Routing the backup camera's display through a single infotainment power fuse means a power problem anywhere on that board becomes a rear-visibility problem everywhere behind the truck. Screens replaced dedicated gauges and telltales because consolidation is cheaper and looks premium. Every consolidation is also a single point of failure, and this recall is what a single point of failure looks like when the one point is built from a part that might not be what it says on the bag.[2]

Counterfeit electronics are not a new anxiety, but they are a growing one. ERAI's 2024 report logged 1,055 suspect counterfeit and nonconforming parts, up 25 percent year over year and the highest count since 2015. ERAI's own incident file lists counterfeit semiconductors that reached braking systems, airbag deployment systems, and high-speed train braking in Europe. A backup-camera display board is not the most dramatic destination on that list. That is exactly the point: the fakes have been climbing the criticality ladder for a decade, and now one may be parked in your driveway.[3][4]

So what do you do? If you bought a 2026 Ranger with the 10.1-inch screen, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; the affected VINs have been searchable since September 25. Expect an interim letter around October 5, which will tell you the risk exists and that Ford will contact you again when there is something to schedule. There is currently nothing to schedule, so do not drive to the dealer expecting a repair; the remedy is still under development. Until then, if your screen goes dark, drive the truck like it has no backup camera, because for legal purposes it doesn't: mirrors, shoulder checks, and a spotter in tight spaces. Ford's number for this campaign is 26C42, customer service 1-866-436-7332. And if you are shopping used in a year, a dead screen on a Ranger is not a cosmetic flaw. It is a truck that fails a federal safety standard.[1][2]
