GM Recalled the Sierra for Flying Grille Parts. The Grille Parts Kept Flying.

By Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2027

GM recalled 70,768 Sierra 1500s because the upper grille deflector could detach at speed. On October 9, 2026, NHTSA opened recall query RQ26003 anyway. ODI has 118 complaints of the deflector detaching while driving, and 21 of them came from trucks that had already received the recall fix.[1]

A recall query is NHTSA's audit tool for recalls. It asks two questions: did the recall cover the right vehicles, and does the repair actually work? The original recall, 25V-060, filed February 6, 2025, covered only 2022 Sierra 1500s with chrome deflectors built after the mid-cycle refresh. Coverage spans the 2022 through 2024 model years, an estimated 535,639 trucks. That is 7.6 times the population GM recalled. The recall reached 13.2 percent of the trucks now under review.[2]

The paper trail has a subplot. On January 19, 2024, a GM employee flagged a detached deflector through the company's internal Speak Up for Safety program. GM opened an investigation on February 27, then closed it on May 23, finding no defect trend. When warranty claims started climbing, the issue was reopened on December 11, 2024, and the recall was filed 57 days later. Thirteen months from employee flag to recall filing. Seven of them spent with the case closed.[3]

21
complaints say the deflector detached after recall 25V-060 was already performed — 17.8% of NHTSA's complaint file

The hardware story is simple. Eight attachment points hold the deflector; they fracture over time, and if all eight break, the part departs the truck. GM says drivers may hear a rattle from the front first. The complaints describe windshields cracked by the flying trim and nearby drivers swerving to avoid it, and NHTSA's opening summary describes the detachment as occurring "without detectability." No crashes, injuries, or fatalities are listed in the file. Yet.[1]

Fairness requires the rest of the ledger. NHTSA has not found a defect, ordered an expansion, or declared the remedy inadequate; a query is an audit, not a verdict, and many close quietly. One hundred eighteen complaints across 535,639 trucks is 0.022 percent. GM told NHTSA the 2023 and 2024 trucks used parts outside the supplier's suspect window, which is precisely the claim under review. And the post-repair complaints cannot tell us a failure rate, because complaints are not a census of repaired trucks. Caveats noted, the pattern is hard to admire: a fix under audit, a recall covering an eighth of the population in question, and a repair rate of about 64 percent as of January 2026, leaving roughly 25,500 recalled trucks still unfixed.[4]

13.2%
of the 535,639 trucks in the probe population were covered by the original recall 25V-060

If you drive a 2022 Sierra 1500 with the chrome grille, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and check whether 25V-060 is open or was completed. If the fix is done, listen for a rattle from the front at speed and do not assume the dealer visit settled it. If you drive a 2023 or 2024 Sierra, your truck was excluded from the recall entirely; if the deflector rattles or lets go, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov, because owner complaints are what opened this file in the first place.
