# Research — #1485: The RV Recliner Fire Trio Was a Quartet. The Fourth One Is the Biggest.

## Story in one line
Four RV makers filed recalls in twelve days with the identical NHTSA sentence about a power-seat cord fuse that cannot be trusted to stop a fire, and the biggest filer, Forest River with 6,275 trailers, was the one nobody's story included: the trio this site counted on November 2 was a quartet, and the missing fourth is 58 percent of the total.

Forest River's recall (NHTSA 26V704, filed Oct 22, 2026) covers 6,275 RVs and trailers across ten model lines, and trade-press roundups surfaced it November 9-10 (Trucks, Parts, Service; RV News). The defect is a fire risk in the room people sleep in, the remedy is not available yet, and owner letters do not go out until December 21.

## Key verified facts
- **The four campaigns, one sentence.** All four NHTSA filings carry the identical defect description: "The power cord fuse may not provide adequate circuit protection." All four carry the identical consequence: "In the event of a short circuit, inadequate protection increases the risk of a fire."
- **The quartet census:**

| Campaign | Maker | Units | Filed | Remedy | Letters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 26V680 | Alliance RV | 3,686 | Oct 16, 2026 | Replace power cord | Dec 7, 2026 |
| 26V704 | Forest River | 6,275 | Oct 22, 2026 | Inspect + replace power cord | Dec 21, 2026 |
| 26V710 | Heartland RV | 518 | Oct 27, 2026 | Replace harness AND cord | Nov 30, 2026 |
| 26V714 | Ember RV | 259 | Oct 28, 2026 | Replace power cord | Nov 23, 2026 |
- **Total: 10,738 trailers.** Forest River alone is 6,275, or 58.4 percent of the total, and 1.4x the other three combined (4,463). The trio piece counted 4,463. The real count is 2.4x that.
- **Twelve days.** All four filings landed between October 16 and October 28, 2026. Four competing manufacturers, same component family, same failure mode, same fortnight.
- **Forest River's reach.** Ten model lines, six model years: 2022-2027 Cedar Creek, 2023-2027 FR3, 2023-2026 Palomino, 2024 Cardinal, Wildcat, Shasta Oasis, 2024-2026 Hardside, Palomino Real-Lite Camper, 2026 Puma Tracks, and Puma. The defect is not a bad week at one plant; it is a component family across the industry's largest RV maker.
- **No fix yet.** Per the Margarian summary of the filing, no remedy is currently available to Forest River owners; dealers will inspect and replace the cord once the program starts, and letters go out December 21, 2026, two months after filing. Ember owners heard November 23, Heartland November 30, Alliance December 7. Forest River owners wait longest and number the most.
- **No fire, injury, or crash is listed in the NHTSA summaries for any of the four campaigns.** All four are preventive recalls, filed before the fuse failed in the field, as far as the public record shows.
- **The remedy split still holds.** Heartland alone replaces the harness and the cord; the other three replace the cord only. Heartland found more of the problem than its competitors did, or its harness is part of the problem. Either way, three makers treat the cord as the whole defect and one treats it as half.

## Original contribution (Clara's math and synthesis)
1. **The quartet census, and the correction.** The site's own November 2 piece counted three makers and 4,463 trailers. The count was wrong, and the error is the story: the missing fourth filer is bigger than the three counted makers combined. 10,738 is the real number, and Forest River is 58 percent of it. A reader who saw the trio piece and checked only those three brands would have missed the largest recall of the four.
2. **The identical-sentence test.** Four separate manufacturers filed four separate recalls with the identical NHTSA defect sentence, word for word. That does not happen when four companies make four independent mistakes. It happens when they buy the same component family from the same supply chain. The filings do not name the seat supplier, and this piece does not guess one, but the wording is the fingerprint: same cord, same fuse, same failure, four badges.
3. **The waiting gap, quantified.** Forest River owners filed October 22 and hear December 21: sixty days with a known fire risk in the fuse of the seat they sit on, and no remedy available in the meantime. Across all four makers, the letter dates stretch from November 23 to December 21, which means the last owners to learn about this are the most numerous ones. The recall system notifies in the order the paperwork clears, not in the order of risk.
4. **The bedroom problem.** A car-seat fuse is a nuisance. An RV power-seat fuse is the only thing between a short circuit and a fire in a box people sleep in, far from a fire station, with one narrow door out. The stakes are not the cord; they are the room the cord runs through.

## Limitations (what this story does NOT prove)
- The filings do not name a shared seat or cord supplier, and this piece does not identify one. The identical wording strongly suggests a shared component family, but that is inference from the filings, not a finding in them.
- No fire, injury, or crash is listed in the NHTSA summaries, so the real-world failure rate is unknown. The fuse may never have failed in the field; the recall is preventive on the public record.
- The 10,738 total counts potentially affected units, not confirmed defective units. The Part 573 filings estimate the population with the component, not the number of fuses that will actually fail.
- Forest River's "no fix currently available" is per the November summary of the filing; the remedy program may have started since. Owners should check the VIN rather than assume the December date still holds.
- The quartet framing covers the four campaigns with this exact defect sentence filed in October 2026. Other RV seating recalls with different wording are outside this count.

## Strongest counterargument
This may be the system working, not failing. Four manufacturers found the same marginal fuse, filed within twelve days of each other, and recalled before any fire was reported. The identical wording could reflect NHTSA's standardized defect language rather than a shared supplier, and the staggered letter dates could reflect printing and mailing logistics rather than indifference. Forest River's sixty-day gap looks bad, but the alternative, mailing letters before dealers have cords to install, strands owners with a warning and no fix. The counterargument has force: nobody was hurt, the fuse was caught by the process, and a quartet of preventive recalls is arguably the recall system at its best. The reply is that the system still left the largest group waiting longest, and a fuse whose only job is stopping fires should not need four recalls to get right.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2022-2027 Cedar Creek, 2023-2027 FR3, 2023-2026 Palomino, 2024 Cardinal, Wildcat, Shasta Oasis, 2024-2026 Hardside or Palomino Real-Lite Camper, or a 2026 Puma or Puma Tracks? Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, referencing campaign 26V704 or Forest River 51-2109. Do not wait for the December 21 letter.
- Own a 2025-2027 Alliance Valor or 2026-2027 Delta, a Heartland Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, or Road Warrior, or a 2024 Ember E-Series? Same check: campaigns 26V680, 26V710, and 26V714. Ember letters went out November 23, Heartland November 30, Alliance December 7.
- The fix is free at the dealer in every case: inspect and replace the power cord (plus the harness, for Heartland). Get it done before the next trip where anyone sleeps in the trailer.
- Buying a used RV from any of these model years? The VIN check is the only reliable screen. A fuse is not visible in a walkthrough, and a seller has no reason to mention a recall letter they may never have opened.
