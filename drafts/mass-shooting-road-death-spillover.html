Mass Shootings Kill People Who Never Heard the Shots

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
August 27, 2026
14.3%
Spike in fatal traffic crashes nationwide the day after a major mass shooting, according to a 2026 JAMA Psychiatry study analyzing FARS data from 2008 through 2023.

Researchers at Brigham and Women's Hospital examined the ten deadliest mass shootings in the United States between 2008 and 2023 and found something that should change how you think about your commute: fatal traffic crashes jumped an average of 14.3 percent the day after each event, adding roughly twenty extra deaths per shooting nationwide.[1] Not near the crime scene. Everywhere.

Eight of ten events showed the spike. The cumulative extra road fatalities across all ten shootings equaled approximately 75 percent of the people killed in the shootings themselves, yet not one of those traffic deaths appears in any gun-violence database, any congressional hearing tally, or any memorial fund accounting.[1] They are filed in FARS as ordinary crashes on ordinary roads on ordinary days that happened to follow massacres broadcast live to 330 million phones.

Patel and colleagues tracked Google Trends data alongside the fatality spikes and found that peak search interest for each shooting coincided almost exactly with the peak in road deaths the following day, which suggests a mechanism that transportation researchers have largely ignored: emotional saturation.[1] NHTSA tracks alcohol impairment, drug impairment, speeding, and distracted driving as crash factors in FARS, but there is no checkbox for grief, no field for doomscrolling, and no code for a driver who cannot stop thinking about a school full of dead children while merging onto I-95.

The effect held across age groups, genders, urban and rural settings, and weather conditions, which eliminates the most obvious confounders and points toward something systemic rather than demographic. Consider the strongest counterargument: ten events is a small sample, correlation does not prove causation, and other mass-media emotional shocks like celebrity deaths or natural disasters might produce similar patterns unrelated to gun violence specifically. Fair enough. But the consistency across eight of ten events, the Google Trends correlation, and the geographic dispersion all survived the researchers' quasi-experimental controls. FARS logged approximately 38,000 annual traffic deaths during the study period.[2] Twenty extra deaths in a single day represent a signal large enough to clear statistical noise.

So after the next mass shooting dominates your news feed, consider skipping non-essential driving for twenty-four hours, or at the very least, put the phone in the glove box and turn off news radio before you start the engine. Grief is a form of impairment that no breathalyzer catches and no ADAS system mitigates, and the twenty people who will die in the invisible second wave tomorrow do not know yet that they are part of a body count nobody is keeping. Pull over if you cannot focus, and take transit if it is available, because your commute is not worth becoming a footnote in a dataset that will never connect your death to the bullet fired six states away.
