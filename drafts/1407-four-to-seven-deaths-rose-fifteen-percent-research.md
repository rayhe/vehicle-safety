# Research: #1407 — Deaths Fell 4.3% in 2024. For 4-to-7-Year-Olds, They Rose 15%.

## Angle
In the year American roads got safer for nearly every age group, the two youngest
brackets went the wrong way. Children under 1 rose 17% (65 to 76 deaths) and
children aged 4 to 7 rose 15% (212 to 244), while total traffic deaths fell 4.3%.
The 4-to-7 group is booster-seat age, and a third of the 4-to-7-year-olds killed
as vehicle occupants were unrestrained. The spike interrupts a decade of
improvement for that group (down 13% since 2015).

## Verified facts (all from sources above)
- Total traffic deaths 2024: 39,254, down 4.3% from 41,025 in 2023 (DOT HS 813 791).
- Child deaths (age 14 and younger): 1,032 in 2024, up 1% from 1,023 in 2023,
  down 10% from 1,144 in 2015. 3% of all traffic deaths. 3 children killed and
  an estimated 439 injured every day.
- Child deaths by age group, 2023 to 2024 (DOT HS 813 811):
  under-1 +17% (65 to 76); 1-to-3 -12% (193 to 170); 4-to-7 +15% (212 to 244);
  8-to-12 -3% (315 to 305); 13-to-14 flat (238 to 237).
- 4-to-7 trend 2015 to 2024: down 13% (282 to 244). Under-1 trend 2015 to 2024:
  up 33% (57 to 76). 13-to-14 up 19% over the decade (199 to 237).
- Of the 1,032 child deaths: 783 (76%) occupants, 249 (24%) nonoccupants;
  172 child pedestrians, 56 child pedalcyclists.
- Restraint: of 677 child passenger-vehicle occupants killed, restraint known
  for 585, of whom 229 (39%) were unrestrained. For 4-to-7 killed occupants:
  170 total, 98 restrained, 50 unrestrained, 22 unknown (34% unrestrained
  of known). 13-to-14 killed occupants: 57% unrestrained of known, the worst
  of any child group.
- Driver correlation (Table 2): when the driver was restrained, 71% of the
  killed children were restrained; when the driver was unrestrained, 65% of
  the killed children were also unrestrained.
- Child safety seats cut fatal injury 71% for infants under 1 and 54% for
  toddlers 1 to 4 in passenger cars; seat belts cut fatal injury 45% for
  front-seat occupants 5 and older in cars.
- Alcohol: 221 (21%) of the 1,032 child deaths were in alcohol-impaired
  crashes. 160,850 children injured in 2024.
- Nonmotorist deaths age 5-9: 75 in 2024 vs 57 in 2023 (+32%), FARS Trends.
  Pedestrians killed age 5-9 in 2024: 48, of whom 35 (72.9%) at
  non-intersections.
- School-transportation 10-year review: 204 school-age children killed
  2015-2024; 55% of school-age pedestrians killed were ages 5 to 10; 66% of
  school-age pedestrian deaths away from intersections; deadliest hour
  7:00-7:59 a.m.

## Original calculations (The Crash Report)
- Divergence: overall deaths -4.3% vs 4-to-7 deaths +15% = a 19.4-point gap,
  the widest age-group divergence in the 2024 report.
- Share: 4-to-7 deaths were 23.6% of all child deaths in 2024 (244 of 1,032),
  up from 20.7% in 2023 (212 of 1,023).
- Unrestrained share: 50 of the 148 known-restraint 4-to-7 occupant deaths
  (34%) were unrestrained; applying the 71%/54% effectiveness figures, a
  restrained 4-to-7-year-old in the same crashes would have had roughly
  half the fatality risk.
- Combined youngest-bracket toll: under-1 plus 4-to-7 deaths rose from 277
  in 2023 to 320 in 2024 (+43 deaths, +15.5%), while every other child age
  group fell or held flat.

## Strongest counterargument
Small numbers. The 4-to-7 jump is 32 additional deaths, and the under-1 jump
is 11; a single multi-fatality crash can move a bracket like this by several
percent, and the 2024 file is an Annual Report File subject to revision when
finalized. The decade trend for 4-to-7 is still down 13%, and total child
deaths are down 10% since 2015. This may be noise, not a turn. The article
must say so plainly and not present one year as a trend.

## Limitations
- FARS counts deaths within 30 days on public roadways only; children who die
  later, or in driveways and parking lots, are excluded (see the site's
  two-death-counts piece for the full gap).
- Restraint use is unknown for 14% of killed child occupants; the 39%
  unrestrained figure is of known cases only.
- FARS records restraint presence, not correct installation; a misused car
  seat counts as restrained.
- The fact sheet does not break the 4-to-7 deaths down by occupant vs
  nonoccupant, so the restraint story covers only the occupant share.

## Actionable takeaways
- Booster-seat age is 4 to 7: a child belongs in a booster until the seat belt
  fits properly, typically 4 feet 9 inches tall and 8 to 12 years old. A belt
  that rides up on the belly is a belt that will injure, not protect.
- The driver's belt is the child's belt: when the driver was unrestrained,
  65% of the children who died were unrestrained too. Buckle yourself first.
- School-age pedestrians die away from intersections (66%) and in the morning
  (7-8 a.m. peak). Slow down on residential streets before school, not just
  in marked school zones.
