The Deadliest Car in America Has 147 Horsepower

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 2, 2026

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute. The single deadliest vehicle in the United States, measured in deaths per mile traveled, is not a Hellcat. It is not a Mustang. It is a Hyundai Veloster, and the base engine made 147 horsepower, roughly the output of a midrange lawn tractor. Ten years of federal crash data, 337 models ranked, and the winner of the death lottery is a discontinued three-door hatchback that cost less than a used Corolla.[1]

8.54
Veloster deaths per 100M miles, highest of 337 models in FARS 2014–2023

Let the scale of this sink in. Every parent fears the Mustang, which posts 6.02, and the Veloster beats it by 42 percent. Across the sports car class, the average is 1.95. The Veloster dies at 4.4 times the class average and 8.4 times the sedan average. Five hundred ninety-eight people died in Velosters over the study window, 884 fatal crashes, from a fleet of about 87,500 cars.[1]

Your first theory is speed. Kill it. The volume-selling Veloster carried a naturally aspirated 2.0-liter four making 147 horsepower, a powertrain described in period reviews as underpowered. This car could not outrun its own reputation, let alone anyone else's. Your second theory is impairment. Kill that too. Veloster drivers test positive for alcohol or drugs in just 17.4 percent of fatal crashes, a thoroughly unremarkable figure in a dataset where some models clear 30.[1]

What remains is the part nobody puts in the brochure: the car is a costume. The Veloster was styled like a hot hatch, sold like a hot hatch, parked by its buyers with the swagger of a hot hatch, and driven by people who had purchased the fantasy of speed at a price point where the engine could not deliver it. Add the 275-horsepower Veloster N halo variant pouring aggressive drivers into the same model stats, a short wheelbase and low mass that forgive nothing when the physics get real, and young first-car buyers who treat traffic like a lobby they are trying to clip through. Horsepower was never the variable. The driver bought the movie poster and then tried to live in the movie.[2][3]

The universe has a sense of humor, and it is dark. Hyundai discontinued the Veloster after 2022, so the only way to buy one now is used, which is exactly where it gets dangerous: a cheap, sporty-looking hatch for a teenager, recommended because "it's only 147 horsepower." Read the rate again. Eight point five four. The shape of the car turned out to matter more than what was under the hood. If a Veloster is on your shopping list, check its IIHS ratings (it never earned a Top Safety Pick), run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and understand that you are not buying 147 horsepower. You are buying everything the previous owner did with it.[4]
