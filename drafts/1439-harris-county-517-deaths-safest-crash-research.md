Harris County killed 517 people on the road in 2025, more than any other Texas county and more than Dallas and Bexar counties combined, yet a crash in Harris County was nearly six times less likely to kill than one in Polk County. Houston crashes constantly and survives; rural Texas crashes rarely and dies.

## Verified facts

### Harris County, 2025 (TxDOT CRIS via Reyes, export processed Sept 25, 2026)
- **517 deaths**, most of any Texas county; runner-up Dallas 280. Harris alone out-killed Dallas (280) + Bexar (195) combined (475).
- Population 5,009,302; **10.32 deaths per 100,000 residents**, 3rd among the Big Six (Travis 10.71, Dallas 10.54 lead).
- **124,339 crashes** and 516 deaths in the companion Reyes facts analysis = **4.15 deaths per 1,000 crashes**. County table lists 517; facts piece lists 516 — a one-death wobble between two extractions of the same CRIS database, processed months apart. Disclosed in limitations.
- Harris share of Texas: 124,339 / 618,706 crashes = **20.1% of all crashes**, but 517 / 3,814 deaths = **13.6% of all deaths**. Over-represented in crashes, under-represented in deaths.
- 340.7 crashes per day in the county; one death every 16.9 hours.
- 2,758 suspected serious injuries in the county (Simmons & Fletcher, TxDOT data).

### The lethality gap (Reyes facts piece, same CRIS export)
- **Polk County: 1,021 crashes, 25 deaths = 24.49 per 1,000 — 5.9x Harris County's 4.15.**
- Rural Texas: 135,549 crashes (22%) and 1,717 deaths (45%); lethality **12.67 per 1,000 outside city limits vs 4.34 inside** — 2.9x.
- 15 of 254 counties = 50.1% of all Texas deaths; 9 counties (Harris, Dallas, Bexar, Tarrant, Travis, El Paso, Collin, Williamson, Montgomery) = 1,590 deaths, 41.7%. 27 counties recorded zero deaths.
- Speed ladder: 75-mph roads 21.50 deaths per 1,000 crashes vs 30-mph streets 3.19 (6.7x). Rural roads posted 70+ mph: 27.08 per 1,000 vs urban streets at 35 mph or less: 2.81 — **9.6x**.
- 2 a.m. is the deadliest hour (19.45 per 1,000 vs 2.86 at 4 p.m., 6.8x); 2 a.m. Monday the deadliest hour of the week. Christmas Day: 905 crashes, 15 fatal, 16.57 per 1,000 — deadliest single day.

### City of Houston, 2025 (TxDOT Crashes in Cities and Towns, processed April 7, 2026, via Steve Lee)
- **60,317 crashes** — 250 more than Dallas (24,677) + Fort Worth (15,680) + El Paso (14,725) + Arlington (4,985) combined (60,067). 2.4x Dallas alone.
- 165 crashes per day; one every 8.7 minutes.
- **273 fatal crashes, 293 deaths** — more than any other Texas city; more than Dallas (172) + Fort Worth (112) combined (284).
- 1.073 deaths per fatal crash, highest of any major Texas city (statewide city average 1.058).
- 1,249 suspected serious injury crashes — 13.4% of the statewide city total.
- Fatal-crash rate 4.53 per 1,000 crashes, below the statewide city average of 4.63 and well below Austin (8.52), Fort Worth (6.95), Dallas (6.69). Houston crashes were less likely to be fatal than crashes in Austin, Dallas, or Fort Worth — there were just far more of them.
- Houston = 14.7% of all Texas city crashes, 14.6% of city traffic deaths.
- 17x more fatal crashes than Baytown, Pearland, League City, and Missouri City combined (273 vs 16).
- Cross-check: Reyes' later CRIS export counts Houston at 67,076 crashes and 304 deaths, a higher crash total than the Cities and Towns report's 60,317, but lands on the same 4.53 fatality rate. Two cuts of the same database agree on the rate; the absolute counts differ by processing date and geographic definition. Disclosed in limitations.

### Pedestrians and the homicide comparison (Axios Houston via Simmons & Fletcher, May 2026)
- Houston 2025: **99 pedestrian deaths, 10 cyclist deaths**; 280 fatal crashes citywide, 154 on city-operated streets rather than interstates.
- Leading contributing factor cited by police: a driver's failure to maintain a single lane.
- 2024 comparison: Houston recorded more than 300 traffic deaths against 330 reported homicides (murder, manslaughter, justifiable homicide). Traffic was closing in on murder as a cause of death in Houston.
- Statewide: 724 pedestrians killed in 2025; 84% died away from an intersection; 76% died in the dark.

### Statewide context (TxDOT CRIS via Reyes; NHTSA 2025 early estimates)
- Texas 2025: **618,706 crashes, 3,814 deaths, 17,975 suspected serious injuries**. One crash every 51 seconds; one death every 2 hours 18 minutes.
- No deathless day on Texas roads since November 7, 2000 (TxDOT #EndTheStreakTX).
- National: 36,640 estimated U.S. deaths in 2025, down 6.7% from 39,254 in 2024; rate 1.10 per 100M VMT.

## Original contribution
The volume-vs-severity decomposition, computed here for the first time on this site: Harris County is 20.1% of Texas crashes but 13.6% of Texas deaths, the exact inverse of rural Texas (22% of crashes, 45% of deaths). The county that kills the most Texans is, per crash, one of the safest places in the state to wreck — and the 5.9x Polk County ratio plus the 9.6x high-speed-rural-vs-low-speed-urban ratio turn a body-count story into a speed-and-geography story. The Harris > Dallas + Bexar combined comparison is also new.

## Strongest counterargument
Harris County's low lethality could be an artifact of reporting, not safety: Houston's dense freeway network and heavy traffic produce enormous numbers of low-speed fender-benders that inflate the crash denominator, while rural counties under-report minor crashes, deflating theirs. If rural minor crashes were reported at Houston's rate, the gap would narrow. The counterargument has force on the denominator — but it cannot erase the numerator: 517 real deaths is still the largest body count in Texas, and the speed-ladder data (75-mph roads 6.7x deadlier than 30-mph streets, same reporting system) shows lethality tracks speed, not just reporting. Also, the two independent Houston cuts (Cities and Towns vs CRIS export) agree on the 4.53 rate, which argues against a pure reporting artifact.

## Limitations
- Counts come from law-firm compilations of TxDOT's public CRIS database and TxDOT's published reports, not from a direct TxDOT query by this newsroom. The methodology is stated in the sources and the arithmetic checks out, but this is a secondary presentation of a public primary database.
- Processing dates differ across sources (April 7, 2026 for the Cities and Towns report; September 25, 2026 for the Reyes CRIS export), and TxDOT revises as late reports arrive. Harris County deaths read 517 in the county table and 516 in the companion facts analysis; Houston city deaths read 293 (Cities and Towns), 300 (Axios, April), and 304 (Reyes CRIS export). The article uses the published-report figures as primary and discloses the alternates.
- FARS 2014-2023 is the site's usual backbone; this piece uses 2025 TxDOT data, which is preliminary and will be revised in the FARS Final File in early 2027.
- Lethality per 1,000 crashes is a derived rate, not a TxDOT-published metric; it conflates exposure (VMT), speed, and reporting completeness.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you drive in Houston: the danger is volume, not per-crash lethality — 165 crashes a day means your odds of being in one are high even though most are survivable. The fatal risk concentrates after dark and at 2 a.m., and on high-speed roads, not in rush-hour gridlock (4 p.m. is the busiest and one of the safest hours per crash).
- If you drive rural Texas: a crash is 2.9x more likely to kill you than one inside city limits, and 9.6x more likely on a 70+ mph rural road than on a 35-mph-or-less city street. Slow down on two-lane highways; the speed limit is the risk factor.
- Pedestrians: 84% of Texas pedestrian deaths happened away from intersections, 76% in the dark. Cross at intersections, wear something reflective after dark.
- Check the TxDOT county tables for your own county before assuming the big city is the dangerous place — in Texas, the deadliest crashes per mile happen where the fewest people live.
