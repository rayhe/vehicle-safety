# Research: The All-New RAV4 Flunked IIHS's Top Safety Award. The Winner Has Triple the Real-World Death Rate.
**Slug:** 1244-rav4-misses-tsp-kicks-wins-lab | **Journalist:** Clara Rollover | **Kicker:** The Gap

## Verified ratings (IIHS official page go.iihs.org/2026RAV4 + reporting)
- **2026 Toyota RAV4 (redesigned, hybrid/PHEV only):** moderate-overlap front = **Acceptable**. Driver: all G. Rear passenger: head/neck A, chest A, thigh G, restraints/kinematics A. Technical: chest index 36, shoulder belt tension 3.9 kN, rear neck tension 2.1 kN. Small overlap G/G, side G, headlights G, V2V prevention G.
- **2026 RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid:** moderate-overlap = **Marginal** (Acceptable rear chest, Poor rear-passenger restraints/dummy kinematics). The heavier electrified variant protects rear occupants WORSE.
- **2026-27 Audi Q3:** moderate-overlap = Poor; Good elsewhere.
- **2026 Chevrolet Trailblazer:** moderate-overlap = Poor; also Poor V2V prevention, Poor headlights, Marginal pedestrian prevention.
- **2027 VW Jetta:** Good small + moderate overlap, but Marginal headlights/pedestrian prevention; not rated on reminders/V2V.
- **2026-27 Nissan Kicks:** Top Safety Pick. Redesigned 2025 Kicks initially scored **Poor** on moderate overlap; Nissan revised rear seat belts; only builds **after June 2026** qualify. Build-date trap: check the driver's door-jamb certification label.
- Failure mechanism (CarBuzz): RAV4's shoulder belt rides up too far on the rear dummy, raising head/neck/chest injury risk.
- 2026 criteria: Good required in all three crashworthiness tests for any award (previously Acceptable sufficed in moderate overlap).

## FARS 2014-2023 (NHTSA, via local fars_output.js)
- Toyota RAV4: deaths 914, rate **0.19**/100M VMT, fleet 3.76M; impairment: any 18.4% (alc 14.3%, drug 7.6%)
- Hyundai Tucson: rate **0.34**, deaths 669; any-impaired 17.6%
- Nissan Kicks: rate **0.69**, deaths 339; any-impaired 19.3%
- **Kicks/RAV4 ratio: 3.63x.** The only award-winner of the batch has the worst decade fatality rate of the trio.
- (Chevrolet Trailblazer rate 2.83 is nameplate-polluted by the 2002-2009 body-on-frame generation; not used for comparisons.)

## Novel contribution (original to this piece)
1. **Lab/road divergence:** prior Crash Report piece #960 showed IIHS lab and FARS agreeing (Pilot vs Highlander). Here they split: the Kicks wins in the lab with a 0.69 real-world rate; the RAV4 loses in the lab with a 0.19 real-world rate. Award status and decade death rate point opposite directions.
2. **The PHEV penalty:** electrification made the rear seat worse, not better. Standard hybrid = Acceptable; PHEV = Marginal with Poor rear restraints. Same body, different kinematics.
3. **Build-date lottery as consumer hazard:** two award asterisks in recent rounds (Telluride headlights #874, Kicks seat belts) — a buyer can't tell a TSP car from a non-TSP car of the same model year without reading the jamb sticker.

## Limitations
- FARS covers ALL model years of each nameplate (2014-2023 crashes); the 2026 RAV4 and revised Kicks are not in the FARS data at all. The lab/road comparison is generational, not same-vehicle.
- Kicks entered US sale in 2018, so its rate spans fewer model years; also skews toward young/first-time buyers (impairment 19.3%), a demographic confound, not purely engineering.
- IIHS small-overlap RAV4 ratings are manufacturer-test-assigned (Toyota-conducted), not Institute-conducted.
- Moderate-overlap rear-dummy chest index 36 vs RAV4... (no direct comparator published).

## Strongest counterargument
The award miss is arguably noise: RAV4's rear injury measures are Acceptable, not Poor, and the 2026 criteria bar moved under Toyota. A 0.19 fatality rate is one of the lowest on record; real-world outcomes beat the lab. The Kicks award is for a fixed car; its 0.69 rate reflects the pre-fix fleet. Don't over-read a one-grade gap.

## Actionable insights
- Shopping a Kicks: check the door-jamb sticker; only post-June-2026 builds qualify.
- Shopping a RAV4: rear-seat occupants face moderate injury risk; the PHEV is worse than the hybrid.
- If the award matters to you: Tucson (TSP+) is the segment benchmark, per CarBuzz/Autoblog table.
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls regardless.
