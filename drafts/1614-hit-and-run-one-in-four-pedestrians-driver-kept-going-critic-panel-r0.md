## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
Lede lands hard ("nearly five a day, every day, for a year"), pull stat (25%) is the thesis in one number, and the piece has a clean arc: totals that look like good news, the share-break that isn't, the darkness engineering section, the honest counterargument, the actionable close. ## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.8/10

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.7/10
Citations are real and checkable (NHTSA DOT HS 813 791 / 813 818, IIHS Fatality Facts June 2026, GHSA, AAA Foundation 2018). "Leaving the scene is a felony in most states" is broadly accurate for injury/death hit-and-run. The weak spot: "A crash is a civil matter and an insurance claim" over-simplifies (a fatal crash can be criminal even without flight, e.g. vehicular manslaughter/DUI); the intended contrast (flight escalates it) survives, but the sentence as written is not strictly true. No invented statutes, no fabricated case law.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.9/10
Genuine original contribution: the share-break finding (20% → 24% in 2020 → 25% since), the volume-vs-behavior split, the per-day toll calculations (~4.8 pedestrians, ~7.6 people), and the 94% striking-vehicle figure. Cross-checks done: 2,758 total reconciles with NHTSA's 5.3% decline (153/2,911 = 5.26%); 1,749 appears identically in three sources; the 55.7% and 29% increases verified by arithmetic. Limitations stated (30-day FARS window, state-varying classification, CRSS injury estimates, no motive field). Counterargument at full strength. Dings: "exactly since 2020" imprecision (24% in 2020); the 2015 pedestrian total used for the 29% figure differs by 9 between sources (5,486 IIHS table vs 5,495 secondary summary) — the percentage holds either way, but the research note should have named the IIHS-table figure.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
