**Working title:** "109 People Died on Scooters in 2024. FARS Doesn't Count a Single One as a Pedestrian."

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
NHTSA's 2024 Traffic Safety Facts buries a second vulnerable-road-user body count in Table 10: 271 "persons on personal conveyances" killed in 2024, excluded from the headline 7,080 pedestrian deaths. Stand-up/seated scooter deaths jumped 68% in two years (65 to 109) while pedestrian deaths fell, and the single largest subcategory is wheelchair and mobility-aid users (123 dead) — the most invisible victims in American traffic statistics.

## Verified facts
- **FARS 2024 ARF, Table 10:** 271 persons on personal conveyances killed in 2024 = 0.7% of 39,254 total US traffic deaths. Trend: 252 (2022), 238 (2023), 271 (2024).
- **Standing or seated scooter:** 65 (2022) → 81 (2023) → 109 (2024). **+68% in two years.** FARS taxonomy: stand-up scooters (the Lime/Bird-style rentals), seated scooters; e-bikes are coded separately as bicyclists, not in this table.
- **Mobility aid device:** 136 → 105 → 123. Wheelchair and mobility-scooter users killed in traffic. Largest single subcategory in 2024.
- **Non-self-balancing board (skateboard):** 32 → 32 → 22. **Skates:** 0 → 1 → 4. **Self-balancing board:** 1 → 0 → 3.
- **Headline pedestrian deaths 2024:** 7,080 (NHTSA, via medicaldaily). Pedestrian-adjacent total including conveyances: **7,351**. Pedestrian deaths declined ~3% (NHTSA early estimates) while scooter-conveyance deaths rose.
- **CPSC April 2026 micromobility report (2017-2024):** 206 e-scooter deaths; ~80,000 e-scooter ED-treated injuries; all-micromobility ED visits 37,300 (2017) → 149,100 (2024), roughly 4x. Counts are anecdotal/media-based, not nationally representative; 2023-2024 likely incomplete (death-certificate lag up to 2 years).
- **Consumer Reports, Aug 2026 (via Detroit Free Press):** advises against riding e-scooters; tested-model speeds rose 15.5 mph → 28 mph since 2022; standing posture raises headfirst-fall risk; no federal, industry, or independent safety baseline; CR safety advocacy director William Wallace: consumers cannot trust manufacturers are putting safety first. CR's key practices: helmet, stay off phone.
- **NYC Aug 2026 enforcement announcement (via CleanTechnica, Oct 7, 2026):** 14 stand-up scooter deaths in NYC 2017-2025; **52% involved devices capable of exceeding 20 mph**. NYC using capability data to justify enforcement against sellers of prohibited high-speed machines.
- **NYC Council press, Sept 30, 2026:** e-bikes, e-scooters, and mopeds accounted for ~4.5% of all pedestrian injuries 2020-2023 (NYC Comptroller 2024 report); H1 2026: 738 e-bike injuries + 13 fatalities vs 601 + 10 in H1 2025.
- **Germany, Destatis H1 2026 (via particle.news, Sept 24, 2026):** e-scooter deaths 28 (+13 YoY) — the ONLY road-user group with rising fatalities while total deaths fell 9% to 1,233; 15-17yo e-scooter incidents 2,076 (+70%); researcher Siegfried Brockmann: severe cases dominated by single-vehicle crashes; small wheel diameters and curb edges are specific mechanical hazards.
- **NYU Langone Health study, April 2026 (via Milwaukee Journal Sentinel):** electric micromobility trauma injuries rose 2018-2023; pedestrians struck by devices suffer brain injuries at nearly double the rate of riders.
- **LA Children's Hospital data (via medicaldaily, Oct 2026):** kids' e-bike/e-scooter injuries nearly quadrupled; 53% of injured patients riding illegally under CA rules (66% of e-scooter riders); AAP: under-16 should not operate e-scooters; CA requires 16+ with license/permit, helmets 17 and under.
- **AAA E-Ride Ready campaign (June 2026):** free E-Ride Safety Guide; CPSC review found micromobility injuries rose ~23%/yr 2017-2022 (360,800 ED visits, 233 deaths); medical experts: crashes commonly involve vehicles, pedestrians, curbs, mailboxes.
- **Milwaukee 2025:** 1M+ rental e-scooter rides; 266 resident complaints (up from 172); 75% improper parking, 16% sidewalk riding; sidewalk riding illegal there.

## Original contribution (Crash Report cross-tabs)
1. **The taxonomy gap:** FARS person-type coding puts scooter/skateboard/wheelchair deaths in a separate table from pedestrians, so the quoted national pedestrian toll (7,080) understates vulnerable-non-occupant deaths by 271. Summing the two (7,351) and tracking the conveyance subcategory's growth (+68% scooters vs -3% pedestrians) is analysis nobody published.
2. **The mobility-aid stat:** 123 wheelchair/mobility-scooter users killed in 2024 is the category's largest line item and appears in zero news coverage found. The victims least able to dodge traffic are the least counted.
3. **Denominator honesty:** the CleanTechnica point applied to FARS — absolute scooter deaths are rising fast, but per-trip risk needs the trip denominator (Milwaukee: 1M rides in one city in 2025; 48-city analysis: ~0.15 car-miles displaced per shared-scooter trip). Rising deaths can coexist with falling per-trip risk. State both.

## Limitations
- FARS "standing or seated scooter" does not split rental (Lime/Bird) vs privately owned, or electric vs kick scooters. The 109 mixes all of them; it is wrong to write "109 rental-scooter deaths." Frame as the category that contains the rental deaths.
- CPSC fatality counts are anecdotal (media/death-cert surveillance), not nationally representative; 2023-2024 counts likely incomplete.
- FARS 2024 is the ARF (annual report file), subject to revision in the final file.
- German and NYC figures are separate jurisdictions with different exposure; not directly comparable to FARS.
- The fact sheet gives no helmet-use, alcohol, or time-of-day breakdown for the conveyance subcategory, so the drunk-riding and nighttime shares cannot be quantified nationally here.

## Strongest counterargument
271 is 0.7% of US traffic deaths, and the headline trends are good: overall deaths down 4.3%, pedestrian deaths down ~3%, nine straight quarters of declining fatalities at the time. The scooter increase is 44 deaths over two years against 39,254. And per-trip, shared scooters displace car miles (0.15/trip in the 48-city analysis), so the mode may be net safety-positive versus the car trips it replaces. The "not counted" framing is a taxonomy complaint, not a cover-up: FARS does count these deaths, in their own table, and any analyst who wants them can find them in Table 10.

## Actionable insights
- Riding a rental scooter: wear a helmet (head injuries are the dominant severe outcome; NYU: struck pedestrians get brain injuries at ~2x the rider rate, so you endanger others too). Don't drink and ride; don't ride on sidewalks (illegal in most cities, and the complaint data shows it's where the conflicts happen).
- Parents: AAP says under-16 shouldn't operate e-scooters; in CA 16+ with license/permit is the law and 66% of injured e-scooter kids in the LA study were riding illegally. Set the rule before the first ride, not after the ER visit.
- Cities: the NYC finding is the policy lever — 52% of stand-up scooter deaths involved devices capable of 20+ mph. Enforce the speed-cap sale rules you already have; the federal baseline doesn't exist (CR's point), so local enforcement is the whole game.
- Wheelchair and mobility-aid users: 123 killed in 2024. If your city times crosswalks for a 3.5 ft/s walker, the 123 are the cost of that assumption. Reflective gear and lit crossings are the near-term individual actions; the structural one is signal timing.
