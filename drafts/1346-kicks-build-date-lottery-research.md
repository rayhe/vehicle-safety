# Research: #1346 — The Build-Date Lottery (IIHS small-SUV rear-seat retest, Oct 22, 2026)

## Event
On Oct 22, 2026, IIHS released updated moderate-overlap-front crash test results for six small crossovers/sedans, with a twist: the 2026 Nissan Kicks went from Poor to Good after Nissan quietly revised the rear seat belts on units built after June 2026. The revised Kicks earned a 2026 Top Safety Pick award (one rung below Top Safety Pick+; the vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention system rated Marginal).

## The scoreboard (updated moderate overlap front, rear-seat focused)
| Vehicle | Rating |
|---|---|
| 2026-27 Nissan Kicks (built after June 2026) | Good |
| 2027 VW Jetta | Good |
| 2026 Toyota RAV4 | Acceptable |
| 2026 Toyota RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid | Marginal |
| 2026-27 Audi Q3 | Poor |
| 2026 Chevrolet Trailblazer | Poor |

All earned Good in small overlap front and side tests. The difference is the back seat.

## Key details
- IIHS updated the moderate overlap test in 2022 (added a rear-seat dummy behind the driver; 40 mph partial-front contact with barrier) and tightened rear-seat-belt-force scoring in 2024. Back seats had fallen behind front-seat protection for decades.
- Nissan redesigned the Kicks for the 2025 model year; it initially scored Poor. Nissan revised rear seat belts AND seat belt load limiters on builds after June 2026; retest = Good. Only post-June-2026 Kicks qualify for the award. Build date / VIN determines which hardware you get.
- Audi modified the Q3's rear belts after May 2026 (per Wisevoter/Motor1 aggregation) and STILL failed: rear lap belt slipped off the dummy's pelvis onto the abdomen, shoulder belt rode toward the neck, moderate chest-injury risk. Good in every other category; the Poor moderate-overlap alone disqualified it from Top Safety Pick (Good is required).
- Trailblazer: Poor moderate overlap + Poor front crash prevention (vehicle-to-vehicle) + Poor headlights + Marginal pedestrian crash prevention. One of Chevy's cheapest, most popular small SUVs.
- RAV4 PHEV (Marginal) did worse than the RAV4 hybrid (Acceptable); extra weight of the plug-in system is the suspected culprit (lap belt shifted onto abdomen in the PHEV).
- 2026 TSP criteria: Good in moderate overlap front, small overlap front, side; acceptable-or-good headlights on every trim.

## Novel angle (kill-test verdict: PASS)
1. **The build-date lottery.** The rating applies to a production-date slice, not the nameplate. A June 2026 Kicks and a July 2026 Kicks are different cars from a safety standpoint. Same for the Q3's May 2026 belt change. Nobody shopping used cars checks the door-jamb build date. They should.
2. **The cheapest car beat the luxury car.** Kicks starts ~$22,730 MSRP; the 2026 Q3 (single S Line quattro trim) starts $43,700 + $1,295 destination. Roughly half the price, a full two ratings better in the rear seat.
3. **Audi's fix failed where Nissan's worked.** Both automakers revised rear belts mid-year. One earned Good; the other still submarines dummies. Mid-year running changes are now a shadow safety-recall system: no recall, no retrofit, early buyers keep the Poor hardware.
4. **Nameplate-inheritance bias (original FARS cross-tab).** FARS 2014-2023 gives the Trailblazer a 2.83 death rate per 100M VMT, 14x the RAV4's 0.19 and 4x the Kicks' 0.69. But the Trailblazer FARS number is dominated by the 2002-2009 body-on-frame TRUCK (430 deaths in 2003 alone; new crossover: 9-11/yr since 2021). The IIHS Poor is on the new crossover. Two different vehicles, one nameplate, one misleading statistic. Kicks 0.69 vs RAV4 0.19: the real-world morgue says the RAV4 is 3.6x safer per mile than the Kicks despite the Kicks' shiny new Good rating. The lab test and the field data measure different things.

## Counterargument (full strength)
The IIHS moderate-overlap test is one controlled 40 mph scenario with dummies, not real crashes. FARS real-world data shows the Kicks (0.69) is far worse per mile than the RAV4 (0.19) — the vehicle that just "failed" is the one killing fewer people. A Good rear-seat rating does not make the Kicks the safest choice; mass, geometry, and driver demographics dominate real outcomes. Also: mid-year fixes reward late buyers and strand early ones with no remedy — Nissan deserves credit for fixing it, not for the process.

## Limitations
- FARS 2014-2023 covers prior generations (old Kicks, old RAV4, truck-era Trailblazer); rates do not describe the 2026 vehicles tested.
- IIHS ratings are lab evaluations, not field crash outcomes; no exposure denominator.
- Build-date cutoff details (June 2026 Kicks, May 2026 Q3) come from IIHS via auto press; Nissan/Audi have not published the hardware change notices publicly.
- Sample: six vehicles in one test round.

## Actionable takeaways
- Shopping a 2026-27 Kicks? Check the driver's door-jamb build-date sticker: 07/26 or later gets the revised belts and the Top Safety Pick. A 2026 built in May is the Poor version. Same nameplate, different car.
- Shopping used: ratings follow build date, not model year. Always verify.
- RAV4 shoppers: the hybrid's Acceptable beats the PHEV's Marginal; if the plug-in's weight penalty matters to you, the hybrid is the safer pick for rear passengers.
- Q3/Trailblazer: the Q3 aced everything else, so front-seat adults are well protected; the Trailblazer's stack of Poors (moderate overlap, V2V crash prevention, headlights) is the worst combination in the round.
