# Research: DOT Erases Five Proven Cyclist Protections

## Angle
The federal government's FHWA quietly removed five proven safety countermeasures from its official list in July 2026 — including bike lanes, speed cameras, and road diets — while cyclist deaths sit at a 40-year high. The word "bicyclist" was literally deleted from the "pedestrian/bicyclist" section header. This is a policy-data paradox: the agency tasked with road safety is actively dismantling its own evidence-based playbook during a cyclist death crisis.

## Key Data Points

### Cyclist Deaths (NHTSA/FARS)
- 2025: 1,148 cyclist deaths (+4% YoY) — 40-year high (Carscoops/NHTSA, Jul 12 2026)
- 2023: 1,166 (FARS final) — highest since 1980 (League of American Bicyclists)
- 2010: 623 cyclist deaths (modern low point)
- 2010→2025: ~84% increase in cyclist deaths
- 2023: 49,989 cyclist injuries (+8% YoY)
- Florida (234), California (145), Texas (106) = 42% of national count (2023 FARS)

### Overall Traffic Deaths (Contrast)
- 2025: 36,640 total deaths (lowest since 2019, -6.7% from 2024's 39,254)
- Q1 2026: 7,770 deaths (-4.3% YoY), fatality rate 0.99/100M VMT (lowest Q1 since 2014)
- 2025 fatality rate: 1.10/100M VMT (second-lowest ever recorded)

### DOT/FHWA Policy Changes (July 2026)
- FHWA removed 5 of 28 "Proven Safety Countermeasures" from its list (NPR, Jul 16 2026)
- Removed strategies:
  1. Bike lanes — reduce crashes by up to 53%
  2. Speed safety cameras — reduce crashes by 54%
  3. Variable speed limits — reduce freeway crashes by up to 34%
  4. Appropriate speed limits for all users — Seattle saw 26% decrease in fatalities
  5. Road diets — reduce crashes by 19-47%
- The word "bicyclist" deleted from "pedestrian/bicyclist" section header
- No public announcement or explanation; changes found by NPR
- DOT Secretary Sean Duffy called bike lanes "DEI bike lanes"
- Duffy later asked Congress to "restrict" federal grants for bike lanes
- Court ruling (2026): government did NOT dispute that protected bike lanes reduce collisions

### Speed Limit Correlation (League of American Bicyclists / NHTSA)
- 62% of cyclist deaths with reported speed limits occurred on roads ≥40 mph
- 45 mph = most common speed limit where a striking driver killed a cyclist
- Few cyclist deaths on 20 mph speed limit roads (<20 per year)
- 55 mph speed limits = most common for overtaking-driver cyclist fatalities

### International Context
- US per-capita road death rate ~6x higher than UK
- 100+ countries have lower road death rates than US
- Other countries did NOT see similar cyclist death spikes despite smartphone adoption
- US has particularly weak hood-height and pedestrian/cyclist safety requirements

## Original Contribution
Cross-reference the FHWA's own crash-reduction percentages for removed countermeasures against current cyclist death geography. If 62% of cyclist deaths occur on 40+ mph roads (712 of 1,148 in 2025), and bike lanes reduce crashes by up to 53%, the math suggests up to 377 fewer deaths annually if those corridors had protected infrastructure. The government's own archived data proves these measures work — and the government just deleted that data from its website.
