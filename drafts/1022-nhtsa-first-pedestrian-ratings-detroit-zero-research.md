# Research: NHTSA First-Ever Pedestrian Safety Ratings (MY2027) — #1022

**Slug:** 1022-nhtsa-first-pedestrian-ratings-detroit-zero
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — kicker: The Gap)
**News peg:** Sept 22, 2026 — NHTSA released first-ever pedestrian-protection assessments for MY2027 vehicles under NCAP 5-Star program.
**Not in queue:** verified — no existing draft/story covers NCAP pedestrian crashworthiness ratings.

## Facts

- **102 vehicle models from 10 manufacturers** met the new performance criteria in the inaugural round (NHTSA press release, Sept 22, 2026).
- **10 manufacturers = 13 brands:** Subaru, Toyota (+Lexus), Nissan (+Infiniti), Mitsubishi, Mazda, Honda (+Acura), Hyundai (+Kia), Kia, Tesla, Ford. All Japanese/Korean except Tesla and Ford. (Repairer Driven News, Sept 23, 2026)
- **Tesla:** one model only (Model 3). **Ford:** one model only (gas-powered Explorer).
- **Zero GM models** (Chevrolet, GMC, Buick, Cadillac — nothing). **Zero Stellantis models** (Jeep, Ram, Dodge, Chrysler — nothing). **Zero VW Group, BMW, Mercedes, Rivian.**
- **Not every trim level** of listed models meets the criteria.
- Tests measure injury risk to head, hips, legs, knees via three impact scenarios:
  - Child and adult headform impacts to hood and windshield area
  - Upper leg impacts to hood leading edge
  - Full lower-limb impacts to bumper
- Program structure: a **separate category** within NCAP — does not change the 5-star overall rating, which remains crashworthiness-based (mirrors the ADAS pass-fail framework, gcn.com). Lives on NHTSA.gov/ratings, not folded into the window-sticker star rating (Streetsblog critique, Sept 29, 2026).
- **Methodology provenance:** NHTSA's late-2024 final decision based the protocols on Euro NCAP documents (Pedestrian Testing Protocol v8.5, Oct 2018; VRU Assessment Protocol v10.0.3, June 2020). Phase-in planned from MY2026 using **head, upper-leg, and lower-leg impact data voluntarily submitted by manufacturers** — the agency's published plan has the feds scoring manufacturer-submitted data in the first model years.
- **Euro NCAP lag:** Euro NCAP has assessed pedestrian protection since **1997** (29 years). Pedestrian protection folded into the overall star rating in Jan 2009; AEB-for-pedestrians added Nov 2015.
- NHTSA also postponed an NCAP update by one model year (Sept 22, 2025 Federal Register notice) after auto-industry concerns.
- Auto Innovators (John Bozzella): "NHTSA isn't rating new safety technologies fast enough or often enough."

## Novel contribution (original analysis)

1. **The Detroit-absence arithmetic.** Of the three Detroit automakers, exactly one appears, with one model. GM — the largest US auto seller — has zero models meeting the criteria. Stellantis has zero. The companies whose lineups are heaviest on full-size pickups and tall SUVs (the vehicle classes most lethal to pedestrians) are precisely the ones missing from the forgiving-front-end list.
2. **Selection effect framing.** The pass list skews toward brands whose US lineups are car/crossover-heavy. Low hood leading edges score well; a 5,500-lb pickup cannot fake a low hood. The rating doesn't punish trucks — physics does.
3. **Program-design gap vs. Europe.** Europe folds pedestrian protection into the headline star rating printed everywhere. The US version is a side category on a website. Consumers will not see it on the Monroney sticker.

## Kill test: PASS

- Newsworthy: first federal rating of its kind, 8 days old, directly answers "which new cars are safest for pedestrians."
- Novel: nobody has run the brand-list arithmetic (GM/Stellantis zero) or the 29-year Euro lag against this announcement; Streetsblog took a swing at the program design but from the "ban pickups" angle, not the consumer-buying angle.

## Counterargument (full strength)

Pedestrian crashworthiness ratings measure how forgiving a car is *after* it hits someone. The bigger lever is not hitting them at all (pedestrian AEB, lower speeds) and the vehicle's mass/height at impact. The pass list skews toward brands selling smaller vehicles — Subaru and Honda don't sell a full-size pickup, so of course their hoods are lower. GM's absence may reflect its truck-heavy mix more than a safety culture deficit. Also, pedestrian deaths track exposure (walking rates, road design) as much as vehicle design; a rating can't fix a stroad. And 102 models is a minority of the ~300 nameplates on sale.

## Limitations

- Per NHTSA's published plan, the first model years score **manufacturer-submitted** impact data, not independent NHTSA crash testing. Self-reported grades.
- Separate category: does not affect the 5-star rating and is not on the Monroney sticker — discoverability is poor.
- Only MY2027; used-car shoppers get nothing.
- Ratings measure design forgiveness, not crash avoidance or real-world pedestrian exposure.
- Trim-level granularity: "meets criteria" may not apply to the trim on the lot.
