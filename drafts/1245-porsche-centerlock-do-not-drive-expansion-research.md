# Research: Porsche Centerlock Recall Expansion (26V682) — "Do Not Drive" for 548 More

## Story thesis
Porsche's October 2024 centerlock recall (24V809, 2,251 cars, Do Not Drive) was supposed to capture every 2024 track-special Porsche with an out-of-spec center-lock wheel bolt. It didn't. Two years later, after two field incidents of fractured center locks (May 2026, August 2026, no injuries), Porsche matched supplier data against production records and found 548 more cars carrying the same out-of-spec hardware. The new recall, NHTSA campaign **26V682** (filed Oct 16, 2026), again carries a Do Not Drive warning, and owners may not hear about a remedy until Dec 15, 2026 — nearly two months of a $150K+ car sitting parked.

**Kill test answer:** Newsworthy, yes. Novel angle: this is a recall of a recall's *scoping process*. The original identification method failed the same way twice — Porsche couldn't trace which of its own cars got defective parts. The paradox: a brand built on obsessive engineering couldn't count its own defective bolts. Wheels detaching at speed on 640-hp cars is visceral. Actionable: 2024 GT4 RS / Spyder RS / 911 Turbo / Turbo S / GT3 / GT3 RS / S/T / Carrera GTS owners must check VIN (searchable since Oct 20, 2026) and stop driving immediately.

## Hard facts (all verified)

- **Campaign:** NHTSA 26V682000 (Porsche internal: ATA1), Report Received Date 16/10/2026. Component: WHEELS:LUGS/NUTS/BOLTS/STUDS. [NHTSA recall record via api.nhtsa.gov]
- **Population:** 548 vehicles, all 2024 model year. (NHTSA record says "certain 2024 718 Cayman GT4 RS, 911 Turbo S, 911 Turbo, 911 S/T, 911 GT3 RS, 911 GT3, 911 Carrera GTS, and 718 Spyder RS vehicles.")
- **Per-model breakdown (press, from NHTSA filing):** 911 Carrera GTS 168; 911 Turbo S 94; 718 Cayman GT4 RS 73; 718 Spyder RS 70; 911 GT3 50; 911 GT3 RS 41; 911 S/T 39; 911 Turbo 13. Total = 548. (Autoblog, Oct 22, 2026)
- **Defect:** center lock wheel bolt may fracture; wheel may detach. NHTSA consequence: "Wheel detachment can result in a loss of vehicle control, increasing the risk of a crash."
- **Remedy status:** Do Not Drive. Dealers check the center lock fastening system and replace out-of-spec parts free of charge. Owner notification letters expected **December 15, 2026** — nearly 2 months after filing. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since **October 20, 2026**; dealers notified Oct 20, 2026. Porsche customer service: 1-800-767-7243.
- **Expansion lineage:** explicitly "expands previous NHTSA recall number 24V809" (Oct 8, 2024; 2,251 vehicles, same defect, also Do Not Drive).
- **Triggering events:** two field incidents involving fractured center-lock components — May 2026 and August 2026. No injuries reported. (europesays/KBB, Oct 21-22, 2026)
- **Root cause of the miss:** Porsche's original process for identifying which vehicles got out-of-spec parts "had missed some potentially affected parts." The new population was found by matching supplier data against production dates and build records — a traceability exercise the first investigation somehow didn't do completely.
- **Warning sign:** per Porsche, drivers may feel perceptible vibration before the wheel detaches. (europesays)
- **Owner outreach:** phone, email, and MyPorsche app over 1-2 weeks from ~Oct 20.
