## Story in one line
A Houston owner of a 2020 Hyundai Palisade paid out of pocket to fix an air conditioner that would not cool, then filed a one-person petition with NHTSA — and on November 16, 2026, the agency opened Defect Petition DP26013, putting 80,418 Palisades under federal review over windshields that will not defog.

## Key verified facts
- **The petition:** received by NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation Sept 17, 2026; opened as DP26013 on Nov 16, 2026 — 60 days from receipt to opening. The petition asks NHTSA to investigate "potential visibility and other safety concerns" in 2020 Hyundai Palisades.
- **The defect as alleged:** A/C compressor pulley and hub assembly failure robs the car of windshield defogging. The A/C compressor is what dehumidifies air for the defogger; a dead compressor means the defroster blows humid air at a fogged windshield.
- **The numbers:** population 80,418 (2020 model year only); 4 ODI incidents; 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injury incidents, 0 fatality incidents. ODI report numbers: 11688567, 11689901, 11763168, 11764892.
- **Hyundai already knew:** TSB 24-HA-003H tells dealers that "certain 2020-2025 Palisade (LX2) vehicles may experience reduced A/C performance due to an out of specification A/C compressor pulley and hub assembly," with a procedure to inspect and replace the compressor assembly or just the pulley via a service kit. The TSB's "24" prefix dates it to 2024 — roughly two years before the petition.
- **The petitioner:** Houston owner; A/C blowing warm/hot air, compressor not working; vehicle beyond warranty, paid for the repair himself. Dealer visit June 24, 2026 for the A/C issue plus two safety recalls; dealer said compressor and expansion valve needed replacement. Quote: "Since the summer season has just started, we have no choice but to have the repair done. We are hoping that if this petition is elevated to a safety recall, we can perhaps file a reimbursement of the compressor repair cost later." He frames it as safety, not comfort: the A/C problem "in real-world conditions creates a safety hazard, particularly in high-heat regions such as Houston, TX where we reside."
- **What happens next:** NHTSA decides whether to grant or deny the petition. Grant means opening a defect investigation (PE), which can lead to an engineering analysis and eventually a recall. Deny ends it. There is no deadline published and no recall attached today.
- **The scope gap (original finding):** the petition's population is one model year, 80,418 vehicles. Hyundai's own TSB covers six model years, 2020–2025, of the same out-of-spec part. If NHTSA grants the petition and the investigation follows the TSB's own scope, the population at issue is several times the petition's 80,418. The petition is the tip; the TSB is the iceberg.

## What this does NOT prove (limitations)
- Four ODI reports across 80,418 vehicles is a 0.005% report rate. No crashes, no injuries, no deaths are attributed to this failure. Cannot claim anyone has been hurt.
- A defect petition is not a recall and not a finding of a defect. NHTSA denies most petitions. The agency may deny DP26013.
- The TSB says affected vehicles "may experience reduced A/C performance" — it does not say every 2020–2025 Palisade will lose defogging, and it does not state a failure rate.
- The petitioner's vehicle is a single data point, and the other three ODI reports are unverified owner complaints.
- Whether the compressor failure actually leaves the windshield unfoggable in all conditions (vs. degraded) is not established in the public record. The defroster can still run on heated air; the question is whether it clears fog fast enough to be safe.
- No cost figure for the petitioner's repair is published. Do not invent one.

## Strongest counterargument
Four complaints, zero injuries, and a comfort feature. The A/C is not a safety system in the way brakes or airbags are, the car still has a heater-based defroster, and Hyundai already published a TSB with a service kit — the system worked, the dealer network knew what to do, and the petitioner got his car fixed. A reasonable reader could conclude this is a warranty gripe dressed in safety language by an owner who wants reimbursement. The counter to the counter: Hyundai's own TSB concedes the pulley is out of specification across six model years, not one; NHTSA's own component code files this under VISIBILITY, not comfort; and a defogger that cannot dehumidify is a visibility failure, which is exactly the kind of slow, unsexy failure that kills people in rain and humidity without ever producing a dramatic crash report.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2020 Palisade? If your A/C blows warm or the compressor will not engage, ask the dealer to inspect under TSB 24-HA-003H — the bulletin exists and names the pulley and hub assembly. Get it in writing whether the repair is covered or goodwill.
- Own a 2021–2025 Palisade? The same TSB covers your car. Same ask, same bulletin number.
- If your defogger cannot clear the windshield, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov — ODI complaints are literally the raw material that opened this petition. Four reports got a federal docket number; your report could be the fifth.
- Keep every repair receipt. If NHTSA grants the petition and a recall follows, prior repairs are reimbursable — but only if you can prove you paid for them.
- Buying a used 2020–2025 Palisade? Ask whether the A/C compressor pulley was replaced under the TSB, and run the defogger on a humid day before signing.
