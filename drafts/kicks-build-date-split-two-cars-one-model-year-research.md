## The data (site FARS, 2014–2023, fars_output.js)

Novel cross-tab: the award-winner's historical fleet (Kicks, rate 0.69) is 3.6× deadlier per mile than the test-failure's fleet (RAV4, rate 0.19). IIHS rear-seat kinematics and real-world FARS outcomes are measuring different things — and right now, in the real world, the RAV4's rear-seat problem has not translated into bodies.

## Limitations (must appear in article)
- FARS window (2014–2023) cannot validate the June-2026 hardware change; no crash data exists yet on post-June-2026 Kicks or the redesigned 2026 RAV4.
- Trailblazer FARS deaths are overwhelmingly the 2002–2009 generation; do not read them as the current crossover's score.
- IIHS moderate overlap is one 40-mph frontal scenario; a Good rating there is not a whole-vehicle safety verdict.
- Q3 absent from the FARS dataset (below volume threshold or naming).

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
The RAV4's 0.19 fatality rate is elite — one of the lowest in the entire FARS dataset. Its Moderate-overlap Acceptable/Marginal is a single lab scenario in which its rear-seat dummy kinematics looked bad; it has killed almost nobody per mile in the real world. The Kicks just earned an award on a test vehicle, while its actual fleet is 3.6× deadlier per mile. If you are choosing a car for a child in the second row today, the FARS data says: RAV4, and it is not close.
