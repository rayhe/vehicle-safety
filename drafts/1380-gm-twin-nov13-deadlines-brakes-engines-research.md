# Research — #1380: GM's Twin November 13 Deadlines (EA26005 + EA26006)

**Slug:** `1380-gm-twin-nov13-deadlines-brakes-engines`
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread Columnist; paradoxes, counterintuitive findings)
**Kicker:** Existential Dread
**Date:** November 3, 2026
**News peg:** GM Authority, November 3, 2026: NHTSA granted GM a partial extension on the eBoost brake investigation, moving nine questions to November 13, 2026. That is the same date GM's answers are due in the separate L87 engine investigation.

## Story in one line
On a single day, November 13, 2026, General Motors must answer NHTSA's field-data questions in two unrelated engineering analyses: brakes that may quit helping and engines that already failed once after a recall fix. Combined population under investigation: 2,162,563 vehicles.

## Kill test: PASS (with honest prior-coverage note)
This site has covered each investigation separately: #1367 (L87 Nov 13 extension, L87 only), #1050 / #979 / #835 / #790 (eBoost, eBoost only). No prior article combines the two deadlines, and no prior article runs the combined-population or combined-report arithmetic below. The novelty is the synthesis: same information-request date (September 15, 2026), same original deadline (October 30, 2026), same extension pattern (partial, field-data questions only), same new deadline (November 13, 2026), two different safety systems, zero nameplate overlap. If either investigation alone were the story, this would be a duplicate. The shared calendar is the story.

## Facts (verified, primary/credible sources)

### Investigation 1: L87 6.2L V8 engine seizure — EA26005
- Opened August 20, 2026 (Engineering Analysis; escalation of Recall Query RQ26001, opened January 2026).
- Population: **997,743** model-year 2021-2026 Chevrolet Silverado 1500, Tahoe, Suburban; GMC Sierra 1500, Yukon, Yukon XL; Cadillac Escalade, Escalade ESV with the L87 built after March 1, 2021.
- Alleged defect: engine seizure from internal component failure.
- ODI reports: **946 reports of L87 engine failure** in vehicles that went through recall 25V-274 or were built after its production window.
- Prior recall 25V-274 (April 2025): **597,571** vehicles, 2021-2024, connecting-rod and crankshaft defects. Remedy split: failed inspection got an engine replacement; passed inspection got thicker 0W-40 oil, a new fill cap, and a revised manual.
- Information request EA26005-01 sent September 15, 2026 (6 requests). Request 1 (VIN-level production data) stayed due October 30, 2026. Requests 2-6 (failure reports, incident detail, documents, warranty data, GM's own assessment) extended to **November 13, 2026** by an October 25, 2026 memo. GM's stated reason: still analyzing field data under the expanded defect definition from RQ to EA.
- Penalty clause in the letter: up to **$27,874 per violation per day**, ceiling just under **$140 million**.
- Sources: GM-Trucks.com, November 1, 2026, reporting from NHTSA's public docket documents (EA26005-01 letter, October 25 extension memo); NHTSA EA26005 opening resume (August 20, 2026), as previously verified in #1367 research.

### Investigation 2: eBoost electronic brake system — EA26006
- Opened August 21, 2026 (Engineering Analysis; escalation of Preliminary Evaluation PE24011, opened April 11, 2024 on 2023 Cadillac Lyriq complaints).
- Population: **1,164,820** vehicles (prior research #1050, from the EA26006 opening resume).
- Vehicles: 2023-2026 Cadillac Lyriq, Chevrolet Colorado, GMC Canyon; 2024-2026 Buick Enclave, Envision, Chevrolet Blazer EV, Equinox EV, Traverse, GMC Acadia, plus Cadillac Celestiq, Optiq, Cruise Origin; plus 2024-2026 Acura ZDX and Honda Prologue built by GM.
- Failure: possible **fractured spindle** inside the eBoost unit. Symptoms: hard brake pedal, "Brake System Failure" warning, loss of brake assist.
- Incident counts (EA26006 opening resume, via #1050): **745 unique incidents** (227 ODI complaints + 531 manufacturer reports, duplicates removed), **22 crashes or fires**, 5-6 injury incidents, **6 injuries**, **0 fatalities**.
- Dispute at the core: GM says ABS, stability control, and traction control remain functional until the vehicle comes to a complete stop, with warnings, lamps, and a 43 mph speed limit. NHTSA has complaints saying brake assist can fail immediately while slowing down, not only after a stop.
- Information request sent September 15, 2026. Original deadline October 30, 2026. NHTSA memo dated October 16, 2026 granted a partial extension: **nine questions now due November 13, 2026**.
- Sources: GM Authority, November 3, 2026 ("GM Gets Deadline Extension For NHTSA Brake Investigation"); EA26006 opening resume figures as verified in #1050 research.

## Original contribution (required)
1. **Combined population, computed:** 1,164,820 + 997,743 = **2,162,563** vehicles across the two investigations. No nameplate appears in both lists (L87 list is full-size trucks/SUVs; eBoost list is midsize trucks, crossovers, and EVs), so the sum is not double-counting vehicles, though one household could own one of each.
2. **Combined report volume, computed:** 946 + 745 = **1,691** failure/incident reports sitting in the two dockets.
3. **Report-rate comparison, computed:** L87: 946 / 997,743 x 100,000 = **94.8 reports per 100,000** investigated vehicles. eBoost: 745 / 1,164,820 x 100,000 = **64.0 per 100,000**. The engine investigation's report rate runs about 48 percent higher than the brake investigation's, even though the brake population is larger.
4. **Recall-coverage gap, computed:** 597,571 / 997,743 = **59.9 percent** of the investigated L87 population was covered by the original recall. The other ~400,000 vehicles are post-cutoff builds whose engines are in the investigation precisely because the "fixed" population kept failing or the cutoff did not hold.
5. **Calendar symmetry, observed:** both information requests September 15, 2026; both original deadlines October 30, 2026; both extensions partial (production data stayed due October 30 in EA26005; only specified questions moved in EA26006); both new deadlines November 13, 2026. Two dockets, one deadline, one automaker.
6. **eBoost crash share, computed:** 22 / 745 = **2.95 percent** of reported eBoost incidents involved a crash or fire, about 1 in 34.

## Limitations (required)
- Reports are not confirmed defects. The 946 L87 reports and 745 eBoost incidents are complaints and field reports, not adjudicated failures; duplicates were removed in the eBoost count, but neither docket publishes a confirmed-failure rate.
- No recall exists in either investigation today. An Engineering Analysis is one of the last steps before a recall decision, not a defect finding, and either EA can close with no finding.
- The two populations are estimates from NHTSA opening resumes, not VIN-confirmed defective counts. Population-under-investigation must not be reported as "2.16 million defective vehicles."
- The penalty figure ($27,874/day, ~$140M ceiling) is verified for the L87 information-request letter only. This article does not claim the same clause applies to the eBoost letter.
- No FARS linkage: neither investigation has published fatal-crash counts tied to the defect (eBoost: zero fatalities on record; L87 docket figures cited here do not include a fatality count, and none is claimed).
- Overlap caveat: nameplate lists do not overlap, but the combined figure counts vehicles, not owners or households.

## Strongest counterargument (required)
A deadline is not a verdict, and two deadlines on one day are not twice a verdict. Extensions like these are routine in complex investigations; GM asked for more time because NHTSA widened the defect definition, which is what an agency is supposed to do as it learns more, and a two-week slip on paperwork says nothing about whether either system is defective. The eBoost incident rate is 0.064 percent of the investigated population with zero deaths, and the mechanical brake fallback still exists even when assist is lost. The L87 report rate, while higher, still represents fewer than 1 in 1,000 investigated vehicles. NHTSA may close both EAs in 2027 with no recall, and this article's November 13 framing would then read as calendar drama about a paperwork day. That outcome is genuinely possible, and the article says so.

## Actionable takeaways (hard gate)
- If you own a 2021-2026 full-size GM truck or SUV with the 6.2L V8: keep every repair order showing recall completion date, remedy type (replacement engine vs. 0W-40 oil), and mileage. Those are literally the fields NHTSA demanded per VIN. Bearing knock or metal debris in the oil filter means a dealer visit now, not at the next service.
- If you own a 2023-2026 Lyriq, Colorado, Canyon, Blazer EV, Equinox EV, Traverse, Acadia, Enclave, Envision, Optiq, or a GM-built Acura ZDX / Honda Prologue: know the eBoost signature (hard pedal plus "Brake System Failure" message). Keep braking through it (mechanical fallback exists), pull over, and do not restart-and-forget.
- For both groups: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and if your vehicle already failed after a recall repair, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov. The 946 L87 reports are what pushed a query into an engineering analysis.
- Do not wait for November 13 to mean anything for your car. That date is GM's paperwork deadline, not an owner repair deadline and not a recall decision date. Any decision realistically lands in 2027.
