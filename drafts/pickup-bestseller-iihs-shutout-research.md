# Research: America's Bestselling Vehicles Can't Pass a Crash Test

## Angle
America's three best-selling vehicles — the Ford F-150, Chevrolet Silverado, and Ram — account for 23,192 occupant deaths and were involved in 49,908 fatal crashes (FARS 2014-2023). In 2026, none of them can earn an IIHS safety award. Only the Tesla Cybertruck and Toyota Tundra did.

## Self-Critique Gate
- **Genuinely surprising?** YES. Consumers assume bestsellers = safe. The fact that America's TOP THREE sellers all fail IIHS is counterintuitive.
- **Novel cross-tab?** YES. Mapping IIHS 2026 award results against FARS body counts for pickup trucks. Nobody else has quantified the "unendorsed death toll."
- **Distinct from existing articles?** pickup-body-count covers raw deaths but not IIHS. pickup-death-divergence covers trend divergence. Neither cross-references IIHS 2026 shutout.
- **Verdict:** PROCEED.

### Key Findings
1. F-150 + Silverado + Ram = 23,906 FARS deaths, ~52,000 fatal crashes. ZERO IIHS awards.
2. For every F-150 occupant who dies, 1.18 people in other vehicles die. F-150 is a net death exporter.
3. Ram 1500 (newer separate model) has the lowest crash lethality of ANY vehicle in FARS at 0.341 — meaning in 66% of fatal crashes involving a Ram 1500, someone ELSE dies while the Ram occupant survives. Export ratio: 1.93:1.
4. Tundra (1,223 deaths, rate 0.94) earned TSP — lower death count AND IIHS endorsed.
5. Cybertruck earned TSP+ (the highest award) — a truck that didn't exist when these deaths happened now sets the standard the Big Three can't meet.
