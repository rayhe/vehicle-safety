# Research: SK On Electrode Misalignment — Two OEMs, One Defect

## Angle
SK On, one of the largest EV battery suppliers in the US, has had the same electrode misalignment manufacturing defect trigger recalls at two different automakers in 2026. VW recalled 44,551 ID.4s in January; Hyundai/Kia recalled 14 EVs (Ioniq 5, EV6, EV9) in July. Both trace to misaligned electrodes in SK On-supplied battery cells. Same root cause, same "park outside" advisory, same full-pack replacement remedy. This is the EV equivalent of the Takata pattern — one supplier's quality escape cascading across badge lines.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely surprising? YES. Most EV buyers don't know their VW and their Hyundai share a battery supplier. The connection between two separate recalls at two separate OEMs to a single manufacturing process failure at one Korean factory is not obvious from the recall headlines. The story maps a supply chain vulnerability that grows as EV adoption scales.

## Primary Sources

### 1. NHTSA Recall 26V030 (VW ID.4 — larger campaign)
- 43,881 MY 2023–2025 ID.4 EVs + 8,526 in Canada
- SK Battery America (SK On subsidiary) supplied high-voltage battery modules
- Modules may overheat → fire risk
- Remedy: battery software update + pack replacement if needed
- Owner notification by March 20, 2026

### 2. NHTSA Recall 26V028 (VW ID.4 — acute defect)
- 670 MY 2023–2024 ID.4 EVs
- Misaligned electrodes in certain battery cell modules
- "Elevated fire risk" — more acute than the broader recall
- Interim instructions: park outside immediately after charging, avoid enclosed garages

### 3. NHTSA Recall (Hyundai/Kia — Jul 2026)
- 6 Hyundai Ioniq 5 (MY 2023-2024)
- 7 Kia EV6 (MY 2022-2024)
- 1 Kia EV9 (MY 2024)
- Total: 14 vehicles
- Root cause: misaligned electrodes in battery cell (SK On supplier)
- Internal short circuit → thermal runaway → fire
- Advisory: charge ≤80%, park outside
- Remedy: full battery system assembly (BSA) replacement
- Hyundai notification letters: Aug 31, 2026; Kia: Aug 7, 2026

### 4. SK On Context
- Subsidiary of SK Innovation (South Korea)
- One of the largest EV battery suppliers globally
- Supplies cells/modules to: VW, Hyundai/Kia, Ford (F-150 Lightning, E-Transit)
- SK Battery America operates a factory in Commerce, GA
- Second factory in Glendale, KY (joint venture with Ford: BlueOval SK)
- The electrode misalignment is a manufacturing process failure at the cell level — it affects the internal geometry of the anode/cathode layers

### 5. Technical Context
- Electrode misalignment: when anode and cathode layers aren't properly aligned during cell assembly, the layers can make physical contact → internal short → localized heating → thermal runaway
- Thermal runaway is self-sustaining: once started, the chemical reaction continues even without external power
- Battery fires burn at extreme temperatures (2,000°F+), are difficult to extinguish, and can reignite hours or days later
- A full battery pack replacement on an EV costs $10,000-$20,000+ — making these the most expensive recalls per vehicle in automotive history

## Novel Contribution
Cross-OEM supplier mapping of a single root cause (electrode misalignment) across two separate recall campaigns. The comparison reveals:
- Same defect, same supplier, same remedy → but different recall populations (44K vs 14)
- The 14-vehicle Hyundai/Kia recall suggests a narrower quality escape from a specific production run, while the VW recall suggests a broader process issue
- Both share the unprecedented "park outside" advisory — an admission that the vehicle itself may spontaneously combust
- SK On also supplies Ford's EV batteries — if the same process failure occurred in Ford's battery line, a third OEM could be affected

## Limitations
- FARS data (2014-2023) predates meaningful EV fleet volume — cannot cross-reference with fatality rates
- The 14-vehicle Hyundai/Kia recall is so small it could be dismissed as noise, but the VW recall at 44K+ vehicles validates the systemic concern
- SK On's total production volume and defect rate are not public
- We don't know if Ford's BlueOval SK batteries (different factory) share the same process

## Strongest Counterargument
SK On's quality control caught the Hyundai/Kia defect at 14 vehicles — that's precisely what quality systems are designed to do. The Takata comparison is inflammatory: Takata killed 27+ people over 15+ years while knowingly shipping defective inflators. SK On has zero injuries and is proactively recalling. A well-functioning safety recall system and a systemic supplier failure look very different.
