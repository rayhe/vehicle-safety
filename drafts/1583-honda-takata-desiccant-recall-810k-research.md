# Research — #1583 Honda Takata Desiccant Recall (26V765)

## Angle
Honda recalled 810,592 cars on November 19, 2026 for Takata airbag inflators that were supposed to be the safe ones. These inflators use a moisture-absorbing desiccant (13X) specifically designed to prevent the ammonium nitrate propellant degradation that killed the original Takata inflators. Honda's own June 2020 analysis predicted no rupture risk before 2027 at the earliest. The recall landed in November 2026. The fix had an expiration date, and the clock ran out early.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: filed November 19, 2026, covered by Reuters/KBB/NY Post November 20-24. Novel angle: this is not another Takata recall, it is the recall of the *fix itself* — the desiccated inflators Honda and NHTSA agreed in 2020-2021 were safe. The original contribution is the timeline arithmetic: Honda's 2020 prediction (no risk before 2027) versus the November 2026 defect determination, plus the per-model breakdown and the Toyota parallel recall (314,029 US vehicles, campaign 26V748, filed November 12) making ~1.6 million vehicles in one week. The site has covered Takata before (takata-stellantis-do-not-drive, counterfeit-airbag-grenade, unfixed-recall-epidemic) but never the desiccant failure or 26V765.

## Verified facts (from Part 573, 26V765)
- Campaign: 26V765, filed November 19, 2026. Honda manufacturer recall nos. QQH, BQK, MQJ, KQI.
- Total: **810,592 vehicles. Estimated percentage with defect: 100%.**
- Models:
  - 2012-2013 Honda Civic: 413,125 (just over half the total)
  - 2012-2013 Honda CR-V: 197,045
  - 2013 Honda Accord: 165,225
  - 2013 Acura RDX: 20,554
  - 2013 Acura ILX: 14,643
- Geography: only vehicles originally sold or ever registered in 9 states + 5 territories known for high temperature, high humidity, large temperature fluctuations: California, Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, South Carolina, Florida, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, Northern Mariana Islands, U.S. Virgin Islands.
- Defect: frontal airbag inflators use phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate (PSAN) propellant (2004L) with a desiccant (13X) intended to control moisture. Over time, high absolute humidity + repeated temperature cycling degrades the propellant, increasing porosity and causing embrittlement. On deployment the degraded propellant fractures more than intended, increasing burning surface area and gas generation rate; gas generation may exceed the inflator's discharge capacity, causing excessive internal pressure and potential rupture. Metal fragments could pass through the airbag cushion, causing serious injury or fatality.
- Component manufacturer: Takata (no longer exists).
- Chronology (the timeline that is the story):
  - June 2020: Honda submitted final analysis report on desiccated PSPI-X and PSDI-X inflators to NHTSA. No degradation seen in field-recovered parts. Statistical analysis indicated degradation would not sufficiently progress to create rupture risk **before 2027, at the earliest**.
  - December 2021: NHTSA, Honda, and Exponent agreed on a plan for continued investigation.
  - 2022-2026: Honda shared ongoing results with NHTSA.
  - May 2025: simulation testing under severe environmental conditions showed signs of propellant degradation, but inflators remained within normal operating parameters.
  - May 2025-November 2026: continued investigation; results did not show statistically significant deviation from the 2020 prediction.
  - **November 19, 2026: Honda determined a defect related to motor vehicle safety existed and decided to recall.**
- As of November 19, 2026: **no warranty claims, no reports of injury or death** related to this issue.
- Remedy: dealer replaces driver and/or passenger frontal airbag inflator with an improved part, free. Remedy inflators are from a **different supplier** and use a propellant with a **different chemical composition** that does not degrade long-term under heat/humidity/temperature swings. Owner notification letters scheduled to begin December 18, 2026. Dealer notification November 20, 2026.
- Related recall: expands/relates to prior campaigns; the filing lists no Do Not Drive or Park Outside advisory.

## Parallel Toyota recall (context)
- Toyota campaign 26V748, filed November 12, 2026: 314,029 US vehicles (790,000 worldwide). Models: 2002-2010 Lexus SC430, 2011 IS250, 2017 GX460, 2003-2008 Corolla, 2005-2006 Tundra, 2005-2007 Sequoia, 2016-2017 4Runner. Takata front passenger inflator.
- Combined Honda + Toyota: ~1.6 million vehicles in one week (Reuters, Nov 20).

## Scale context
- Takata recall overall: almost 70 million vehicles in the US alone (KBB); ~67 million airbags recalled since 2008; ~400 drivers/passengers injured in the US (NY Post, Nov 23, 2026).
- The Civic alone (413,125) is more than half of this recall, and the Civic is one of the best-selling cars in America. These are not rare cars; they are commuter cars, many now on second or third owners who never got the first Takata letter.

## Original contribution
1. **The 2020 prediction vs 2026 reality:** Honda's own statistical model said no rupture risk before 2027 at the earliest. The defect determination came November 19, 2026, weeks before that floor. The model was wrong by design: it measured degradation in recovered parts, not in the worst-case climate population.
2. **The desiccant was the industry's answer to Takata.** This recall is the first large-scale admission that the answer itself degrades. The remedy abandons PSAN entirely for a different chemical composition from a different supplier, which is Honda conceding the propellant family, not just the batch.
3. **The geography is the tell:** 9 states + 5 territories, all hot and humid. The same climate map as the original Takata crisis. The desiccant bought time, not immunity, and the time it bought was measured in years, not decades.
4. **The 100% defect rate** on 810,592 vehicles: Honda is not saying some inflators might degrade. It is saying every one of them carries the defect. Compare with typical recalls where the estimated defect rate is a fraction of the population.

## Limitations / what this does NOT prove
- No ruptures, injuries, or deaths have been reported for these specific inflator types. The risk is modeled and observed in simulation (May 2025 degradation signs), not demonstrated in a field failure. The article must not imply anyone has been hurt by these inflators.
- The 2027 floor was a statistical prediction ("at the earliest"), not a guarantee; Honda says results did not show a statistically significant deviation from the 2020 prediction, yet still determined a defect existed. That tension is the story, but it is not proof Honda hid anything.
- No FARS data links these inflators to deaths; this is a recall story, not a fatality analysis.
- Recall completion rates for 13-year-old cars are historically low; the article can note that risk but cannot state a completion rate for this campaign (none exists yet).

## Strongest counterargument
The honest defense of Honda and the desiccant program: the system worked. Monitoring caught degradation in simulation in May 2025, before any field rupture, and Honda recalled 810,592 cars with zero injuries attached. The 2020 analysis was a statistical floor, not a promise, and a precautionary recall with no injuries is exactly what a functioning safety regime looks like. The desiccant bought six-plus years of safe service for inflators that would otherwise have been recalled in 2015. That case deserves full strength in the article.
