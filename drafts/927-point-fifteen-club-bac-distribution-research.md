# Research Notes — Article #927: "The .15 Club"

**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk Chief)
**Proposed kicker:** Sobriety Report
**Date:** 2026-09-18

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The drunk-driving crisis is not marginal drinkers at .09. It is the ".15 Club": 68% of 2024 alcohol-impaired-driving fatalities involved a driver at BAC ≥ .15 (nearly twice the legal limit), and the single most common BAC among drinking drivers in fatal crashes was exactly .15. The .15+ crowd outnumbers the rest of alcohol deaths more than 2-to-1.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Fresh NHTSA TSF 2024 report data (DOT HS 813 815) plus the Aug 2026 NHTSA Labor Day campaign stats (505 deaths, 77% of alcohol-impaired drivers who died at ≥.15).
- **Novel angle?** Yes. The site's own June 2026 story ("legal-bac-2028-killed-below-limit") covered the BELOW-limit end (2,028 died at .01–.07). Nobody has covered the OTHER end: the high-BAC distribution. The mode-BAC-is-.15 stat is a gut punch nobody has written.
- **Overlap check:** #897 (drunkest-drivers vs safest cars), alero-beater-effect (car age), utah-005 (policy) — all adjacent, none duplicative. This is the BAC distribution story.

## Primary sources (4)
1. **NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts 2024 Data: Alcohol-Impaired Driving (DOT HS 813 815)**, crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813815
   - 13,500 drivers with alcohol in their systems in fatal crashes (2024); 84% (11,367) had BAC ≥ .08; 56% (7,547) had BAC ≥ .15
   - 11,904 alcohol-impaired-driving fatalities; **68% (8,097)** in crashes where at least one driver had BAC ≥ .15
   - **Most frequently recorded BAC among drinking drivers in fatal crashes: .15 g/dL**
   - Prior DWI convictions: 6% of alcohol-impaired drivers in fatal crashes vs 2% sober (3x more likely)
2. **NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts 2023 Data: Alcohol-Impaired Driving (DOT HS 813 713)**, crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/Publication/813713
   - 12,429 alcohol-impaired fatalities; 67% (8,272) in crashes with driver BAC ≥ .15; modal BACs .14 and .16
3. **NHTSA Labor Day campaign data (trafficsafetymarketing.gov via TechTimes, 2026-08-17)**
   - 2024 Labor Day window (Aug 30 6pm – Sep 3 5:59am): 505 crash deaths; 170 (34%) alcohol-involved
   - **77% of the alcohol-impaired drivers who died that weekend had BAC ≥ .15** — nearly twice the .08 legal limit
   - 40% of drivers 18–34 who died that weekend had been drinking at/above .08
   - NSC analysis: average Labor Day toll 10% higher than comparable non-holiday weekends; 39,254 died in 2024 (107/day)
4. **NHTSA holiday campaign press release** (nhtsa.gov/press-releases): 12,429 alcohol-impaired deaths in 2023 = nearly 1/3 of all crash fatalities; men 21–34 highest percentage; one death every 42 minutes.

## Original contribution (novel calculation)
- 2024: 8,097 of 11,904 alcohol deaths involve ≥.15 BAC. The rest: 3,807. Ratio ≈ **2.1:1** — the .15 club accounts for more than double the rest combined.
- Only 16% (1,933 of 13,500) of drinking drivers in fatal crashes were in the .01–.07 zone; the enforcement/policy debate centers on the limit line while the body count lives far above it.
- Pairing with the site's own below-limit story: the curve's two tails (below .08 marginal and ≥.15 hardcore) both matter, but the .15 tail is ~4x the .01–.07 tail by death count (8,097 vs 2,028).

## Limitations
- FARS only captures fatal crashes — injury-only crashes invisible; high-BAC drivers may be overrepresented in fatal crashes relative to their prevalence on the road.
- NHTSA estimates BACs when test results unknown; 56% ≥ .15 figure includes imputed values.
- The 77% Labor Day figure is a holiday window, not the full year; it likely overstates the annual average (68%).

## Strongest counterargument
The site's own June story showed 2,028 people died in 2024 with a driver at .01–.07 — below the limit in 49 states. Focusing all policy energy on the .15 club concedes thousands of marginal-BAC deaths. Utah's .05 law cut fatal crashes 19.8% by shifting the whole distribution, not by targeting the hardcore. Both tails are policy-relevant; the .15 club is where the volume is, the marginal zone is where the low-hanging policy lever sits.

## Actionable takeaways
- Ignition interlocks for ALL convicted drunk drivers (not just high-BAC or repeat offenders) — NHTSA/CDC identify them as the single most effective countermeasure (~70% re-arrest reduction while installed); only ~1/3 of states mandate for first offenders.
- If you host: the .15 club is the target population for "plan before the first drink" — these are not two-beer dinners, they are impaired driving as a habit.
- Report suspected impaired drivers: call 911 with vehicle description, plate, road/direction — holiday enforcement data shows it works.
