# Research: The CAFE Weight-Safety Paradox

## Angle
NHTSA simultaneously enforces two programs that work against each other: CAFE fuel economy standards push vehicles lighter, while FARS data shows lighter vehicles die at higher rates. The same agency mandates the cause and counts the casualties.

## Kill Test
- Novel for the site? YES — 0 stories mention CAFE, fuel economy standards, or the regulatory self-contradiction
- Genuinely newsworthy? YES — ties to Q1 2026 record-low fatality rate (0.99/100M VMT) and the ongoing fleet composition shift from sedans to SUVs
- Data-supported? YES — FARS class-level rates show a 2.5x gap between SUVs and sedans

## Key Data Points (FARS 2014-2023)
- **SUVs: 0.055 deaths per 100M VMT** (safest class)
- **Vans: 0.065** 
- **Pickups: 0.089**
- **Sedans: 0.139** (2.5x SUV rate)
- **Sports Cars: 0.269** (4.9x SUV rate)

### Lightest vehicles (CAFE beneficiaries) death rates:
- Chevrolet Spark: 1.28
- Chevrolet Sonic: 1.40
- Kia Rio: 1.07
- Ford Fiesta: 1.02
- Nissan Versa: 0.90

### Heaviest vehicles death rates:
- Ford F-250: 0.43
- Ford F-350: 0.15
- RAM 1500: 0.13

### The paradox calculation:
If every sedan driver switched to the average SUV, the death rate for those drivers would drop from 0.139 to 0.055 — a 60% reduction. That's 89,127 sedan deaths × 60% = ~53,000 fewer deaths over the decade. But CAFE standards INCENTIVIZE building lighter sedans, not heavier SUVs.

## Primary Sources
1. **NHTSA FARS 2014-2023** — death rates by vehicle class, model-level data
2. **NHTSA Q1 2026 fatality data** — 0.99/100M VMT, record low  
3. **National Academy of Sciences, 2002** — "Effectiveness and Impact of Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) Standards" — found 1,300-2,600 additional deaths/year from CAFE-driven downsizing
4. **IIHS vehicle size and weight research** — documents the mass-safety relationship

## Strongest Counterargument
Modern vehicle engineering (advanced high-strength steel, crumple zones, ESC) has partially decoupled weight from safety. A well-engineered light car (Honda Fit rate 0.72) can outperform a poorly engineered heavy one (Tahoe rate 2.49). CAFE pushed innovation, not just weight reduction. The 2015 NAS update acknowledged this.

## Limitations
- FARS death rates reflect the ENTIRE fleet (2014-2023), including old and new models
- Weight is not the only variable — vehicle age, driver demographics, usage patterns all contribute
- CAFE footprint-based standards (since 2011) partially address the size penalty
- Our data doesn't directly measure curb weight — we're using class as a proxy
