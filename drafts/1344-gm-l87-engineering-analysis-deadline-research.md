# Research: GM L87 6.2L Engineering Analysis — The October 30 Deadline (EA26005)

## Angle
NHTSA escalated the GM 6.2L V8 probe from a recall query to a full Engineering Analysis (EA26005), the most serious investigation level, and fired a six-request information letter at GM with a response deadline of October 30, 2026, yesterday. The novel material: Request 6 demands GM explain what warning a driver gets before the engine destroys itself, and 26 of the 499 post-remedy failures happened AFTER a complete engine replacement, meaning the nuclear option did not work either. Meanwhile GM is telling a federal judge the original recall already fixed everything, which is precisely the question NHTSA opened an investigation to answer.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: the deadline passed yesterday; this is the first chapter of the L87 saga since the recall-query stage covered in #864 and #1025, and the material is all new (escalation to EA, six-question letter, litigation contradiction, post-replacement failures as a category). Novel angle: NHTSA asking the manufacturer to describe the warning signs of a failure its own remedy was supposed to prevent, plus the original calculations below. PROCEED.

## Verified facts (primary/secondary sources)
1. **Recall 25V-274 (GM N252494000), April 24, 2025:** 597,571 vehicles, L87 6.2L V8 engines built March 1, 2021 to May 31, 2024. Cause: connecting rod and crankshaft manufacturing defects (sediment/debris in oil passages, out-of-spec crankshaft dimensions and surface finish), attributed to a supplier. Remedy: dealer inspection; pass gets 0W-40 oil (was 0W-20), new filter, new oil cap; fail gets replacement engine. Sources: rohnertparktransmission.com explainer (updated Oct 15, 2026); squeezed.app engine-recall roundup.
2. **RQ26001 opened Jan 16, 2026:** NHTSA recall query to assess whether the 25V-274 remedy was working. Escalated to **Engineering Analysis EA26005 on Aug 20, 2026**, population 997,743 vehicles, 2021-2026 model years (Silverado 1500, Sierra 1500, Tahoe, Suburban, Yukon/Yukon XL, Escalade/ESV). Covers failures after the recall remedy AND engines built after the recall's production window. Source: roadethos.com (published Oct 30, 2026); torquenews.com (Oct 27, 2026).
3. **The numbers:** 499 owner complaints to NHTSA of engine failure after the recall remedy (473 after the oil change, 26 after a full engine replacement). GM itself reported 6,953 post-remedy complaints. 191 failure reports involve L87 engines built after May 31, 2024, outside the recall window. One replacement 6.2L seized after 3,000 miles. Sources: torquenews.com; roadethos.com.
4. **The September 15 letter:** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation sent GM a six-request information demand, response due Oct 30, 2026 (partial response required if GM could not finish). Request 1: VIN, engine part number, design version, build date, recall completion date, and remedy type for every truck, letting investigators test whether failures follow a specific engine version or build date. Requests 2-4: every complaint, field report, crash, injury, fire, lawsuit, and arbitration tied to the problem, with mileage at each incident. Request 5: paid warranty and goodwill claims, plus repeat repair mileage. Request 6: the cause, the failure mechanism, the failure mode, the safety risk, and what warnings a driver gets before the engine fails. Source: torquenews.com (Oct 27, 2026).
5. **Engineering Analysis status:** the most serious level of defect investigation NHTSA conducts; final stage before regulators can formally press for a recall. It is not a recall and not a defect finding. Source: roadethos.com (Oct 30, 2026).
6. **Litigation:** GM has asked a federal judge to dismiss a related lawsuit, arguing the recall campaign already provides the remedy plaintiffs seek, a position that depends entirely on the remedy working. Source: roadethos.com (Oct 30, 2026).
7. **GM cooperating:** GM told the Detroit Free Press it continues to cooperate with NHTSA's evaluation (via warranties.autos summary).

## Original calculations (novel contribution)
- 6,953 / 597,571 = **1.16%**: GM's own post-remedy complaint count against the recall population (context, not a failure rate, since complaint counts and denominators are imperfect).
- 26 / 499 = **5.2%**: share of post-remedy failures that occurred after a complete engine replacement, the remedy's maximum intervention.
- 997,743 - 597,571 = **400,172 additional vehicles** pulled into the investigation population vs. the recall, a **67% expansion**.
- 191 post-window failures: evidence the production dates may have been drawn too narrowly.

## Strongest counterargument
GM's position has real weight: 499 complaints to NHTSA and 6,953 to GM are complaint tallies, not a failure rate, and complaints are self-selected by angry owners. The 0W-40 oil is a legitimate engineering mitigation that compensates for marginal tolerances; thicker oil does not change the metal but it is not a sham fix. The investigation population of 997,743 is not a failure count. And expanding a recall window is a big deal, engines fail for many reasons, and NHTSA has not attributed a defect, a crash, or an injury in the EA26005 opening summary. State this at full strength.

## What this article must NOT claim
- That GM missed the Oct 30 deadline or failed to respond (unknown; partial responses allowed).
- That NHTSA found a defect. EA is an investigation, not a finding.
- 1.16% as an established failure rate. It is a complaint-rate framing, labeled as such.
- Lawsuit details beyond "GM asked a federal judge to dismiss" (no case name verified).
- Any crash, injury, or death count tied to these failures. None is attributed.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2021-2026 Silverado/Sierra/Tahoe/Suburban/Yukon/Escalade with the 6.2L? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and check for campaign 25V274. If the recall is incomplete, complete it: the inspection path can still catch an engine that needs replacement.
- If your engine was "fixed" with the 0W-40 oil: watch for knocking, metallic noise, or low oil pressure. That is the early warning, and NHTSA is now officially asking GM what the warning looks like, which means do not wait for an answer before paying attention.
- If your engine fails post-remedy: file an NHTSA complaint. The 499 complaints are what escalated this to an Engineering Analysis.
- Keep the recall completion paperwork. A post-remedy failure is now a documented category regulators are actively tracking.
- Shopping used 2021-2026: 2025-2026 models are in the investigation but NOT in the recall. Extra caution warranted there.
