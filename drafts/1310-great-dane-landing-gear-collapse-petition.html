A Federal Petition Says Great Dane's Trailer Legs Are Collapsing. That's File Number Five in 22 Months.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
February 12, 2028

Let's talk about the two steel legs holding tens of thousands of pounds of refrigerated freight above the concrete while a dock worker cranks the landing gear handle. On October 23, 2026, a petition landed at NHTSA's defect office alleging those legs are collapsing on 2017 Great Dane refrigerated trailers equipped with Jost landing gear, and the agency opened file DP26012 to decide whether the petition deserves a full investigation.[1] A defect petition is not a finding. It is a citizen raising a hand and saying: look at this.

Landing gear is the least glamorous load path on a semi-trailer, which is exactly why it matters. Two crank-down legs near the nose take the full weight of the loaded trailer every time the tractor pulls away for a drop-and-hook. When a leg buckles, the front of the trailer slams down. Nothing about that failure mode is subtle, and nobody standing under the nose survives the physics debate.

5
Structural files against Great Dane in 22 months: missing kingpin welds (Dec 2024), insufficient support-gear bracing welds (Dec 2024), missing rear impact guard channels (Jul 2026), a do-not-use coupler recall (Oct 2026), and now the landing gear petition (Oct 2026)

DP26012 does not arrive in a vacuum. In December 2024, Great Dane recalled 225 model-year 2025 Champion dry freight trailers because missing kingpin welds could prevent the upper coupler from securely connecting the trailer, letting the trailer detach.[2] The same month, the company recalled 1,757 model-year 2025 Everest SS refrigerated trailers built at its Wayne, Nebraska plant for insufficient welds on support gear bracing mounts, with a stated consequence of the trailer collapsing; Great Dane estimated 5% of the population carried the defect.[3] Transport Canada ran the mirror image of that recall and added operational guidance: do not use the trailer without a tractor attached, or without safety stands in place, until the welds are fixed.[4]

The pattern kept accumulating this year. In July 2026, Great Dane recalled 11 units of the 2027 Champion built without rear impact guard extension channels, failing the federal rear-impact standard.[5] In October, campaign 26V656 hit 69 model-year 2027 Champions whose upper coupler may carry the wrong bottom plate, letting the kingpin protrude so the trailer cannot couple properly; NHTSA's record carries a do-not-use advisory, the strongest owner guidance short of a stop-sale.[6][7] Two weld recalls, a missing guard channel, a coupler plate escape, and now a landing gear petition. Different components, different years, one manufacturer, and a shared theme: the steel keeps not being where the specification says it should be.

The Jost detail is the part worth zooming in on. Jost is a landing-gear manufacturer, which means the petition names a supplier component, not just a Great Dane weld.[1] If the legs buckling are Jost's, the failure lives one layer down the supply chain, and no amount of weld inspection at Wayne, Nebraska touches it. That distinction matters because the first four files all trace to factory escapes the company could catch on its own line. A supplier-component failure is a different engineering question: specification, procurement, acceptance testing, and field monitoring across fleets that may have bought the same Jost legs on other brands' trailers.

What you can actually do: if you operate 2017 Great Dane refrigerated trailers, check whether your units run Jost landing gear and add a close landing-gear inspection to the pre-trip, looking for cracked welds, bent legs, and uneven crank-down. DP26012 is a petition review, not a recall, so there is no campaign number to check at nhtsa.gov/recalls yet; fleet managers should track the DP26012 docket for the grant-or-deny decision, which federal procedure puts around January 2027. For the 2024 support-gear recall population, follow the Transport Canada guidance: park suspect trailers with a tractor attached or safety stands in place until repaired.[4] And if you run 2027 Champions covered by 26V656, the do-not-use advisory is still the word: park them until the coupler is inspected.[6]

What this article did not prove: no complaint count, crash count, injury count, or petitioner identity is public for DP26012 as of this writing, so nothing here quantifies how often the landing gear has actually collapsed. The petition's population is model year 2017 only, which means age, corrosion, maintenance history, and duty cycle are all live alternative explanations that a granted investigation would have to rule out. Unit counts for the December 2024 Champion kingpin-weld recall (225) and the July 2026 impact-guard recall (11) come from trade and recall-database coverage, not from re-verified Part 573 reports this run. And five files is a count of federal actions, not a failure rate; it cannot by itself say what fraction of Great Dane's fleet is affected.

Fair counter, stated at full strength: Great Dane is one of the largest trailer builders in North America, and a company moving that much steel will generate federal paperwork. The Wayne plant weld escapes were self-reported and recalled, which is the regulatory system functioning as designed; a fast voluntary recall is what you want to see. A 2017 reefer is nine model years old, and landing gear on a decade-old trailer is a wear component living in road salt and dock abuse, which points at maintenance economics more than design. True, all of it. Also true: five structural files in 22 months, including a do-not-use order and a collapse petition, is not what a healthy quality system produces, and "the system caught it" stops being reassuring somewhere around the fourth catch.
