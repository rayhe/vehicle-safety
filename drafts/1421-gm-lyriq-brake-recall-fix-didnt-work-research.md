# Research — #1421: GM's Brake Recall That Proves the Last Brake Recall Didn't Work

**Slug:** `1421-gm-lyriq-brake-recall-fix-didnt-work`
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — technical but accessible; the BSCM slip-calculation mechanism is her beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date:** 2026-11-07

## Headline candidate
"GM Fixed Your Lyriq's Brakes in 2024. The Car That Proved It Didn't Work Hit a Garage in April."

## Facts (verified, 4 primary/credible sources)

**NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Report 26V717** (GM filing, Oct 29, 2026 — primary source):
- **67,380 vehicles**, estimated percentage with defect: **100%**
- Vehicles: 2023–2027 Cadillac Lyriq, 2024–2027 Chevrolet Blazer EV, 2025–2026 Cadillac Celestiq, 2026–2027 Cadillac Vistiq — performance all-wheel drive (pAWD, RPO X0E) only
- Defect: brake system control module (BSCM) software defect; slip calculation may misread wheel movement, causing a **temporary loss of braking at low speeds**. Normal braking returns on the next brake press.
- Trigger sequence, per GM's own testing: brakes lightly applied at low speed during a sharp turn, vehicle hits a bump or depression (such as at the end of a driveway), driver momentarily releases and reapplies the brakes — the slip calculation misreads wheel movement and braking drops out.
- **Chronology:** April 10, 2026 — GM technical expert files a Speak Up for Safety report after a 2024 Lyriq struck a garage at low speed. The subject vehicle was built with the software improvements implemented as the remedy for the previous recall, **24V589**. GM duplicated the condition in testing, confirmed the 24V589 remedy did not address this pAWD scenario, opened a new investigation Aug 18, 2026. SFADA decided on the recall Oct 22, 2026.
- **24 field complaints** potentially related, received Feb 28, 2024 – June 11, 2026. **20 minor accidents, 2 with allegations of minor injuries.** No fatalities reported.
- Vehicles covered by 24V589 with pAWD are included here and closed under 24V589, because this BSCM update addresses both conditions.
- Remedy: free BSCM software update, over the air or at a dealer. No do-not-drive or park-outside advisory.
- Production corrected: 2027 Celestiq; Blazer EV 2027 production Aug 8, 2026; Lyriq 2026 production Sept 7, 2026; Vistiq 2027 production Oct 21, 2026.
- Dealer notification Oct 29, 2026; owner letters estimated Dec 14, 2026; VINs searchable Oct 29, 2026.
- GM recall N262570900; NHTSA campaign 26V717000.

**CarBuzz, Nov 6, 2026** (Evan Williams): the software fix is **not yet ready for 2023–2024 Lyriq models** — the oldest cars in the recall, and the ones from the first recall, wait longest. Fix available now for other models; OTA where enabled, otherwise dealer.

**The Truth About Cars, Nov 2026:** confirms 24 field complaints Feb 24, 2024 – June 11, 2026, and the 2023–2024 Lyriq fix delay.

**The Brake Report, Nov 2026:** confirms campaign numbers, model-year population, OTA/dealer remedy, Dec 14 notification date.

**Context — prior coverage on this site (#1050, Oct 2, 2026):** NHTSA opened Engineering Analysis EA26006 on Aug 21, 2026 into GM's eBoost electro-hydraulic brake system (1,164,820 vehicles under investigation, 745 incidents, 22 crashes/fires, 6 injuries, 0 fatalities). That is a *different mechanism* (spindle fracture / loss of brake assist) from 26V717's BSCM slip-calculation defect, but the same vehicle family and the same 2024 recall (24V589) sit at the center of both. This article must state the distinction explicitly, not conflate them.

## Original contribution (required)

1. **The population math nobody ran:** the Lyriq is 44,585 of 67,380 vehicles — 66% of the recall. The 2024 Lyriq alone is 22,819 vehicles, 34% of the entire recall. The car that triggered the investigation (a 2024 Lyriq) is the single largest cohort, and it is also the cohort with no fix ready yet.
2. **The accident rate per complaint:** 20 of 24 field complaints involved a minor accident — 83%. Recall complaints usually run mostly no-crash; here nearly every complaint is a crash. That ratio is the tell that the defect is not theoretical.
3. **The timeline math:** the triggering SUFS report landed April 10, 2026; the recall decision came Oct 22 — 195 days later, during which GM's own testing duplicated the failure. And the complaint window opens Feb 28, 2024, meaning some of these 24 complaints predate the 24V589 remedy and were effectively counted twice: once as evidence the first fix was needed, once as evidence it failed.
4. **The driveway test:** the trigger sequence is not an exotic edge case. Light braking, sharp turn, bump, release, reapply — that is the exact choreography of backing out of a driveway or turning into a parking garage. The defect lives in the most ordinary maneuver a car performs.

## Kill test — verdict: PROCEED

Genuinely newsworthy: a filed recall (not an investigation), a documented case where the previous recall's fix was already installed on the car that failed, 20 crashes, and the oldest cars waiting longest for the remedy. Novel angle: the recall as a sequel — GM is now fixing the fix. Not a data dump: the numbers serve the paradox.

## Limitations

- 24 complaints is a small numerator; GM's 100% defect estimate is a population estimate for the suspect software, not a measured failure rate per vehicle.
- "Minor accidents" and "minor injuries" are GM's characterizations in the Part 573; no independent crash reports were available at research time.
- Whether the 2023–2024 Lyriq fix delay is weeks or months is not stated in the filing; CarBuzz/TTAC report only that it is not ready.
- EA26006 (eBoost) remains open and is a separate mechanism; do not merge its 745 incidents into this recall's 24 complaints.

## Strongest counterargument

GM duplicated the failure only under a precise five-step sequence, the loss of braking is temporary and self-corrects on the next pedal press, there are no serious injuries or fatalities, and the company moved from SUFS report to recall in about six months with a free OTA fix. A fair reading: this is the recall system working as designed — a narrow software edge case caught, filed, and fixed. The counter to the counter: the same system already "fixed" this once in 2024, and the car that proved the fix incomplete was driving on the fixed software.

## Actionable takeaways

- Own a 2023–2027 Lyriq, 2024–2027 Blazer EV SS/PPV, 2025–2026 Celestiq, or 2026–2027 Vistiq with performance AWD? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — searchable since Oct 29, 2026.
- 2023–2024 Lyriq owners: no software is ready yet. Until it is, treat low-speed sharp turns over bumps (driveway exits, garage ramps) with extra following distance, and remember a second firm brake press restores braking.
- Everyone else in the recall: accept the OTA update when prompted, or book the dealer visit. It is free.
