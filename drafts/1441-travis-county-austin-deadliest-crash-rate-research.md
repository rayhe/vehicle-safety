## Verified facts

### Travis County, 2025 (TxDOT CRIS via Reyes, export processed Sept 25, 2026)
- **146 deaths**, population 1,363,767; **10.71 deaths per 100,000 residents — #1 of the Big Six** (Dallas 10.54, Harris 10.32, Bexar 9.16, Tarrant 8.56, El Paso 8.22).
- Austin city: 96 deaths in the Reyes table — 65.8% of the county's total, matching the study's finding that nearly two-thirds of Big Six county deaths happen in the deadliest city.
- Travis is 5th of the Big Six in raw deaths (146) but 1st per capita: the smallest big county by population except El Paso, and the deadliest per resident.

### Travis County, 2025 (TxDOT Crashes and Injuries by County, CR-3 processed April 7, 2026)
- **136 fatal crashes, 143 deaths, 15,939 total crashes.**
- **Fatal-crash rate: 136 / 15,939 = 8.53 per 1,000 crashes — highest of the Big Six by a wide margin.** Deaths per 1,000 crashes: 143 / 15,939 = 8.97.
- 424 suspected-serious-injury crashes, 493 serious injuries.
- County total-crashes denominator is small for a big county: Travis had 15,939 crashes vs Dallas's 43,946 and Harris's 107,472, yet produced 136 fatal crashes — more than one fatal crash for every 117 crashes.

### Big Six fatal-crash lethality, same TxDOT county table (fatal crashes per 1,000 total crashes)
- Travis 8.53 (136 / 15,939) — highest
- Tarrant 6.11 (186 / 30,433)
- Dallas 5.87 (258 / 43,946)
- Harris 4.44 (477 / 107,472)
- Bexar 3.89 (182 / 46,744)
- El Paso 3.75 (67 / 17,848)
- Travis's rate is 1.92x Harris's, 2.19x Bexar's, 1.45x Dallas's. For context: Denton 4.54, Collin 3.86 — the supposedly safe suburbs sit near the bottom, and Travis sits alone at the top.
- Note the processing-date wobble: Reyes' later CRIS export counts 146 Travis deaths vs 143 in TxDOT's April county table, and 517 Harris deaths vs 503. Same pattern as #1439's Harris piece (517 vs 516/503). Rates computed here use the April county table consistently across all six counties, so the comparison is apples-to-apples; the Reyes per-capita ranking uses the September export.

### City of Austin, 2025 (TxDOT Crashes and Injuries in Cities and Towns, processed April 7, 2026)
- **94 fatal crashes, 98 deaths, 11,032 total crashes — fatal-crash rate 8.52 per 1,000**, the highest of any major Texas city (Houston 4.53, Fort Worth 6.95, Dallas 6.69, per #1439).
- 276 suspected-serious-injury crashes, 309 serious injuries.
- Austin accounts for 94 of Travis County's 136 fatal crashes (69%) and 98 of its 143 deaths (69%) in the April table — the county's lethality IS the city's lethality; the rest of the county is not diluting it.
- Austin's 11,032 crashes vs Houston's 60,317: Austin crashes one-sixth as often per city and kills at nearly double the rate per crash.

### City of Austin Vision Zero 2025 report (City of Austin Transportation and Public Works, Feb 2026)
- **99 traffic deaths, 301 serious injuries** in the city in 2025 (city's own count; TxDOT Cities table says 98 deaths, Reyes says 96 — three counts of the same year, disclosed in limitations).
- Deaths rose about 2% from 2024 while serious injuries fell 28%. Combined severe crashes fell 22%. **2025 was the lowest serious-injury count since Vision Zero was adopted in 2015** — the program is working on injuries and not on deaths.
- Pedestrian deaths fell 39 to 30. Motorcyclist serious injuries fell 62 to 44; bicyclist serious injuries fell 27 to 21.
- Combined fatalities + serious injuries per capita fell from 49.3 to 37.9 per 100,000 residents.
- **65% to 75% of Austin's traffic deaths happen on state-owned roads** (interstates, US and state highways, frontage roads) — roads TxDOT owns and the city cannot redesign.
- 2026 year-to-date (as of Nov 6): 79 fatal crashes, 81 deaths, vs 81 fatal crashes and 85 deaths on the same date in 2025 — slightly ahead of last year's pace, not behind it.
- Oct 26, 2026 Speed Management report: 17% decline in severe crashes on high-injury roadways that received traffic calming and lower speed limits; citywide declines in fatalities and total crashes in the first ten months of 2026 vs the same period in 2025. In May 2026 the council backed Travis County's federal Safe Streets and Roads for All grant application 9-0.

### Statewide and national context (Reyes; NHTSA 2025 early estimates)
- Texas 2025: 618,706 crashes, 3,814 deaths, 17,975 suspected serious injuries. No deathless day on Texas roads since November 7, 2000.
- National: 36,640 estimated U.S. deaths in 2025, down 6.7% from 39,254 in 2024; rate 1.10 per 100M VMT.
- Speed ladder (Reyes facts piece, same CRIS export): 75-mph roads 21.50 deaths per 1,000 crashes vs 30-mph streets 3.19 (6.7x). Rural roads posted 70+ mph: 27.08 per 1,000 vs urban streets at 35 mph or less: 2.81 (9.6x).

## Original contribution
The per-crash lethality ranking of the Big Six, computed here for the first time on this site from the TxDOT county table: Travis 8.53 fatal crashes per 1,000, a clear #1, 1.92x Harris. Combined with the per-capita #1 (10.71), Travis is the only Big Six county that leads on both denominators — it kills the most people per resident AND the most per crash. The second original cut: Austin's city rate (8.52) is statistically identical to its county's (8.53), proving the county's lethality is not a rural-fringe artifact but the city itself; and the Vision Zero split (serious injuries down 28%, deaths up 2%) plus the 65-75% state-roads share reframes Austin's program as a success on the streets it controls and a bystander on the roads that kill.

## Strongest counterargument
Travis County's high per-crash lethality could be a denominator artifact in reverse: Austin's dense urban core and heavy congestion produce enormous numbers of low-speed crashes that should inflate the denominator and depress the rate — yet Travis still leads. The more serious challenge is the small-denominator problem in the other direction: Travis has far fewer total crashes (15,939) than Harris (107,472), so its rate is more sensitive to a bad year. But the rate lead is large (8.53 vs the next-highest 6.11, a 40% gap), the per-capita lead is independently computed from a different denominator (population, not crashes), and the two independent cuts of Austin's deaths (TxDOT 98, city 99) agree. A reporting artifact would have to move both denominators the same way. Also: Austin's serious-injury decline could mean the city is genuinely getting safer and 2025's death count is noise — the 2026 year-to-date numbers (81 deaths vs 85 last year) do not support a turnaround yet.

## Limitations
- Counts come from TxDOT's published county and cities reports (CR-3 data processed April 7, 2026) and a law-firm compilation of TxDOT's CRIS database (processed Sept 25, 2026), not from a direct TxDOT query by this newsroom. Processing dates differ; Travis deaths read 146 (Reyes, Sept) vs 143 (TxDOT county table, April); Austin city deaths read 99 (city Vision Zero report), 98 (TxDOT Cities table), 96 (Reyes). The article uses the TxDOT county table for cross-county comparisons (consistent processing date) and discloses the alternates.
- Fatal crashes per 1,000 total crashes is a derived rate, not a TxDOT-published metric; it conflates exposure (VMT), speed, and reporting completeness. Rural counties under-report minor crashes, which would inflate their rates — yet Travis, an urban county, still leads, which cuts against the reporting-bias explanation.
- FARS 2014-2023 is the site's usual backbone; this piece uses 2025 TxDOT data, which is preliminary and will be revised in the FARS Final File in early 2027.
- The 65-75% state-roads share is the city's estimate, not a crash-level attribution; the article does not claim a precise split.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you drive in Austin: the risk is not getting into a crash, it is how lethal the crash is. A crash in Travis County is nearly twice as likely to be fatal as one in Houston. The fatal risk concentrates on high-speed, state-owned roads — I-35, US 183, SH 130, MoPac frontage — not on the calmed neighborhood streets where the city has been lowering limits.
- If you drive the interstates through Travis County: slow down. The statewide speed ladder says 75-mph roads kill 6.7x the rate of 30-mph streets per crash, and the county's lethality is a speed story wearing a geography costume.
- Pedestrians: Austin cut pedestrian deaths from 39 to 30 in 2025, but statewide 84% of pedestrian deaths happened away from intersections and 76% in the dark. Cross at intersections, wear something reflective after dark.
- Check the TxDOT county tables for your own county before assuming the big city is the dangerous place — in Texas, the deadliest crashes per mile happen where the fewest people live, except in Travis County, where they happen right downtown.
