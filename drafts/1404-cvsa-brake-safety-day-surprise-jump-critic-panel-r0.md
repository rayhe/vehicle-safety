## Scores

### 1. General Editor — 8.7/10
Structure follows the template exactly: kicker, headline, lede with the key stat bolded, pull stat, seven body paragraphs, references, limitations disclaimer. Headline is a pure data contrast (nobody warned / one in seven) that earns its sensationalism. Lede front-loads 4,021 inspections and 574 parked trucks without throat-clearing. Deductions: the news peg is ~6 months old (event April 14, release spring 2026, article Nov 5) and the piece never dates the release in the lede, the same staleness knock #1403 took; and the body runs ~1,000 words against the guide's 300-500 range, consistent with recent drafts but still long. The YoY comparison is the justification for running it now, and it is stated, but a reader checking the dateline will still wonder why April data is November news.

### 2. Voice Coach — 8.8/10
Distinctly Rex: "April 14 was a Tuesday, and nobody told the trucks," "Rust is not a manufacturing defect; it is a maintenance schedule that nobody kept," "The brakes did not get worse in a year; the warning did." No swapped-bylines risk; this could not be a Vin or Clara piece. Sentence rhythm script PASSES comfortably: variance 424.2 (target ≥200), short 6.7% (target ≤15%), long 44.4% (target ≥15%). Zero literal em dashes. Zero banned phrases. "The" starters 1.2%. Deductions: the rhythm fix merged several fragments into longer constructions, which cost a little of Rex's staccato punch (the "Still." one-word sentence survived and works); and the "For scale" paragraph reads like a data insert rather than Rex talking, slightly off-voice. The merged "No judgment call, no bad day" lead-in is the better version anyway.

### 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.9/10
Honest about the jump's fragility in the counterargument paragraph: smaller inspection pool, drums-and-rotors emphasis pushing inspectors toward exactly the failing components, 2025's 8.7% possibly the outlier rather than 2026's 14.3%, and the rate sitting inside the 2018-2024 band. The missing-BSW claim is carefully bounded to what was checked ("absent from CVSA's results page as checked Nov. 5"), not asserted as never published anywhere. No crash victims exploited, no anecdotes invented. Deductions: the "one in four fails a full inspection" line in the first draft cited a Level I rate the article never showed the reader — caught in review and replaced with the in-article 19.0% Roadcheck figure. The 70% other-vehicle-occupant split is attributed to the NHTSA 2024 overview via the site's own prior verified article rather than a directly re-read source; acceptable chain, but second-hand.

### 4. Social / Shareability — 8.8/10
Headline is the share: one number, one failure mode, one surprise. Pull stat (14.3%) is clean and quotable. Best shareable lines: "Nobody prepares for the one they do not," "Rust is a maintenance schedule that nobody kept," and the closer "The brakes did not get worse in a year; the warning did." The 64% jump is the argument people will screenshot, and it needs its 8.7-to-14.3 context, which the article gives in the same paragraph. Minor deduction: no single-sentence pull quote isolated as a blockquote, and the most viral framing (announced 19.0% vs surprise 14.3%) is deliberately hedged as directional, which is honest but costs shares.

### 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Every number traces to a named primary source with a verifiable page: CVSA 2026 BSD release (4,021 inspections, 574 OOS, US 460/3,301, Canada 112/716, 313 meeting the 20% criterion, 121 hose/tubing, 47 steering-axle, 40 air-loss, 193 other, drums/rotors 43 with 21 OOS, rusted rotor 24, PBBT 26/349, Level I 3,643/4,021) and CVSA results page series (2018-2026 rates). Arithmetic spot-checked: 574/4,021 = 14.27% → 14.3%; 398/4,569 = 8.71% → 8.7%; +5.6 points; 14.3/8.7 = 1.64x → 64% relative; 1,595/11,531 = 13.8%; 1,667/10,358 = 16.1%; 1,273/10,091 = 12.6%; 1,290/9,132 = 14.1%; 570/4,898 = 11.6%; 26/349 = 7.45%; 3,643/4,021 = 90.6%; 3,753/5,340 = 70.3% → "70 percent". The 20% rule on a two-axle truck (1 of 4 brakes = 25%) is correct. The Roadcheck 19.0% (10,350/54,575) was verified in #1403. No legal claims made. Deduction only: the 70% split's citation chain runs through the site's July 8 article to DOT HS 813 791 rather than a fresh direct read of that overview.

### 6. Research Rigor — 8.8/10
Original contribution, four parts: (a) the 2018-2026 Brake Safety Day rate series assembled from CVSA's results page, with the YoY jump computed (+5.6 points, 64% relative) and the largest-move claim checked against every adjacent pair; (b) the announced-vs-unannounced contrast (Roadcheck 19.0% all-cause vs BSD 14.3% brakes-only), explicitly labeled directional; (c) the PBBT machine cross-check (7.45% measured failure) as an inspector-judgment-free corroboration; (d) the missing 2026 Brake Safety Week results observation, bounded to the checked page. Limitations are covered in the counterargument paragraph and the disclaimer (non-random sample, shrinking pool, emphasis effects, Mexico n=4 excluded). Strongest counterargument is stated at full strength before being answered. Deductions: the peg is 6 months old, so the "news" is the analysis, not the event; and no US-only BSD rate trend is available because CVSA publishes country splits only for 2026.

### 7. Data Presentation — 8.7/10
Pull stat isolates the single most important number (14.3%) with a plain-language label. Rates are given with their bases (per inspection, per violation) every time they appear. The 64% figure is shown with its inputs (8.7% to 14.3%) rather than asserted. The series is presented in prose with every year named, which reads well but is the same table-in-prose deduction #1403 took: an 8-row year/rate table would have been clearer, though prose matches site convention. Minor deduction: the country split (US 13.9% vs Canada 15.6%) appears only in the research file, not the article; the article's "47 jurisdictions" framing is accurate but the split would have added a useful comparison.
