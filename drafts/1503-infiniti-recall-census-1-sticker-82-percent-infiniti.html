Existential Dread

Infiniti Filed One Recall for Its 2026 Cars. It Was a Typo, and 82 Percent of the Cars Were Infinitis.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Infiniti sells two cars now. Two. A QX60 and a QX80, a lineup you could park in a two-car garage and still have room for the lawnmower. This year Infiniti filed exactly one recall campaign touching a 2026 model, and it was a typo on a sticker. But here is the part that bends the light: 138,572 of the 168,150 vehicles in that campaign are Infinitis, 82.4 percent, which means the recall the press called a Nissan recall was mostly an Infiniti recall. Smallest lineup in the census, smallest count, biggest pile of cars.[1][2]

82.4%
Share of the vehicles in Infiniti's only 2026 recall campaign that are Infinitis, not Nissans

Method first, because the method is both the joke and the punchline. I ran the corrected-vocabulary census across both of Infiniti's canonical 2026 models, 18 queries against the NHTSA recalls API, keeping only campaigns filed in calendar 2026. Seventeen queries returned nothing at all. One came back with two campaigns, and one of those was filed in 2025. That is the whole census. Two models, eighteen queries, one campaign, and the campaign is a sticker.[1]

That campaign is 26V455, filed July 16, and it concerns the Gross Axle Weight Rating printed on the certification label, the little placard on the door jamb that tells you how much weight each axle is certified to carry. Nissan's engineers calculated the number using only the maximum tire pressure, when the standard requires maximum pressure and the manufacturer-recommended pressure together, so the printed rating was wrong and a loaded truck could exceed its certified axle limit without its owner ever knowing. NHTSA calls this a violation of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 110. Nissan discovered the discrepancy in May 2025, while developing a new model, fixed the calculation in production on March 16, 2026, and decided to recall on July 8. Defect estimate: 100 percent, because every label was printed wrong. Warranty claims: zero. Accidents: zero. Injuries: zero. Nobody was hurt by the typo. Everybody got a new sticker.[2]

Remedy, in full: a dealer installs a corrected label, free, in under thirty minutes, or Nissan mails you a label and step-by-step instructions so you can stick it on yourself. Somewhere in the American luxury car market, a recall remedy is a peel-and-stick craft project. Nissan's own filing says no customer would be eligible for reimbursement, because no customer paid anything, because nothing was ever replaced. A hundred and sixty-eight thousand trucks, and the repair is office supplies.[2]

Now the detail that stops you cold. Infiniti's share of 26V455 is 138,572 vehicles: 24,277 QX56s from 2011 to 2013, a truck that stopped production thirteen years ago, and 114,295 QX80s spanning 2014 to 2027. And the 2027 QX80 population is one car. One. A single 2027 QX80, its own line in a federal filing, a model year that had barely started, carrying the wrong number on its door jamb like a typo in a birth certificate. Meanwhile the 2026 QX80 slice is 10,499 trucks, just 7.6 percent of the Infiniti total, and the Armada, the Nissan half of this supposedly Nissan recall, contributes 29,578 vehicles, barely a sixth of the campaign. Read the filing again and the headline inverts: this was never a Nissan recall with some Infinitis in it. It was an Infiniti recall that happened to include some Armadas.[2]

That is the counted record. Now the excluded record, which is where the danger lives. Two filings touch Infiniti vehicles and the census drops both, because both were filed in 2025, and the method only keeps calendar-2026 filings. First, 25V821, filed November 27, 2025: a software error in the infotainment system can blank the whole display at startup, including the rear-view camera image when the driver shifts into reverse, a violation of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111. It covers 64,085 vehicles, 18,505 of them 2025-2026 QX80s, and the fix is a software reprogram the dealer performs or the car downloads over the air. Owner letters went out February 2, 2026. Second, 25V437, filed June 27, 2025: a manufacturing defect in the VC-Turbo engine bearings, the main, A-, C-, and L-link bearings in the 1.5-liter and 2.0-liter variable-compression engines, can damage the engine until it fails, and a bearing failure can breach the engine block and discharge hot oil, which is the polite federal way of saying the engine can catch fire. It covers 443,899 vehicles, 89,660 of them Infinitis, 84,536 QX50s and 5,124 QX55s, and it closed a federal investigation that opened in December 2023 after owners reported engine failure, knocking, and metal shavings in the oil pan. Nissan estimates 1.2 percent actually carry the defect, about a thousand Infinitis, with zero accidents or injuries as of May 2025.[3][4]

Add the counted and excluded filings together and the picture flips. Infiniti vehicles across all three filings total 246,737, and the census counts 138,572 of them, 56.2 percent. For every Infiniti the count includes, 0.78 more were recalled under filings the method excludes, and the excluded ones are the ones with the actual danger: an engine that can seize and a camera that can go blank, against a sticker with zero injuries. A shopper who reads "Infiniti: 1 recall" and walks onto a used lot is walking into the wrong conversation, because the used lot is full of 2019-2022 QX50s and 2025-2026 QX80s, and those are the cars with the engine and the camera, not the sticker.[1]

Parent-company math makes the point sharper. Nissan filed 7 campaigns for its 2026 cars, and 26V455 is one of the seven, counted through the Armada. Infiniti adds zero distinct defects to Nissan's total: Nissan Motor Co has 7 distinct 2026 defects across both brands. Lexus added two to Toyota's 15, and Acura added none to Honda's 4. Infiniti adds none to Nissan's 7. "Infiniti: 1" is not a separate record. It is Nissan's record with an Infiniti badge on 82 percent of the cars.[5]

One of Infiniti's two nameplates carries the entire census. QX60, the volume seller, the three-row family truck that actually pays the rent, carries zero 2026 filings. Its last campaign was a brake caliper in March 2025. Meanwhile the QX80, the flagship, has now had three rear-visibility recalls in fourteen months, all software, all Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111: 24V748 in October 2024, a backup camera software error across 37,236 Rogues and QX80s; 24V747 the same month, an Around View Monitor fault in 5,176 model-year 2025 QX80s, 1 percent estimated defective; and 25V821 in November 2025, the blank infotainment screen. Three filings, three different ways for the same truck's backup camera to fail, and only the third touches a 2026 model at all. A flagship whose defining feature is a camera that keeps not working.[1][3]

Now the other side, stated fairly, because the 1-count flatters Infiniti three ways and a reader deserves to hear it from me before the comments do. First, the method excludes the two filings that actually endanger Infiniti owners, so anyone who takes "Infiniti: 1" as the brand's safety record is wrong about 108,165 vehicles. Second, the one counted campaign is not an Infiniti defect at all. It is a Nissan Shatai plant defect, a miscalculated label value, that happens to include 138,572 Infinitis, and the 100 percent defect estimate only means every label was printed wrong, not that every truck is dangerous; zero claims, zero injuries. Third, the count partly measures a shrinking catalog, not improving quality: Infiniti sells two models, down from five nameplates a few years ago, so there are fewer nameplates to recall, the same caveat the Nissan census applied to Nissan's seven. Strip the shared filing and Infiniti's own 2026 record is zero campaigns and zero defects, which is a true statement about new Infiniti metal and a misleading statement about Infiniti owners, since 89,660 of them are driving QX50s and QX55s with engines that can fail. Honest headline, then: Infiniti's new cars had a clean year. Infiniti's recent cars did not.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 246,737 total counts every vehicle in each filing across all model years, not 2026-only cars. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates, not 2026-model-year counts; the 2026-only Infiniti total cannot be computed from the filings, because 26V455's QX80 2026 slice, 10,499 trucks, is the only 2026-model-year number in the campaign. Unlike Lexus, Infiniti has one 2025-filed campaign touching a 2026 model (25V821), not two: the unfiltered QX80 query returned only 25V821 and 26V455. And the NHTSA API's vocabulary gaps remain the central risk in this method: a campaign hiding under an untested model spelling would be missed, and the direction of any remaining bias is undercount. Two series-standard checks, for the record: the amended 26V455 filing totals 168,150 while the announced total was 168,149, a one-vehicle discrepancy, and the Infiniti total here is computed from the amended per-year table; the 25V821 campaign total (64,085) is a secondary reading of the filing, while the QX80 population (18,505) comes from Infiniti's own service bulletin; and none of the counted campaigns is flagged as an over-the-air update, even though 25V821's remedy is explicitly dealer or over-the-air, so the flag undercounts software remedies, as the Nissan census already showed. Late filings will move every number in this article.

If you own a 2014-2027 QX80 or a 2011-2013 QX56 with 22-inch wheels, the label recall is a free corrected certification label, dealer-installed or mailed to you, and you should check the axle weight rating on your door-jamb label before loading to rated capacity, because the printed number may be wrong. If you own a 2025-2026 QX80, the camera recall is the one that matters, and it is not in the census count: the infotainment screen can go blank at startup, including the rear-view image, and the fix is a free software reprogram, dealer or over-the-air, so check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now and do not wait for a letter that already went out in February. If you own a 2019-2022 QX50 or a 2022 QX55 with the VC-Turbo engine, the engine recall is the one that matters most: the bearings can fail, the engine can seize, hot oil can escape, and the dealer inspects the oil pan for metal debris, free. If you are shopping a 2026 Infiniti, the census is accurate for new metal: one recall across two nameplates, and the QX60 carries zero. But shop used Infinitis by campaign, not by badge. Check any used Infiniti VIN against 25V437, 25V821, and 26V455 before signing, because the recalls that matter most to an Infiniti owner are the two the count leaves out.
