## The event (data peg)

- Autoevolution (Oct 1, 2026), using NHTSA's recall database: through October 1, 2026, **Ford issued 78 recalls**, vs **35 for FCA US LLC (Stellantis North America)** and **25 for General Motors**. Ford also holds the **two largest recalls of 2026**: campaign 26V104 (4,381,878 vehicles, integrated trailer module software) and 26V237 (1,392,935 vehicles).
- Ford's own framing (TheNewsWheel, Feb 2026): an "intensive strategy to quickly find and fix hardware and software issues and go the extra mile to help protect customers." Ford says it **more than doubled the size of its safety and technical expert teams** and expanded testing of key vehicle systems including powertrains.
- Autoevolution's read: the elevated recall activity is deliberate, problems uncovered before customers report them, part of a quality turnaround ahead of the 2027 model year Fathom EV on the new Universal Electric Vehicle platform.
- Context: industry-wide, H1 2026 auto recalls affected nearly 24M vehicles on pace for a five-year high, with Sedgwick's Chris Harvey noting it "does not necessarily indicate a decline in vehicle quality" but reflects common platforms/components amplifying single defects (covered by #1060, different thesis: fat campaigns, not self-reporting strategy).

## The remedy data (original contribution)

- NHTSA Recall Quarterly Report for 26V104 (static.nhtsa.gov, filed per 49 CFR 573.7, Q2 2026 report dated Jul 21, 2026): population **4,381,878**; total remedied **2,876,853** as of Jul 21, 2026 — roughly **65.6% fixed within five months**, mostly via over-the-air software update. Unreachable: 42,319.
- Reuters/David Shepardson (Feb 26, 2026): Ford aware of **407 incidents** potentially related to 26V104; **no crashes** attributed; Ford reopened its investigation in January 2026 after a December NHTSA monthly meeting.
- Ford's own Part 573 filing: estimated defect rate **1%** of the recalled population. Headline number 4.38M; expected actual defect vehicles ~43,800.

## Kill test

- Newsworthy? Yes: fresh Oct 2026 data, counterintuitive thesis (most recalls = most honest automaker, or the worst quality — the paradox IS the story), distinct from #749 (the 26V104 race-condition story itself) and #1060 (industry-wide fat campaigns).
- Novel angle: nobody frames campaign-count leadership as a self-reporting quality metric. Vin's beat exactly.
- Actionable: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; enable OTA updates (2.87M fixed without a dealer visit); read the defect-rate line (1%), not the headline count; prefer manufacturers that self-report over ones that wait for NHTSA investigations.
