## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10
Lede is pure Clara: "Before you sign that lease" opens, the defect is in the first paragraph, and the seam-not-anchor reveal lands in the lede's last line. Structure is clean: what, why the seat matters, filing precision, interim remedy, perspective, counterargument, action. Deductions: prose runs ~700 words against the 300-500 guide (recent norm is longer, but the counterargument paragraph carries two ideas that could be one), and one sentence misstates the vehicle's seating geometry (see Rigor). The "eight weeks" paragraph is the piece's best: a prohibition with a calendar attached.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Distinct Clara: direct, practical, slightly angry on behalf of parents, and the catchphrase opener is hers verbatim. Rhythm script passes on all three metrics after the fragment merges, and the remaining fragments ("And middle is exactly the seat...") are deliberate, not accidental. No banned phrases, no "The" starters to speak of. One overreach: "Start with the seat itself" is mild throat-clearing for a writer who otherwise starts mid-thought. Fix it and this is a 9.5.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.5/10
Counterargument stated at full strength and conceded where fair ("the system working exactly as designed"). No victims to exploit: the piece states plainly that NHTSA reports zero crashes, injuries, or complaints, and the disclaimer repeats it. The one ethical soft spot is the juxtaposition: 550 child occupant deaths and 167 unrestrained deaths sit two paragraphs from a stitching defect with no injuries, and a fast reader could leave with a body count the defect does not have. The piece inoculates against this twice, but the numbers still do emotional work the defect has not earned. Half a point off; the inoculation is good enough to ship.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.5/10
Headline is built to travel: a carmaker stitching child-seat anchors shut is a sentence people forward to the group chat. Pull stat "71%" is the shareable number, and the "safest seat is the broken one" frame is the pull quote. Deducted half a point because the piece is long for social skimming and the kicker ("The Gap") is generic; the story's real kicker is the center seat, which the headline already carries.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
The citations check out against the acknowledgement letter: campaign 26V732, filed November 4, 2026, acknowledgement November 16, 3,914 units, 2027 Atlas only, FMVSS 225, VW number 74iF, letters January 3, 2027, VINs searchable November 10, 2026, remedy "repair the seams," advisory scoped to LATCH/ISOFIX installation in the center seat. "Fails a federal standard" is the correct characterization of a noncompliance recall. Deduction: the "three rows of LATCH positions" sentence misdescribes the vehicle (see Rigor), which is a factual-accuracy failure even though no statute is misstated. Fix it and this is a 9.0.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.0/10
Primary source read in full (the acknowledgement letter), IIHS children page read in full, NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts Children 2024 cited to its DOT number, prior-generation Atlas FARS rate correctly labeled as the earlier generation, limitations and counterargument present and honest. Two rigor failures: (a) "One of the three rows of LATCH positions is closed" is wrong — the Atlas second row has three seating positions, not three rows of LATCH positions; the center position is one of three LATCH positions in one row. (b) "the 800,000-car Takata wave announced the same week" is loose: Honda's announcement was November 20, five days before this article's November 25 date and in the prior calendar week; "announced days earlier" is what the evidence supports. Original contribution (safest-seat inversion, stitching-as-defect, prohibition-with-a-calendar) is real and survives the fixes.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
Every number traces to a cited source: 3,914 units, 71%/54% car-seat effectiveness, 795/550/167 child deaths, 76% rear-seat share, 0.06 prior-gen Atlas rate, 60 days filing-to-letters ("about eight weeks" verified). The pull-label's 71% is correct and properly scoped to infants in passenger cars. Deducted half a point for the two imprecise sentences flagged under Rigor, which are data-presentation failures, not just prose slips.
