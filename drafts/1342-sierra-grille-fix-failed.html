GM's Sierra Grille Fix Didn't Hold. 21 Trucks Lost Their Grilles Anyway.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

On October 9, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened recall query RQ26003 against a recall. Not a vehicle. A recall. Because 21 of the 118 owner complaints in the agency's file describe the upper grille deflector detaching after the truck had already received the recall repair meant to stop exactly that.

21
Detachments reported after the "fix." Out of 118 complaints total.

Here is the paradox in its Sunday suit. In February 2025, GM recalled 70,768 2022 Sierra 1500s under campaign 25V-060, warning that the eight attachment points holding the chrome grille deflector could fracture over time until, with all eight gone, the bar was free to leave the truck at highway speed. The remedy said to reinforce the old clips and keep them, rather than replace anything, and twenty-one owners are now telling the feds the old clips left anyway.

The scope looks worse than the remedy, because RQ26003 names 535,639 trucks across the 2022 through 2024 model years, more than seven times the original recall population. When GM filed 25V-060, its defense was that 2023 and 2024 trucks got deflectors from outside the supplier's suspect production window, so they didn't need to be included. NHTSA's complaint file now includes those model years, which is precisely the question a recall query exists to ask: did the recall cover the right trucks, and did the repair actually work?

Fairness requires the counterargument, and it's a strong one. This is a trim bar, not a brake booster. The opening summary lists zero crashes, zero injuries, zero fatalities across 118 incidents. Some owners report cracked windshields and swerving neighbors, but a loose bar with an audible rattle before departure (GM's own filing says it rattles first) is a lesser hazard than most things NHTSA investigates. The 21 post-repair complaints are owner allegations, not verified failures, and "18% of complaints" is not a failure rate when nobody knows the denominator of repaired trucks. A recall query is an audit, not a verdict.

And yet. The timeline reads like a noir procedural where the detective gets told to stop looking. A GM employee flagged a detached deflector on a driven 2022 Sierra through the company's internal Speak Up for Safety program on January 19, 2024. GM opened an investigation, then closed it on May 23, 2024, finding no defect trend. It reopened the case on December 11, 2024, when warranty claims started climbing, and the recall itself came two months after that. If you own a 2022 through 2024 Sierra 1500, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 25V-060 and whether the repair shows complete. Listen for the rattle; that is the warning GM itself documented. If you drive a 2023 or 2024, no recall covers you today, so if your deflector lets go, file at nhtsa.gov. Owner complaints are, quite literally, what opened this query.
