Your Speedometer Is Lying to You. Ford Just Recalled 102,159 F-Series to Prove It.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 17, 2027

Somewhere in Dearborn, an engineer is explaining how the speedometer on America's best-selling truck became an unreliable narrator. On October 20, Ford recalled 102,159 F-Series pickups because the analog cluster's speedometer and tachometer needles can stick or drift, showing the driver a speed that isn't the speed.[1]

102,159
F-Series trucks recalled for speedometers that invent their own numbers (NHTSA 26V-653)

Ford's entire working backbone is on the affected list: 2025 and 2026 F-250 through F-600 Super Duties built between December 2024 and April 2026, 2026 F-150s, and the 2026 F-750, the last of which covers trucks built on a single day, November 21, 2025.[2] Federal law has an opinion about this. FMVSS No. 101, "Controls and Displays," requires the speedometer to work; these trucks are being recalled because theirs might not.[3]

Here is the detail that made me set down my coffee. Buried in the same filing: owners with the redundant digital speedometer "may notice a difference between the 2 speeds displayed."[2] Two instruments, one dashboard, two speeds, and no indication which one is lying.

Your truck is now a courtroom with two witnesses contradicting each other under oath, and you're the one who pays the ticket.

That is the real sting of this defect, and it isn't mechanical, it's legal. A speedometer exists so drivers can obey speed limits; a speedometer that invents numbers turns legal compliance into a guessing game. School zones, construction zones, every radar gun in America: all of them assume the needle tells the truth, but on 102,159 trucks, it might not.

Ford's defense deserves a fair hearing, because it's a decent one: the company says it knows of no crashes or injuries tied to the defect, many affected trucks carry the digital readout as a backup, and the fix is a free instrument-cluster module replacement at the dealer.[2] No one has been hurt, as far as the record shows, and the failure mode is a display-standard violation, not a structural one. But "no injuries yet" is a thin blanket in October, when the remedy letters don't arrive until December.

26V-653 is also one more line on a ledger that has become genuinely hard to read. By mid-October, Ford had filed 75 recalls in 2026 alone, after setting the all-time single-year record with 153 in 2025, nearly double the mark GM set in 2014.[4] Ford says the flood is intentional housekeeping: double the safety staff, more testing to failure, pull the truck before the problem pulls you. Whether you find that convincing depends on how much benefit of the doubt you extend a speedometer that lies.

Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; the campaign has been searchable since October 7. If your truck carries both readouts, cross-check them now. A persistent disagreement between needle and digits is the tell. Interim letters go out around October 26, the remedy letter follows in December, and the replacement costs you nothing.[3] Until then, distrust the needle and verify the digits. A dashboard that argues with itself is at least honest about one thing: it can't be trusted.
