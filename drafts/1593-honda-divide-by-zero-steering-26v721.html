Somebody Divided by Zero in Your Steering. Honda Recalled 635,808 Cars.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 26, 2026

Honda recalled 635,808 vehicles this month because the software steering your car can divide by zero. Not metaphorically. A floating-point division inside the Driver Attention Monitor's road-condition compensation puts a zero on the bottom, throws an internal error, and the electric power steering software answers by switching off all assist. Your steering wheel goes heavy, the warning lights come on, and a system built to notice when you are drowsy has just taken away the thing you steer with.[1]

1,788
Days from Honda's first report of the steering fault (November 29, 2021) to its decision that it was a safety defect (October 22, 2026).

Three nameplates carry the bug: 383,110 Honda Pilots from 2023 to 2025, 177,100 Acura MDXs from 2022 to 2024, and 75,598 Acura TLXs from 2021 to 2025. Honda's own filing estimates that every single one of them, 100 percent, carries the defective software configuration. Supplier is Robert Bosch Automotive Steering of Florence, Kentucky, and all 42 affected steering rack part numbers are rewritable, which means the fix is a dealer reflash, no new hardware, no charge. Fault only shows up while driving straight, which is the one time nobody expects the wheel to fight back.[1]

Timeline is where this stops being a math error and starts being an institutional one. Honda got its first report on November 29, 2021. Complaints kept coming between August 2023 and August 2025, but returned parts tested clean, because dealers or the supplier had already deleted the stored error codes before Bosch saw them. Bosch finally found the failure by analyzing returned parts on August 22, 2025. Honda recreated it from dashcam footage that December, confirmed in February 2026 that it only happens driving straight, and decided on October 22, 2026 that it was a safety defect. Five years, minus six weeks. One injury, 146 warranty claims, no deaths, and a defect that hid behind wiped diagnostic codes the whole time.[3]

Now compare it with its sibling. Acura recalled the 2025 RDX last year for a steering fault with the same failsafe outcome, loss of assist, from a different calibration error. That recall estimated 0.3 percent of its 17,334 vehicles defective. This one is 100 percent of 635,808. Same supplier family, same philosophy of handling errors by killing assist, and the difference between a calibration slip and a configuration error turns out to be the difference between a few dozen cars and a small city. One more hole in the paperwork: the 2025 MDX is not in this recall, even though the 2025 Pilot and TLX are, and the filing never says why.[4]

Honda's side deserves a full hearing. Failure needs a narrow set of conditions, only shows up driving straight, and leaves the car steerable, just heavier. One injury in roughly four years across 635,808 vehicles is a low-severity record, and the remedy is a free software reflash. On those numbers this is a small defect with a clean fix, and the five-year timeline reflects a genuinely intermittent fault, not foot-dragging. All of that is true. None of it answers the design question: why did a subroutine for watching driver alertness have a kill switch on steering assist at all?

What this story does not prove: no crash count is attributed to this defect in the filing, and the single injury report comes with no circumstances attached. Exact conditions that produce the zero denominator are not public beyond driving straight and the Driver Attention Monitor context. Speed, temperature, and road surface triggers are unknown. Whether the 2025 MDX runs different EPS software or was excluded for another reason is unanswered, and the injury and warranty counts are Honda's own self-reported numbers.

If you own a 2021-2025 TLX, a 2022-2024 MDX, or a 2023-2025 Pilot, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. VINs have been searchable since November 11, and the owner letters do not go out until December 14. If your steering goes heavy on a straight road with the EPS lights on, that is this defect: keep both hands on the wheel, slow down gradually, do not yank it, and get to a dealer. Reflash is free. And if you drive a 2025 MDX, check your VIN anyway. Filing says you are not in the recall. Filing never says why.[2]
