# Research: #1336 — Volkswagen Cut 20% of Your Battery Over the Internet. Audi Is Asking You to Do It Yourself.

**Slug:** 1336-vw-ota-80-cap-audi-honor-system
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — consumer-guidance beat; sequel to her own #866/#1105)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date researched:** 2026-10-30

## Kill test
**Newsworthy?** YES. 31,572 vehicles across two brands share the same battery-fire defect (>80% state of charge can trigger thermal propagation; root cause still unknown; no permanent remedy exists). This week, reporting surfaced how the two brands are enforcing the identical interim instruction — and they could not be more different: Volkswagen pushed an over-the-air update that caps affected ID.4s at 80%, with owners given about a week to opt out; Audi has not forced any limit, leaving 9,048 Q4 e-tron owners to set the cap themselves in the car or the app.

**Novel angle?** YES. The site covered both recalls' announcements (#1105 and #1194 for the ID.4's 26V630; #1268 for the Q4's 26V698). None of them covered the OTA enforcement, the opt-out window, or the two-brand policy split. The story is not the recall — it is what the recall is doing to owners' cars right now, and the asymmetry of who gets to decide.

**Self-critique:** After 1,335 articles, is this "just another ID.4 battery story"? The prior pieces asked "what's wrong." This one asks "who gets to change your car without asking, and who just asks." The remote-derating-with-opt-out is a consumer-rights event as much as a safety event: VW demonstrated it can reach into your car and take 20% of your range, and chose a one-week opt-out as the consent mechanism. Audi's approach trusts the owner but creates an obvious compliance gap — a driver who never sets the limit keeps charging to 100% with the same defect. Both companies admit they do not know the root cause and cannot identify which specific vehicles actually have it (0.1% estimate). That combination — remote enforcement, honor-system alternative, unknown cause, no fix — has not been written here.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)

1. **VW ID.4:** NHTSA campaign **26V630000** (report received Oct 6, 2026), VW recall **93EX**: 22,524 ID.4 electric SUVs, MY 2023-2026. Defect: in certain charging profiles combined with >80% state of charge, a high-voltage battery fault "may trigger thermal propagation within the high-voltage battery." Root cause not identified; VW estimates ~0.1% of vehicles actually affected but cannot identify which. 22 US claims Jan 18, 2024 - Aug 7, 2026. Remedy: under development; interim instruction is charge to max 80%. Owner letters expected Nov 27, 2026. (NHTSA record; electrek, Oct 8, 2026; prior site research #1105/#1194.)

2. **Audi Q4 e-tron:** NHTSA campaign **26V698000** (report received Oct 21, 2026), Audi recall **93SK**: 9,048 vehicles (7,040 Q4 e-tron + 2,008 Q4 Sportback e-tron), MY 2024-2025. Same defect class: high-voltage battery may fail and cause a fire; interim instruction charge to max 80%. Audi is not aware of any Q4 e-tron fires from this issue. Owner letters expected Dec 18, 2026. (NHTSA record; prior site research #1268; motorbiscuit, Oct 31, 2026.)

3. **The enforcement split (new this week):** "Volkswagen has already pushed an over-the-air update that caps affected ID.4s at 80%. Owners had about a week to opt out. VW is also mailing stickers to remind drivers not to charge past 80%." — "Audi hasn't forced a limit on the Q4 E-Tron, so it's up to owners to set the charge limit themselves in the car or the app." (motorbiscuit.com, Oct 31, 2026, citing Jalopnik's reporting of the NHTSA filings.)

4. **Shared unknowns:** Both companies say they still don't know the root cause; both estimate ~0.1% of the recall population actually has the defect but cannot identify which vehicles; neither company has told owners to park outside or stop driving. No permanent fix exists yet. (motorbiscuit, Oct 31, 2026.)

5. **Range math:** A 20% cap on the usable pack means up to 20% less range until a fix arrives — the same "fix" that costs the owner range every day, imposed remotely for VW owners, voluntary for Audi owners.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- ID.4 owners: check whether your car received the OTA cap (charge screen shows 80% max); if you opted out, set the limit yourself — the fire risk is unchanged either way.
- Q4 e-tron owners: set the 80% charge limit in the vehicle or the myAudi app NOW; do not wait for the December 18 letter.
- All: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V630 / 26V698. If you charge past 80% regularly and your car is in the population, stop.
