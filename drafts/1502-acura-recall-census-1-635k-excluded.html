Acura Filed One Recall for Its 2026 Cars. The Big One Covers 635,808 Older Ones.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Acura filed one recall campaign for its 2026 cars this year. One. Before you sign that lease, you might want to see what the number leaves out, because 470,215 Acura vehicles were recalled in 2026 under campaigns the count excludes, including 252,698 that can lose power steering while driving straight. Count says one; fine print says nearly half a million.[1]

1.0%
Share of the 475,021 Acuras recalled in 2026 that the census method counts

Start with the method, because the method is the whole story. I ran the corrected-vocabulary census this site built for Toyota, Honda, and Lexus across all five of Acura's canonical 2026 models, 29 queries against the NHTSA recalls API, keeping only campaigns filed in calendar 2026, and one campaign came back. It touches one model, the MDX, and 28 of the 29 queries returned nothing at all.[2]

That one campaign is 26V332, filed May 22, and it is a seat sensor with a capacitor problem. A capacitor in the printed circuit board of the front passenger seat weight sensor can crack from humidity and short circuit, which means the passenger frontal and knee airbags can deploy in a crash despite an infant in a child seat, a child, or a smaller adult sitting there, exactly the occupants the system is supposed to protect by staying silent. Honda's own filing estimates 1 percent of the 98,892 vehicles carry the defect, about 989 cars, with 228 warranty claims and zero injuries or deaths as of May 14.[3]

Read the cause twice, because it reads like a supply chain parable. A natural disaster hit a tier-2 supplier's plant. The tier-1 supplier, Aisin Electronics Illinois, temporarily changed the base material in the sensor's circuit board and never verified the substitute properly. Then the recall itself had to be expanded three times over, because the supplier miscalculated the end date of the bad-part production run, used inaccurate data to match bad parts to vehicles, and never verified which cars got defective service parts. Original recall 24V064 went out in January 2024. This expansion took until May 2026, and only after a car repaired outside the recall population surfaced in March 2025. Production went back to the verified material in January 2022, which means the bad boards were already out of the factory before the first recall was even filed.[3]

Now the detail that stops you cold. Acura's share of 26V332 is 4,806 vehicles: 2,399 MDXs, 1,083 RDXs, and 1,324 TLXs. And the 2023 TLX population is one car. One. A single 2023 TLX, built February 22, 2023, gets its own line in a federal filing. Somewhere there is a person driving the only 2023 TLX in this recall, a car that exists in the database as a population of one, and the dealer has a sensor with their name on it.[3]

That is the counted record. Now the excluded record, which is where the money is. Honda filed two more Acura recalls in 2026, and the census method drops both because neither touches a 2026 model year. First, 26V721, filed October 29: the electric power steering software can divide by zero while the car is driving straight, and the error handler responds by switching the power steering off entirely. It covers 635,808 vehicles, 252,698 of them Acuras, 177,100 MDXs and 75,598 TLXs, with a 100 percent defect estimate, 146 warranty claims, and one injury. Honda got the first report in November 2021 and decided it was a safety defect in October 2026, 1,788 days later, after two years spent examining returned parts whose error codes had already been deleted. This site covered it in full as #1457, and the steering story there is worth your time.[4][5]

Second, 26V365, filed June 4: rear subframe corrosion in the salt belt. A paint defect lets road salt eat the rear subframe where the suspension mounts, and if it goes far enough the rear suspension fails and the driver loses control. It covers 880,514 vehicles across four model lines, 217,517 of them 2014-2020 MDXs, sold or registered in 23 states plus Washington, DC. Honda estimates 1 percent actually carry the defect, about 8,800 cars, with zero warranty claims and zero injuries as of May 28. Honda has known about the problem since December 2021. Owner letters went out in July.[6][7]

Add all three 2026 filings together and the picture flips. Campaign units total 1,615,214, Acura vehicles total 475,021, and the census counts 4,806 of them, 1.0 percent. For every Acura the count includes, about 98 more were recalled in 2026 under campaigns the method excludes, and that ratio is the article. A shopper who reads "Acura: 1 recall" and walks onto a used lot is walking into the wrong conversation, because the used lot is full of 2022-2024 MDXs and 2021-2025 TLXs, and those are the cars with the steering recall, not the seat sensor.[1]

Now the parent-company math, because it makes the point sharper. Honda filed 4 campaigns for its 2026 cars, and 26V332 is one of the four, counted through the Odyssey. Acura adds zero distinct defects to Honda's total: Honda Motor Co has 4 distinct 2026 defects across both brands. Lexus added two to Toyota's 15, and Acura added none to Honda's 4. "Acura: 1" is not a separate record. It is Honda's record with an Acura badge on one line of one filing.[8]

Four of Acura's five nameplates carry zero 2026 filings touching a 2026 vehicle: the brand-new ADX, the Integra, the RDX, and the ZDX. That last one deserves a pause. ZDX is the electric Acura built on General Motors' Ultium platform, and it has zero campaigns while GM itself filed 15. TLX is absent from the list entirely, because Acura killed it after model year 2025, so there is no 2026 TLX to recall. A five-model lineup with one filing sounds clean until you notice the denominator is five and the flagship sedan is already dead.[2]

Now the other side, stated fairly, because the 1-count flatters Acura twice over and a reader deserves to hear it from me before the comments do. First, the method excludes the two biggest Acura filings of the year by construction, so anyone who takes "Acura: 1" as the brand's 2026 safety record is wrong by 470,215 vehicles. Second, the one counted campaign is not an Acura defect at all. It is a Honda defect, a seat sensor shared across 23 Honda and Acura model entries, that happens to include 4,806 Acuras, and only about 1 percent of those, roughly 48 cars, are estimated to actually carry the cracked capacitor. Strip the shared filing and Acura's own 2026 record is zero campaigns and zero defects, which is a true statement about new Acura metal and a misleading statement about Acura owners, since 252,698 of them are driving MDXs and TLXs that can lose power steering on a straight road. Honest headline, then: Acura's new cars had a clean year. Acura's recent cars did not.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 1,615,214 campaign-unit total counts every vehicle in each filing across all model years, not 2026-only cars. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates, not 2026-model-year counts; the 2026-only Acura total cannot be computed from the filings, because 26V332's MDX 2022-2026 slice spans five model years. Unlike Lexus, Acura has no 2025-filed campaigns touching a 2026 model: the unfiltered MDX query returned only 26V332, and the other four nameplates returned no rows at all. And the NHTSA API's vocabulary gaps remain the central risk in this method: a campaign hiding under an untested model spelling would be missed, and the direction of any remaining bias is undercount. One series-standard check, for the record: none of the counted campaigns is flagged as an over-the-air update, and the excluded steering recall's remedy is a dealer software reprogram, not a download, so the flag and the filings agree here as they did for Lexus. Late filings will move every number in this article.

If you own a 2022-2026 MDX, the seat sensor recall is a free dealer sensor replacement, and the warning sign is the SRS light on with the passenger airbag indicator staying off. If you own a 2022-2024 MDX or a 2021-2025 TLX, the steering recall is the one that matters, and it is not in the census count: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, do not wait for the December 14 letter, and watch for steering that suddenly goes heavy while driving straight, with EPS warning lights on the dash. If you own a 2014-2020 MDX and live in the salt belt, the subframe recall is a free inspection plus a reinforcement kit, and if the subframe is corroded the dealer repairs or replaces it free. If you are shopping a 2026 Acura, the census is accurate for new metal: one recall across five nameplates, and the ADX, Integra, RDX, and ZDX carry zero. But shop used Acuras by campaign, not by badge. Check any used Acura VIN against 26V721 and 26V365 before signing, not just 26V332, because the recalls that matter most to an Acura owner are the two the count leaves out.
