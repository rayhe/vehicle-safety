# Research — #1569: The White House Took Credit for the Safest Half-Year on Record. The Truck Numbers Say Otherwise.

## Angle
On November 16, 2026, the White House announced traffic deaths had fallen "to the second-lowest level in American history following President Donald J. Trump's crackdown on illegal alien truck drivers." Three claims are packed into one sentence, and all three wilt when checked against the federal government's own numbers. The "second-lowest level" is a rate, not a death count. The death count is third-lowest in the table. And the share of deaths involving large trucks was exactly the same before the crackdown as after it: 13.6% in 2024, 13.6% in 2025. The decline the White House is claiming started in the second quarter of 2022, three years before the April 2025 executive order it credits.

## Key verified facts
- **The claim:** White House, Nov 16, 2026: traffic deaths "fallen to the second-lowest level in American history following" the truck-driver crackdown.
- **Rate vs. count:** NHTSA's "second lowest" refers to the RATE (1.02 vs 1.01 in H1 2014). On raw deaths, H1 2026 (16,750) is third-lowest in NHTSA's 13-year table, behind H1 2014 (15,035) and H1 2015 (16,193). "Second-lowest level in American history" is wrong on the count and mislabeled on the rate, since NHTSA's comparison runs to 2014, not to "American history."
- **Truck share, flat:** 2024: 5,340 large-truck-crash deaths / 39,254 total deaths = 13.60%. 2025: 4,982 / 36,640 = 13.60%. Identical to two decimal places. Truck deaths fell 7% in 2025 while total deaths fell 6.7%: trucks fell at the same rate as everything else, which is what you would expect if the crackdown had no truck-specific effect at all.
- **Timing:** The national decline began in Q2 2022: 17 straight quarters of year-over-year declines (NHTSA Figure 1). The executive order on English proficiency is dated April 28, 2025. Large-truck deaths fell 8% in 2023 (5,969 to 5,478) and 2.5% in 2024 (5,478 to 5,340), both before the order. A cause cannot start three years after its effect.
- **The stall inside the good news:** Q1 2026 deaths fell 5.0%; Q2 fell 0.2%. Deaths ROSE in May and June 2026 vs 2025. The "sharp drop" the White House describes happened in the first quarter, before most of the enforcement actions it cites had accumulated (the out-of-service count was 26,000 in August, 34,000 by November).
- **Enforcement numbers check out:** DOT's own Nov 12 release confirms 34,000 drivers out of service and 31,000+ licenses revoked. The dispute is not the enforcement numbers. It is the causal arrow drawn from them to the death count.
- **NHTSA attributes nothing:** NHTSA's H1 2026 release does not attribute any part of the decline to the driver enforcement, immigration policy, or commercial vehicle inspections. NHTSA's April 2026 full-year release did list enforcement among several factors for the 2025 decrease (see counterargument).

## Original calculations (for the article)
- **Share arithmetic:** 5,340 / 39,254 = 0.13603 = 13.6%. 4,982 / 36,640 = 0.13597 = 13.6%. The truck share of American road deaths did not move a tenth of a point in the year the crackdown began.
- **Count ranking:** H1 2014: 15,035. H1 2015: 16,193. H1 2026: 16,750. Third-lowest, not second.
- **Counterfactual (label as rough):** if the truck share had fallen even one percentage point in 2025 (to 12.6%), that would mean about 366 fewer truck-crash deaths than actually occurred. The observed change is zero.
- **Pre-dating:** Q2 2022 to April 2025 is 11 quarters, roughly 2 years 10 months, of decline before the executive order.

## What this does NOT prove (limitations)
- The H1 2026 report does not break out large-truck deaths, so the share comparison uses full-year 2024 vs full-year 2025, not H1 2026. A truck-specific effect in the first half of 2026 would not show up in this arithmetic yet.
- All 2025 and 2026 figures are preliminary statistical projections, subject to revision when the FARS Annual Reporting File lands.
- FMCSA's separate 2025 count (4,330 truck-crash fatalities, down 13.7%) differs from NHTSA's 4,982. Different methodology (carrier-reported vs FARS census). Do not mix the two series; the share arithmetic uses the NHTSA series only.
- A flat aggregate share does not prove zero effect: a small truck-specific improvement could hide inside estimation noise, and the crackdown's full effect may lag. The claim being checked is the White House's, and the evidence does not support it. That is not the same as proving the opposite.
- The April 2026 NHTSA release did credit enforcement among other factors for the 2025 decline. Include it honestly (see counterargument).

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
The administration's defenders have a real point: 34,000 unqualified drivers removed from the road is a large intervention, and NHTSA's own April 2026 release listed "cracking down on unqualified truck drivers" among the reasons traffic deaths fell in 2025. Removing drivers who cannot read road signs should, on first principles, make roads safer, and the full effect of removals that accumulated through late 2025 may not be visible in full-year 2025 numbers at all. The 13.6% share is an aggregate; it cannot rule out that truck crashes fell in specific corridors or that worse outcomes were prevented. And the White House's underlying rate claim is real: 1.02 is genuinely the second-lowest first-half rate NHTSA has recorded. The counter to the counter: a claim that X caused Y needs Y to move differently where X acted. Truck deaths moved exactly like everything else, the decline predates the policy by three years, and the quarter the White House is celebrating (Q1 2026, -5.0%) came before the enforcement numbers it cites had mostly accumulated. Credit requires evidence of a difference. There is none in the federal record.

## Actionable takeaways
- Read the federal number before the press release about it: NHTSA's Crash Stats reports are public at crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov, and the first page states exactly what "second lowest" refers to.
- When a politician claims credit for a trend, check the start date. A decline that began in Q2 2022 cannot have been caused by an April 2025 order.
- Rate and count are different numbers. "Second-lowest rate" and "second-lowest deaths" are not interchangeable, and the gap here is about 1,700 lives.
- The nine-month 2026 projections arrive in late December 2026. That release will include the first sub-category data covering the crackdown period in full. That is the number that can actually test the claim.
