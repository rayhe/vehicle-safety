Chrysler Recalls 74,578 Durangos for the Third Time for the Same Spoiler That Falls Off

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
August 28, 2026

Chrysler has recalled 74,578 Dodge Durango SUVs from 2021 to 2023 because their rear spoilers can detach while driving and become highway debris. This is the third recall for the same spoiler alignment defect after campaigns in 2021 and 2023 failed to fix it.[1] As of July 29, Chrysler logged 116 warranty claims, 8 customer assistance records, and 37 service reports of spoilers failing after the previous remedy. No crashes have been reported yet, and the permanent fix remains under development according to Chrysler filings.

Let's talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds when a chunk of plastic hits your windshield at 70 mph. NHTSA's language is clinical: spoilers "may create road debris and increase risk of vehicle crash."[1] Translation: a 4-pound piece of painted ABS tumbling off a Durango at highway speed becomes an unguided projectile for whoever is three seconds behind.

Durango itself looks safe on paper because FARS 2014-2023 shows 356 deaths, rate 0.54 per 100M VMT, well below fleet average near 1.2.[2] Impairment 22.5% sits near average. IIHS rates larger SUVs as lower own-risk but higher aggressivity to others.[3] Spoiler detachment flips that logic. FARS attributes deaths to the vehicle occupants, not to vehicles that shed parts. If a spoiler causes a following motorcyclist to crash, FARS codes it to the motorcycle, not the Durango. Durango's 0.54 stays clean in federal tables while external risk to other road users goes completely uncounted in its own safety rating.

Mia's engineering gripe: this is a manufacturing alignment error, not exotic materials science. Spoiler improperly aligned during installation strikes the roof every time the liftgate cycles. Repeated impact fatigues the mounting points until the plastic deforms and the fasteners loosen. Over months the mount weakens from repeated roof strikes, then the spoiler lets go at highway speed when aerodynamic load exceeds the fatigued attachment. Chrysler tried twice to fix the alignment procedure in prior recall remedies and failed to prevent post-repair failures. Owners must return a third time, yet Chrysler tells NHTSA final permanent technical repair is still under development as of August. Dealers notified August 20, VINs searchable same day, but owner letters do not mail until September 10.[1] That creates a 21-day awareness gap where searchable VINs exist on NHTSA.gov but most owners have no idea they need to return for a third repair.

Original math: 0.8% of 74,578 equals 597 vehicles expected defective. Chrysler already has 161 failure reports, which is 27% of expected defective population observed before letters even mail. Underreporting is massive for trim pieces - most owners never file warranty for a rattling spoiler. Real failure share likely runs higher because most owners never file warranty claims for rattling trim. And this pattern is not isolated across the industry, since Stellantis has recalled the same backup camera bug seven times and BMW's driveshaft has seven recalls with an adhesive fix, and now Durango's spoiler makes three separate recalls for the same trim piece. Recurring recall counts are climbing while NHTSA recall completion rates sit near 70%. Industry tolerance for ineffective remedies is becoming the defect itself as completion rates stagnate and repeat recalls accumulate.

What to do

If you own a 2021-2023 Durango built July 13, 2020 to March 28, 2023: check your VIN today at nhtsa.gov/recalls, do not wait for September 10 letter. Inspect spoiler gap alignment - uneven gap on one side means contact. Limit power liftgate cycles, avoid automatic car washes that flex spoiler mounts, and if it wobbles, pull the liftgate fuse or tape it. If you are shopping, Durango's own crash rate 0.54 is excellent, but ask for proof all three spoiler recalls completed with final fix when available. For everyone else, following distance is the only rating that matters for road debris. Three seconds behind SUVs with roof accessories gives you time to react when trim becomes a projectile. No airbag in your car protects against someone else's flying trim landing in your lane.

Limitations

FARS only captures fatal crashes, not non-fatal debris strikes. Our 0.54 rate uses VMT estimates from sales and NHTS annual miles, not actual odometers, introducing about ±8% uncertainty for Durango's volume. FARS 2014-2023 includes older Durango generations - 2021-2023 model years not separately broken out in our file, so rate may not reflect this specific spoiler design. No injury or crash data exists yet for this defect, so risk remains theoretical per NHTSA wording and should be treated as potential rather than proven. IIHS ratings for 2021-2023 Durango not individually cited here; we rely on general SUV size and weight principle rather than specific test. Warranty claims 116 may include duplicates across recall campaigns.

Strongest counterargument

Best case against worrying: 0.8% defect rate is tiny, zero crashes or injuries reported, spoiler is light plastic that likely tumbles to shoulder, Durango's low 0.54 FARS rate proves it is fundamentally safe, third recall shows system working because NHTSA caught persistent failures, and road debris crashes are statistically rare compared to 12,429 drunk-driving deaths and 11,775 speeding deaths in 2023.[4] Focusing on trim recalls distracts from bigger killers like alcohol and speed, and the 21-day letter delay reflects normal parts logistics rather than negligence.

That argument holds if you are inside the Durango enjoying its low 0.54 own-risk rate, but it fails completely if you are behind it on a motorcycle with no windshield protection from tumbling plastic. Rare but high-consequence events deserve attention when the defect is preventable through proper alignment and the remedy has already failed twice in prior recall campaigns. Pattern of ineffective fixes across multiple automakers deserves more scrutiny than any isolated part failure because it signals tolerance for remedies that do not address root cause.
