# Research: #1192 — Side Underride Death Count

**Slug:** 1192-side-underride-death-count
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread / Investigation)
**Angle:** NHTSA's own cost-benefit case against side underride guards ($1.2B to save 17.2 lives/yr) is computed on a death count that GAO, IIHS, and Congress all agree is wrong. The Stop Underrides Act 2.0 (S. 3775 / H.R. 7354, Feb 2026) orders NHTSA to recount the bodies in FARS. Zero of 1191 prior Crash Report articles covered underride.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: 2026 federal bill, NHTSA missed three self-imposed deadlines, 300+/yr deaths claimed.
- Novel? Yes: zero prior underride coverage; the "cost-benefit built on a broken denominator" paradox is original; the 1969 promise detail is original to this treatment.
- 3+ primary sources? NHTSA ANPRM (Fed. Reg. 2023-08451, docket NHTSA-2023-0012), GAO-19-264, FARS, IIHS (via GAO), bill text S. 3775/H.R. 7354 (119th Congress), DOT Unified Regulatory Agenda. VERDICT: PROCEED.

## Verified facts
1. **Stop Underrides Act 2.0**, introduced early Feb 2026. Senate S. 3775 (Gillibrand, Luján; cosponsors incl. Blumenthal, Duckworth, Warren); House H.R. 7354 (Cohen, DeSaulnier, Ross). Referred to Senate Commerce Committee 2026-02-04. Provisions: (a) DOT Secretary must finalize side-underride-guard rule for NEW trailers/semitrailers/single-unit trucks within 18 months of enactment; guards must stop a passenger vehicle at impact speeds up to 40 mph; (b) restart DOT Advisory Committee on Underride Protection; (c) National Academies study on underride prevalence incl. front-of-truck; (d) GAO study of 2022 rear-guard rule implementation; (e) NHTSA must review FARS and correct misclassified underride crashes, incl. pedestrians/cyclists/motorcyclists; (f) free law-enforcement training on identifying underride crashes; (g) public DOT underride research website. Bill findings: 31,500 lives since NHTSA's formation with "no substantial progress"; sponsors cite "at least 300" underride deaths/year. Bill also asks that guards integrate aerodynamic design to offset fuel costs.
   Sources: FreightWaves 2026-02-05; Land Line 2026-02-06; CCJ Digital 2026-02-06; truckpartsandservice.com; LegiScan (SB3775, 119th Congress); intermodal.org 119th Congress bill matrix (Sept 2026).
2. **NHTSA April 2023 ANPRM cost-benefit** (the number industry cites): 89 light-vehicle occupant deaths + 409 serious injuries/yr in two-vehicle side-underride crashes. At 97% effectiveness for impacts at/below 40 mph, NHTSA projected 17.2 lives + 69 serious injuries saved/yr at full fleet equipage. Cost: $970M–$1.2B/yr (hardware, install, lifetime fuel). ~$2,990/trailer (2020 dollars); +450–800 lbs per trailer; $200–450M in lifetime fuel costs for each year's new trailers; ~260,000 new trailers/yr; ~2,000 public comments. Cost per equivalent life saved: $73.5M–$103.7M vs NHTSA value of statistical life $11.9M (6–9x). Safety benefits valued up to $166M/yr.
   Sources: FreightWaves analysis of ANPRM; Overdrive; Land Line; coalition comments PDF (ports.org).
3. **The undercount**: GAO-19-264 (2019): FARS 2008–2017 recorded 189–253 underride deaths/yr (avg ~219) — under 1% of all traffic deaths, 5.5% of large-truck deaths. GAO: likely undercounted — no uniform national collection; many police crash forms lack an underride field; officers document inconsistently; FARS analysts then lack info to categorize. IIHS told GAO it uses general rear-impact crash data as a proxy because FARS undercounts underride. UMTRI researchers agreed FARS misses cases. Eric Hein study (2023, filed as ANPRM comment): FARS coding manual says "two vehicles cannot be considered to underride and override simultaneously," so when the car report codes underride, the truck report MUST be coded "No Underride or Override Noted" — systematic undercount by rule; side underride "much more common than recorded in FARS" (16 case listings vs news reports). NHTSA's own law-enforcement pamphlet cites GAO's underreporting finding.
   Sources: GAO-19-264 PDF; safetyresearch.net; Hein PDF (annaleahmary.com); nhtsa.gov underride pamphlet.
4. **Three missed deadlines**: NHTSA's Unified Agenda listed side underride guards (RIN 2127-AM54) "Analyzing Comments" at 12/00/2024 (Fall 2024 agenda), then 01/00/2026 (Spring 2025), then 07/00/2026 (current). Still Prerule Stage, still tied to April 2023 ANPRM, nothing listed after "Analyzing Comments."
   Source: Hein Aug 21, 2026 comment letter on DOT Regulatory Agenda (annaleahmary.com PDF).
5. **1969**: US DOT stated it intended, "after technical studies have been completed," to extend underride protection to the sides of large vehicles. 57 years ago; still pending.
   Source: annaleahmary.com STOP Underrides Act page.
6. **Tech exists**: IIHS crash-tested an aftermarket side-guard design; ~100 sets sold at ~$2,500/trailer (per GAO report). Truck Trailer Manufacturers Association: supports side guards "if they ever become both justified and technologically feasible." Congressionally mandated Advisory Committee (ACUP) June 2024 400-page report: majority position that side guards are "both necessary and cost effective."
   Sources: safetyresearch.net (GAO summary); trailer-bodybuilders.com.
7. **Industry counter**: OOIDA: "one of the costliest federal trucking mandates in history"; operational concerns incl. grounding on low-clearance railroad crossings (1,160 per FRA), weight cutting freight capacity, maintenance burden on small truckers. FreightWaves: "a billion-dollar fix for a distraction problem" — many side underrides begin with a car drifting into the trailer's side.
   Sources: Overdrive; Land Line; FreightWaves.
8. **Rear guards (context)**: 49 CFR 393.86 already requires rear impact guards on most interstate trailers; NHTSA's 2022 final rule upgraded FMVSS 223/224. Side and front have no federal standard.

## Novel calculation (show math in article)
- NHTSA: 17.2 lives saved = 89 deaths × (share at ≤40 mph) × 0.97 → share at ≤40 mph ≈ 20%. DERIVED: ~4 of 5 side-underride deaths happen at speeds no guard is designed to survive.
- Sensitivity: if true deaths are 2× NHTSA's count (178), lives saved ≈ 34, cost/life ≈ $37M–$52M (still 3–4× VSL). At 3× (267): ≈52 lives, ≈$25M–$35M per life (still 2–3× VSL). CONCLUSION: even generous undercount corrections don't clear NHTSA's $11.9M VSL bar. The honest case for the bill is moral (31,500 dead), not economic.
- Assumptions stated: multiplier is illustrative, not measured; 97% is a test-condition figure; full fleet equipage takes decades at 260k new trailers/yr.

## Limitations (dedicated paragraph in article)
- No current verified national underride count exists — cuts both ways; advocates can't prove the higher number either.
- Hein sample: 16 case listings vs news articles — small, non-random.
- 31,500 is the bill's finding, not an independently verified count.
- Rear-guard 2022 rule's real-world effect still being evaluated (GAO study pending).

## Actionable insights
- Drivers: the side gap is unguarded by federal law — don't cruise alongside trailers; pass decisively or drop back. Side underrides happen in lane changes, turns, and drifts: exactly when you're beside the trailer.
- Fleets: aftermarket side guards exist (~$2,500/trailer); at least one design is IIHS crash-tested.
- Everyone: the FARS-recount provision is the sleeper — all future cost-benefit math runs on that data. Docket NHTSA-2023-0012 on regulations.gov.
