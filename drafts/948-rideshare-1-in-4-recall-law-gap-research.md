# Research: Your Uber Has a 1-in-4 Chance of an Unrepaired Safety Defect. Federal Law Only Protects Rental Cars.

## Story Angle
Carfax checked 438,000 VINs from registered taxi and rideshare vehicles across 16 major U.S. cities (Sept 14, 2026) and found **more than 1 in 4 carried at least one unfixed safety recall** — worse than the ~1 in 5 rate for private vehicles nationally. NYC medallion cabs: 38.6%. Seattle: 37.5%. LA: 11%.

The twist nobody drew: rental car companies are **federally required** to ground recalled vehicles within 24-48 hours — the Raechel and Jacqueline Houck Safe Rental Car Act of 2015, passed after two sisters (20 and 24) died in a recalled Enterprise PT Cruiser on Highway 101 in Oct 2004 ($15M verdict, June 2010, Enterprise admitted negligence). **No equivalent law covers taxis or rideshares.** The GAO (GAO-23-105996, May 2023) states flatly: "Federal law does not require that vehicle owners — with the exception of rental car companies — remedy open safety recalls." Only one state, Maryland, requires ridesourcing vehicles be certified annually as recall-free. Uber/Lyft deactivate only "Do Not Drive" recalls (~0.6% of affected rideshare vehicles, ~2,000 nationwide per GAO) — everything short of that rides on the driver's goodwill, and passengers can't check the VIN.

So: the vehicle you're contractually handed the keys to at a Hertz counter is federally protected. The vehicle a stranger drives you home in at 1 AM is not — and it's 35% more likely to carry an open recall.

## Key Facts & Sources

### 1. Carfax investigation (Sept 14, 2026) — PRIMARY
- 438,000 VINs from registered taxi/rideshare vehicles, 16 major U.S. cities + Maryland state data, via public records requests/open-data portals; checked with Vehicle Research Search Service (CARFAX + Alliance for Automotive Innovation JV)
- **27% had at least one open (unfixed) recall** vs ~1 in 5 (20%) vehicles nationwide
- NYC medallion cabs: **38.6%**; Seattle: 37.5%; LA: 11%; Boston: 9%
- Randall Orr (VP Data Products, CARFAX): "If you're paying for a ride, you should be able to count on the vehicle being safe, too, but our data shows that isn't always the case."
- Counter-quote in the release — Elena Sierra (Transportation Alliance): "not all recalls present the same level of safety risk. They range from software updates and labeling corrections to more significant mechanical issues."
- URL: https://www.carfax.com/press/carfax-investigation-more-than-1-in-4-taxis-and-rideshares-in-major-u-s-cities-have-unfixed-recall

### 2. GAO-23-105996 (May 2023) — PRIMARY
- Rideshare vehicles (Uber/Lyft trip data, Aug–Dec 2022): **16% had an open safety recall nationally**; Texas as high as 20%
- ~0.6% of affected (~2,000 vehicles) carried "Do Not Drive" recalls (airbag ruptures, seatbelt detachments, engine fires)
- "Federal law does not require that vehicle owners — with the exception of rental car companies — remedy open safety recalls"
- Passengers "can't easily discern if the vehicle transporting them is subject to an open safety recall since they don't have access to their drivers' unique VIN number"
- Of six selected states, **only Maryland requires ridesourcing vehicles be certified annually as not having open safety recalls**
- Uber/Lyft: suspend vehicles with Do Not Drive recalls, share recall notices, ToS says drivers must monitor/repair
- URL: https://www.GAO.GOV/assets/gao-23-105996.pdf

### 3. Consumer Reports (Aug 2019) — corroboration
- 1 in 6 Uber/Lyft cars in NYC + Seattle had open recalls (94,000 VINs); ~1 in 4 NYC traditional for-hire vehicles (taxis/limos/liveries) too
- NYC TLC requires 3 inspections/year — **none check for open recalls** ("We've been checking for open recalls, but administratively and not as a part of the vehicle inspection process" — TLC deputy commissioner)
- Taxi fleet operator quote: "We don't necessarily bring the car into the dealer to get it fixed... If the vehicle gets to a dealer, it can sit there for days until it gets back on the road, and that's what we don't want." (downtime = lost income is the mechanism)
- "Uber and Lyft would be wise to adopt the same practices the rental car companies have adopted nationally... rather than waiting for a high-profile tragedy to occur before they screen out vehicles with unrepaired safety recalls"
- URL: https://www.consumerreports.org/ride-hailing/uber-and-lyft-cars-have-open-safety-recalls/?EXTKEY=AFLIP

### 4. The Houck law — PRIMARY (statute)
- S.1173, Raechel and Jacqueline Houck Safe Rental Car Act of 2015, folded into the FAST Act (signed Dec 2015); effective June 1, 2016
- Requires rental companies (fleets >35 vehicles) to ground vehicles under safety recall within 24 hours of notice (48h for recalls covering 5,000+ fleet vehicles); extends NHTSA enforcement authority to rental companies
- Origin: Oct 2, 2004 — Raechel (24) and Jacqueline (20) Houck rented a 2004 Chrysler PT Cruiser from Enterprise (Capitola, CA); the model had been recalled a month earlier for a power steering hose that could leak, ignite, and cause loss of steering; the hose failed on Highway 101 near King City, the car crossed the median into an 18-wheeler and burned; both died
- Alameda County jury awarded parents $15M on June 9, 2010; Enterprise admitted negligence as sole proximate cause; internal testimony: on busy days recalled cars were rented as standard practice ("If all you have are recalled vehicles on the lot, you rent them out. It was a given.")
- URLs: https://www.congress.gov/bill/114th-congress/senate-bill/1173 ; https://www.schumer.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/schumer-boxer-mccaskill-re-introduce-safe-rental-car-act-to-keep-rental-cars-under-recall-off-the-roads-and-ban-rental-car-companies-from-renting-or-selling-recalled-vehicles ; https://www.autorentalnews.com/71346/enterprise-to-pay-15-million-in-wrongful-death-lawsuit

### 5. NHTSA recall lookup — PRIMARY
- https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls — free VIN check; all recall repairs free at dealers

## Original Calculations (for article)
- 27% of 438,000 = **~118,000 taxi/rideshare vehicles** carrying at least one open recall in the sampled cities
- NYC medallion 38.6% vs national private 20% = **1.9x** the private-vehicle rate; vs LA 11% = **3.5x city-to-city gap**
- 27% (Carfax 2026, taxi+rideshare) vs 16% (GAO 2022, rideshare only): methodologies differ (taxi inclusion + older NYC medallion fleet in Carfax sample), so present as "not directly comparable, but the direction is not improvement" — do NOT claim the rate rose 11 points
- 0.6% × 16% ≈ ~1 in 1,000 rideshare trips in a Do Not Drive vehicle nationally (GAO) — small but nonzero, and those are airbag-rupture/seatbelt-detachment/engine-fire class defects

## Strongest Counterarguments (must include)
1. **Severity inflation (Sierra/Transportation Alliance):** "open recall" includes label misprints and software updates. 27% is a ceiling on risk, not a measure of it. GAO's own data: only ~0.6% of affected rideshare vehicles carried Do Not Drive recalls. The scary headline number mostly reflects low-severity campaigns.
2. **Fleet age, not for-hire negligence:** taxis run high mileage and drivers buy used; older vehicles accumulate more recall campaigns over their lifetime. The NYC 38.6% may partly measure medallion fleet age, not indifference.
3. **Platforms do act at the extreme:** Uber/Lyft told GAO they deactivate Do Not Drive vehicles and push recall notices; drivers are independent contractors responsible for their cars — the same standard as any private owner.
4. **Source incentive:** Carfax sells recall-check products and vehicle history reports; a frightening press release is also marketing. Methodology (public-records VINs, 16 self-selected cities) is not a national random sample.

## Limitations (must include)
- Carfax 27% counts ANY open recall; no severity breakdown published — cannot say how many are dangerous
- Sample = registered fleets in 16 cities + MD that publish VINs via public records; not nationally representative; NYC/Seattle overweighted in coverage
- GAO 16% figure is 2022 trip data — 4 years old; Carfax 27% is 2026 but includes taxis (GAO excluded taxis), so the two are not directly comparable
- No data on crashes or injuries caused specifically by open recalls in for-hire vehicles — the article argues risk exposure, not body count
- Driver-side mechanism (downtime cost) is supported by one fleet operator quote (2019) and economic logic, not a driver survey

## Actionable Insights (required gate)
- Before your next airport run: a rental car is federally required to be recall-free (grounded within 24–48h of notice). Your Uber is not. If the choice is between them and safety is the tiebreaker, the law has already picked a winner.
- Passengers cannot check a driver's VIN — but drivers can: free at nhtsa.gov/recalls and carfax.com/recall; every recall repair is free at the dealer
- Maryland is the only state requiring annual recall-free certification for ridesourcing vehicles — the policy template exists; every other state chose not to copy it
- Buying the used car you'll drive for Uber/Lyft? Run the VIN first — older/used cars carry accumulated open campaigns, and the seller has no legal duty to disclose them
