# Research: #899 — AAA "Speeding Is the New Normal" (Aug 2026)

**Slug:** 899-aaa-speeding-new-normal-nimby
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread) — sequel to his #784 EU ISA piece
**News peg:** AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety study released August 18, 2026: "New Research Finds Most Drivers Think Speeding Is Normal, Putting Everyone At Risk."

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: yes. First large attitudinal dataset of 2026 on speeding culture, with a clean paradox: 90%+ of drivers believe speed humps work, fewer than half want one on their street; 82% want mandatory speed limiters for serious speeders but only 51% want even advisory ISA in their own car. Nobody has covered this study on the site (only referenced in draft #832's flow-of-traffic piece). Novel angle vs. prior site coverage: #784 (EU ISA mandate) was about the hardware; this is about the American attitude that will keep the hardware out.

## Primary sources
1. AAA Newsroom (primary), Aug 18, 2026: https://newsroom.aaa.com/2026/08/new-research-finds-most-drivers-think-speeding-is-normal-putting-everyone-at-risk/
   - 3 in 10 drivers agree "No one actually drives the speed limit."
   - Many consider driving 10 mph over acceptable; nearly half believe keeping up with traffic is safer than obeying the limit.
   - Reasons to speed: around slower drivers 31%, reach destination faster 25%, avoid holding up traffic 21%.
   - Reasons to slow: avoid ticket 79%, avoid crash 72%, bad weather 60%.
   - Countermeasure support: police presence 87% (crash-history areas) / 88% (potential), ~70% automated speed cameras.
   - Advisory ISA favored over auto-limiting; support falls as restrictiveness rises (no-override = no thanks).
   - Speed humps: >90% say effective, <50% support installing in their own neighborhood.

## Core paradox (the spine of the piece)
- "Punish them, not me": 82% want mandatory limiters for *serious speeders*; only 51% want even a *warning beep* in their own car. The offender is always someone else.
- The ticket asymmetry: the #1 reason drivers slow down (79%) is avoiding a ticket, not avoiding a crash (72%). Enforcement works; conscience is optional.
- NIMBY safety: >90% effective belief vs <50% neighborhood support for humps.
- "No one drives the speed limit" (30% agree) + "keeping up with traffic is safer" (~half): the posted limit is treated as the minimum, enforcement as the tax.

## Limitations (state at full strength)
- Self-reported survey data; social desirability bias cuts both ways (underreported speeding, overreported countermeasure support).
- Regional vs national numbers mixed; cite each with its frame.
- Correlation of attitudes to actual crash causation is inferential; FARS speeding coding (29%) is the hard anchor.
- AAA Foundation is funded by motor clubs/insurers — note the funding, keep the skepticism in the piece.
