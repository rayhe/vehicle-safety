Ford's Tailgate Cable Fails Under Heavy Loads. Holding Loads Is Its Only Job.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

Ford recalled 30,123 of its newest trucks because a steel cable can fail under a heavy load. Not a sensor. Not software. A cable, on a tailgate, whose entire working life consists of holding weight while somebody loads something heavy onto the gate it holds up.

NHTSA campaign 26V689000, Ford internal number 26S78, covers certain 2026-2027 F-150s and 2026 F-250, F-350, and F-450 Super Duty trucks, and its summary sentence reads like a riddle: the left-side tailgate cable may fail under certain heavy load conditions.[1] When it fails, NHTSA says, the tailgate may drop onto the rear bumper, increasing the risk of injury.[1]

30,123
2026-2027 F-150 and 2026 Super Duty trucks under recall 26V689000 for a left-side tailgate cable that may fail under heavy load, with no dashboard warning of any kind

Your truck will not tell you it is happening. Ford's own report states there are no cluster messages, no malfunction indicator lights, and no other electronic messaging to the customer.[2] A cable snaps in silence, which is fitting, because a cable has never had anything to say.

Then comes the calendar, which is where this recall turns from a parts story into a patience test. Ford filed the recall on October 20, 2026. VINs do not become searchable on NHTSA.gov until December 7, a gap of 48 days during which an owner cannot confirm whether their truck is one of the 30,123. Interim letters, the ones that warn you without fixing anything, go out December 14. Remedy letters, the ones that actually summon you to a dealer for a free replacement cable, are anticipated in January 2027, seventy-plus days after the filing.[1] For seven weeks, the recall exists and the lookup does not.

None of this is the F-Series tailgate's first appearance in federal paperwork, which is the part Ford would presumably prefer you forget. In late 2019 and early 2020, Ford recalled 261,617 Super Duty trucks because water getting into the tailgate wiring could short the electric latch release switch and pop the gate open on its own, dropping unsecured cargo onto the road behind a moving truck.[3] In January 2021, NHTSA opened recall query RQ21-001 after eleven owners complained the gate was still opening after the recall repair, and the agency eventually counted 39 such complaints before closing the query once Ford ran a customer satisfaction program.[4] Latch electronics, then a questioned latch remedy, now a mechanical cable. Three chapters, one component family, zero shared failure mechanisms. A tailgate, it turns out, is not one part that fails one way; it is a small committee of parts, each taking its turn.

One detail in the new filing deserves a second look: only the left-side cable is recalled. A tailgate hangs on two cables, and NHTSA's consequence is the gate dropping onto the bumper rather than falling off entirely, which suggests the right cable holds. Ford's public summary does not say why the left cable alone is suspect, or whether the right cable is built the same way. Maybe the left cable carries a different load path. Maybe it came from a different batch. Ford has not said, and until it does, owners are left to wonder whether the cable that is not recalled is the one they should trust.

What you can actually do: if you own a 2026-2027 F-150 or a 2026 F-250, F-350, or F-450 Super Duty, stop standing on the tailgate and stop treating a heavy load on the gate as routine until the cable is replaced. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting December 7, 2026; before that date, a model-year match alone will not confirm anything. When the remedy letter arrives in January, the replacement cable is free, and Ford's recall line is 1-866-436-7332, recall 26S78.[1]

What this article did not prove: no crashes, injuries, or deaths are cited in the NHTSA record or the press coverage for this defect, and no count of failed cables exists in the public summary, so 30,123 is the recall population, not a failure count, and the failure rate is unknown. "Certain heavy load conditions" is undefined in the public summary: no weight threshold, no test condition, no word on whether ordinary loading or only rated-capacity loads trigger it. Whether the unrecalled right cable shares the left cable's construction is also unstated, so this article claims nothing about it either way.

Fair counter, stated at full strength: this is the mildest tailgate failure Ford has shipped. Old defect dropped cargo onto the highway at speed; this one drops a gate a few inches onto a bumper, only under heavy load, with no reported crash or injury anywhere. Fix is a cable swap, free, on 30,123 trucks out of the 732,139 F-Series trucks Ford sold in the US in 2024.[6] If you never load the gate near its limit, you may never meet this defect at all. True, all of it, and also the reason the defect is insidious rather than reassuring: the people most likely to meet it are the people using the truck as a truck, loading it heavy, standing on the dropped gate, with no dashboard warning of any kind.
