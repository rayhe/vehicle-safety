# Research: #895 — Ford F-150 fuel-tank trilogy (2023-2027 strap recall)

## News peg
Reuters, September 12, 2026 (today): Ford recalling 223,472 U.S. vehicles — certain 2023-2027 F-150s — for leaky and improperly secured fuel tanks that may detach while driving. Dealers will inspect and replace fuel tank straps, free of charge. A detached tank is a road hazard (crash risk); a fuel leak can cause engine stall or fire.
- https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/ford-recall-about-223500-us-vehicles-over-fuel-tank-issues-2026-09-12/

## The trilogy (novel contribution)
Nobody has connected these three campaigns into one genealogy:
- 2011: 11V-385 — 1.1M trucks (1997-2004 F-150) — salt corrosion eats the straps — tanks drop.
- 2022: 22V-414 — 1 truck (2021 F-150) — frame shipped without the strap bracket — tank never properly attached.
- 2026: (Sep 12) — 223,472 trucks (2023-2027 F-150) — straps fail again — tanks may detach; some also leak.
Fifteen years, three campaigns, one component, one failure mode (tank separates from truck), three different root causes.

## Arithmetic (novel)
- Scale: 223,472 / 828,832 F-Series sold 2025 = 26.96% — roughly one quarter of a model year's US sales.
- 2011 fire chain: 243 tank-drop reports -> 95 leaks (39.1%) -> 9 sparks -> 2 fires. Illustrative only: today's campaign is "leaky AND improperly secured," a combination the 2011 fleet did not have (leak + drop together is the worst pairing).
- 2022 campaign population was literally 1 vehicle — the smallest possible recall for the same component family.

## Limitations
- 2011 was corrosion on 10-14-year-old trucks in salt states — age + environment, arguably maintenance physics rather than a design defect.
- 2022 was a single-vehicle assembly miss (missing bracket), not a systemic defect.
- The 2026 Part 573 report is not yet public (recall announced today via NHTSA advisory); root cause (strap design? supplier batch? torque?) is not yet known. "Leaky and improperly secured" are two distinct conditions; the split between them within the 223,472 is unknown.
- No crash/fire/injury counts have been reported for the 2026 campaign.
- Estimated defect percentage for 2026 campaign unknown (2011: corrosion; 2022: 100% of 1).

## Strongest counterargument
These are three different failures, not one recurring defect, and lumping them is narrative convenience. Corrosion on decade-old salt-state trucks is chemistry, not engineering. A single missing bracket in 2022 is a one-off. The honest reading: Ford's fuel-tank retention has failed in three unrelated ways, which is arguably worse than one repeated mistake — it suggests no single fix closes the category. Also fair: Ford self-reported and recalled each time, including a 1-truck recall in 2022; the system detecting and correcting is the process working.

## Actionable takeaway
Own a 2023-2027 F-150: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. Warning signs per the 2022 573 report: new noise or vibration from under the truck bed area (a loose tank announces itself). Fuel smell = park it outside, away from structures, and call the dealer — the 2011 chain was leak -> spark -> fire. Remedy (strap inspect/replace) is free.
