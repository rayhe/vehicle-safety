# Research Notes: May 2026 State Majority Reversal

## Angle
The NSC May 2026 preliminary data shows the first month in 2026 where MORE states saw traffic death increases than decreases. The national safety "streak" is fraying at the state level.

## Primary Sources

### NSC Preliminary Estimates — May 2026 (released ~Jul 20, 2026)
- Through May 2026: 25 states UP, 20 states + DC DOWN, 4 SAME
- Biggest increases: DC +129% (9 more), Rhode Island +56% (5), North Dakota +42% (11), California +25% (242), Delaware +21% (8), Iowa +18% (17), Mississippi +13% (26), Kentucky +11% (26)
- Biggest decreases: West Virginia -45% (48 fewer), Hawaii -37% (22), Illinois -28% (129), Kansas -23% (35), Maryland -20% (38), Oklahoma -20% (51), Idaho -18% (15), Utah -17% (17), New Hampshire -15% (6), Tennessee -14% (60)
- Source: injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/overview/preliminary-monthly-estimates/

### NSC April 2026 data
- Through April: 28 states DOWN, 3 SAME, 19 states + DC UP
- California +39% (243 more), DC +220% (11), Rhode Island +86% (6)

- 7,770 deaths, down 4.3% YoY

### NHTSA Full Year 2024

## Novel Contribution
Nobody has connected these three data points:
1. Q1 2026: 33 states improving → national 0.99 rate (record-good)
2. April 2026: 28 states improving → 20 worsening 
3. May 2026: 20 states improving → 25 worsening (MAJORITY FLIP)

The national headline says "progress." The state-level data says "the progress is concentrating in fewer states while more states backslide." This is not a seasonal effect — comparing May-to-May (same month YoY). It's a structural divergence where the national average is increasingly driven by a shrinking group of improving states.

California alone adds 242 more deaths — a state with 39M people going 25% wrong while the nation celebrates sub-1.0 rates.

## FARS Cross-Tab
California's FARS footprint: The state hosts the most registered vehicles and VMT. Its increase should alarm because it's not a small-state statistical artifact — it's real population and real miles.

## Strongest Counterargument
NSC estimates are preliminary and mature over 12 months. May data has the lowest maturity level and routinely adjusts upward as fatalities are confirmed. The state-by-state comparison is May vs. a more mature May 2025 estimate. This asymmetry could make the reversal look worse than it is. Additionally, natural variation means monthly state-level data is noisy — a few severe multi-fatality crashes can swing a small state's percentage significantly.

## Limitations
- NSC estimates ≠ final FARS counts; substantial revision expected
- Small states (RI, ND, DE) see huge % swings from tiny absolute changes
- Comparing preliminary May 2026 to more-mature May 2025 introduces maturity bias
- No causal mechanism identified for the reversal (weather? enforcement changes? legislation?)
- The 25-vs-20 split doesn't weight by population or VMT — a 25% California increase matters more than a 56% Rhode Island increase in absolute terms

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely surprising? YES. The national narrative is "road deaths declining, sub-1.0 rate, best in 15 years." The state data shows the opposite is starting to happen — a majority of states are now getting worse. That contradiction is newsworthy and will concern anyone who assumed the decline was universal.
