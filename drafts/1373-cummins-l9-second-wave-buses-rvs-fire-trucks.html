One Bad Hose, Four More Recalls: Now It's in Fire Trucks and City Buses

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 3, 2026

Before you sign anything, check whether your vehicle has a Cummins L9 in it. A single defective part, a remote fuel filter hose that can separate from its fitting, leak diesel, and stall the engine, has now forced recalls across freight trucks, city buses, motorhomes, and fire apparatus.[1][2] Cummins recalled 39,052 of the engines itself in September. PACCAR followed with 26,729 trucks in October. And the filings kept coming anyway.

3,179
Vehicles in the confirmed second wave: Daimler trucks, Tiffin motorhomes, and Sutphen fire trucks

October 7 brought Daimler Truck North America: 2,592 Freightliner and FCCC vehicles with the same hose defect, campaign 26V660.[3] October 8 brought two more. Tiffin recalled 177 Allegro Bus and Phaeton motorhomes, campaign 26V662, built on its PowerGlide chassis.[1] Sutphen recalled 410 model-year 2021 fire trucks, campaign 26V661.[2] Read that last one twice. Fire trucks, the vehicles that drive toward fires, were recalled because they can leak fuel near an ignition source and stall on the way there.

Then came the coda. On October 27, Nova Bus recalled 453 LFS and LFS Artic transit buses for remote fuel filter hoses that may separate from the fitting, campaign 26V712, with owner letters not expected until December 26.[4] Day after Christmas. For buses that carry dozens of passengers each, on fixed routes, through city traffic, with a known fuel-leak defect on file.

Two numbers in this story deserve a second look, because they look contradictory and they are not. Cummins and PACCAR estimate only about 3% of their recalled population actually carries the defective hose, which works out to roughly 800 of PACCAR's 26,729 trucks.[5] Tiffin's federal filing, meanwhile, lists an estimated defect percentage of 100%.[1] Both can be true. Tiffin built its list from engine serial numbers Cummins sent over, so every one of its 177 motorhomes carries a suspect hose by construction, while the 3% figure describes a much larger, unfiltered population. Denominators matter. A recall that covers 177 vehicles at 100% suspect is not smaller than a recall that covers 26,729 vehicles at 3% defective. It is more certain.

Here is what the cascade now totals, counting only figures filed with regulators: 39,052 Cummins engines, 26,729 PACCAR trucks, 2,592 Daimler vehicles, 177 Tiffin motorhomes, and 410 Sutphen fire trucks, for 68,960. Add Nova's 453 buses and the tally reaches 69,413. Autocar has filed its own recall too, with a count that was still unknown at research time, and trade press reports more than a dozen manufacturers bought the affected L9 engines.[5] Every one of those numbers is a floor. None of them is a ceiling.

Fairness demands the strongest version of the other side, and it is a good argument. Nobody has reported an injury anywhere in this chain. Cummins identified the defect, every downstream manufacturer filed promptly, and every remedy is free. A two-month gap between filing and letters reflects parts logistics, not concealment, and the fix itself is specific: replacement hoses with improved connector crimps, parts 6552994, 6553001, and 6553018, with Cummins new builds off the suspect hoses since April 11, 2026.[1] One more honesty note, because it matters: Nova's filing uses identical defect wording but does not name the Cummins part in the record reviewed, so its link to this cascade is probable, not confirmed. Do not let anyone, including me, tell you otherwise.

Still. A defect that started in freight has now reached the vehicles you ride in, sleep in, and call when your house is on fire, and the only early warning system in the meantime is your nose. Diesel smell, a visible leak, or a low fuel pressure warning means park it and call the dealer. That should not be the safety plan for a city bus.

What to do, concretely. Transit agencies running 2022-2024 Nova LFS or 2023 LFS Artic buses: call Nova at 1-800-350-6682 and cite CR5969, and do not wait for December 26. Tiffin Allegro Bus or Phaeton owners from model years 2022-2025: call Tiffin at 1-256-356-8661 (TIF-150) or any Cummins dealer; letters arrive November 20. Fire departments with 2021 Sutphen trucks: Cummins dealers replace the hoses free under C7311, with letters December 7. Anyone shopping a used 2021-2027 medium or heavy vehicle with a Cummins L9: demand paperwork proving the crimped-connector hose replacement was done, because this hose does not respect brand boundaries. And everyone: run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before the letter arrives, since the letters, without exception in this cascade, take about two months.
