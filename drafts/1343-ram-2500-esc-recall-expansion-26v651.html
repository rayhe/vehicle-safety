Ram's Second Recall for the Same Steering Defect Catches 1,724 Trucks It Missed

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

Before you tow your boat with that new 2026 Ram 2500, you might want to see this. FCA is recalling 8,329 of the trucks because a steering column control module can silently switch off electronic stability control while you drive, with no warning until the dashboard light comes on after the system is already gone.[1]

1,724
Additional trucks caught in this expanded recall beyond the April 2026 scope

This is an expansion, not a debut. FCA recalled 6,605 of these trucks in April for the same module problem. Six months later, the population grew by 1,724, a 26 percent increase that tells you something useful: the first recall was scoped wrong.[2] Recalls get bigger as build data improves, which is how the system is supposed to work, but if you bought a 2026 Ram 2500 after April and your VIN was not in the first batch, you have been driving for months with a defect FCA already knew about. Nice.

Here is what the defect actually does, per the NHTSA filing. Trucks built between September 13 and November 15, 2025 got steering column control modules with an internal fault. The module can reset itself unexpectedly mid-drive, and when it does, ESC drops offline immediately. Your first indication is the stability control warning light. Already off. No gradual degradation, no chime beforehand, no driver action that triggers it.[1] The filing states plainly that the trucks fail to comply with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 126, the rule that has required ESC on new vehicles since the 2012 model year.[3]

Why does losing ESC matter? Because it is one of the most effective safety systems ever bolted to a car. IIHS research found it cuts fatal single-vehicle crash risk roughly in half and fatal single-vehicle rollovers by 72 to 75 percent.[4] A heavy-duty pickup towing at highway speed is exactly the vehicle where an unexpected loss of stability control should frighten you. In the 2014-2023 FARS window, the Ram 2500 line recorded 153 driver and passenger deaths at a low but nonzero rate of 0.13 per 100 million miles traveled; those were older trucks, not these 2026s, and the point is not that Ram 2500s are deathtraps, it is that a mandated safety system quietly going dark on a new truck is the wrong direction.[5]

Now the part where I am fair to FCA, because somebody should be. The company estimates the defect rate at 0.1 percent, which works out to roughly eight of the 8,329 trucks. The fault is intermittent and can reset and recover on its own. FCA reports zero crashes and zero injuries tied to it. And an FMVSS noncompliance finding is a legal determination about the design, not proof that thousands of trucks are about to roll over on the interstate.[2] That is the honest read.

But here is the honest read for you, the owner, which is different. Owner notification letters do not go out until November 24. Your VIN became searchable on October 13. Do not wait six weeks for a letter about a steering module. Run your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, reference FCA recall 90D or NHTSA 26V651000, and if yours is in the population, schedule the free module replacement now.[1] Until the fix is in, if that ESC light flickers on mid-drive, treat it as the system being off, because that is exactly what it means: slow down and widen your following distance. A $60,000 truck should not make you play guessing games with the one system the federal government decided was too important to be optional.
