## Story in one line

Honda is recalling 16,387 S2000 roadsters (NHTSA 26V749, reported Nov 12, 2026) because a fuel flange joint assembly may have been built without brazing material, so the fuel line can loosen, leak, and stall the engine. The cars are 2004-2005 models. The defect did not leave the factory with them: it arrived later, in a parts bin, when supplier Sanoh Industrial moved its brazing operation and shipped 67 replacement parts made without brazing. Honda found out in December 2025, from one customer in Japan.

## Key verified facts

- **Campaign:** NHTSA 26V749000; Honda number LQD. Report received by NHTSA Nov 12, 2026. Recall issued by Honda Nov 5, 2026.
- **Population:** certain 2004-2005 Honda S2000, built April 14, 2003 to Sept 21, 2005; 16,387 vehicles.
- **Defect:** the fuel flange joint assembly (part 16623-PCX-003) may have been manufactured without brazing material at the joint-to-flange connection. The press-fit connection can loosen and separate; fuel may leak, fuel pressure drops, engine loses acceleration or stalls, and may not restart.
- **Consequence (NHTSA wording):** "An engine stall while driving increases the risk of a crash or injury."
- **Supplier:** Sanoh Industrial Co., Ltd. (Japan). Sanoh moved where it did its brazing and did not properly verify its process or work instructions afterward. 67 replacement parts were manufactured without brazing material and shipped.
- **Discovery:** December 2025 — a customer in Japan who had recently had the fuel flange joint assembly replaced reported problems. Honda investigated the supplier, in January 2026 began looking for a used vehicle to test, ran tests in May 2026, and spent five to six months investigating before issuing the recall Nov 5, 2026.
- **Harm so far:** as of Nov 5, 2026, no warranty claims and no reports of injury or death in the US.
- **Remedy:** dealers inspect and replace the fuel flange joint assembly with a new part, free of charge. Owner notification letters expected Jan 4, 2027. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning Nov 20, 2026.
- **Japan:** parallel recall (CAA 00000036121) published Nov 12, 2026, action from Nov 13, 2026.

## Original calculations (for the article)

- **The population exceeds the sales:** US S2000 sales were 7,320 (2004) + 7,780 (2005) = 15,100. The recall covers 16,387. Difference: 1,287 cars, or 8.5% more than the two model years' combined US sales. Honda's population review "included vehicles potentially repaired with the defective part" — so at least 1,287 of the recalled cars are in the population because of a repair, not because of how they were built. (Label the 1,287 as the arithmetic gap, not a confirmed count of repaired cars; the mechanism is Honda's stated population definition.)
- **The bad parts were a rounding error:** 67 defective replacement parts against a 16,387-car population is 0.4%. The recall is 245 cars wide for every 1 bad part actually made. That is the shape of a service-parts recall: the defective population is tiny, the exposed population is everyone who might have touched the part.
- **The timeline:** part built 2003-2005; supplier moved brazing at some point after; customer complaint Dec 2025; recall Nov 5, 2026. From the youngest affected car (built Sept 2005) to the recall is 21 years and 2 months. From the customer complaint to the recall is 11 months.
- **The car is now a collector item:** Hagerty's October 2026 price guide puts a #3-condition 2004 S2000 at $32,200, and 12-month auction data averages $39,400 for 2004-09 cars. The recall lands on a car people now buy as an investment, not a commuter.
- **Share of the model:** 16,387 is 24.6% of all 66,547 S2000s ever sold in the US.

## Limitations

- The 1,287-car gap is arithmetic (16,387 minus 15,100), not a Honda-stated figure; Honda has not published how many recalled cars are in the population because of a repair versus because of their build date. The article must say the population "includes" repaired cars, not that exactly 1,287 were repaired.
- Sales figures are calendar-year US sales (Wikipedia/Hemmings); the recall's built window (April 14, 2003–Sept 21, 2005) straddles model years, so the 15,100 comparison is approximate, not exact. A car built in late 2003 is a 2004 model; a car built in 2005 could be sold in 2006. The gap is directionally robust (the recall is bigger than both years' sales) but the precise 1,287 depends on the sales-year convention.
- No crash, injury, or fire has been tied to this defect in the US. The risk is a stall and a leak, not a demonstrated fire. Do not imply fires happened.
- The exact date Sanoh moved its brazing operation is not in the public record I could verify; do not state a year for the move.
- Part 573 chronology details beyond the USA Today/automotor summaries were not independently pulled; the Dec 2025–Nov 2026 timeline comes from Honda's account as reported.

## Strongest counterargument

"This is a nothing recall. Sixteen thousand cars, zero injuries, zero warranty claims, a part that fails only if a 20-year-old roadster happened to get a bad replacement flange fitted at some point. Honda caught it from one complaint and fixed it for free. The system worked." That is mostly true, and the article should say so. The counter to the counter: the system worked only because one owner in Japan noticed and complained, and because Honda spent 11 months chasing a used car to test. A defect that announces itself only as a fuel leak on a car with no fuel-pressure warning, in a fleet where most cars are now second- or third-owner weekend cars, is exactly the kind that goes unnoticed until a stall happens at speed. The recall is small. The lesson — that the parts bin can be more dangerous than the assembly line — is not.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- If you own a 2004-2005 S2000: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today (searchable from Nov 20, 2026). Do not wait for the Jan 4, 2027 letter.
- If your S2000 ever had fuel-system work — especially a fuel flange joint (part 16623-PCX-003) replaced — you are in the population even if your car was built clean. Bring the repair receipt to the dealer; the inspection is free.
- Smell fuel, lose power, or get a no-restart after a stall: pull over, do not keep cranking, call Honda at 1-866-784-1870.
- Buying a used S2000: ask for the recall clearance. A 2004 car in #3 condition is a $32,200 asset; an open fuel recall is a negotiating point and a free dealer visit.
