**Article:** 1,103 Cyclists Died in 2024. Most of Them Died in the Dark.
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone | **Date:** 2026-11-10 | **Round:** 0
**Pre-panel gates (verified by script, not opinion):** em dashes 0 (max 3), banned phrases 0, The-starters 4.0% (max 15%), CSS `class="story"` + `../style.css` (no story.css), hero real JPEG 1920x1280 hash e09ad28d, rhythm PASS (variance 526.7, short 7.3%, long 53.7%), body ~1,020 words incl. references.
**Pre-panel fix:** darkness paragraph originally said the 6-9 p.m. window "is the deadliest in every season"; corrected to "the deadliest window is 6 to 9 p.m." because in summer it ties the 9 p.m.-midnight block at 19% (fact sheet Figure 1). The total-column figure (23%, highest of all eight intervals) supports the corrected wording.

## 1. General Editor — 8.7/10
Structure holds: lede reframes the "good news" decline (record year to third-worst), darkness paragraph, light-truck engineering paragraph, pull stat, geography paragraph, who-dies paragraph, alcohol/helmet paragraph, injuries-divergence paragraph, counterargument, limitations, action close. Two nits: the "What This Means for You" paragraph is one long block addressing four different audiences (riders, drivers, shoppers, cities) and would read better split in two; and the headline's "Most" rests on 52%, a bare majority, which is defensible but thin. Neither is fatal.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.8/10
Distinctly Mia: engineering-first, judgmental about design ("A modern pickup's hood sits at a grown man's chest height with a blind zone stretching yards ahead of the bumper"), and the pivot line "This is an engineering story before it is a behavior story" is the piece's thesis in one sentence. No banned phrases, no AI tells in the scan. Rhythm passes with real variance (526.7). One flat spot: the parallel openers "Look at when they died" / "Now look at what hit them" repeat the same device in adjacent paragraphs. Minor.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.8/10
The moral reasoning is the article's spine and it is handled straight: it refuses the easy story (reckless, drunk, unhelmeted rider) and the easy opposite story (innocent rider), giving alcohol and helmets their own paragraph in full before showing they explain a minority of the toll. No victim-blaming of the dead, no self-congratulation, no policy prescription dressed as data. The line "a pickup's blind zone starts at the bumper and the rider's protection ends at his skull" is sharp but fair. No issues.

## 4. Social Shareability — 8.6/10
Headline is built to travel: a number, a surprise (dark beats daylight), and a villain (the light truck). The pull stat (552) is self-contained and quotable. The 2-to-1 truck ratio, the third-worst-year framing, and the seven-cities-with-zero-deaths contrast are all shareable. Weakness: no single sentence in the body as sharp as the headline; the closest ("a sensor and sightline failure wearing a crash report") is good but buried in paragraph three.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
All figures check against DOT HS 813 822: 1,103 deaths (-6% from 1,173), dark 52% vs daylight 44%, light trucks 552 vs cars 282 (SUVs 292, pickups 220), 84% front impact (869/1,035), 60% not at intersections, 81% urban, Florida 208 (18.9%), Louisiana rate 0.96 vs national 0.32 (3.0x), Tucson 1.44 (4.5x national), seven zero-death major cities, 87% male, average age 48, 65+ 237 deaths (21.5%), alcohol 33% of fatal crashes, 21% of killed riders BAC .01+, injuries 52,887 (+6% from 49,989). IIHS corroboration checks: 1,075 count, third-highest ever, 68% unhelmeted, +73% since 2010. The NHTSA/IIHS 28-death boundary difference is stated with its reason (motorized bicycles since 2022). The summer-tie wording was fixed pre-panel (see above). One soft spot: the hood-height line is illustrative color, not a measured figure; it reads as engineering judgment rather than a sourced stat, which is acceptable in Mia's voice but should not be quoted as data. No invented citations.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution: the 1.96x light-truck-to-car ratio, the 53.3% light-truck share, the dark-vs-daylight death count (~574 vs ~485), the injuries-per-death rise (42.6 to 47.9, +12.5%), the three-state concentration (FL+CA+TX = 40.8%), and the decade comparison (cyclist deaths +73-78% since 2010 vs total traffic deaths +19%) are all computed from the fact sheet, none published in it. Counterargument is stated at full strength (fleet artifact, unmeasured exposure, evening volume, post-pandemic spike). Limitations are honest: ARF provisional status, CRSS sampling error, no bicycle-miles denominator, no bike-lane variable, count-boundary difference. Methodology is shown, not gestured at.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.7/10
Pull stat plus inline ratios carry the numbers well, and percentages are always paired with counts. The references section is complete with six sources and working links. A small state table (Florida 208, Louisiana 0.96 rate, Tucson 1.44) would have made the geography ranking scannable at a glance; prose does the job but the reader has to hold four numbers in their head. The 84% front-impact figure deserves a second pull stat or bold treatment.
