The Gap

Your Tail Lights Can Die Without Warning. You Would Never Know.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 30, 2026

Before you drive home tonight, you might want to see this. Chrysler just recalled 13,330 Dodge Durangos because damaged terminals in the tail light wiring harness can kill the tail lights outright. NHTSA campaign 26V701000, filed October 22.[1] The recall number is A2D, and if you own a 2026 Durango, this one is yours to check, not wait for.

13,330
2026 Dodge Durangos recalled for tail lights that can silently fail — a defect the driver can never detect from behind the wheel.

Here’s the part that should make you angry. When headlights die, you know: the road goes dark. When tail lights die, nothing changes inside the car. No warning light, no chime, no dimming on your dashboard. You drive on, perfectly comfortable, while everyone behind you loses the one signal that tells them where your bumper ends. The first person to notice the defect is the person who rear-ends you. And the crash report will blame them.

At fault: damaged terminals in the tail light wiring harness, a violation of FMVSS 108, the federal lighting standard.[1] Dealers will inspect and replace the tail light assemblies free of charge. Chrysler’s customer line is 800-853-1403. Owner letters go out December 3. But your VIN has been searchable on nhtsa.gov since yesterday, October 29.[4] That is a five-week gap between “you can find out” and “we’ll tell you,” and Chrysler filled it with silence.

What to do. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now, tonight, before you drive anywhere after dark.[4] Then do the storefront test: back up to a lit window at night, hit the lights, look at the reflection. If the tails are out, call the dealer in the morning; the repair is free, and driving a 2026 Durango with dead tail lights is driving a car that is legally noncompliant and invisibly dangerous from behind.

Automakers recall engines, airbags, brakes. Fine. But this is the defect that outsources the consequences to strangers. You will never see your own tail lights fail. The family behind you will. That is the gap this recall is really about, and it closes the moment you type your VIN into a website.
