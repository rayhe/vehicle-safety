# Research: GM Sierra 1500 Grille Deflector — Recall Query RQ26003
Slug: `sierra-grille-deflector-rq26003` | Number: 1242 | Journalist: Mia Crumplezone
Date researched: 2026-10-22

## The story
NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened **Recall Query RQ26003** on October 9, 2026,
after 118 owner complaints that the **upper grille deflector** on 2022-2024 GMC Sierra 1500
pickups detaches while driving. **21 of the 118** allege it detached AFTER the truck had
already received the recall repair (25V-060, filed Feb 6, 2025). Population: ~535,639 trucks.

## Key facts (primary sources)
- RQ26003 opened 2026-10-09; subject "Upper Grille Deflector Detachment"; manufacturer
  General Motors, LLC; estimated population 535,639 (2022-2024 Sierra 1500).
  Source: NHTSA ODI investigation summary (mirrored at oemdtc.com/investigation/RQ26003/).
- 118 complaints total; 21 allege detachment post-25V-060 repair; some report cracked
  windshields or evasive maneuvers by surrounding vehicles. No crashes/injuries/deaths
  in NHTSA's file.
- Recall 25V-060 (GM number N242490120, NHTSA campaign 25V060000): filed 2026-02-06...
  correction: **2025-02-06**. Covered only 70,768 2022 Sierra 1500s with a chrome deflector
  built after the mid-cycle refresh (built Nov 9, 2021 - Sep 29, 2022). Excluded: 2022s
  without chrome deflector, ALL 2023 and 2024 model years. Owner letters mailed Mar 17, 2025.
  Remedy: dealers reinforce or repair the attachment points.
- GM's internal timeline (per The Autowire): employee flagged a detached deflector via
  Speak Up for Safety on Jan 19, 2024. GM opened investigation Feb 27, 2024, CLOSED it
  May 23, 2024 finding "no defect trend." Reopened Dec 11, 2024 when warranty claims climbed.
- GM told NHTSA the 2023-2024 trucks used deflectors from outside the supplier's suspect
  production window — the reason they were excluded from 25V-060. RQ26003 asks whether that
  cutoff was right.
- GM counted 1,225 complaints + 4 accident allegations by end of 2024 (pre-recall population).
- ~64% of recalled trucks had the repair as of January 2026 (GM Authority via NHTSA filings).
- 2022-2024 Sierra 1500s carry GM's mid-cycle-refresh front end where the upper grille
  deflector is held by eight attachment points that GM says can fracture over time; if all
  eight break, the part detaches at speed. GM's filing noted drivers may hear a rattle first.

## Original contribution (novel calculation)
1. **Recall-scope gap: 7.6x.** RQ26003's population (535,639) is 7.6x the recalled population
   (70,768). Only ~13% of the queried trucks were ever recalled for this defect.
2. **Post-repair detachment share: 17.8%.** 21 of 118 complaints (17.8%) allege the deflector
   came off AFTER the recall remedy was applied — i.e., among complainants, nearly 1 in 5
   failures happened to "fixed" trucks.
3. **Pre-recall complaint intensity:** 1,225 complaints + 4 accident allegations across the
   70,768-truck recall population (end of 2024) = ~1.7% complaint rate on a narrow population,
   yet GM first closed the file as "no defect trend" (May 2024) and waited 7 more months.

## Kill test: PASS
- Newsworthy: federal recall query opened 13 days ago; flying trim at highway speed is a
  classic road-debris hazard; 535K trucks on the road.
- Novel angle: not "another recall" — it's the *recall that didn't stick*. The remedy itself
  is under review (a Recall Query tests the adequacy of scope AND remedy). 21 post-repair
  detachments is the design-engineering hook: what does "fixed" mean when eight plastic tabs
  still fracture in the field?
- Mia Crumplezone beat: design analysis — what happens when a 3-4 ft trim bar lets go at
  70 mph, the eight-point attachment design, and what a driver should listen for (the rattle).

## Design/engineering angle (Mia's)
- The upper grille deflector is wide trim across the top of the grille; held by eight
  attachment points. GM's own filing: attachments fracture over time; if all eight break,
  the part detaches while driving. Possible rattle warning.
- A detached trim piece at highway speed: cracked windshields reported; surrounding vehicles
  swerving. NHTSA lists risk to truck, nearby vehicles, motorcyclists, pedestrians.
- Actionable: 2022-2024 Sierra 1500 owners — check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 25V-060;
  if you hear a rattle from the front end, treat it as the detachment warning GM described
  and get to a dealer; following drivers: give the truck shedding its face some room.

## Limitations
- Complaints are self-reported allegations; 118 complaints over ~535K trucks is a thin signal
  (~22 per 100K). NHTSA files no crashes, injuries, or deaths so far.
- The 21 post-repair detachments are allegations in complaints, not confirmed repair-failure
  verifications. GM has not stated a theory for why a repaired truck would lose the part again.
- "Outside the supplier's suspect window" (GM's basis for excluding 2023-2024) is GM's claim;
  the query exists precisely to test it.
- Not yet: GM's formal response to the recall query (due to NHTSA). No expanded recall announced.

## Strongest counterargument
GM recalled fast (4 months from reopening investigation to filing) and the remedy reinforces all
eight attachment points; 21 allegations may include unrelated damage, road strikes, or
misdiagnosed damage from non-defect sources. The query may close with a finding that the recall
was adequate and the out-of-window model years fail for ordinary reasons.
