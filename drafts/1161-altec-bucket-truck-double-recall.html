Altec Recalled Its Bucket Trucks Twice in Seven Days: One for the Step That Falls Off, One for the Boom That Won't Stow

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 14, 2026

Two recalls. Seven days. Same trucks. Altec Industries filed one safety recall with NHTSA in the first week of October and a second the next week, and both target the bucket trucks that lineworkers climb every morning. The first recall says the boom might not stow, so you can drive off with the bucket still in the air. The second says the step might not be welded on, so the piece of metal you stand on to get into the bucket can separate under your boots. Combined, the two campaigns cover 6,923 aerial devices, and the Digger Derrick got hit by both.[1][2]

Altec safety recalls filed with NHTSA in October 2026, seven days apart, on the same class of work trucks

Recall one is the welding one. Altec campaign CSN-3374 covers 2020 through 2026 AA, AN, and AM series aerial devices, plus 2024-2026 Digger Derricks and 2024-2025 A-Class trucks: 6,780 units in all. The turntable-mounted platform access step, the step you plant your boot on before forty feet of nothing, may have been improperly welded between the step tube and the mounting plate.[1] A step that separates or goes unstable while you are on it is a fall hazard in the most literal sense, and Altec will replace the steps. Owner letters go out November 27, and fleet managers can call 877-462-5832 today.

The second one is a wiring problem. Campaign CSN-3370 covers 2021-2026 Digger Derricks and 2023-2025 TA Series aerials: 143 units. Improper wiring can keep the boom stow auto-neutral from engaging, and that feature exists for exactly one reason, so the truck cannot be driven with the boom elevated.[2] Without it, a driver rolls out with the boom up, the truck exceeds its recorded travel height, and overpasses do not negotiate. Letters for this one go out November 20.

Here is the part that should bother you more than either recall alone, because Altec has a weld problem. In 2022 the company recalled DT65 and DT85 aerial devices because the weld between the base boom and the auger stow bracket could crack.[5] Around 2020 it was pedestal-to-turntable bolts with a specific head marking that could fail. In May 2026, another 213 units went back because the ground-level emergency stop might not stop the hydraulics when the machine was in ePTO mode.[4] Now a step weld. That is not a batch. That is a pattern.

To be fair, and it costs me nothing to say it: finding your own defects and filing with NHTSA is how the system is supposed to work. Two recalls in a week can mean two good catches rather than two disasters, and 6,780 units across seven model years is a modest footprint for the step campaign, a wide net cast to catch a bad run of welds.

What to do: if your fleet runs Altec AA, AN, or AM series trucks from 2020 to 2026, Digger Derricks from 2021 on, TA Series from 2023 to 2025, or A-Class units, inspect the turntable step welds and test the boom stow auto-neutral this week instead of waiting for the November letters. And if the letters are late, call Altec at 877-462-5832 and reference CSN-3374 and CSN-3370.

Limitations

NHTSA’s “potentially affected” counts are estimates, not defect rates, and neither filing names a complaint or incident count, so we cannot say how many steps actually separated or how many booms were driven unstowed. FARS tracks roadway fatalities, not occupational falls from bucket trucks; the fall data lives in the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ fatal-injury census, which we did not query, so this story makes no body-count claims. Altec is an upfitter: the recalls cover the aerial equipment, not the truck chassis, so fleet managers should check chassis-OEM recalls separately. The weld “pattern” reading is an inference from four filings over six years, not a root-cause finding.
