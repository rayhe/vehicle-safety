# Research Notes: Chevrolet Sonic — Five Stars, Deadliest Subcompact

## Core Finding
The Chevrolet Sonic earned NHTSA 5-star overall crash test rating AND IIHS Top Safety Pick designation. Yet FARS data shows it has the highest fatality rate (1.40/100M VMT) of any subcompact car in America — double the rate of the Aveo it replaced.

## FARS Data (2014-2023)

### Sonic vs Aveo
| Metric | Chevrolet Sonic | Chevrolet Aveo |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Death rate | 1.40/100M VMT | 0.69/100M VMT |
| Total deaths | 494 | 138 |
| Fatal crashes | 655 | 155 |
| Deaths/crash | 0.754 | 0.890 |
| Fleet size | 306,250 | 175,000 |
| Impairment % | 20.0% | 22.0% |
| Alcohol % | 14.7% | 16.6% |

### Model Year Deaths (Sonic)
Peak: MY2004-2009 era shows 35-47 deaths/yr (likely Aveo misclassification or international market)
Sonic-era (2012-2020): 32-37 deaths/yr for 2012-2015, declining to 6-7 by 2019-2020

## Crash Test Ratings
- NHTSA: 5/5 overall (frontal 5/5, side 5/5, rollover 4/5)
- IIHS: Top Safety Pick (earned at launch in 2012)
  - Good: moderate overlap, side, roof, head restraints
  - Marginal: small overlap front (2012-2013, improved to Good by 2015)
- Aveo: Acceptable frontal, Marginal in other three IIHS categories

## Background
- Sonic launched 2012, replaced Aveo (a rebadged Daewoo Kalos)
- Built at GM Orion Assembly in Lake Orion, Michigan (first US-built subcompact in years)
- GM invested ~$545M to retool Orion for Sonic production
- Discontinued after 2020 model year
- IIHS explicitly noted Sonic was a safety improvement over Aveo

## Novel Cross-Tabulation
Sonic's impairment rate (20.0%) is LOWER than the Aveo's (22.0%). The Sonic's higher death rate can't be attributed to worse driver behavior. The car itself — despite acing every crash test — kills at twice the rate of the car it replaced.

## Strongest Counterargument
Fleet size and exposure differences. The Sonic (306K fleet) had more vehicles on road than the Aveo (175K), giving it more total exposure. But the RATE already controls for VMT. A fair counterpoint is that the Sonic may have attracted a different demographic — younger urban buyers who drive more aggressively or in more dangerous environments — but the impairment data doesn't support a "wilder driver" theory.

## Limitations
- FARS VMT estimates use national averages, not model-specific odometer data
- Rate calculations have ±15% uncertainty for models with <500K fleet
- Can't distinguish who dies (occupant vs other vehicle occupant vs pedestrian)
- No control for geographic distribution (urban vs rural mix)
