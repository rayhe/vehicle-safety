### Source 3: FMVSS 206 History (Federal Register + NHTSA interpretations)
- Door lock requirements in place since 1968 (April 27, 1968 amendment)
- Standard amended February 6, 2007, effective September 1, 2009 (harmonization with global regulations)
- June 3, 2026: NHTSA published final rule removing "obsolete requirements" from FMVSS 206, effective July 6, 2026 — cleanup only, NOT adding electronic door egress requirements
- The standard assumes mechanical door handles exist. It has NEVER addressed what happens when doors require electrical power to operate.

- Compliance required: September 1, 2029
- Total: ~6 years from NPRM to mandatory compliance
- Heavy vehicle AEB: delayed from April 2024 to January 2025+ (still not finalized)

## Original Calculation: The Rulemaking Timeline

NHTSA's announcement today is at STEP ZERO of the rulemaking process:

Step 0 (Today): NHTSA agrees to "gather information and determine if rulemaking is appropriate"
Step 1 (~1-2 years): Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) + comment period
Step 2 (~1-2 years): Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) + comment period  
Step 3 (~1 year): Final rule published
Step 4 (~3-5 years): Phase-in / compliance date

AEB precedent: NPRM June 2023 → compliance Sept 2029 = 6.25 years
But AEB was already at the NPRM stage. The door egress standard is PRE-ANPRM.

Conservative timeline: 2026 + 2 years (ANPRM) + 2 years (NPRM) + 1 year (final rule) + 3 years (compliance) = 2034

Optimistic: 2032
Pessimistic: 2036+

US new vehicle sales: ~16M/year
Electronic handle adoption growing: est. 20-30% of new vehicles in 2026

Over 2026-2034 (8 years): ~128M new vehicles sold, est. 25-35M with electronic handles, ZERO subject to a federal egress standard.

## Novel Angle
This is NOT an article about Tesla door handles (already done). This is about the REGULATORY ARCHITECTURE failure: FMVSS 206 was written in 1968 for an era of mechanical handles. It's been FIFTY-EIGHT years. NHTSA just agreed to THINK about whether cars should have doors that open reliably after a crash. Using AEB as precedent, the first cars required to comply won't roll off a line until the early 2030s.

The irony: NHTSA's own FMVSS 206 cleanup rule removing "obsolete requirements" took effect July 6, 2026 — THREE WEEKS AGO. They cleaned up a standard they'd already let become obsolete, rather than updating it for the 21st century.
