# #973 Research — "The First Federal Robotaxi Rule Lands in 2030. About 85 Million Rides Happen Before It."

**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage · **Kicker:** Existential Dread · **Date:** 2026-09-22

## News hook
On Sept 17, 2026, NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison announced (at the AI Horizons PGH Summit in Pittsburgh) the formal execution of a cooperative agreement with SAE International launching **A2SCEND** — Advancing America's Safety and Competitiveness for the Evolution of National Deployments — a three-year, $5M consortium to gather data that will inform the **first federal performance standard for automated driving systems (ADS)**.

## Key facts (all sourced)
1. A2SCEND: 3-year, $5M cooperative agreement between NHTSA and SAE Industry Technologies Consortia; first announced July 30, 2026 at the SAE Automated Transportation Symposium in San Diego; formal execution disclosed Sept 17, 2026. Goal: measurable driving competencies for AVs + repeatable methods to assess them ("ADS competency" is the top priority). Neither the speech nor the July materials attach a completion date. Sources: eletric-vehicles.com, Repairer Driven News (Sept 22), SAE press release via Repairer Driven News, JDSupra/Holland & Knight.
2. No enforceable standard before ~2030: outside analysts' estimate, based on a 3-year data-gathering phase plus rulemaking and comment. "By which point Waymo alone expects to be operating well beyond its current 4,000 vehicles." Source: eletric-vehicles.com.
3. Waymo scale today: 500,000+ paid rides per week across 15 US cities (plus expansion in Europe/Asia). Source: eletric-vehicles.com.
4. Zoox: first-ever commercial exemption for a purpose-built robotaxi, July 30, 2026 — up to 2,500 vehicles/year for two years, after ~4 years of federal scrutiny including a 105-vehicle fleet recall and Part 555 filings on eight safety standards. Source: eletric-vehicles.com, tech-times.
5. Tesla Cybercab: NHTSA Audit Query AQ26002 opened Sept 3, 2026 (the day paid rides began in Austin; ~1,000 vehicles, no steering wheel/pedals/mirrors); escalated to Special Order Sept 10, response under oath due Sept 30, 2026. Tesla certified rather than seeking exemption; the audit tests that gap. FSD engineering analysis covers ~3.2M Teslas. Sources: eletric-vehicles.com, TeslaNorth.
6. First-responder friction: NHTSA call to action to AV developers on July 8, 2026 after documenting a "clear pattern" of driverless vehicles interfering with police/fire/paramedics — incidents included AVs driving into active emergency scenes, blocking ambulances, ignoring flares, cones, hand signals. "Concrete steps in response, including at least one recall." Austin Fire Captain Matthew McElearney told NBC News he was unconvinced the touchscreen emergency controls were a "silver bullet." Sources: eletric-vehicles.com, JDSupra.
7. Self Drive Act of 2026: House Energy and Commerce subcommittee reviewed earlier in 2026; would raise the annual AV deployment exemption cap from 2,500 to 90,000 vehicles per automaker (36x) and establish a federal framework for vehicles without traditional steering wheels. Source: eletric-vehicles.com.
8. NHTSA's five-part AV package (summer 2026): Zoox exemption; A2SCEND; interim final rule letting pre-exemption-manufactured vehicles qualify for commercial exemption + streamlined Part 555 applications; first refresh of federal AV guidance since AV 2.0 (2017) with a public docket on emergency responder interactions, safety management systems, remote assistance, post-crash protocols; Robomart delivery vehicle under exemption review. Source: JDSupra/Holland & Knight.
9. Morrison quotes: "Our approach is not to take a hatchet to current standards and give AVs free reign based on an assumption that they'll be safe. Instead, we're using a scalpel to make necessary technical updates while still placing safety first." Described posture as "deliberative urgency." Source: eletric-vehicles.com.
10. DOT Secretary Sean Duffy's July 22, 2026 letter to Congress: one of DOT's top surface-transportation-reauthorization priorities is "advancing safe AV innovation" — modernize regulations, establish a voluntary AV pilot program with preemptive authority, lift restrictive deployment caps, develop tailored FMVSS for driverless vehicles. Source: JDSupra.

## Original contributions (required)
- **Rides-before-rule calculation:** 500,000 paid rides/week × 171 weeks (Sept 22, 2026 → Jan 1, 2030) = **85.5 million paid rides**, Waymo alone, at today's pace, before any enforceable federal ADS competency standard exists. Conservative: excludes Tesla, Zoox, fleet growth, and Europe/Asia expansion.
- **36x exemption multiplier:** Self Drive Act would raise the per-automaker annual exempt-vehicle cap from 2,500 → 90,000, a 36-fold increase, while the competency standard is still unwritten.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? YES — the formal start of America's first robotaxi safety rule was announced 5 days ago, and the timeline math (standard ~2030 vs. half a million rides a week today) is a real paradox nobody in the queue has done. Novel angle: we are not waiting for the technology; the technology is waiting for no one, and the regulator just admitted the rulebook is four years out.

## Limitations
- "No earlier than 2030" is outside analysts' estimate, not NHTSA's stated date; NHTSA attached no completion date.
- 500,000 rides/week and 4,000 vehicles are Waymo figures via secondary reporting; current pace assumed flat (conservative vs. stated growth).
- This article is about rulemaking timelines, not a claim that current robotaxis are unsafe.

## Strongest counterargument
Exemptions are not lawlessness: the Part 555 process exists and is working (Zoox did 4 years of it). Writing the standard from real operational data (A2SCEND) rather than in advance is arguably the correct regulatory design — premature hardware-specific rules are exactly what Morrison says they're avoiding ("scalpel, not hatchet"). And state/local patchworks do exist today, so the road is not unregulated. If the data supports that, it says so.
