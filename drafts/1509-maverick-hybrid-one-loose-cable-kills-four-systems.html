One Loose Cable Can Shut Down a Maverick Hybrid's Steering, Brakes, Lights, and Drive Power

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Ford's smallest truck has a 12-volt problem that refuses to stay fixed. Filed November 2, the newest chapter covers 8,650 model year 2022 Maverick Hybrids and one small part: a terminal eyelet, C1617E, where the positive battery cable meets the high-current Battery Junction Box. When that eyelet works loose, the truck can lose power steering assist, power brake assist, exterior lighting, and drive power. All at once. From one piece of bent metal.[1][2]

Follow the wire and the failure makes a grim kind of sense. A single eyelet feeds the Battery Junction Box, and the box feeds everything downstream, so a truck that loses four systems has not suffered four failures. It has suffered one, at the top of the chain. Ford's own filing puts the defect estimate at 1 percent, roughly 87 trucks out of 8,650, built between February 3, 2021 and May 12, 2022, all with the 2.5-liter hybrid drivetrain and a suspect eyelet unit that had not already been repaired under an earlier customer satisfaction program.[1]

What killed the joint is almost poetic. Hydroxy acetic acid, left over from the dip-solder step that built the terminal eyelet, can corrode the ultrasonic weld attaching the terminal to its cable. Read that again slowly. A chemical from the manufacturing process ate the weld that the same process created, which means the defect was baked in on the factory floor, invisible to every dealer inspection and every software update Ford has thrown at this truck's electrical system since. Supplier Yazaki built the cable assembly, part number NZ6T-14300-CA, and no owner, no mechanic, and no dashboard warning can spot a weld corroding inside a sealed connection.[1]

Ford moved fast this time, by Detroit standards. Its Critical Concern Review Group opened an investigation on July 17, 2026, after owner complaints to NHTSA described loss of motive power while driving. Eleven such complaints arrived, every one alleging the truck lost power on the road, alongside 427 potentially responsive warranty claims, of which only 11 report a loss of motive power while driving. On October 27 the Field Review Committee approved a field action, and the recall was filed five days later. As of October 13, Ford knew of zero accidents and zero injuries tied to the condition, which is the good news, and also the reason most owners will ignore the letter when it arrives on November 23.[1][2]

One of those eleven complaints deserves a longer look. Filed March 18, 2026, it comes from a 2022 Maverick Hybrid owner who describes a complete electrical failure at about 15,933 miles, followed by a no-start condition, with no warning beforehand. A dealer found voltage was not reaching the Body Control Module because of a fault in the battery cable and wiring system. Seven months before the recall, one owner had already lived through the exact failure Ford would later describe in federal paperwork, and the truck gave him nothing. No light, no chime, no warning of any kind.[3]

Context makes this recall bigger than its truck count. Five earlier campaigns on the same model year already dealt with the 12-volt battery or the software watching it, from the 24V267 software fix through the 25V019 battery replacement, which covered 272,817 vehicles after Ford admitted its first fix could not detect failing batteries fast enough. Add this one and the 2022 Maverick has now needed six separate 12-volt actions, on a truck that already carries 28 recall campaigns in total. Bad batteries, blind software, a drain that took Ford two years and eight service messages to chase down, and now a weld eaten by its own solder bath. A system that fails six different ways is not having bad luck.[3][4]

Do not wait for the envelope. VINs have been searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls since November 9, a full two weeks before the letters go out, and the fix is a free cable replacement at a dealer, not an over-the-air update. Call Ford at 1-866-436-7332 and quote recall 26S81, or book with a dealer and ask for the work under that number. Gas Mavericks and 2023-and-later hybrids are not in this campaign. If your truck ever lost all its electricals with no warning, file a Vehicle Owner Questionnaire with NHTSA at 1-888-327-4236, because eleven of those complaints are what opened this investigation. And if you already paid a shop to fix a loose or damaged battery cable on a 2022 Maverick Hybrid, keep the invoice and ask Ford whether it qualifies for reimbursement, since the public record carries no sentence on that point.[2][3]

Two things this article did not prove. First, whether the replacement cable fixes the chemistry or merely repeats it: the filing never says if the new eyelet is redesigned or simply re-soldered under a corrected process, so owners getting the fix cannot tell from the paperwork whether the problem is engineered out. Second, how often the failure actually strikes at speed: the filing gives no per-claim detail on the eleven complaints, no speeds, no road types, and the 1 percent estimate is Ford's own, derived from supplier records it did not publish. Eight thousand six hundred fifty trucks is a small recall, and zero known accidents is a real number. A skeptic could call this a footnote. That skeptic would be right about the scale, and wrong about the point, because the point was never the count. It was that a weld can be killed by the bath that built it, and a truck can lose everything, silently, from a part most owners will never see.
