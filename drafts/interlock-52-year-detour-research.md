# Research: The 52-Year Seatbelt Interlock Detour

## Angle
America mandated seatbelt interlocks in 1973, then Congress killed them in 1974 after public backlash. 52 years later, GM is the only automaker voluntarily re-introducing them as "Buckle to Drive." Meanwhile, 48% of all passenger vehicle occupants killed in 2024 — roughly 10,900 people — were unrestrained. Original calculation: universal interlocks could save 2,400–6,500 lives per year.

## Key Sources

### 1. NHTSA 2024 FARS (released April 2026)
- 39,254 people died in traffic crashes in 2024
- 22,713 passenger vehicle occupants killed
- **48% of those killed were unrestrained** (based on known belt use)
- Fatality rate: 1.19/100M VMT
- Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/traffic-deaths-2025-early-estimates-2024-annual

### 2. Seatbelt Effectiveness (NHTSA Seat Belt Safety page)
- Front-seat passenger car: reduce fatal injury risk by **45%** (Kahane, 2015)
- Front-seat passenger car: reduce moderate-critical injury by **50%**
- Light truck occupants: reduce fatal injury risk by **60%**
- Light truck occupants: reduce moderate-critical injury by **65%**
- 77% of totally ejected vehicle occupants were killed
- Only 1% of restrained occupants were totally ejected vs 30% of unrestrained
- Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/seat-belts

### 3. 1973 Interlock Mandate (History.com + Congressional records)
- 1973: NHTSA required all 1974-model vehicles to have starter interlock (couldn't start without belts)
- Interlock contributed to ~7× increase in seatbelt use
- Congress received MORE complaint letters about interlocks than about Nixon's Saturday Night Massacre
- 1974: Congress killed interlock mandate (Motor Vehicle and Schoolbus Safety Amendments, P.L. 93-492)
- Also mandated buzzer limited to 8 seconds
- Sponsor: Rep. Louis Wyman (R-NH) and many others (at least 8 bills introduced)
- Source: https://www.history.com/articles/seat-belt-laws-resistance

### 4. GM Buckle to Drive (GM press releases)
- **2020:** Introduced on Colorado, Traverse, Malibu, Canyon (Teen Driver mode)
- **2022:** Expanded to Silverado, Sierra, Hummer
- **2024-2026:** Standard on most GM models
- Mechanism: prevents shifting out of Park for 20 seconds unless driver buckles up
- Some models also require front passenger to buckle
- Can be turned off through Settings menu (not a true interlock)
- Teen Driver mode makes it mandatory
- Source: https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/topic/us/en/2025/nov/1119-Holiday-road-safety-click.html

### 5. Seatbelt Usage Rates (NSC + NHTSA)
- National front-seat usage: ~91% (2024)
- Rear seat usage: "far lower" (no precise number from GM)
- Pickup truck rear seat: 70% of killed occupants were unrestrained
- SUV rear seat: 63% of killed occupants were unrestrained
- Van: 57% unrestrained
- Passenger car: 50% unrestrained
- New Hampshire: only state with no seatbelt law (78.2% usage, 59% of deaths unrestrained)
- California: primary law, 97.5% usage, 43% of deaths unrestrained
- Source: https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/occupant-protection/seat-belts/data-details/

### 6. Pickup Truck Owners Push Back
- Truck owners argue: farm use, trailer backing, short-distance maneuvers don't need belts
- Pickup trucks have highest unrestrained death rate in FARS
- Source: pickuptrucktalk.com reaction to Buckle to Drive

## Original Calculation

### Deaths preventable by universal seatbelt interlocks:
- 2024: 22,713 passenger vehicle occupants killed
- 48% unrestrained = ~10,902 unrestrained deaths
- Seatbelt effectiveness: 45% (car) to 60% (truck) fatality reduction

**If interlocks forced 100% compliance:**
- Low: 10,902 × 0.45 = 4,906 lives saved
- High: 10,902 × 0.60 = 6,541 lives saved

**If interlocks achieve 50% additional compliance (realistic — some bypass):**
- Low: 5,451 × 0.45 = 2,453 lives saved
- High: 5,451 × 0.60 = 3,271 lives saved

**Cumulative cost (1975-2024, 50 years):**
- Total US traffic deaths 1975-2024: approximately 2.0 million
- Average unrestrained fraction: ~45-55% → ~900,000-1,100,000 unrestrained deaths
- At 45% effectiveness: 405,000-495,000 deaths potentially preventable
- Even at 25% additional compliance × 45% effectiveness = ~100,000-125,000 lives

### The 52-Year Body Count
If universal interlocks saved just 2,500 lives/year for 50 years = **125,000 lives** lost because Congress preferred constituent mail volume over crash data.
