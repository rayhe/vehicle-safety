# Research — #1433: Denton County's Deaths Jumped 61% While Its Neighbors' Fell

**Slug:** `1433-denton-county-deaths-up-61-percent-neighbors-fall`
**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (Data Visualization Editor; county-level statistical comparison is his beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date:** November 8, 2026

## Story in one line
Denton County, Texas recorded 50 traffic deaths in the first nine months of 2026, up 61% from 31 a year earlier, while crashes barely moved (+0.5%) and the two counties next door both got safer. The crashes didn't get more frequent. They got more lethal.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** YES. Fresh data (TxDOT CRIS exports processed October 15-16, 2026; analysis published late October 2026), a 61% death surge in a major metro county, and a clean divergence against two adjacent counties measured the same way, in the same window, from the same database.
- **Novel angle?** YES. Full queue grep: zero slugs contain "denton", "collin", "tarrant", or "dfw". No prior article has used the TxDOT CRIS county series. The novel contribution is the lethality arithmetic (deaths per 1,000 crashes) plus the three-county divergence table — the same metro, the same nine months, three different directions.
- **3+ primary sources?** YES. (1) TxDOT Crash Records Information System (CRIS) query exports, via the Wooley Law Firm's published county analyses, which reproduce the CRIS figures with quarterly breakdowns; (2) National Safety Council H1 2026 preliminary estimate (18,460 deaths, -2%); (3) NHTSA Q1 2026 early estimate, DOT HS 813 833 (7,770 deaths, -4.3%, rate 0.99).
- **Actionable?** YES. Denton County drivers: I-35E alone logged 1,503 crashes in nine months; Q1 (Jan-Mar) was the deadliest quarter despite the fewest crashes; the fatal-crash rate per crash rose ~60%, which is a severity problem, not a volume problem — seat belts, speed, and impairment are the levers that decide whether a crash kills.

## Facts (verified November 8, 2026)

### Denton County, Jan 1–Sep 30, 2026 vs same period 2025 (TxDOT CRIS)
| Measure | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported crashes | 10,029 | 10,075 | +0.5% (+46) |
| People injured | 3,918 | 3,999 | +2.1% (+81) |
| Suspected serious injuries | 223 | 235 | +5.4% (+12) |
| Traffic deaths | 31 | 50 | **+61.3% (+19)** |
| Fatal crashes | 31 | 49 | +58.1% (+18) |

- 49 fatal crashes produced 50 deaths: all but one were single-fatality crashes.
- County averaged ~37 crashes and ~15 injured people per day over the 273 days.
- Records processed through October 15, 2026 (2026 file) and October 16, 2026 (2025 file).

### Deaths by quarter (Denton)
| Quarter | Crashes | Injured | Serious | Deaths 2026 | Deaths 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (Jan–Mar) | 3,164 | 1,173 | 69 | **20** (19 fatal crashes) | 6 |
| Q2 (Apr–Jun) | 3,381 | 1,306 | 72 | 15 | 13 |
| Q3 (Jul–Sep) | 3,530 | 1,520 | 94 | 15 | 12 |

- Q1 had the **fewest** crashes and injured people of any quarter, and the **most** deaths. Q1 2026 deaths were +233% vs Q1 2025 (+14 of the +19 annual increase).
- Q3 had the highest crash, injury, and serious-injury totals; deaths flat vs Q2 at 15.
- Q3 vs Q3 2025: crashes 3,448 → 3,530 (+2.4%), injured 1,390 → 1,520 (+9.4%), serious 76 → 94 (+23.7%), deaths 12 → 15 (+3).

### Where in the county
- City of Denton: 2,320 crashes (+9.5% vs 2,119), ~23% of county crashes. Lewisville: 1,909 (-7.4% vs 2,062). Together ~42%.
- Fastest-growing crash counts: Fort Worth's Denton County portion 155 → 238 (+53.5%), Sanger 155 → 208 (+34.2%), Northlake 225 → 291 (+29.3%), Flower Mound 538 → 568 (+5.6%).
- Busiest roadway labels: I-35E 1,503 crashes (-7.7% vs 1,629); US-380 415; SH-121 398; I-35 367 (+6.1%); US-377 319; I-35W 282 (+14.2%); FM-423 266; SH-114 225; Business SH-121H 219; FM-407 212. Loop 288 134 → 164 (+22.4%); FM-156 100 → 121 (+21%).

### Commercial vehicles are not the story (Denton)
- CMV crashes: 770 → 814 (+5.7%). CMV injuries: 239 → 226 (-5.4%). CMV serious injuries: 25 → 17 (-32%). CMV deaths: 4 → 3.
- The death surge is in non-commercial crashes: 27 → 47 non-CMV deaths (+74%).

### The neighbors, same window, same database
| County | Crashes 2026 | Deaths 2025 → 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Denton | 10,075 (+0.5%) | 31 → 50 | **+61.3%** |
| Tarrant | 24,868 (+0.8%) | 151 → 142 | -6.0% (-9) |
| Collin | 12,148 (-1.5%) | 52 → 49 | -5.8% (-3) |

- Tarrant: 12,495 injured (+3.7%); CMV deaths rose 13 → 18 even as overall deaths fell.
- Collin: 5,462 injured (-9.2%); suspected serious injuries 267 → 329 (+23.2%) even as deaths fell.
- Three-county combined deaths: 234 → 241 (+7). Denton's +19 exceeds its neighbors' combined -12.

### Statewide context (TxDOT CRIS, same series)
- Texas commercial-vehicle crashes Jan–Sep 2026: 32,983 (+0.5%); CMV deaths 406 → 439 (+8.1%).
- Texas September 2026 alone: 50,048 crashes, 20,951 injuries, 298 deaths (~9.9/day, up from 9.5/day in August).

### National context
- NSC H1 2026 preliminary: 18,460 deaths, -2% vs H1 2025; mileage death rate 1.12 (-3.4%). The national improvement rate collapsed from -13% a year earlier (#1402).
- NHTSA Q1 2026 early estimate (DOT HS 813 833): 7,770 deaths (-4.3%), rate 0.99, 16th consecutive quarterly decline; 30 states + Puerto Rico down in Q1.
- NHTSA has not published an H1 2026 early estimate as of November 8, 2026; the Q1 report promised H1 projections "in late September."

## Original contribution (Axle's math, show work)
1. **Lethality per 1,000 crashes, Denton:** 2025: 31 / 10,029 x 1,000 = **3.09**. 2026: 50 / 10,075 x 1,000 = **4.96**. Change: **+60.5%**. Crashes were flat; each crash became ~60% more likely to kill. This is the article's core number: a severity story, not a volume story.
2. **Fatal-crash share:** 2025: 31 / 10,029 = 0.31% of crashes fatal. 2026: 49 / 10,075 = 0.49%. The chance any given crash was fatal rose by more than half.
3. **Q1 lethality:** Q1 2026: 20 / 3,164 x 1,000 = **6.32** deaths per 1,000 crashes. Q3 2026: 15 / 3,530 x 1,000 = **4.25**. Q1 crashes were ~49% more lethal per crash than Q3's, despite Q1 having the fewest crashes of any quarter. Winter-quarter severity, not volume, drove the year.
4. **Non-commercial death surge:** total deaths 31 → 50 (+19); CMV deaths 4 → 3 (-1). Non-CMV deaths: 27 → 47 = **+74.1%**. Trucks are exonerated in Denton; passenger-vehicle crashes did the killing.
5. **Three-county divergence:** Denton +19 vs Tarrant -9 vs Collin -3. Denton alone added more deaths than its two neighbors combined saved. Combined three-county deaths rose only 234 → 241 (+3.0%) — the metro total barely moved while Denton swung hard.
6. **Deaths per day:** Denton 2026: 50 / 273 days = one death every ~5.5 days. 2025: 31 / 273 = one every ~8.8 days.

## Limitations (required)
- **Small numbers:** 19 extra deaths. A handful of bad crashes swings the percentage; 49 of 50 fatal crashes killed exactly one person, so there is no single multi-fatality event driving the total — but the base (31) is small enough that year-over-year county comparisons are noisy.
- **No causation:** CRIS records as published in this analysis do not break out speed, impairment, belt use, road type, or time of day for the fatal crashes. The data establishes that deaths rose while crashes did not; it does not establish why.
- **Not exposure-adjusted:** figures are raw counts, not rates per VMT or per capita. Denton County is growing fast (Northlake crashes +29%, Fort Worth portion +54%), so part of the crash count reflects more driving. Growth cannot explain the lethality jump, but it is a confounder for the volume figures.
- **Preliminary:** records processed through October 15-16, 2026; late-filed reports can revise counts slightly upward.
- **Source chain:** the figures come from a law firm's published analysis of TxDOT CRIS query exports, not from a TxDOT publication. The methodology (two exports, matching date ranges, same county filter) is stated in the source and the arithmetic checks out against the published tables, but this is a secondary presentation of a public primary database.

## Strongest counterargument (required)
Nineteen deaths is a small absolute number, and county-year comparisons are noisy by nature. Denton's 2025 base of 31 may simply have been a lucky year, and 2026 a reversion: Q1 2026's 20 deaths against Q1 2025's 6 looks dramatic, but one bad winter weekend can add several fatal crashes to a quarter. The neighbors' declines (Tarrant -9, Collin -3) are equally small in absolute terms and could be noise in the other direction. The honest reading: Denton's 2026 is a real, verified increase in the public record, the lethality-per-crash math is robust to the volume confound, but a single nine-month window cannot distinguish a deteriorating county from a bad draw. The next two quarters decide which it is.
