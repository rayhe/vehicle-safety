# Research: Ford's Backup Camera — Six Recalls in Six Years, $165M Fine, Still Not Fixed

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Ford has recalled its rearview camera in six separate NHTSA campaigns since 2020, covering more than 3.3 million vehicles, paid the second-largest civil penalty in NHTSA history for dragging its feet on the first one, and in October 2026 NHTSA opened a formal query into whether one of the fixes even worked.

## Verified facts (primary sources)

### The six campaigns
| Campaign | Date | Vehicles | Defect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20V575 | Sep 2020 | 620,246 | Faulty rearview cameras, 2020 F-Series etc. |
| 2022 + 2024 expansions of 20V575 | 2022, 2024 | ~24,000 | Same problem, added vehicles |
| 24V684 (Ford 24S59) | Sep 2024 | 144,516 | Maverick: CTR memory leak freezes camera image; Ford aware of 2 accidents, 0 injuries |
| 25V270 (Ford 25S42) | Apr 2025 | 160,729 | Loose/damaged PCB terminal header connector, blank/distorted image; expands 22V082 |
| 25V315 (Ford 25S49) | May 2025 | 1,075,299 | SYNC 4 APIM software v1.7-1.9: image delays, freezes, or never displays; F-150 alone 527,371 |
| 25V572 (Ford 25S89) | Sep 2025 | 1,456,417 | Expansion of 25V270: analog cameras go blank, distorted, or inverted; 44,123 global warranty claims, 195 US VOQs, 18 minor crashes, 0 injuries; defect rate 2.7% |
| 26V489 (Ford 26C37) | Jul 2026 | 47,587 | Camera "Views" menu overlay doesn't auto-dismiss in Reverse, partially covers test objects; FMVSS 111; APIM software before v2.4.12; only 8-inch SYNC screens |

### Tally math (original)
Counting each expansion once (25V572 supersedes 25V270; the 2022/2024 additions fold into the 2020 campaign):
620,246 + 24,000 + 144,516 + 1,075,299 + 1,456,417 + 47,587 = **3,368,065 campaign vehicles**.
Caveat stated in article: campaign populations overlap (same truck can appear in two campaigns), so this is a count of recall actions, not unique vehicles.

### The $165 million fine
- NHTSA consent order announced **Nov 14, 2024**: $165M civil penalty, second-largest in NHTSA's 54-year history (only Takata's $200M airbag order is bigger).
- Breakdown: $65M upfront, $55M deferred, $45M for performance obligations.
- Findings: Ford failed to recall vehicles with defective rearview cameras in a timely manner, gave NHTSA inaccurate/incomplete recall information, failed to submit quarterly recall reports on time.
- Terms: 3-year consent order (extendable to a 4th year), independent third-party monitor, quarterly Ford-NHTSA meetings, Ford must review **all recalls issued over the last three years** and file new recalls if scoping was wrong.
- The 2020 recall at the center: 620,246 vehicles in the US (700,000+ in North America); NHTSA opened its investigation Aug 2021, about a year after the recall.
- Fine arithmetic: $165,000,000 / 620,246 = **~$266 per vehicle** in the original recall.

### The fix that may not have fixed it
- **RQ26004**, opened Oct 23, 2026: NHTSA recall query into 24V684. Five owner complaints (VOQs) allege frozen camera images *after* the recall remedy was performed. Population 273,301 (2022-2024 Maverick/Maverick Hybrid with Connected Touch Radio). No crashes in the query's five-report summary. A recall query can trigger mandatory recall authority if the prior remedy is found insufficient.
- Post-repair complaints were on file as early as April-May 2025, about 18 months before the query opened.

### After the consent order
- Ford filed three more camera campaigns after the Nov 2024 consent order (25V315, 25V572, 26V489) plus the RQ26004 query.
- **Customer Satisfaction Program 25N17** (March 2026, Ford service bulletin): no-cost, one-time rearview camera repair for 15 years from warranty start, **12,554,966 vehicles** (2015-2025 model years, multiple lines). This is a service program, NOT a safety recall — but it shows the scale of the camera problem.
- Ford's 2026 recall count: 63 recalls by early August 2026, more than double the next-highest automaker (per industry tracking cited Aug 3, 2026).

### Why the camera matters (backover context)
- NHTSA's FMVSS 111 final rule (Mar 2014, Cameron Gulbransen Kids Transportation Safety Act of 2007): backover crashes killed an average of **~210 people and injured ~15,000 per year** before cameras were required. Children under 5 were **31%** of backover deaths; adults 70+ were **26%**.
- Rule expected to save **58-69 lives per year** once the fleet was equipped. Cameras mandatory on all new vehicles under 10,000 lbs from May 1, 2018; must show a 10-by-20-foot zone behind the vehicle.

## Limitations / counterargument
- Campaign populations overlap: the same F-150 can appear in 25V315 and 26V489, so 3.37M is recall actions, not unique vehicles. Stated explicitly.
- The $165M fine covered conduct around the 2020 recall, not the later campaigns; the later recalls are not evidence Ford violated the consent order. The irony is noted, not overstated.
- RQ26004 is a query, not a finding: five post-repair complaints against a 273,301 population. No crashes in the query summary.
- 25V572's 2.7% defect estimate means ~39,000 of 1.45M vehicles actually had the defect — most recalled vehicles were fine.
- The 25N17 program (12.5M vehicles) is a customer-satisfaction action, not a safety recall; do not present it as one.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2015-2019 Ford/Lincoln? Check 25V572 (analog camera). Own a 2021-2025 Ford/Lincoln with SYNC 4? Check 25V315 — the fix is an over-the-air update, no dealer visit needed.
- Maverick 2022-2024 owners: the 2024 camera fix is now under federal review (RQ26004). If your camera still freezes after the recall repair, file a VOQ at nhtsa.gov — five complaints opened this query.
- Any Ford owner: check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. The camera is the single most-recalled component in Ford's modern history.
