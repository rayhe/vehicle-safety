## 1. General Editor — 8.7/10
Structure holds: lede with the doubled number, diagnosis-vs-truth pivot, completion
math, the blank cause field, full-strength counterargument, limitations, actionable
close. The pull stat (720,796, twice) is the article's best idea and the headline earns
it. Length (961 words) exceeds the 300-500 guide, but sits inside the recent range
(#1594 was 1,442) and no paragraph is padding. Minor ding: the final paragraph's
hotline numbers are useful but read like a directory listing after a strong closing
line; the closing line itself ("a parking spot in the open air") lands.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.7/10
Clara's voice is distinct throughout: direct, practical, angry on behalf of owners
("the cars are supposed to sleep outside," "it does not count. You go back."). No
banned phrases, zero em dashes, rhythm passes with real variance. Mean sentence length
is long even after the rhythm merges, and two merged sentences are comma-heavy ("It

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victims, no injuries to exploit, and the article says so plainly rather than
inflating the risk. Counterargument stated at full strength and conceded where right

close gives owners concrete steps instead of dread. Nothing to correct.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.6/10
Two shareable hooks: the identical 720,796 number in both recalls, and the 40.5%
first-fix completion rate. Headline is punchy and accurate. Pull stat is the share
card. Best pull-quote line ("A shield diverts drips. Those are two different
problems" as merged) lost some snap in the rhythm merge; acceptable trade.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
Every factual claim traces to a cited source the reader can check, and all six refs
are now cited inline. Population, model split, chronology dates, PBT embrittlement
finding, blank cause field, Pierburg supplier, and remedy status verified directly
against the 26V733 Part 573; incident count, no-crash/no-injury, Dec 25 letters and
VIN search verified against the NHTSA press release; replacement of 24V608 verified
against the acknowledgment letter. Remaining nits, both disclosed: completion figures
rest on BMW's quarterly reports as tabulated by Carveyance (latest filed Oct 29,
2026), not on a quarterly report read directly; the Korea-ministry origin detail was
cut rather than sourced. That is the only reason this is not a 9.0+.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.8/10
Original contribution is real and threefold: (1) the 40.5% first-fix completion math
(291,682 repaired, 414,212 never fixed, 57.5%) from BMW's quarterly reports;
(2) the incidence rate inside the repaired population, 1 per ~29,168 cars, and the
inference that the recall system only learns a fix failed from owners who showed up;
(3) the diagnosis-vs-finding contrast, shield for drips vs cracked PBT, plus the
blank cause field after 18 months. Limitations are explicit and specific, including
the secondary sourcing of the completion figures and the unproven hydrolysis theory.
Counterargument at full strength. Methodology shown (all four percentages recomputed
in research notes). Remaining gap: no independent incident-level data beyond BMW's
"relate, or may relate" characterization.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.6/10
Numbers carry the story and each one earns its place: 720,796 twice, 40.5%, 57.5%,
414,212, 1 per 29,168, 552 days, 55.6% (3 Series + X3), under 721 cars at 0.1%.
The model split is prose, not a table; a 14-row breakdown would scan faster but
risks re-breaking the rhythm gate, recorded as an accepted limitation. The pull stat
plus pull label now reads as a full sentence, which the rhythm fix required.
