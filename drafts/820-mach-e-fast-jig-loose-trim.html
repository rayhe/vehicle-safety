Ford Sped Up the Assembly Line. The Glue Never Got the Memo.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
September 4, 2026

In February 2023, a supplier bolted a new assembly jig onto the Mustang Mach-E quarter window line in Mexico. It did exactly what it was designed to do: install the trim faster. **What nobody recalculated was the chemistry, and now 86,543 Mach-Es are under federal recall because their rear window trim can detach at highway speed.**[1]

22 months
Between the day the supplier fixed the assembly line and the day Ford told owners

Adhesive primer is not decorative. The trim piece around the Mach-E's rear quarter glass is bonded with a primer that needs cure time, and cure time is not negotiable. Ford's own 573 report says the faster jig cut installation time so aggressively that the primer never finished curing. The bond was insufficient from the first vehicle off the new jig. Every Mach-E built between February 2023 and October 2024 with that trim is suspect.[1]

Here is the part that should bother you. The supplier figured this out and fixed it on September 28, 2024, by adding a timer that blocks trim installation until 30 seconds have elapsed, which is the entire engineering delta between a bonded trim piece and a flying one. But Ford's Field Review Committee did not approve the field action until July 21, 2026, almost two years after the line was fixed and three years after the first warranty claim arrived in July 2023. In between, 219 owners reported loose or detached trim. Nobody died, which is luck rather than design.

And it is not just a cosmetic panel flapping in the wind. When Ford handed a trim piece to NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation in June 2026, the agency's engineers looked at the sharpness of the trim edges and concluded it could present a hazard to other road users, which is to say the motorcyclist behind you.[1]

To be fair to Ford, and I am rarely feeling generous: the company traced the defect to specific production windows using supplier process records, estimated only 1 percent of the 86,543 vehicles are actually affected, and found the problem through its own warranty-claim surveillance. The observed rate in warranty data is even lower, about 0.25 percent. This is how the system is supposed to work, just slower than anyone would like. The investigation that started in May 2026 genuinely needed months of field-performance analysis.

What I cannot forgive is the physics of it. Somewhere a process engineer celebrated a cycle-time reduction, and nobody in the room asked what the primer needed, because every adhesive has a cure schedule and every cure schedule has a clock. You do not get to vote on chemistry.

If you drive a 2023 through 2025 Mach-E: your VIN becomes searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls on September 7, owner letters go out September 10 through 14, and dealers will inspect or replace the trim free. Listen for the warning signs Ford lists in the report. Wind noise that was not there before, a squeak, a rattle, a trim piece that looks less than fully seated. And if you see a loose black strip on the highway, give it room. It might be some Mach-E's quarter window trim, finally free.[2]
