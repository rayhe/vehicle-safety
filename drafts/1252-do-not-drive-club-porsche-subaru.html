Do Not Drive: Porsche Just Parked 548 Supercars, Subaru Just Parked 8

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
December 16, 2027

There is exactly one sentence the auto industry deploys when a car might kill you in motion: do not drive it. This week it got used twice, on populations 68.5 times apart in size. Porsche told owners of 548 center-lock 911s and 718s to park them because their wheels could detach; Subaru told owners of 8 Crosstreks and Foresters to park them because a front ball joint can separate from the control arm.[1][3]

556
vehicles carrying the industry’s scariest warning this week: do not drive

Porsche’s recall adds 548 cars to the 1,851 already recalled in 2024: 2024 911 GT3, GT3 RS, Turbo, Turbo S, S/T, Carrera GTS, plus 718 Cayman GT4 RS and Spyder RS, all with center-lock wheels. The defect report says certain center-lock wheel fastening components may have been manufactured out of specification and could fracture, which in the worst case means a wheel leaves the car. The expansion came after two field incidents in May and August 2026 involving fractured center-lock hardware, followed by a supply-chain review that found unrecorded parts transfers from the same out-of-specification batch. Owner letters go out December 15, and the federal advice until the inspection is the sentence: park the car.[2]

Subaru’s recall covers eight vehicles. Not eight hundred: eight. On certain 2026 Crosstreks and Foresters, the castle nut on the left-front lower control arm ball joint may be improperly fastened, and if the nut walks off, the ball joint separates from the control arm and the driver loses control of that corner of the car. Dealers will inspect and replace the assembly free of charge, and owner letters go out December 16. A defect this small with a warning this severe is what happens when the failure mode is binary: either the nut is torqued or the wheel folds.[3]

Run the numbers and you get the shape of the threshold. Porsche’s affected population is 68.5 times Subaru’s, and the failure is a supplier materials problem traced through a batch, while Subaru’s is an assembly torque problem traced to a single fastener on eight cars. Population size is not what earns the warning. The warning goes to a failure mode where the next drive could be the event, with no intermediate symptom an owner can monitor. Neither filing reports injuries or crashes tied to these defects, which is worth saying plainly, because the warning is precautionary, not a body count. Both defects are fasteners, and that shared detail is worth more than the contrast: a lug system and a castle nut. Seventy years of automotive engineering, and the thing that gets 556 cars parked in a single week is metal bits holding other metal bits.

What you do depends on which garage you are in. If you own a 2024 911 GT3, GT3 RS, Turbo, Turbo S, S/T, Carrera GTS, or a 718 Cayman GT4 RS or Spyder RS with center-lock wheels: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, and if your car is in the population, do not drive it to the dealer, call for a flatbed. If you own a 2026 Crosstrek or Forester: the same VIN check applies, and the same flatbed advice, because the defect is small but the consequence is a separated ball joint. Boring fixes beat dramatic failures: the drill bit, the inspection, the flatbed.[4]
