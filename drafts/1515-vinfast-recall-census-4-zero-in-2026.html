VinFast Has Filed 4 Recalls in Its Entire US History. One of Them Steered the Car Itself

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 15, 2026

I ran the census on every VinFast sold in America. Four models on VPIC’s 2026 list, ten queries against NHTSA’s recalls API, every result cross-checked against two independent aggregators, and the entire US recall history of VinFast fits in four rows: 4 campaigns, all on the VF 8, zero filed in 2026. Smallest census in the series. Also the only one where the signature defect was a safety system doing the exact opposite of its job.[1]

6,314
VF 8s recalled because the lane-keep assist could activate in wide turns and steer hard enough that drivers struggled to override it

Recall 25V-559, filed August 28, 2025, is the one that matters. NHTSA opened its investigation, PE24025, in September 2024 after 14 owners reported the lane-keep system yanking the wheel in sweeping turns, sometimes toward walls, sometimes after the driver had switched the system off. One owner told the agency it happened around 30 times. By the time the investigation closed, 17 complaints were on file, and NHTSA’s Vehicle Research and Test Center had done something unusual: it reproduced the defect itself, driving a 2024 VF 8 until the system misbehaved on command.[2] Regulators do not usually have to test-drive a defect into existence. VinFast blamed torque-ratio tuning across the driver-assist system. Fix is a software update, free, over the air.

Buried in the closing resume is the part nobody should skim past. Investigators reviewed a fatal crash, VOQ #11585817, a VF 8 that left the roadway at high speed. ODI found the crash conditions did not match what its test center had reproduced, and found no evidence linking the crash to the lane-keep behavior. It also could not rule the link out, because the car’s event data recorder was destroyed by fire in the collision, and the data it held is gone.[2] Four people died in that crash, according to CarComplaints’ reporting, and the question of whether the car steered itself into it is now permanently unanswerable.[3] ODI says it will keep monitoring. Monitoring is what you do when the black box is ash.

Everything else in the census is small and ordinary. Recall 23V-355, May 2023, updated display software in 999 cars. Recall 24V-369, May 2024, replaced airbags with inactive ignitors in 225 cars. Recall 24V-933, December 2024, replaced a driver center airbag that could strike the driver’s arm in 4,888 cars.[4] Add them up and the campaign-vehicle incidences total 12,426, but the same early VF 8s appear in several campaigns at once, so that number is not a count of distinct cars. VinFast sold so few cars here that the 6,314-car steering recall covered effectively every VF 8 it had delivered by then. Four recalls, one car, and the car in question is the only one that ever mattered.

One more wrinkle, and it is a border. Transport Canada recalled the same 2023–2025 VF 8 in January 2026, recall 2026038, because a software problem could release the electronic parking pawl on its own while the parking-brake wiring harness corroded, which together mean the car can roll away while parked.[5] No US campaign covers that defect as of today. Same cars, same parking brake, one side of the border recalled and the other not. VinFast’s American recall count for 2026 is zero either way, and that zero is doing a lot of work it does not deserve.

Consider the denominator, because it explains the whole census. VinFast sold fewer than 400 cars in the United States in the first half of 2026, and 389 were registered from January through July.[6] In October, Green SM, a ride-hailing company owned by Vingroup, the same conglomerate that owns VinFast, put 500 VF 8s to work as taxis in Los Angeles. Read that again: the taxi fleet is bigger than the brand’s retail sales. VinFast has launched a 2027 VF 8 at $40,400 with an updated driver-assistance suite, which is the car the brand is betting on, and the bet is being placed by a sister company buying its own cars to drive strangers around in them. A brand can have a perfect recall record in 2026 by the simple expedient of having almost no customers.

Fair objection: four recalls is the smallest number in the series, and zero in 2026 looks like a clean bill of health, and the count is mostly a function of a tiny denominator. That reading is not wrong. But it misses the shape of the four. Two are airbags, one is a display, and one is a driver-assist system that grabbed the steering wheel, and that last one is the only defect in the series that NHTSA’s own test center had to drive a car to reproduce. A small census with a steering defect in it is not a clean bill of health. It is a small brand whose worst problem was the feature it advertised as the future, and whose answer to slow sales was to sell the cars to itself.

What this census does not prove: a campaign count says nothing about injury rates, and with 67 owner complaints on file for the VF 8, the complaint base is too thin for any rate calculation worth printing. NHTSA’s recalls API does not return 25V-559 for any model-year query, a gap documented in the research file, so any future census that trusts the API alone will silently lose the most important campaign. The four-fatality figure for the VOQ #11585817 crash comes from CarComplaints; ODI’s resume calls it a fatal crash without a death count. No unit count was available for the Canadian parking-pawl recall. Campaigns filed after November 15 are not counted.

If you own a 2023–2025 VF 8, all four recalls apply to your car, and all four have remedies available. Get the 25V-559 software update first: if your lane-keep assist has ever yanked the wheel in a sweeping turn, that update is the difference between a system that behaves and one that fights you. Check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; the fix is free and over the air. If you park on a slope, mind the Canadian recall: use the parking brake every time, because the parking-pawl defect has a US campaign nowhere in sight. If you ride in a Green SM taxi in Los Angeles, you are riding in a 2023–2025 VF 8, the exact population of all four recalls, and the update is what stands between you and a steering wheel with opinions. And if you are shopping, remember that the VF 9, VF 7, and VF 6 have zero recalls and almost no American sales history to judge by. Zero recalls on a car almost nobody bought is not a safety rating. It is a rounding error with a badge on it.
