# Research: #863 — The Pedestrian Test That Got Delayed a Year

Journalist: Clara Rollover | Kicker: The Gap | Date: 2026-09-09

## Angle (1-2 sentences)

In December 2024, NHTSA finalized the biggest overhaul of the 5-Star Safety Ratings since 1978, adding pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping, blind-spot tech, and — critically — a crashworthiness pedestrian protection program that would grade how a vehicle's front end treats people it hits. In September 2025, after the Alliance for Automotive Innovation lobbied for more time, NHTSA pushed the whole package to model year 2027 — and the part of the rule that was explicitly the reason for the delay was the pedestrian test procedures. Meanwhile pedestrian deaths ran 7,080 in 2024, still 18% of all traffic fatalities.

## Kill test

- Newsworthy: YES. The NCAP overhaul is the first major change to federal ratings since 1978; the pedestrian portion is the part written in response to the pedestrian-death crisis; the delay (Sept 2025, Autoblog/Reuters) got almost no consumer-press follow-up; a GCN reframe (Aug 2026) and GHSA's March 2026 pedestrian report make the "was the delay worth it" question fresh again. Zero prior coverage on vehicle-safety.org (no ncap/delay/pedestrian-protection slug exists).
- Novel: YES. Site has no NCAP coverage at all. Original contributions: (1) 2015-2024 pedestrian death arithmetic from NHTSA's own fact sheet (DOT HS 813 818, June 2026); (2) composition analysis of NHTSA's MY2026 crash-test list (29/38 SUVs, 2 sedans); (3) the delay-mechanics framing — the Alliance's stated reason for the delay was literally the missing pedestrian test procedures, i.e., the pedestrian-protection half of the rule is what bought the delay, while the 5-star occupant ratings consumers actually shop by were never the problem.

## The facts

### NCAP finalization (Dec 3, 2024)
- NHTSA published a final decision notice: adds four ADAS to NCAP (pedestrian automatic emergency braking, lane keeping assist, blind spot warning, blind spot intervention), enhances performance evaluation of existing ADAS, finalizes a 10-year roadmap for 2024-2033. Responds to Section 24213 of the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law.
- DOT's announcement added the crashworthiness pedestrian protection program: evaluates the ability of a vehicle's front end to mitigate pedestrian injuries and fatalities in vehicle-to-pedestrian impacts.
- Fast Company (Dec 2024): the 5-star crash-test ratings themselves would not change; consumers would see green check marks on NHTSA's site for the ADAS features; early pass/fail, scored comparisons later. Buttigieg: the evaluation should include "how the design of a vehicle could prevent a crash or make it less fatal for someone outside the vehicle."

### The delay (Sept 23, 2025, Autoblog citing Reuters)
- NHTSA pushed the NCAP updates from MY2026 to MY2027 after automakers said they needed more time.
- The Alliance for Automotive Innovation pushed back in April 2025, claiming NHTSA hadn't published updated pedestrian crash test procedures in time for the 2026 rollout, and that few vehicles would qualify for a five-star rating as 2026 models. (Under NCAP, automakers conduct their own tests to NHTSA-specified procedures and report results.)
- NHTSA to Reuters: a delay would "provide industry additional time to implement these technologies," while still implementing the requirements.
- Same piece: NHTSA plans minimum performance standards for AEB by calendar 2029 — systems must avoid frontal collisions at speeds up to 62 mph. Automakers have also sought to push that back.

### The Model Y "first" (May 2026, carscoops; GCN Aug 2026 reframe)
- The 2026 Model Y became the first vehicle to pass the new ADAS tests: all 4 new categories plus the 4 original (forward collision warning, crash imminent braking, dynamic brake support, lane departure warning). Vehicles built on/after Nov 12, 2025 hold a 5-star crashworthiness rating (frontal, side, rollover aced).
- The ADAS evaluations are pass/fail checkmarks and do NOT affect the 5-star rating, which remains crashworthiness-based.
- NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison: "By successfully passing these new tests, the 2026 Tesla Model Y demonstrates the lifesaving potential of driver assistance technologies and sets a high bar for the industry."
- GCN's key caveat (Aug 2026): full implementation was delayed a year to MY2027 after industry lobbying, so the Model Y being "first" doesn't mean it's the only capable vehicle — it means it's the first NHTSA formally tested under the new framework.

### Pedestrian deaths (NHTSA DOT HS 813 818, "Pedestrians: 2024 Data," June 2026)
- 2024: 7,080 pedestrians killed, -3.9% from 7,367 in 2023. A pedestrian killed every 74 minutes. 18% of all traffic fatalities (39,254 total). 71,635 pedestrians injured (+5.0% from 2023).
- Trend: 2015: 5,494 (15%) -> 2016: 6,080 (16%) -> 2017: 6,075 -> 2018: 6,374 (17%) -> 2019: 6,272 -> 2020: 6,565 -> 2021: 7,470 -> 2022: 7,593 (18%, peak) -> 2023: 7,367 -> 2024: 7,080.
- Context facts from the sheet: 76% of pedestrian fatalities occurred in the dark; 84% in urban areas; 69% male; alcohol (driver and/or pedestrian) involved in 43% of fatal pedestrian crashes; 73% not at intersections; nearly 1 in 4 (24%) were hit-and-run.
- GHSA, March 25, 2026 (preliminary H1 2025): 3,024 pedestrians killed Jan-Jun 2025, -10.9% YoY — the largest decline since GHSA began tracking 15 years ago (371 fewer deaths). Still 2.5% above H1 2019 (2,951).

### NHTSA's MY2026 test list (nhtsa.gov, "2026 Model Year Vehicles Selected for Testing")
38 vehicles selected for 5-star testing. Composition: 29 SUV (76.3%), 3 pickup/crew cab (Silverado 1500, F-250, Maverick HEV), 1 cargo van (Transit Wagon), 1 passenger van (Kia Carnival), 1 station wagon (Crosstrek Hybrid), 1 hatchback (Leaf Plus), 2 sedans (Sentra, Corolla = 5.3%).

## Original computations (the novel contribution)

1. **The 2015-2022 climb:** pedestrian deaths rose 38.2% from 5,494 (2015) to 7,593 (2022), and pedestrians' share of all traffic deaths rose from 15% to 18%. The 2024 total (7,080) is 28.9% above 2015. Two straight years of decline (2023-2024) have not recovered even half the 2015-2022 increase.
2. **The test fleet is SUVs:** 29 of 38 vehicles NHTSA selected for MY2026 5-star testing are SUVs (76.3%); sedans are 2 of 38 (5.3%). The agency crashes what sells — and what sells is tall, heavy, and hard for a pedestrian to survive being hit by. (NHTSA's own pedestrian crashworthiness program is the part designed to reckon with exactly that, and it's the part that slipped to MY2027.)
3. **Delay arithmetic:** Dec 2024 final rule -> MY2027 applicability. The pedestrian procedures the Alliance said weren't ready are the same procedures that would grade front-end pedestrian injury mitigation. At 2024's rate, ~7,080 pedestrians will die in the calendar year the tougher ratings don't apply. (Descriptive, not causal — the delay didn't kill anyone; it deferred the information shoppers would have had.)

## Thesis

The five-star rating on the window sticker grades how a car protects the people inside it. The part of the 2024 overhaul that would have graded how cars treat the people outside them — pedestrian AEB performance and a front-end pedestrian crashworthiness program — was pushed to model year 2027 because the industry's lobby said the pedestrian test procedures weren't ready. The delay is real and the falling pedestrian death toll doesn't excuse it: at 7,080 deaths a year, the information shoppers were supposed to get in 2026 arrives a model year late, and the stars everyone shops by still can't see a pedestrian.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)

The delay was honest procedure, not sabotage. NHTSA itself hadn't published the updated pedestrian crash test procedures by the Alliance's April 2025 pushback — and under NCAP, automakers run the tests themselves to NHTSA's spec, so you cannot comply with a procedure that doesn't exist. Rushing it would have produced junk ratings. Meanwhile the technology isn't waiting: pedestrian AEB is already standard on the vast majority of new vehicles, IIHS already grades pedestrian front crash prevention today (the Canyon just scored Acceptable), and pedestrian deaths are genuinely falling — 2024 was the second straight annual decline and H1 2025 posted the largest drop GHSA has ever recorded. NCAP is a voluntary consumer-information program, not a mandate; a delayed checkmark made nobody less safe. The Alliance asked for a year; the agency gave a year; the ratings still arrive.

## Limitations

- The delay was reported (Autoblog via Reuters, Sept 2025) as a planned agency action; final MY2027 implementation details should be checked against NHTSA's current NCAP pages — the "delayed" framing follows the reporting, and NHTSA's own statement framed it as still implementing the requirements.
- The ~7,080/year figure is descriptive context (2024 ARF), not a causal claim about the delay.
- The MY2026 38-vehicle list is a selection for testing, not a mandate; NHTSA selects high-volume sellers, so the 76% SUV share reflects the market, not necessarily agency preference.
- FARS_BY_MODEL in the site's dataset has no pedestrian split, so model-level pedestrian fatality rates cannot be computed from this site's FARS extract; the trend numbers come from NHTSA's published fact sheet, not the site's own query.
- GCN's "artifact of the delay" reading of the Model Y "first" is one outlet's framing, cited as such.

## Actionable insights

- If you shop by stars: understand what they grade. The 5-star rating measures occupant crash protection (frontal, side, rollover). It does not grade pedestrian protection — and the ADAS checkmarks (pedestrian AEB, lane keeping, blind spot) are separate pass/fail marks that don't change the star count. Until MY2027, no federal rating tells you how a car's front end treats a pedestrian.
- The information exists anyway, just not from NHTSA: IIHS rates pedestrian front crash prevention (Good/Acceptable/Marginal/Poor) on its vehicle ratings pages. Check your model there — especially if you drive a tall SUV or pickup in a city, at night, where 76% of pedestrian deaths happen.
- If your car isn't among NHTSA's 38 selected MY2026 vehicles, there is no federal crash-test result for it at all this year — look to IIHS.
- Night driving is the exposure that matters: 76% of pedestrian deaths happen in the dark. Pedestrian AEB works; make sure yours is on (some systems let drivers disable it) and keep the windshield camera/sensor area clean.
