**Researched:** 2026-11-27 (08:30 run)

## The story in one paragraph

On November 13, 2026, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened Engineering Analysis EA26007, "No Restart After Auto Start/Stop Engages," covering an estimated 1,033,632 model-year 2024–2026 Honda Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline and Pilot vehicles plus the Acura MDX. The complaint is simple: the engine shuts off at a stop to save fuel, the driver lifts off the brake, and nothing happens. Owners filed 701 complaints. The filing lists no crashes, injuries, fires or deaths. This is the third federal probe into the same failure on Honda's V6 family haulers since 2022, and the newest cars got a case of their own because NHTSA says they appear to have different root causes and respond differently to repairs than the older ones. Honda issued fresh service bulletins (26-090 and 26-019) on October 30, 2026, two weeks before the probe opened, and told dealers there is a shortage of replacement parts.

## The three-probe timeline (original contribution: the count nobody headlines)

| Probe | Opened | Model years | Vehicles | Reports at open/close |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PE22005 (Preliminary Evaluation) | June 3, 2022 | Pilot 2016–2020 | 194,731 | 1,045 at closing |
| EA25004 (Engineering Analysis) | March 26, 2025 | Six models, 2015–2025 (Pilot, Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline, TLX, MDX) | ~2,209,466 (est.) | 1,348 at opening |
| EA26007 (Engineering Analysis) | November 13, 2026 | Five models, 2024–2026 | 1,033,632 (est.) | 701 at opening |

**Calculations (mine, from the table above):**
- Same symptom, three federal cases, **4 years 5 months** from PE22005 to EA26007.
- Complaint rate, new probe: 701 / 1,033,632 = **0.068%**, about **1 in 1,472 vehicles**.
- Complaint rate, old probe at opening: 1,348 / 2,209,466 = **0.061%**, about **1 in 1,639 vehicles**. The new cars fail at essentially the **same rate** as the old ones, despite NHTSA saying the root causes differ. The fix changed; the failure rate did not.
- Combined open-probe population: EA25004 (~2.21M) + EA26007 (~1.03M) overlap heavily. Every vehicle in EA26007 except the 2026 model year is **simultaneously in two open federal investigations into the same symptom** — a 2024 or 2025 Pilot owner is covered by both files at once.
- Earlier probe's harm record: **4 crashes, 2 with injuries** (EA25004 file, per CarBuzz). EA26007 lists none so far. The danger is not hypothetical; it is documented in the sibling file.
- Honda's 2023 fix ran in two stages and NHTSA upgraded the case anyway in March 2025 because reports kept coming, many from owners who said the countermeasures had already been done. The fix did not hold, and the agency said so in its own paperwork.

## Why this is newsworthy (kill test)

- **Novel angle:** not "another recall" — there is no recall. It is the third time the same failure has drawn a federal case, and the twist is the split: NHTSA carved the newest cars into their own investigation because they fail differently and don't respond to the old repair. A regulator admitting the fix doesn't transfer is the story.
- **Scale + who it hits:** 1.03 million vehicles, and they are the default three-row family haulers — Pilot, Odyssey, MDX. The drivers who stop at school crossings and left-turn lanes all day are exactly the ones who get stranded in one.
- **The feature fights back:** Auto Idle Stop re-enables itself every time the car is started. Owners cannot permanently switch it off. The only owner-side countermeasure is pressing the OFF button on every single drive.
- **The fix that shrinks the feature:** Honda's own bulletin admitted part of the remedy was software that makes idle-stop engage less often. The repair for the fuel-saving feature is to use it less.
- **Timely:** probe opened November 13, 2026; Honda's new bulletins October 30, 2026; parts shortage means affected owners get priority and everyone else waits.

## Limitations (must appear in the article)

- EA26007 is an **investigation, not a recall and not a defect finding**. NHTSA has made no determination that the start-stop system is defective. The article must say this plainly and more than once.
- The 1,033,632 population figure comes from **Reuters, citing NHTSA**; NHTSA's own opening resume (per CarIndust) gives no vehicle count. Treat the figure as Reuters' number, attributed.
- Complaint counts differ by source: **701** per the NHTSA opening resume (MotorBiscuit, Autoblog); Reuters reports **700**. I use 701 as the filing's number and note the rounding.
- "Different root causes" is NHTSA's characterization in the opening resume; the agency has not published what those causes are. Do not speculate about which part fails in the 2024–2026 cars.
- The 2023 two-stage fix (software, then starter/relays/valves) applied to the older population under EA25004/PE22005. Whether it applies to the EA26007 population is exactly what NHTSA says is in doubt — do not present it as the fix for the new cars.
- The 1-in-1,472 complaint rate is my own calculation from the filing's complaint count and Reuters' population estimate; complaints are self-reported and undercount real failures, so the true failure rate is higher by an unknown amount.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear in the article)

Seven hundred complaints across a million cars is 0.07%. No crashes, no injuries, no deaths in this file. The engine eventually restarts or the owner restarts it; the car is stranded at a light, not hurtling into a wall. Honda issued new service bulletins two weeks before the probe opened, which is a manufacturer acting, not stalling. A third probe into the same symptom could just as easily be read as the system working: complaints arrive, the agency opens a file, the manufacturer cooperates, and the slow machinery of defect investigation grinds forward. The honest reply: the story is not the 701 complaints, it is the 4.5-year arc. The same failure has now drawn three federal cases, the 2023 fix did not stop the reports, and the newest cars fail at the same rate as the old ones for reasons NHTSA admits are different. A defect that survives its own repair program and reappears in the redesigned cars is not a closed file. It is a pattern.

## Actionable takeaways

- Own a 2024–2026 Pilot, Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline or MDX? Press the Auto Idle Stop OFF button every time you start the car — the system re-enables itself at every ignition cycle, so there is no set-and-forget.
- If your engine has failed to restart after auto stop, file a complaint with NHTSA at nhtsa.gov (Vehicle Safety Hotline 1-888-327-4236). EA26007 exists because 701 owners did exactly that; the complaint count is what opened the case.
- Ask your dealer about Honda service bulletin 26-090 / Acura 26-019 (October 30, 2026). Honda says replacement parts are short and vehicles already experiencing the problem get priority — get on the list before yours strands you, not after.
- Buying a used 2024–2025 Pilot or MDX? It sits in two open federal investigations at once. Check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and ask the seller whether the 2023 countermeasures were performed — and know that NHTSA says those countermeasures may not fix the new cars anyway.
