## The news hook (Oct 7, 2026)
Waymo published "Sober Drivers Still Face Nearly 4x Nighttime Risk" (waymo.com/blog/2026/10/sober-driving-benchmarks/) summarizing two peer-reviewed studies:

1. **Journal of Safety Research (accepted for publication):** exposure-reconstruction model cross-referencing FARS toxicological data with real-world telematics to estimate miles driven by sober (zero BAC) vs impaired drivers across the 50 most populous US urban areas.
2. **IRCOBI paper:** evaluated 37,654 fatal cases from 2023 FARS against USDOT's Safe System Approach.

## Key numbers (all from primary source unless noted)
- Removing alcohol-involved drivers from the urban fatal-crash pool cuts the fatal crash involvement rate by **23%** (1.42 → 1.10 incidents per 100M miles).
- Geographic spread: Houston 31% reduction, Minneapolis 13% — impaired-driving exposure is local, not national.
- **Even 100% sober drivers face 3.1–3.9x higher nighttime fatal crash risk than daytime risk.**
- Over 2% of nighttime drivers in some urban areas (1 in 50) are over the legal BAC limit.
- IRCOBI: 77% of 2023 fatal cases involved at least one of impairment/speeding/unbelted; 9.2% involved all three; **only 8.2% involved strictly human-behavior factors alone.**
- Waymo estimates autonomous systems could address up to 83% of system failures in passenger-vehicle fatal crashes.
- MADD CEO Stacey D. Stewart publicly backed the research.

## Draft thesis
Everyone assumes the nighttime fatality curve is a drunk-driving curve. Waymo's zero-BAC cohort says no: even if you vaporized every drunk driver tonight, urban fatal crash involvement would fall only 23% — and a sober driver at night still faces roughly four times the daytime risk. The real killers are darkness, geometry, speed, and road design. That means alcohol-interlock tech (mandated by the infrastructure law) caps out at a fraction of the nighttime problem — the other 77% of the fatal convergence needs lights, lower speeds, and road redesign. Actionable take: if you drive at night, sobriety is necessary but nowhere near sufficient; the defenses that matter are headlights that actually work (recall check), speed discipline, and seatbelts.

## Limitations to state explicitly
- Waymo's exposure model is an estimate: national databases don't track VMT by BAC, so the zero-BAC baseline comes from reconstructed telematics + FARS toxicology, not direct measurement.
- Urban-only (50 most populous US urban areas); rural roads carry ~41% of traffic deaths and behave differently.
- The 83%-addressable-by-AV claim is Waymo's own analysis of its own tech — self-serving; report as their estimate with attribution.
- The study compares urban fatal crash involvement rates, not national fatality totals.
