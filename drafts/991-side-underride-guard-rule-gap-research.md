# Research: #991 — The Guard That Isn't There (side underride)

## Angle
Three ways a car can slide under a semi-trailer. Only one is federally guarded.
Rear underride: regulated (49 CFR 393.86 / FMVSS 223/224). Side underride:
no federal standard at all. NHTSA has been "reviewing" a side-guard mandate
since 2023 with no rule finalized. A bill in Congress (Stop Underrides Act
2.0) claims 31,500 underride deaths since NHTSA's founding with "no
substantial progress."

## Facts (all attributed, no fabrication)
- Rear impact guards required on most trailers under 49 CFR 393.86; side
  guards have NO federal safety standard. (robinettelaw.com, Sep 16 2026;
  truckpartsandservice.com)
- NHTSA opened a formal review of a side-guard mandate in 2023; as of early
  2026 still working through public comments. (businessinsurance.com
  MarketersMedia, Sep 18 2026; lifestyle.us-az-culturalfoundation.com)
- Industry cost estimate cited in comments: $3,740+ per trailer, 450-800 lbs
  added weight. (trucking industry comments via businessinsurance.com)
- Death counts: official federal data ~219 underride fatalities/yr, but state
  reporting is inconsistent and advocates/researchers estimate the true toll
  at 300-600+/yr. (callfob.com, 2026)
- Stop Underrides Act 2.0 (H.R. 7354 House / S. 3775 Senate; sponsors: Reps.
  Steven Cohen, Mark DeSaulnier, Deborah Ross; Sens. Kirsten Gillibrand,
  Ben Ray Lujan): requires side underride guards on new trucks/trailers that
  prevent passenger-compartment intrusion at impacts up to 40 mph; also
  improved data collection, independent research, law-enforcement training.
  (truckpartsandservice.com)
- Bill text claims: since NHTSA's founding 50+ years ago, underride crashes
  have claimed 31,500 lives with "no substantial progress." (bill sponsors,
  via truckpartsandservice.com)
- Guards would also cut fuel use via aerodynamic integration, per the bill.
- Why it kills: trailer floor sits above car crumple zones, bumper, airbag
  sensors, seat-belt tensioners; occupant compartment takes the hit. Advocacy
  framing: "It's not the crash that kills; it's the underride."
  (annaleahmary.com — AnnaLeah & Mary for Truck Safety)
- Stoughton trailer guard qualified for IIHS award (per
  truckpartsandservice.com).

## Caveats / counterarguments (state at full strength)
- Death-count claims vary wildly (219 official vs 31,500 cumulative vs
  300-600/yr estimates); article must attribute each number to its source
  and note the undercount controversy explicitly.
- Industry argument: retrofit cost, weight/fuel penalty, maintenance burden;
  cost-benefit projections are what NHTSA is actually weighing.
- No per-model FARS data exists for underride; this is an aggregate/
  regulatory story, not a model-rate story. Say so.

## Actionable insight (required gate)
- If you drive for a living or run a fleet: side guards are commercially
  available now (e.g., IIHS-award-qualified designs); spec them without
  waiting for the mandate.
- If you are a citizen: H.R. 7354 / S. 3775 is the live bill; the ask is
  that your rep co-sponsor or push NHTSA to finalize the 2023 review.
- Defensive driving angle: never linger alongside a trailer's midsection;
  the gap is the kill zone, and no airbag deploys for a trailer floor.
