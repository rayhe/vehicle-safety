## Story in one line
The National Safety Council estimates 428 people will die on U.S. roads during the 2026 Thanksgiving holiday period (Nov 25-29), a 2% decrease from last year that is smaller than the estimate's own margin of error — while 104 of those 428 deaths, nearly a quarter of the forecast, would not happen if everyone in the car wore a seat belt.

## Key verified facts
- **The forecast:** 428 deaths, Thanksgiving 2026 period (6 p.m. Wed Nov 25 – 11:59 p.m. Sun Nov 29). Down 2% from the 439 estimated for 2025.
- **The uncertainty:** 90% confidence interval 372-487. Width: 115 deaths. Last year's 439 sits inside it. The "decrease" is 11 deaths against an interval 10x wider.
- **The injuries:** 48,800 nonfatal medically consulted injuries estimated (CI 42,400-55,600), at NSC's 114:1 injury-to-death ratio.
- **The belt math:** belts 45% effective for front-seat passenger-car occupants. 160 lives saved because occupants buckle up. 104 more lives saved if everyone buckled. 160 + 104 = 264 lives riding on belts this weekend. 104 / 428 = 24.3% of the forecast.
- **The 2024 actuals (NHTSA):** Thanksgiving weekend 2024: 286 passenger vehicle occupants killed; 128 (45%) unrestrained. At night, 50% of those killed were unbuckled; daytime 36%.
- **The booze share:** 37% of Thanksgiving-period fatalities involve an alcohol-impaired driver (BAC 0.08+), vs 30% of all traffic fatalities in 2024. NHTSA counted 868 drunk-driving deaths across five Thanksgiving periods (2019-2023) — see #1280.
- **Exposure:** AAA 2026 Thanksgiving forecast: 81.8 million people traveling 50+ miles, a record (per #1280 research, Aronfeld summary of AAA).
- **Enforcement:** Click It or Ticket high-visibility seat belt campaign, Nov 23-29, 2026, nationwide.
- **FARS context (local fars_output.js, 2014-2023):** seat belt use by fatally injured passenger car and light truck occupants was 45.8% (NSC, citing FARS). The national picture from #1415: 91.2% of Americans buckle up, yet 48% of people who die in cars were unbelted; 9,758 unbelted deaths in 2024, ~27 a day.

## Original contribution (novel to this piece)
1. **The noise test:** 428 vs 439 is an 11-death "improvement" inside a 115-death-wide confidence interval. The honest headline is "flat, within error" — the press release's 2% decrease is a rounding artifact of a statistical projection, not a measured improvement.
2. **The preventable slice, quantified against actuals:** NSC's 104-lives figure implies 24% of forecast deaths are a belt choice. The 2024 actuals (45% unrestrained among Thanksgiving occupant deaths) suggest the true preventable share is larger than the estimate's framing implies, because NSC's 45% effectiveness figure is the conservative passenger-car number (light trucks: 50%).
3. **The enforcement window as the actionable hook:** the Nov 23-29 Click It or Ticket campaign means the ticket is the nudge and the belt is the fix, in the same week as the forecast. The article's ask is dated and specific, not generic.

## Limitations
- NSC's 428 is a statistical projection, not a count; actual 2026 Thanksgiving deaths will not be known until FARS final data arrives in 2027. The 2025 actual is not yet final either — 439 was itself an estimate.
- The 104-lives figure applies NSC's 45% effectiveness (front-seat passenger-car occupants) broadly; light-truck occupants see 50%, rear-seat effectiveness differs, and the figure assumes full compliance, which no enforcement campaign achieves.
- Holiday-period definitions differ across sources: NSC's estimate window ends 11:59 p.m. Sunday; NHTSA's Thanksgiving fatality window runs to 5:59 a.m. Monday. The 286 (2024 actuals) and 428 (2026 estimate) are not directly comparable counts.
- The 2% decrease claim and the CI both come from NSC's model; the underlying FARS 2025 Annual Report File was not yet final at publication.
- FARS 2014-2023 local file does not cover 2024-2026; the 45.8% unbelted share among fatally injured occupants is NSC's FARS-derived figure, not computed from the local file.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
A forecast is not a body count, and building an article on 428 projected deaths risks treating a model's output as a fact — the actual number could land anywhere from 372 to 487, and last year's "439" was also just an estimate, so the entire year-over-year comparison is model output stacked on model output. Focusing on seat belts also understates the other two killers in the same weekend: 37% of Thanksgiving deaths involve alcohol, and speeding and distraction fill the rest. A reader who buckles up and drives drunk is still the problem. And the 104-lives figure is a counterfactual, not a plan — no campaign in history has produced 100% belt use, so presenting it as "104 people just need to click" overpromises what enforcement can deliver. The honest version: belts are the biggest single lever, not a magic switch.

## What the article must NOT claim
- That 428 people "will" die, as a certainty. It is an estimate with a stated interval; say so.
- That the 2% decrease is a real improvement. The CI swallows it.
- That all 104 lives are saveable by enforcement. They are saveable by universal belt use, which is a behavior, not a policy outcome.
- That Thanksgiving 2026 is safer than 2025. The estimate says the point estimate fell; the interval says we cannot know that.
- Any specific 2026 Thanksgiving death count. The holiday has not happened yet at research time.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Buckle up in every seat, every trip — including the back seat, where belt use lags and where #1415 showed the dying population concentrates.
- The enforcement window is Nov 23-29, 2026: expect stops, and the ticket is cheaper than the alternative by several orders of magnitude.
- Plan a sober ride before the drinking starts: 37% of Thanksgiving deaths involve alcohol, and the deadliest drunk-driving day of the week is the Saturday after, not Wednesday (per #1280).
- If you are driving the holiday haul, the single highest-leverage thing you control after sobriety is making sure every occupant is belted before the car moves.
