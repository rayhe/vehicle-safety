## Story in one line
Polestar filed exactly one NHTSA recall campaign in 2026, covering 213 model-year 2025 Polestar 3 SUVs whose rear seat backrests were held on by one nut instead of two at the Charleston, South Carolina plant — in the same year the US government told the brand it cannot sell new cars here after 2026, and NHTSA closed a 14-month audit of the backup-camera recalls that defined the brand's safety record.

## Key verified facts
- **The one 2026 campaign: 26V037 (Polestar RP1079), filed Jan 23, 2026 — 213 vehicles.** 2025 Polestar 3, built Oct 6, 2024 – Sept 22, 2025 at the Charleston, SC plant (per press reports of the filing; the RCMN confirms Charleston production). Rear seat backrest striker brackets may be loose: one of two attaching nuts tightened. FMVSS 207 noncompliance. A loose backrest may fall forward in a crash and let cargo enter the cabin. Remedy: inspect and correctly mount/torque the bracket nuts, free. Owner letters by March 22, 2026. No accidents, injuries, or fatalities stated in the filings consulted.
- **Zero 2026 campaigns for the Polestar 1, Polestar 2, and Polestar 4.** The API returns no 26V campaign under any of those model names in any model year queried.
- **The 7-to-1 collapse.** Polestar filed 7 campaigns in 2025 (25V280, 25V293, 25V409, 25V420, 25V453, 25V555, 25V615) and 1 in 2026. The 2025 count includes the third backup-camera recall (25V615, 27,816 Polestar 2s) that NHTSA's audit finally closed on Sept 1, 2026.
- **All-time total: 17 campaigns.** 2021: 2 (21V110 battery software, 21V683 seat belts). 2022: 1 (22V926 12V battery). 2023: 2 (23V361 12V battery, 23V476 ball joint). 2024: 4 (24V477 camera, 24V835 brakes, 24V939 airbag inflator, 24V940 propulsion). 2025: 7. 2026: 1. The Polestar 3 alone accounts for 6 of the 17, all on the 2025 model year.
- **The brand's real 2026 safety event was not a recall.** NHTSA closed Recall Query RQ25004 on Sept 1, 2026, ending a 14-month audit of three Polestar 2 backup-camera recalls. Covered in full by #849 (published Nov 15, 2026); this census cross-references it, it does not re-report it.
- **The brand's real 2026 corporate event was an exit.** On June 25, 2026, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security declined to authorize Polestar under the Connected Vehicles Rule, barring new-vehicle sales in the US from the 2027 model year. Polestar said it would not appeal. It continues to sell existing Polestar 3 and Polestar 4 stock and to support current owners through its service network. Volvo, the Geely sibling, received authorization; Polestar did not. US Q3 2026 sales were about 2,160 cars, roughly 15% of global retail.
- **Census-series context.** Polestar's 1 campaign / 213 vehicles is the smallest non-zero recall year in the series: Porsche filed 0 campaigns in 2026 (#1491); Lucid filed 5 (#1498); VinFast filed 4 with zero 2026 cars (#1515); Subaru's smallest single campaign was 8 cars but it filed 5 campaigns (#1481). Polestar is the only brand in the series whose entire 2026 recall footprint fits in a single parking row.

## Original contribution (Clara's math and synthesis)
1. **The 1-campaign census with corrected vocabulary.** 213 vehicles, found by querying 4 models x 9 model years plus a 2019-2020 gap-check (~36 API queries) that caught a 2020-model-year campaign the main sweep missed. Cross-checked against an independent aggregator (17 all-time, newest Jan 23, 2026). No prior article has summed Polestar's 2026 campaigns.
2. **The 7-to-1 collapse.** Seven campaigns in 2025, one in 2026. The drop is not a safety improvement story: the brand spent 2026 under a federal remedy audit and a sales ban, not building new cars to recall.
3. **The Charleston irony.** Polestar consolidated Polestar 3 production at its Charleston plant to be the American-made EV that could survive the China crackdown. Its one 2026 recall is a Charleston assembly error: a worker tightened one nut instead of two on a seat backrest bracket.
4. **The one-nut math.** FMVSS 207 exists so a seat backrest holds in a crash. With half its fasteners, the backrest can release and let cargo into the cabin. 213 cars, each missing half the hardware on a safety-critical bracket. The fix is a wrench, not a part: inspect, mount correctly, torque both nuts.
5. **The quietest year in the series is the story.** Every other brand's census is a story about volume. Polestar's is a story about absence: one recall, in the year the brand stopped being allowed to sell cars in America. The census is the receipt for a brand running out the clock on its US market.

## Limitations
- The census counts campaigns filed Jan 1 – Nov 15, 2026. Late filings will move the count; the article must date the census.
- Campaign populations count recall actions, not unique vehicles. Moot here: one campaign, one population, no overlap possible.
- The NHTSA API returns HTTP 400 for make+model+year combinations with no matching rows; treated as zero after cross-checking against press, Part 573 filings, and an independent aggregator (AnyRecalls, 17 all-time, newest Jan 23, 2026). A campaign could still hide under an untested model spelling; the direction of any remaining bias is undercount.
- No FARS model-level data exists for the 2025 Polestar 3; this is a recall census, not a fatality-rate story. Do not import Polestar fatality rates as if they applied to these vehicles.
- The Connected Vehicles Rule thread is regulatory, not safety: it explains why the recall count is low (fewer new cars, a brand winding down US sales), not why the cars are safe. Keep the two threads separate in the article.
- The build window (Oct 6, 2024 – Sept 22, 2025) and "213 vehicles" come from the RCMN and press reports of the filing; the RCLRPT Part 573 report itself was not separately retrieved. Do not state a defect-rate estimate; none was published.

## Strongest counterargument
One recall is the safety system working, not failing. 213 cars, zero injuries, a free fix that takes a wrench, and a manufacturer that found its own assembly error and filed it. Compare Ford's 85 recalls in 2026 (per the Detroit Free Press, Nov 9) and Polestar looks like the best-run quality operation in the industry. A census of one is barely a census, and the brand's real 2026 story is a trade-policy ban, not a safety defect — a seat bracket has nothing to do with the Commerce Department. The article must grant all of this fully, then answer it: the count is low because the brand is leaving, not because it got safer — 2025 had seven campaigns, and the one 2026 defect is the most basic failure in the book, a fastener left half-tightened on the car Polestar built in America to prove it belonged here. Both sentences are true at the same time.

## Actionable guidance (required)
- If you own a 2025 Polestar 3: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for **26V037 (Polestar RP1079)**. Only 213 US cars are in it, built Oct 2024 – Sept 2025 in Charleston. The fix is free: the dealer inspects the rear seat backrest striker bracket and torques both nuts. If your rear seatback feels loose or you hear it shift, do not load cargo behind it until the dealer checks it.
- If you own a 2021-2025 Polestar 2: your camera recall is **25V615 (Polestar RP1069)** — even if you already had the earlier camera recalls done, you need this one again. NHTSA closed its audit of the fix on Sept 1, 2026; complaints fell from 275 to 3.
- If you already own a Polestar in the US: Polestar says it will keep selling existing Polestar 3 and Polestar 4 stock and will keep its service network open. Warranties remain valid. The sales ban covers new-model-year sales from 2027, not service for cars already on the road.
- Buying a used Polestar 3: confirm RP1079 was completed and ask for the service record. A 2025 Polestar 3 that never had its seat bracket torqued is still an open recall.
