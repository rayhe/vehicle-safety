Toyota Recalled the Sienna's Seats Three Times in 20 Months. This Time the Safety System Is the Problem.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

Toyota has recalled the Sienna's seats three times in 20 months, and the numbers do not lie, but they do occasionally smirk. NHTSA campaign 26V690, filed October 20, 2026, covers 228,367 model-year 2022-2026 Sienna Hybrid 7-passenger vans whose second-row seatback recliner locks can disengage when the seatbelt pretensioner activates, so a seatback may fail to remain secured in the very crash the pretensioner is responding to.[1] A pretensioner exists to yank you tight against the seat in the first milliseconds of a crash. In these vans, that yank can trip the lock holding the seatback upright.

228,367
Sienna Hybrid vans recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V690 for second-row seatbacks that may not stay secured in a crash

Reuters, reporting the US notice on October 27, puts the mechanism in plain language: in certain frontal crashes, the force could disengage the seat back from its locked position, failing to properly restrain occupants and increasing injury risk.[2] Transport Canada, which published the same recall on October 20 covering more than 24,000 Canadian vans, states the mechanism more precisely: the mechanisms that lock the second-row seat recliners in position may disengage when a seatbelt pretensioner activates.[3] Canada also goes one step further than the US notice found for this article: it tells owners not to use the second-row seats to transport passengers until the repair is complete. That row is the family row, the row where the car seats go, and a do-not-use order on it turns a seven-seat minivan into a five-seat one.

Fixes come in two flavors. Most vans get a software update to the supplemental restraint system electronic control unit, the computer that commands the pretensioners, which means a mechanical lock is being fixed by retuning the electronics that trip it. Vans equipped with the Mobility as a Service package get their two second-row seatbacks replaced outright.[1] Repairs are free, owner letters are expected December 7, 2026, and Toyota's numbers for the recall are 26TB24 and 26TA24.[1] Nobody should wait for the letter to check a VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, because December is six weeks of school runs away.

Three recalls, three mechanisms, two rows, one model. In February 2025, campaign 25V086 recalled 167,802 model-year 2021-2025 Siennas because bolts on the third-row seatbacks may not have been tightened to specification, a torque problem.[4] In October 2025, campaign 25V-668 recalled 54,631 model-year 2025 Siennas because a welding-machine setting change left second-row seat rails with welds that did not penetrate fully, a weld problem that Toyota told NHTSA affected 100 percent of vans built in that January-to-July window.[5] Now a lock-logic problem joins them. Add the three campaigns together and you get 450,800 recall actions, a number that counts overlapping vans more than once, since a 2025 Sienna can appear in all three, and should never be quoted as 450,800 distinct vehicles.

That overlap is where the story gets uncomfortable, because the second recall already produced a cautionary tale about repair delays. A California federal judge ruled on October 15, 2026 that warranty claims over the seat-rail recall may proceed, after plaintiffs alleged that Toyota had yet to repair a single affected vehicle by April 1, 2026, while owners were told not to use the seats or install child restraints there.[6] A software fix should be faster than a rail replacement, and this time the letters carry a December date rather than an open-ended wait, but owners of the previous recall have earned their skepticism honestly.

Fairness requires the counterargument at full strength. A population of 228,367 is not 228,367 failures; it is the number of vans that could have the defect, and no crash, injury, or fatality count tied to 26V690 appears in the NHTSA record summary or the reporting found for this article. Toyota caught this through its own processes, disclosed it in both countries in the same week, and is offering a free fix. Seat recalls are also, in the grand scheme of minivan ownership, a known and survivable annoyance, and the Sienna remains the default answer to the question of what to drive when your family outgrows a sedan. None of that changes the pattern, though, and the pattern is the point: when the same model needs three seat recalls in 20 months for three unrelated reasons, the seat system is telling you something that no single recall notice says out loud.

What you can actually do: if you own a 2022-2026 Sienna Hybrid with second-row captain's chairs, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today and call Toyota at 1-800-331-4331 with recall numbers 26TB24 or 26TA24, rather than waiting for the December 7 letter.[1] Until the software update is done, Transport Canada's advice is the cautious one to follow even in the US: keep passengers, and especially kids in car seats, out of the second row if you can. Shopping a used Sienna, check all three campaigns, 25V086, 25V-668, and 26V690, because clearing one seat recall says nothing about the other two.

What this article did not prove: no injury or crash count is attributed to 26V690 here, because none appears in the records found, and the absence of a count is not evidence that none exists. This article also does not establish whether the US notice carries the same do-not-use instruction as Canada's; the instruction is attributed to Transport Canada only. Finally, the 450,800 figure is a sum of three campaign populations with overlapping vans, not a count of distinct vehicles, and the claim that Toyota "caught this through its own processes" reflects the timing and framing of the filings, not an internal document this article has seen.
