Great Dane's Champion Trailers Can't Stay Hitched. A Sixteenth of an Inch Is Why.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

Great Dane recalled 69 of its newest Champion dry vans because the plate that holds the trailer to the truck is the wrong plate. Not a cracked frame. Not a software glitch. A plate, on the upper coupler, that measures a quarter inch where the specification says five-sixteenths.

NHTSA campaign 26V656, filed October 6, 2026, covers certain 2027 Champion dry vans built from September 1, 2026 with hot-dipped galvanized upper couplers.[1] With the wrong bottom plate installed, the kingpin may protrude, the trailer may not couple properly, and an improperly coupled trailer may detach.[1] Owners are advised not to use their trailers until the repair is completed.[2] That advisory is the tell. Recalls usually say "schedule a repair." This one says "park it."

20%
How much thinner the installed coupler plate is than specified: 1/4 inch installed versus 5/16 inch required, a shortfall of 1/16 inch on the single connection point between a loaded trailer and the truck pulling it

I ran the numbers twice, because a sixteenth of an inch sounds like a rounding error until you divide it out. A quarter inch is 0.250. Five-sixteenths is 0.3125. Divide, and the installed plate is 80% of the specified thickness, which means 20% of the margin is simply not there. On a connection point that carries the entire trailer, 20% is not a tolerance. It is a different part wearing the right part's name.

Here is where a small recall gets interesting in the spreadsheet sense. Great Dane has done this before, on this exact trailer, on this exact component. In December 2024, the company recalled 2025 Champion dry freight trailers because missing kingpin welds could prevent the upper coupler from securely connecting the trailer.[3] Missing welds then, wrong plate now, 22 months apart. Two different manufacturing escapes, one connection point, one consequence. A trailer that can come unhitched is not two separate stories. It is one story with two chapters.

Context from the same week makes the pattern harder to wave away. NHTSA's October 26 commercial-vehicle batch covered 384 trailers and trucks: Great Dane's 69, plus 310 PACCAR trucks with sleeper-bunk restraints that were not secured properly, plus 5 Volvo trucks with loose or missing cab bolts.[2] Great Dane is 18% of that batch and the only one carrying a do-not-use order. Small population, maximum severity, and a repeat component. That combination is what a pattern looks like before anybody calls it one.

One more 2027 Champion escape belongs in the same table, even though it involves a different part. In July 2026, Great Dane recalled 11 units of the 2027 Champion produced without rear impact guard extension channels, failing the federal rear-impact standard.[4] Three escapes on one model year, three components, all caught after production. Any one of them is a bad week. Together they describe a quality system that keeps finding its misses downstream, in federal filings, instead of upstream, on the line.

What you can actually do: if you operate a 2027 Great Dane Champion dry van with a hot-dipped galvanized upper coupler, do not use the trailer until it is inspected. That is Great Dane's own advisory, not a suggestion. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls under campaign 26V656, or call Great Dane at 1-877-369-3493. Owner letters go out December 4, 2026, and the inspection plus upper-coupler replacement, where needed, is free.[1] Fleet managers should pull 2027 Champions built from September 1, 2026 first and measure the coupler plate against the 5/16-inch spec before the letters arrive.

What this article did not prove: no detached trailer, crash, injury, or death is cited in the NHTSA record or the trade coverage for this defect, so 69 is the recall population, not a failure count, and the failure rate is unknown. Production end date and coupler supplier are not in the coverage found, so this article claims nothing about either. Population for the December 2024 kingpin-weld recall was not re-verified this run, so it is cited here without a unit count.

Fair counter, stated at full strength: 69 trailers is a rounding error in a national fleet, and Great Dane caught this one fast. Production began September 1, 2026, and the NHTSA report landed October 6, five weeks later, with a do-not-use order instead of a quiet letter campaign. That is the system working. True, all of it, and also beside the point. Speed of recall does not explain why the same connection point failed quality control twice in 22 months, or why a third 2027 Champion component escaped in the same model year. A fast recall is good. A second coupler recall is a question that a fast recall does not answer.
