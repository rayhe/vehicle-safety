# Research Notes: 1048-ford-77-recalls-record-pace

## Story thesis
Ford's 77th recall of 2026 (Ranger backup camera, 26C42, Sept 28) quietly tied the pre-2025 all-time single-year recall record, GM's 77 from 2014. Ford then broke that record itself in 2025 with 153. The novel data angle: while Ford's recall COUNT is down sharply in 2026 (~103 projected vs 153 in 2025), the vehicles-per-recall has DOUBLED (85k to ~183k), so 2026 has already recalled MORE vehicles (14.05M+) than all of 2025 (12.96M) — fewer fires, bigger explosions. Meanwhile Ford simultaneously won J.D. Power Initial Quality #1 among mainstream brands (first time since 2010). Count is down, blast radius is up, quality scores are up. All three things are true.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? YES. 77 is a milestone that landed 4 days ago and ties a decade-old record. Nobody has written the count-vs-blast-radius comparison.
- Novel angle on data? YES. The "fewer but bigger recalls" inversion + the J.D. Power paradox. Original calculations below.
- After 70+ Ford stories on this site: all recent Ford pieces are single-recall writeups (#1045 F-150 fuel tank, #994 Ranger capacitor, #1033 headlight LED). No aggregate pace/throughput story exists in queue. Passes.

## Original findings / calculations (show math in article)
1. **Pace:** 77 recalls in the 272 days from Jan 1 to Sept 28, 2026 = 1 recall every 3.53 days. Full-year projection: 77 / 272 * 365 = 103.3 recalls.
2. **Blast-radius inversion:** 2025: 12,958,128 vehicles / 153 recalls = 84,693 vehicles per recall. 2026 YTD: 14,037,387 / 75 (pre-Ranger; ~Sept 24) = 187,165 per recall. Per-recall size more than doubled (+121%) even as the count fell.
3. **2026 already passed 2025's volume:** 14,037,387 (75 recalls, ~Sept 24) + 11,451 (Ranger 26C42, Sept 28) = 14,048,838 vs 12,958,128 for all of 2025. Fewer recalls, 1.09M more vehicles.
4. **Record-book paradox:** Ford's 2026 count (77) = GM's 2014 all-time record (77). Ford's own 2025 record (153) nearly doubles it. So "tied the all-time record" is simultaneously true and unimpressive — the record book has a Ford asterisk on top of a Ford asterisk.
5. **The J.D. Power paradox:** #1 mainstream brand in 2026 IQS (first since 2010), up from #15 in 2023, 41 fewer problems per 100 vehicles — the same year it will recall ~14-15M vehicles. Initial quality measures the first 90 days; recalls are lifetime. The two metrics do not contradict each other; they measure different things. State this explicitly.

## Limitations to state
- Counts come from media compilations of NHTSA data, not a direct NHTSA query; snapshots at different dates (July / Sept 9 / ~Sept 24 / Sept 28) are not perfectly comparable.
- "Vehicles per recall" is an arithmetic mean; the distribution is heavily skewed (a handful of multi-million-vehicle campaigns dominate). Median would be lower.
- NHTSA counts recall campaigns; a single campaign can bundle multiple model years/brands. Comparisons across automakers assume consistent campaign-splitting practices.
- The 198-day remedy average covers a 22-recall subset; Ford disputes the underlying analysis.
- J.D. Power IQS measures problems in the first 90 days of ownership across all vehicles sold; recalls measure identified defects over the vehicle lifetime. A brand can genuinely lead both rankings.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
Recall count is a terrible quality metric, and Ford's defenders are right about this much: a recall means the company found a problem and is fixing it free. A high recall count can reflect aggressive self-reporting rather than bad engineering — the alternative is cars that stay broken. GM's ignition-switch era was a low-recall era because GM suppressed the defect, not because the cars were fine. If Ford is finding and fixing more, that is, in isolation, the system working. The honest rejoinder: the defects are still real, the remedy lag is the part that converts "system working" into "owners driving recalled cars for six months," and the per-recall blast radius shows the problems are systemic, not corner cases.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE - must include)
1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — not once; Ford's pace means a clean VIN in March can be dirty by September. Set a calendar reminder quarterly.
2. Before buying used: ask the dealer to confirm all open recalls are COMPLETED, not just identified. A VIN check shows presence, not remedy status.
3. If your vehicle is recalled and the dealer says the fix isn't available, get it in writing and keep driving only if the notice doesn't carry a do-not-drive warning; the 198-day average lag means "remedy under development" can last half a year.
4. 2026 Explorer shoppers: 15 recall campaigns on the 2026 model year alone — check completion status on the specific VIN, not the model.
