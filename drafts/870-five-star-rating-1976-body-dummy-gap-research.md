# Research: #870 — "Your Five-Star Safety Rating Was Earned by a 1976 Body"

## Slug
`five-star-rating-1976-body-dummy-gap`

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
NHTSA's five-star safety rating — the number on every new-car window sticker — is earned by protecting a crash test dummy shaped like the median American man of 1976 (5'9", 171 lbs). Today's median man is 30 pounds heavier, 40.3% of adults are obese, and the modern THOR dummy NHTSA itself endorses has been delayed from 2019 to 2029 while Europe adopted it in 2020.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes: Detroit Free Press investigation published TODAY (Sep 10, 2026) out of Michigan labs (UMTRI, Wayne State, Humanetics); NHTSA's 2026 report to Congress urging THOR adoption; "NCAP on life support" quote.
- Novel angle? Yes: site covered the gender gap (May 2026, crash-test-dummy-gender-gap) but nothing on the obesity/body-size mismatch, the THOR decade-of-delay timeline, or the US-vs-Euro-NCAP gap. No existing story or draft does this.
- Data-backed? Yes: CDC obesity stats (40.3% obese, 31.7% overweight, Aug 2021–Aug 2023), NHTSA report to Congress, GAO 2023 report, Humanetics CEO on the record, Alliance for Automotive Innovation letters on the record.

## Primary sources
1. **Detroit Free Press, "One-third of Americans are overweight. Why aren't crash test dummies?" Sep 10, 2026** (Jamie L. LaReau): https://www.freep.com/story/money/cars/2026/09/10/crash-test-dummies-get-second-look-for-safety-from-michigan-labs/90886527007/
   - Dr. Jingwen Hu (UMTRI): four-window crash simulation — mid-stature female BMI 20, short female BMI 40, 6'3" man BMI 40, and 1976 median man (5'9", 171 lbs, BMI 25.2). Only the 1976 body "meets the airbag perfectly."
   - Federal 5-star tests require protecting only an "average-sized adult male" and a "small-sized adult female" in the front seat. No requirement for taller/heavier body types.
   - Obese occupants face greater lower-extremity and chest injury risk (restraints don't restrain larger bodies as well); smaller occupants can be over-restrained (belts grip too tightly, airbags deploy too close).
   - Dr. Cynthia Bir (Wayne State): "Automakers have looked at obese individuals, but getting it into actual regulation, and then into a dummy, is very difficult."
   - Christopher O'Connor (Humanetics CEO): Hybrid III is "like having a rotary phone. It's analog, it's old." Hybrid III ~$100,000; THOR ~$1.5M with 150+ sensors.
   - The U.S. auto industry has access to larger dummies — "there's just little incentive to use them."
2. **NHTSA 2026 report to Congress**: urged "swift implementation" of THOR dummies; research "reached a maturation point." Committee "anxiously awaiting" THOR 50th percentile male, THOR 5th percentile female, and WorldSID side-impact dummies.
3. **THOR delay timeline** (per Automotive News via Free Press): THOR 50th male was supposed to enter testing in 2019 → pushed to 2022/2023 → proposed Q4 2027 → now proposed Q4 2029 for NCAP. A full decade of slippage.
4. **Euro NCAP**: began using advanced THOR male dummies in 2020 — six years (and counting) ahead of the U.S. proposal.
5. **GAO 2023 report**: "the range in body types among American drivers has shifted substantially beyond what is covered by the current models." NHTSA: safety improved for everyone, but "females and older individuals are at greater risk of death"; "less is known" about higher-BMI occupants.
6. **Alliance for Automotive Innovation**: June letter to NHTSA said THOR small-female dummies "are still largely in a developmental phase and have not yet been demonstrated to provide clear improvements in crash safety"; proposed expanding the current female dummy instead. Sep 2025: NHTSA delayed 5-star program changes at the Alliance's request (pedestrian AEB included).
7. **Michael Brooks, Center for Auto Safety**: NCAP is "on life support"; IIHS has been "far outpacing" NHTSA "for well over a decade."
8. **CDC**: Aug 2021–Aug 2023, 40.3% of U.S. adults 20+ obese (9.7% severe), 31.7% overweight. Median woman: 5'4", 161 lbs. Median man: 5'9", 193 lbs — 30 lbs heavier than the federal dummy.
9. **DOT, December 2025**: accelerated design of more sophisticated dummies in the smallest size (female injury attention) — but no movement on dummies matching the majority of modern Americans.
10. **NHTSA**: estimated 36,640 traffic fatalities in 2025 — a record low (context: the aggregate trend still improves).

## Key numbers for the piece
- 1976 median man: 5'9", 171 lbs → today's median man: 5'9", 193 lbs (+30 lbs, +17.5%).
- 40.3% obese + 31.7% overweight = 72% of adults above "healthy" BMI; the dummy represents ~the 25th percentile of modern male bodies.
- THOR: 150+ sensors, ~$1.5M vs Hybrid III ~$100k (15x cost).
- THOR delay: 2019 → 2029 (10 years). Euro NCAP: since 2020.
- 36,640 deaths in 2025 (record low) — the aggregate trend vs. the body-type gap.

## Original contribution
- The "1976 body" framing: your five-star rating is a certification earned by a body from the Bicentennial.
- The decade-of-slippage timeline (2019→2029) assembled in one place.
- The Europe gap: the same car sold on both continents is crash-tested against a modern body in Europe and a 1976 body in America.
- Vin's paradox: the industry HAS the dummies (Humanetics sells them to automakers for other countries) — "there's just little incentive to use them."

## Actionable insight (hard gate)
- The 5-star rating certifies protection for two body types. Treat stars as necessary, not sufficient — cross-shop IIHS Top Safety Pick ratings, which update their tests far more aggressively than NHTSA's "on life support" program.
- Body-type risk is real per UMTRI modeling: larger occupants face higher chest/lower-extremity injury risk; smaller occupants face over-restraint risk. Seating position and belt fit matter more for non-standard bodies — sit upright, belt low across hips, and don't recline the seat into the airbag's path.
- Honest limit: no federal rating today tells you how YOUR body fares. That rating doesn't exist yet — earliest 2029.

## Limitations
- Pegged to one Free Press investigation; the delay timeline comes via Automotive News as cited there.
- BMI is a crude proxy (CDC notes it can't distinguish fat from muscle); the underlying issue is body mass + geometry vs. restraint design.
- THOR cost ($1.5M) and the Alliance's "developmental phase" claim are industry positions, not independently verified here.
- Aggregate fatalities hit a record low in 2025 — the current system is not failing everyone; the gap is distributional.

## Strongest counterargument
The Alliance isn't entirely wrong: a $1.5M dummy with 150 sensors raises testing costs that get passed to buyers, and NHTSA itself acknowledges overall safety has improved for all occupants (record-low 36,640 deaths in 2025). The industry's "expand the current female dummy" proposal is cheaper and faster. The genuine engineering question is whether THOR's extra sensors predict real-world injury better enough to justify a decade of re-tooling — and the industry says that hasn't been demonstrated. But Europe answered that question in 2020 by just doing it.
