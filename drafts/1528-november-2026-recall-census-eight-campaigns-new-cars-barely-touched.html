November Recalled 855,544 Vehicles. Only 8% of Them Were 2026 Models

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 16, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. Fifteen recall campaigns were filed in November 2026 covering 855,544 vehicles, and if you bought a 2026 model this year, almost none of them were about your car. Eight campaigns touch a 2026-model-year vehicle at all, covering 68,666 vehicles. One BMW recall alone, 720,796 cars built between 2012 and 2018, is 84 percent of the entire month.[1]

Ford carries nearly half the census by itself, 33,908 of the 68,666 vehicles across two campaigns filed on the same day, and Harley carries another 31.5 percent. Everything else in the month’s new-car count is small enough to fit in a parking lot: 286 BMW i5s, 18 Winnebago trailers, five fire trucks. Two of the eight campaigns are label corrections, a missing weight value on a Utilimaster and missing ratings on a Winnebago, which means a quarter of November’s new-car recalls were fixed with a sticker. Nobody in the census got a park-it order or a park-outside order. Not one of the eight is fixed over the air.[1]

Contrast that with what November actually filed. BMW’s 26V733 covers 720,796 cars from model years 2012 through 2018, every one of them previously recalled in 2024 for the same water-pump connector, and roughly ten thermal incidents have now happened in cars that already received that first repair. There is no remedy yet, owner letters start December 25, and NHTSA told 720,796 drivers to park outside until the fix exists.[8] Add Hyundai’s 37,901 airbag computers from 2019 to 2023, Acura’s 14,648 ZDXs from 2024, and a Ford recall of 8,650 Mavericks from 2022, and the pattern is unmistakable. November filed 786,878 vehicles that are not 2026 models, 92 percent of the month, and the 2026 share rounds to a rounding error.[1]

Four of the eight were new to this site, and finding them took a second count. My first pass queried 81 makes and 6,559 model combinations through NHTSA’s API and found four campaigns. A separate sweep of every campaign number from 26V700 to 26V760 caught four more the first pass missed: BMW’s i5, because the API files it under the model spelling “i5 edrive40” and not “i5”; a five-truck Spencer fire-apparatus recall, because Spencer is a make nobody thought to query; a Lamar trailer recall, for the same reason; and a Utilimaster label recall, because the API files it under “utilimaster” and not its parent company, Shyft. Half the census was invisible to the standard method, which is worth remembering the next time anyone quotes a recall count with total confidence. Count twice, trust once.[1]

Two traps in the same sweep are worth naming, because they look like November campaigns and are not. Mack’s 26V719 and Autocar’s 26V722 both carry November acknowledgment letters, November 4 and November 12, but both were filed on October 29, so they belong to October’s count. Daimler’s 26V737, covering 3,894 trucks, was filed November 6 but covers 2027-model-year trucks only, so it sits outside a 2026-model-year census entirely. Filing date is the honest boundary, and letter date is not.[1]

Fair hearing, because the other side has one. Count campaigns instead of vehicles and the split looks nothing like 8 percent: eight of fifteen November campaigns touch a 2026 vehicle, which is 53 percent, and the vehicle share is small only because one giant BMW recall dominates the denominator. Campaign populations here are full-campaign totals, not 2026-only slices, so the 68,666 figure includes 2021 through 2027 vehicles swept in alongside the 2026 ones, which overstates the new-car count, and the month is not over, since no campaign numbered above 26V745 had surfaced as of November 16 but a filing dated November 13 through 16 could still appear. All three caveats are real, and none of them changes the shape of the month, which is a recall system spending November on cars people bought years ago.[1]

Honest limits, because this census has them. It covers campaigns filed November 1 through 12, 2026, as recorded by NHTSA’s recalls API on November 16; a campaign filed later in the month is not in it. Equipment campaigns and tire campaigns are out of scope, consistent with this site’s census series. Crash and injury counts are not stated for most of these eight filings, so the article claims none. Populations are NHTSA “potentially involved” counts, and the 2026-only slice of mixed-year campaigns is not published anywhere, so treat 68,666 as the size of the campaigns that touch a 2026 vehicle, not the number of 2026 vehicles, which is why the headline counts campaigns rather than cars.[1]

What to do: if you own a 2026 Bronco, Explorer Police Interceptor, Harley touring bike, BMW i5, Spencer Rescue, Lamar gooseneck trailer, Utilimaster Upfit, or Winnebago Thrive, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Every fix in the census is free: a torque check, a console replacement, a sensor swap, a software update, new mounting hardware, or a corrected label. If you own a 2012 to 2018 BMW, your recall is the month’s real story: park it outside, away from buildings, until the remedy exists, and expect a letter starting December 25. And the general lesson is worth keeping. A recall announced in November is usually about a car you bought years ago, so when the next one lands, check the model years before you check your pulse.
