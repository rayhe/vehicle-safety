The Axle That Steers the Truck Is Held On by Four Pins Nobody Set

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 21, 2026

Hendrickson is recalling 14,238 front steer axles because the fasteners holding each axle seat to the beam may never have been set properly. Each axle has two seats pressed onto a fabricated beam, held by two Huck fasteners apiece. Four fasteners per axle. That is the entire attachment between the steering axle and the truck itself, and for nearly a year of production, the station that sets them was not setting them.[1][2]

14,238
STEERTEK NXT front steer axles recalled under NHTSA equipment campaign 26E078, built January 16 to December 10, 2025.

A Huck fastener is a pin and a collar. The collar gets swaged, cold-formed, onto the pin, and once it is on, it does not come off without a fight. Unless the swaging never happened. Then the collar can work loose, the pin can walk out of the seat, and the axle seat can shift on the beam. A pin that leaves the truck becomes debris for the car behind it. Hendrickson counted four field reports of pins already missing and five more axles caught by quality control, against its own estimate that about 0.1 percent, roughly 14 axles, actually have the defect. Nine of fourteen already found.[2]

Here is the part that should worry a fleet manager more than the fastener. This is an equipment recall, campaign 26E078, and equipment recalls name a part, not a vehicle. No make, no model, no model year, no VIN lookup. Hendrickson sold the axles to Kenworth, Peterbilt, International, and Blue Bird, which builds school buses. As of November 18, none of the four had filed a vehicle recall tied to the axle. So the defect reaches a school district only if the manufacturer forwards Hendrickson's serial-number list. There is no backstop, no VIN search, no letter to wait for.[2][3]

Hendrickson fixed the process on December 10, 2025. After rework, an axle cannot leave the fastening station until a supervisor or quality engineer checks the collars. Good fix. It arrived eleven months before the recall did. The filing landed November 11, 2026, 336 days after the process changed, and the sources do not say why. Four pins were already reported missing in the field by then.[2]

What to do depends on whose truck it is. Fleet operators with 2025 Kenworths, Peterbilts, Internationals, or Blue Bird buses should ask the manufacturer if their axle serial numbers are on Hendrickson's list. Drivers should listen for a rattle from the steer axle and look at the four collars during a pre-trip walkaround. A properly swaged collar shows at least one of six inspection dots, crossed and indented. The inspection is free, a replacement axle is free, and standard labor is covered. Hendrickson takes calls at 1-630-910-2800 or 1-855-743-3733, recall HTRUCK 2026-01. Fourteen axles, maybe. Zero crashes so far. That is the good news, and it is also the whole point: the time to find a loose pin is before it finds the road.[1][2]
