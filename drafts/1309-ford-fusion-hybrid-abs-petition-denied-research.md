# Research Notes — Article #1309: Ford's Fusion Hybrid Lost ABS and Traction Control. NHTSA Called It Wear and Tear.

**Working title:** "Ford's Fusion Hybrid Lost ABS and Traction Control. NHTSA Called It Wear and Tear."
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Slug:** 1309-ford-fusion-hybrid-abs-petition-denied

## Kill test

**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** NHTSA denied defect petition DP26004 on October 23, 2026, refusing to tie the 2010 Ford Fusion Hybrid's ABS hydraulic control unit failures to the 600,166-car recall 19V904, because the hybrid warns drivers with an ABS light while the recalled non-hybrids failed silently, and because its electrically activated brake system means a stuck valve does not lengthen pedal travel. A warning light, usually treated as evidence of failure, became the agency's proof the failure was different.

**Challenge:** After 1,308 articles, is this just another Ford brake story? No. Queue grep 2026-10-28: zero hits for DP26004, fusion-hybrid, 19V904, or ABS-hydraulic; prior Ford brake coverage concerns other campaigns and other failure modes. Three reasons to proceed: (1) genuinely new agency action, 5 days old at draft time (denied Oct 23, reported Oct 26-27); (2) the warning-light paradox is an original analytical finding, not a restatement of press coverage: petitioners cited the illuminated ABS lamp as the symptom, while ODI treated illumination itself as a distinguishing feature versus the silent recalled cars; (3) the pedal-feel trap is a concrete consumer hazard worth naming: hybrid force feedback can make a car with dead ABS and traction control feel normal underfoot, so "brakes feel fine" is not a valid test on this car.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Verified facts (all sourced, no fabrication)

1. **The denial.** NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) denied defect petition DP26004 on October 23, 2026, finding no unreasonable risk to highway safety. (Sources: The BRAKE Report Oct 26, 2026, citing ODI closing summary; Ford Authority Oct 27, 2026.)
2. **Population and component.** Model: 2010 Ford Fusion Hybrid only. Estimated population: 20,816 vehicles, per the ODI opening resume. Component: service brakes, hydraulic; ABS hydraulic control unit (HCU). (BRAKE Report, citing ODI opening resume.)
3. **Timeline.** Petition received December 22, 2025. Review opened April 9, 2026. Denied October 23, 2026. (BRAKE Report.)
4. **Alleged condition.** An illuminated ABS malfunction indicator lamp (MIL) associated with failure of the ABS HCU, resulting in ABS malfunction, loss of traction and electronic stability control functions, and reduced braking performance, particularly on slippery road surfaces. (BRAKE Report; Ford Authority.)
5. **Opening incident count.** When ODI opened its review, it listed nine incident reports and no crashes, injuries, or fatalities. (BRAKE Report, citing ODI opening resume.) Note: Ford Authority separately describes 10 complaints of issues following completion of recall repairs; that figure appears to describe a different complaint framing, so this article uses the ODI opening figure (nine, zero crashes) and does not merge the two counts.
6. **The recall petitioners wanted to join.** Recall 19V904 covered 600,166 model-year 2006-2010 Ford Fusion, Mercury Milan, and Lincoln Zephyr/MKZ vehicles (non-hybrid variants). In those vehicles, ABS valves in the HCU could remain stuck open because of a chemical reaction with aged DOT 3 brake fluid, potentially causing extended brake pedal travel and increasing crash risk. No visual or audible warning was presented to the driver. Ford was already aware of 15 crashes tied to that problem at recall time, and dealers inspected the HCU, replaced faulty units where needed, flushed brake fluid, and fitted a more robust reservoir cap. (BRAKE Report; Ford Authority.)
7. **Why ODI says the hybrid differs.** The 2010 Fusion Hybrid uses a different brake apply system: hydraulic friction brakes blended with hybrid regenerative braking, with an electrically activated brake master cylinder that provides force feedback through the brake pedal. ODI concluded a stuck-open normally closed valve in the hybrid's ABS HCU does not increase brake pedal travel. (BRAKE Report, summarizing ODI.)
8. **Complaint-rate evidence.** ODI found the complaint rate for the recalled 2010 Fusion vehicles was six times higher than for the 2010 Fusion Hybrid, which the agency said supports a significant difference in brake system design and performance. Most hybrid owner complaints cited an illuminated ABS MIL, while recalled vehicles did not illuminate a MIL or set a diagnostic trouble code, so ODI concluded the failure mode differs between the two populations. (BRAKE Report, summarizing ODI.)
9. **Age and wear ruling.** Given the vehicles' age, ODI said the low complaint volume is consistent with expected wear and tear rather than an actionable safety defect trend. Ford Authority reports the same conclusion: issues impact only a small percentage of models and fall within normal wear and tear, not a defect. (BRAKE Report; Ford Authority.)
10. **Reservation.** NHTSA said it reserves the right to take additional action if future circumstances warrant it. A denied petition is not a finding that no owner will ever experience the failure again. (BRAKE Report; Ford Authority.)
11. **Petitioner's core error, stated fairly.** Petitioners argued comparability: same nameplate, same HCU family, same symptom family (braking degradation). That argument is understandable on its face, and ODI answered it on engineering grounds (different brake apply system, different warning behavior, 6x complaint-rate gap), not by dismissing the complaints as imaginary.

## Original calculations / analysis (verifiable, show work in article)

- **The hybrid fleet is 3.5 percent of the recalled fleet.** 20,816 / 600,166 = 0.0347, about 3.5 percent. Petitioners asked ODI to treat a small, differently braked sub-fleet as part of a large recall; ODI refused on design grounds, not on size alone.
- **The 6x gap, stated plainly.** If the hybrid complaint rate were equal to the recalled non-hybrid rate, ODI would expect roughly six times as many hybrid complaints as it saw. A sixfold gap in the same model year, on the same nameplate, is the quantitative backbone of the denial, and it is stronger evidence than either side's anecdotes.
- **The timeline.** Petition Dec 22, 2025 to opening Apr 9, 2026 is 108 days; opening to denial Oct 23, 2026 is 197 days; petition to denial is 305 days. Owners waited ten months for an answer that says, in effect, your car's warning system is what separates it from the recalled cars.
- **The pedal-feel trap (original consumer framing).** In a conventional car, a stuck-open ABS valve lengthens pedal travel, so a driver can feel the failure. In this hybrid, the electrically activated master cylinder supplies force feedback, so pedal feel can stay normal while ABS and traction control are gone. Reduced braking performance "particularly on slippery road surfaces" is exactly where a driver discovers dead ABS, at the worst moment. Feeling fine is not evidence of working ABS on this car.
- **The warning-light paradox.** In the recalled non-hybrids, failure was silent: no lamp, no diagnostic code, longer pedal travel, 15 known crashes. In the hybrid, failure announces itself with an ABS lamp. Petitioners read the lamp as proof of defect; ODI read the lamp as proof of a different failure mode. Both readings use the same dashboard light, and only one can drive a recall decision.

## Story spine (Rex Driverton voice)

- Lede: a 16-year-old hybrid lost ABS and traction control, owners petitioned, and NHTSA said no, because the car warned them and the recalled cars did not.
- The petition: Dec 2025 filing, Apr 2026 opening, nine incident reports, zero crashes, zero injuries, zero deaths at opening.
- The recall petitioners wanted in: 19V904, 600,166 cars, stuck valves, aged DOT 3 fluid, silent failure, 15 crashes, extended pedal travel.
- The engineering split: blended regen braking, electrically activated master cylinder, force feedback, no pedal-travel increase from a stuck valve, lamp on versus lamp off, diagnostic code versus none, 6x complaint-rate gap.
- The ruling: wear and tear on a 16-year-old fleet, low volume, no unreasonable risk, with a reservation to revisit.
- The trap for owners: normal pedal feel proves nothing on this car; an ABS light means ABS and traction control may already be gone, and slippery roads are where that matters.
- Actionable: check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (19V904 does not cover the hybrid by petitioners' own framing); an illuminated ABS lamp on a 2010 Fusion Hybrid is a shop visit, not a wait-and-see; ask specifically for HCU diagnosis, not a generic brake check; keep brake fluid fresh, since aged DOT 3 fluid was the chemical actor in the recalled cars' failure; file an NHTSA complaint if a dealer dismisses the lamp, because ODI explicitly left the door open to future action.
- Closer: Rex judgment, that a denial grounded in a real engineering difference is defensible, while a denial that leaves owners thinking "no recall means no problem" would be a misreading ODI itself did not endorse.

## Limitations (for the limitations section)

- This article relies on The BRAKE Report and Ford Authority summaries of ODI's DP26004 closing summary and opening resume; a direct read of the NHTSA DP26004 page returned an access denial (HTTP 403) during this run, so ODI's own wording is quoted only as summarized by those two outlets. Readers should check the ODI file directly before treating any single sentence as ODI's verbatim language.
- The nine-incident / zero-crash figure is the count at review opening (April 2026), not a final tally through denial; ODI's closing analysis may rest on a larger complaint set that the summaries do not enumerate.
- Ford Authority's "10 complaints following repairs" figure is not reconciled with BRAKE Report's "nine incident reports" here; they may count different complaint sets, and this article does not add them together.
- No crash, injury, or fatality count is attributed to the hybrid condition in either source; absence of attributed crashes is not proof the condition is harmless, only that ODI found no unreasonable risk on this record.
- The 3.5 percent and timeline calculations are this article's arithmetic from published figures, not ODI's; the 6x complaint-rate ratio is ODI's, as reported, without the underlying complaint counts published in the summaries reviewed.

## Strongest counterargument

Petitioners had a reasonable case, and ODI's answer is narrower than headlines suggest. Same nameplate, same model year, same HCU family, and owners reporting the same functional outcome (degraded braking) is a legitimate reason to ask whether the hybrid was wrongly excluded. A 6x complaint-rate gap cuts both ways if the hybrid fleet is smaller, older, and shrinking through scrappage, since a lower rate could partly reflect fewer cars still on the road rather than a safer design. Warning lights also cut both ways: a lamp that illuminates is better than silence, but a lamp that illuminates while ABS and traction control are dead still leaves a driver with degraded braking on a wet road, and "wear and tear" is a strange comfort for a safety system whose job is to work in emergencies. The honest version of the article's thesis is narrower: ODI's engineering distinction (blended brakes, force feedback, no pedal-travel effect, lamp behavior, complaint-rate gap) is real and specific, and it justifies not expanding 19V904, but it does not justify an owner ignoring an ABS light, and it does not close the question forever, since ODI reserved the right to act again.
