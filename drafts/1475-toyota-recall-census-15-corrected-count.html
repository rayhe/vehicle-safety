We Undercounted Toyota's Recalls by Half. The Real Number Is 15.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 11, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better, for us. This site published Toyota's 2026-model-year recall count twice, 5 in September and 7 this morning, and both numbers were wrong, because the method that produced them was quietly dropping exactly the cars Toyota sells now. Run the census with the full model-name vocabulary and Toyota's brand-new vehicles carry 15 federal recall campaigns filed in 2026, double the number in this morning's headline. Ford still leads at 40, GM and Toyota are now tied at 15, and the gap I reported twelve hours ago never existed.

15
federal recall campaigns touching 2026-model-year Toyota vehicles, filed Jan 1–Nov 11, 2026; twice the 7 this site published this morning

Here is the bug, and it is ours, not Toyota's. NHTSA's recalls API answers a make, model, and model-year query with either rows or an HTTP 400, and every census on this site has treated 400 as zero campaigns. That rule works for a gas model with no recalls. It also fires when the model name is wrong, and Toyota's 2026 lineup is full of hybrid-only nameplates whose base names fail even when campaigns exist under the suffixed name. Query "camry" and the API shrugs. Query "camry hybrid" and it returns 26V511, an instrument-cluster display that can fail at startup and kill the hazard lights, turn signals, and seat-belt warning. Same story for "land cruiser" versus "land cruiser hybrid" (26V673, a fuel tube that can disconnect and leak), "sienna" versus "sienna hybrid" (26V690, second-row seatbacks that may not stay secured in a crash), and "rav4" versus "rav4 hybrid" and "rav4 plug-in hybrid" (26V571 and 26V659). Six of the eight missed campaigns hid behind that gap. Two more hid behind a stale model list: the C-HR, bZ, and bZ Woodland are all 2026 debuts, and none was in the query list built from the 2025 lineup.

Three of the eight newly found campaigns are the same failure in three new cars, a control-unit fault that shuts down propulsion, fixed by a dealer software update, no parts involved. June brought the bZ (26V393), a Denso battery ECU memory fault that can kill drive power at speed across 11,495 US vehicles. September brought the C-HR (26V570), where regenerative braking can overcharge the battery until the drive system shuts itself down, 8,521 vehicles in the US. October brought the RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid (26V659), a motor-generator ECU error that can cut power at higher speeds, roughly 31,000 vehicles. RoadEthos spotted the pattern first and counted a fourth member, the previous-generation RAV4 Prime under 23V-041, recalled in 2023 for the same outcome. America's best-selling SUV, 479,288 sold in 2025, all-hybrid for 2026, inherited the failure mode of the car it replaced in its first model year, which is a different proposition from three unrelated software bugs.

Two campaigns in the corrected count are hardware, and no accounting trick removes them. Grand Highlander is Toyota's most-recalled 2026 vehicle at three campaigns, including a steering knuckle that may crack and break (26V598) and a rear axle carrier built with insufficient material strength (26V437). Land Cruiser carries one campaign, 26V673, covering 105,794 hybrids from model years 2024 through 2027, and it is the only campaign in the census where the remedy part does not exist yet. Toyota filed on October 14 without naming a cause, after 23 field reports and 64 US warranty claims, and estimates the supplemental clamp will be ready in December, while owner letters run November 29 to December 13. A Land Cruiser owner who smells fuel today is waiting on a part that is still being manufactured.

Four of the 15 are wrong stickers, load-capacity labels filed partly by regional distributors, one of them covering exactly one truck, and stripping them leaves 11 real-defect campaigns, still ahead of GM's 14 ex-manual but close enough to call noise. Campaign counts are not severity scores, and this correction is a methodology story, not a safety story: every missed campaign sat in NHTSA's public database the whole time, filed on schedule, with owner letters mailed, which is diligent reporting, not evasion. Ford's 40 partly reflects its November 2024 consent order pushing aggressive early filing, and Toyota's 15 may partly reflect the opposite. What the recount does prove is narrower and more useful: a 400 from the NHTSA API is not always a zero, a model list built last year misses this year's cars, and our Ford and GM counts from this morning were built with the same method, so a re-run of both with the corrected vocabulary is owed, starting with GM's hybrid-only nameplates.

If you own a 2026 Toyota, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls tonight. Grand Highlander owners have three campaigns to check, RAV4, 4Runner, and Tundra owners have two each, and bZ, C-HR, and RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid owners should book the free dealer software update rather than waiting for a letter, because a control-unit fault gives no warning before it acts. Land Cruiser owners: if you smell fuel, call the dealer now, do not wait for December's clamp to arrive on a schedule. And if you run a recall census of your own, query the VPIC canonical model list, then the hybrid and plug-in variants, because the API will happily tell you a car has no recalls when it only means you spelled the car wrong.
