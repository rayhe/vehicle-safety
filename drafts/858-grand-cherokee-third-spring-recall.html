Third Time's the Recall: Jeep's Grand Cherokee Gets a Third Campaign for the Same Springs

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 9, 2026

Your 2021–2023 Grand Cherokee has now been recalled three times for the same rear coil springs: three federal recalls for one spring assembly. FCA's newest campaign, 26V562, covers 328,381 vehicles and officially replaces the two earlier campaigns outright,[1] which means the fixes you already sat through are now void. If you dutifully brought your Jeep in for either of the first two repairs, the company would like you to come back.

3
Federal recall campaigns for one spring assembly, June 2023 to September 2026

Here is the cascade. In June 2023, campaign 23V413 put over 345,000 Jeeps through inspections, with dealers repositioning springs that looked wrong.[3] By January 2026, campaign 26V051 admitted the repairs themselves were the problem, pulling 80,620 vehicles back because dealers had left springs incorrectly installed or in an incomplete repair state, with 20 customer assistance records and 284 warranty claims behind it, and springs detaching after they had supposedly been fixed.[4] And this week, campaign 26V562 gave up on inspection entirely: both lower rear coil-spring isolators get replaced on every single vehicle, sight unseen, because inspection alone is officially not enough.[5]

Add it up and you get roughly 754,000 cumulative recall actions on one spring family over 39 months, though that is not 754,000 unique Jeeps. The populations overlap, and that is precisely the scandal: the same owners keep getting recalled. It took FCA three attempts and three remedy strategies, inspect, then re-inspect the repair, then replace everything, to land on a fix it actually trusts.

When a coil spring detaches at speed, it does not politely lie down. It becomes road debris with intent, a hazard for everyone driving behind you. FCA reports zero crashes and zero injuries tied to the defect, which is genuinely good news,[2] and it is also the strongest defense of the slow timeline: nobody died, so the urgency stayed theoretical while the remedy kept escalating. The recall system surfaced the complaints, the company investigated, and the fix got stricter each round. That is the process working; it just took three tries.

What the data cannot tell us is whether a single death traces to a detached spring, because NHTSA's fatality data has no way to attribute one, which makes the mortality record here clean by construction. The campaign populations overlap heavily, so 754,000 is cumulative actions rather than unique vehicles. And there is an awkward timing problem for owners: interim letters go out September 29 warning about a risk that dealers will not have the parts to fix yet, since the final-remedy date is unannounced, which means the first letter tells you about a problem the second letter has not solved.[5]

So here is what to do. If you own a 2021–2023 Grand Cherokee, Grand Cherokee L, or 4xe with standard coil springs, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, September 9, because the affected VINs go searchable starting today.[5] If you were “fixed” under the 2023 or 2026 campaigns, you are going back, again. Jeeps with air suspension and all 2024-and-later models are excluded, and FCA customer service is 1-800-853-1403. And when that second letter arrives, go, because the third repair is the charm.
