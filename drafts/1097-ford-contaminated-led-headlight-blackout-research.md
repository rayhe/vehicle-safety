# Research: 1097 — Ford's Contaminated LED Chips and the $1,500 Headlight That Dies From a Speck of Dust

**Angle (1-2 sentences):** Ford recalled 41,748 brand-new Expeditions and Super Duty trucks because the headlight assemblies may contain a contaminated LED chip, which can kill daytime running lights, parking lights, high beams AND low beams — every exterior forward light at once. Not a blown bulb you swap for $8: a semiconductor defect sealed inside an assembly you cannot open, on trucks where a new headlight costs four figures.

**Kill-test challenge:** Is this just another recall writeup after 800+ articles? No — three things make it novel: (1) the failure is at the silicon level, a contamination defect, not a design flaw or worn part; (2) the build-date windows are a forensic puzzle — Super Duty trucks built on a single day (Aug 4, 2026) versus Expeditions built across a full year (Jun 24, 2025–Jul 2, 2026) — suggesting one bad chip batch hit the truck line while a longer-contaminated supplier stream fed the SUV line; (3) it continues this site's headlight arc (tesla-headlight-recall, forte-poor-headlights, toyota-ev-headlight-blind-spot): the industry traded owner-replaceable bulbs for sealed LED assemblies, and the failure mode moved down to the chip.

**Original contribution:**
- Build-window forensics (see above) — not stated in any coverage I found.
- The "total blackout" framing: all four forward-lighting functions (DRL, parking, high, low) can die from one contaminated chip, meaning the vehicle loses both seeing AND being-seen simultaneously. FMVSS 108 noncompliance.
- Actionable check owners can do tonight: with lights on, verify low beams, high beams, DRLs, parking lights all function (roadethos). If one is out, it is not a bulb — call the dealer.
- Note the remedy asymmetry: dealers "inspect and replace as necessary" — Ford does not believe all 41,748 assemblies are contaminated; there is no owner-side test for the chip, only the blackout.

**Strongest counterargument (full strength):** Ford's own filing says it is not aware of any accidents or injuries tied to this defect, and a dashboard indicator light illuminates when low/high beams go inoperative — so drivers get a warning before driving blind. The population is also small relative to Ford's volume, and the fix is free and straightforward. One could reasonably argue this is the system working: supplier QC flagged, recall issued, nobody hurt.

**Limitations:** (1) The contamination source and supplier are undisclosed — we do not know which chip fab or Tier-2 supplier is responsible. (2) No public data on how many of the 41,748 vehicles actually carry contaminated chips vs. how many just got swept into the population. (3) NHTSA notice posted Sept 30 per USA Today; the usedcarnews citation gives campaign 26V606000 — I could not directly load the NHTSA campaign page to confirm the number, so the campaign number should be cited cautiously or omitted. (4) FARS does not code "LED chip contamination" — there is no fatality data for this specific defect. (5) Cost of a replacement LED headlight assembly is stated as context (sealed LED units are expensive) — not from a cited source; keep it general or cut.

**Key facts (all sourced):**
- 41,748 vehicles: 2025-2027 Ford Expedition; 2026 F-250 SD, F-350 SD, F-450 SD, F-550 SD (USA Today; Delaware News Journal/NHTSA).
- Defect: headlight assemblies may contain a contaminated LED chip; can cause loss of DRLs, parking lights, high beams, low beams (NHTSA via USA Today, Sept 30 notice).
- FMVSS 108 noncompliance: "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment" (fleetmaintenance.com, citing NHTSA).
- Build windows: Expeditions built Jun 24, 2025–Jul 2, 2026; Super Duty F-250/350/450 produced Aug 4, 2026 (Ford Authority).
- Ford not aware of accidents or injuries (Ford Authority).
- Dashboard indicator light illuminates for inoperative low/high beams (USA Today, citing Ford safety report).
- Remedy: inspect and replace headlight assemblies as necessary, free; owner letters mail Oct 26, 2026; VINs searchable on nhtsa.gov from Oct 26; Ford recall number 26C43; Ford customer service 1-866-436-7332 (Delaware News Journal; Ford Authority).
