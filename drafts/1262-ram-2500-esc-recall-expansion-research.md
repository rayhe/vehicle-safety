# Research — #1262: Ram 2500 ESC Recall Expansion

**Slug:** 1262-ram-2500-esc-recall-expansion
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (safety engineering / safety tech beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Proposed headline:** Ram's Stability Control Can Vanish Without Warning. This Is the Second Recall for the Same Defect.

## Kill test: PASS
- Genuinely newsworthy: a federally mandated safety system (ESC, FMVSS 126) that can fail silently, on brand-new 2026 trucks, and this is the SECOND recall for the same steering-column module defect in six months. The expansion (8,329) is larger than the original (6,605).
- Novel angle vs site history: no prior Crash Report story covers an ESC-loss recall, and none covers a same-defect recall expansion where the failure mode is explicitly "no warning prior to failure." Distinct from the Sierra grille recall-query stories (a trim part falling off) and from the Class 3 AEB story (#1253, a test failure, not a silent mandated-system loss).
- Data anchor: two NHTSA campaigns with exact counts, dates, and remedy, plus NHTSA's own ESC effectiveness research.

## Primary sources

1. **NHTSA recall campaign 26V-651 (26V651000), FCA recall 90D** — report received October 6, 2026. Certain 2026 Ram 2500 pickups "may be equipped with steering column control modules that can cause a loss of electronic stability control." Vehicles fail to comply with FMVSS No. 126, "Electronic Stability Control Systems." 8,329 vehicles potentially affected. No warning prior to failure; once the steering column control module resets, the ESC light illuminates. Dealers replace the steering column control module free of charge. Owner notification letters expected November 24, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov October 13, 2026. FCA customer service 1-800-853-1403. This campaign expands NHTSA recall 26V-244.
   - Reported via: USA Today, Oct 20, 2026 (8,329 trucks, 2026 Ram 2500, recall 90D, no warning prior to failure, letters Nov 24, FCA 800-853-1403); Trucks Parts and Service NHTSA weekly roundup, Oct 19-23, 2026 (FMVSS 126 noncompliance, 8,329 units); Vehicle Service Pros NHTSA recalls Oct 19-23, 2026 (campaign 26V651000, expands 26V244, letters Nov 24, FCA 1-800-853-1403, recall 90D).
2. **NHTSA recall campaign 26V-244 (26V244000), FCA recall 36D** — the original. Reported to NHTSA April 16, 2026. Also certain 2026 Ram 2500 pickups with steering column control modules that can cause a loss of ESC. 6,605 vehicles potentially affected. Owner notification letters mailed May 7, 2026.
   - Reported via: Automotor.news summary of the NHTSA record (26V-244, April 16, 2026, 6,605 units, FCA 36D, letters May 7, 2026; 26V-651 expands it).
3. **NHTSA, FMVSS No. 126 / ESC effectiveness research:**
   - Final rule April 2007 establishing FMVSS 126, requiring ESC on passenger cars, SUVs, vans, and pickups; phased in, applying to all new light vehicles manufactured on or after September 1, 2011 (per NHTSA's October 2025 FMVSS 126 evaluation update, DOT HS 813 749).
   - 2007 effectiveness study (1997-2004 FARS + state data): ESC reduced fatal run-off-road crashes by 36% for passenger cars and 70% for light trucks and vans; reduced fatal single-vehicle crashes (excluding pedestrian/bicycle/animal) by 36% cars / 63% LTVs.
   - October 2025 update (DOT HS 813 749): a vehicle with ESC is 51.6% less likely on average to be in a single-vehicle, first-event rollover crash than a vehicle without ESC, all else fixed.
   - ESC saved an estimated 2,202 lives from 2008 to 2010 alone (634 in 2008, 705 in 2009, 863 in 2010), per NHTSA study reported December 2012.
4. **Production window / defect-rate context (secondary, attributed):** TopSpeed (Oct 21, 2026) reports the affected trucks were built between September 13 and November 15, 2025, and that the filing puts the estimated defect rate at 0.1%. KBB notes the steering column control module is the hub for turn signals, steering-wheel buttons, and steering-angle sensors, and that a defect can disable ESC communications. Treat the 0.1% figure as a filing estimate reported by TopSpeed, not as a verified failure count: 0.1% of 8,329 is roughly 8 trucks, but the recall covers the whole production window because the defective modules cannot be identified by VIN alone in the filing as reported.

## Key numbers (all traced above)
- 8,329 — trucks in expansion recall 26V-651 (FCA 90D), Oct 6, 2026
- 6,605 — trucks in original recall 26V-244 (FCA 36D), April 16, 2026
- 14,934 — combined population across both campaigns (8,329 + 6,605)
- 0 — warnings before failure, per the NHTSA record as reported (light comes on only after the module resets)
- Nov 24, 2026 — owner letters for the expansion; May 7, 2026 — letters for the original
- 51.6% — lower likelihood of single-vehicle first-event rollover with ESC (NHTSA Oct 2025 update)
- 36% / 70% — fatal run-off-road reductions, cars / light trucks and vans (NHTSA 2007 study)
- 2,202 — lives ESC saved 2008-2010 (NHTSA)

## What is NOT claimed
- No crashes, injuries, or deaths are attributed to this defect in the sources found; the NHTSA record as reported states the consequence as increased crash risk, not a crash count. Do not invent one.
- The 0.1% defect estimate is attributed to TopSpeed's reading of the filing; it is not presented as NHTSA's confirmed failure rate.
- Towing context (heavy-duty use, trailers) is framing, not a claim that any towed crash occurred.

## Actionable takeaway (required)
- 2026 Ram 2500 owners: check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable since Oct 13, 2026) rather than waiting for the Nov 24 letter; if the ESC light comes on, treat the truck as ESC-less until the dealer replaces the module free of charge; drive accordingly on wet roads and with a trailer, because the system that brakes individual wheels to stop a slide is the one that is gone.
- Shoppers: a 2026 Ram 2500 in the Sept 13-Nov 15, 2025 build window needs the 90D (and possibly 36D) remedy confirmed before purchase, not just a clean Carfax.
