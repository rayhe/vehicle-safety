## News peg

The profile matches AAA's national data almost exactly.

## Verified facts (read directly from the AAA Foundation brief, Mar 2026)
- 2023: 919,292 police-reported hit-and-run crashes = 15.0% of all crashes (historic high). 242,886 injuries (9.9% of all crash injuries). 2,872 deaths = 7.0% of all traffic deaths, the highest percentage ever recorded. The record count was 2,972 in 2022.
- 2017-2023 totals: 17,464 hit-and-run deaths = 6.3% of all traffic deaths. Total hit-and-run injuries: 1.86 million.
- Pedestrians: 11,019 hit-and-run deaths 2017-2023 = 23.1% of all pedestrian deaths. In 2023: 1,818 pedestrian deaths, 24.9% hit-and-run. Roughly 1 in 4 dead pedestrians dies in a hit-and-run.
- Cyclists: 1,453 deaths = 21.5%; 2023: 274 deaths, 23.5%. Motorcyclists: 1,417 = 3.6%. Vehicle occupants: 3,153 = 1.8%.
- The flee-rate asymmetry: a pedestrian death is ~13x more likely to be a hit-and-run than a vehicle-occupant death (23.1% vs 1.8%).
- Time: hit-and-run deaths peak 11 PM to 2:59 AM. In that window, 33.9% of pedestrian deaths and 43.6% of cyclist deaths are hit-and-runs. Dark conditions: 25.7% of pedestrian deaths, 31.6% of cyclist deaths.
- Long-term trend: hit-and-run share of deaths was 2.6% in 1976, 4.2% in 1982, flat 3.4-4.4% until 2010, then a steady rise with a 2020 COVID-era spike, record counts 2022, record share 2023.
- Driver profile (apprehended drivers): 78.3% male, 71.4% ages 18-44. 40% lacked a valid license (17.0% never licensed, 17.5% suspended/revoked, 2.9% expired/cancelled). 56.5% were driving a vehicle they did not own. 71.2% crashed within 10 miles of home; ~85% within 25 miles.
- Intoxication motive: the brief cites Blomberg et al. (2005) — intoxicated crashers are more likely to flee, and fear of being caught DUI is a specific motive for fleeing.
- Place: large cities (>500k pop): 32.6% of pedestrian deaths are hit-and-runs vs 17.3% rural. Most socially vulnerable quintile of zip codes: 11.7% of all deaths are hit-and-runs (6x the absolute deaths of the least vulnerable quintile: 7,943 vs 1,203); pedestrians 29.8% vs 15.0%.
- Countermeasures: no single fix; preventing crashes prevents hit-and-runs; deterrence works through judicial effectiveness (Denti & Modica, 2024); catching drivers is the realistic lever.
- Fresno peg details: 78-year-old pedestrian pushing a cart, struck Sept 13 at 8:24 PM, driver fled; suspect ID'd via ALPR, vehicle found abandoned under tarps and rocks; suspect Mariah Irion, 30, arrested Oct 9 on felony hit-and-run causing death, vehicular manslaughter, and driving on a suspended license. (Fresno PD via Crime Voice, Oct 15, 2026)
- Newark peg: Oct 11, 2026, 7:44 PM, 62-year-old pedestrian in a marked crosswalk on Route 21 struck by a pickup that fled; driver arrested, charged with second-degree leaving the scene. (Englebrook Independent News, Oct 15, 2026)

## The novel angle (kill test: PASS)
Cross-tabulate WHO gets left on the road against WHO does the leaving. A driver who hits a pedestrian is an order of magnitude more likely to flee than one who hits another car (23.1% vs 1.8%), and the fleer profile is not a panicked everyman: unlicensed, somebody else's car, late night, dark, within miles of home, often intoxicated. Zero coverage in 1,183 site articles. The AAA brief is the first national accounting since Benson et al. 2018.

## Limitations (for the article)
- CRSS excludes crashes never reported to police; the true hit-and-run count is higher than 919,292.
- The flee rate is conditional on a crash happening, not a per-mile risk. It answers "when there's a body, how often is the driver gone?" not "how likely am I to be hit?"
- The driver profile comes only from apprehended drivers; the ones never caught may differ (and the brief is honest about this).
- SVI correlation is not causation; infrastructure gaps (speeds, markings) co-vary with vulnerability.
- Data run 2017-2023; 2024-2026 fatal FARS data were not in the brief. The trend direction after 2023 is unmeasured.

## Strongest counterargument (for the article)
Part of the long-term rise could be better crash attribution rather than worse behavior: FARS hit-and-run coding has improved over decades, so some of the increase from 3.4% to 7.0% is measurement, not moral decay. Also, the flee-rate asymmetry has a mechanical explanation: hitting a pedestrian at night is easier to "not notice" than hitting a car, and a pedestrian crash leaves less evidence that a crash even occurred. The data can't fully separate cold calculation from genuine non-detection.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Dashcam, front and rear: when the other driver doesn't stop, your footage is the only witness. The Fresno driver was caught by ALPR days later; cameras close cases.
- Walk facing traffic with reflective gear. One in four pedestrian deaths is a hit-and-run, and that share hits 33.9% between 11 PM and 3 AM.
- If you're in a crash: get the plate in the first 10 seconds. Fleers decide in seconds.
- Report immediately. Most fleers crash within 10 miles of home and 85% are caught near home when reported fast.
- Your own uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage matters more than you think: 40% of fatal fleers had no valid license, which correlates with no insurance.
