Toyota Recalled 105,794 Land Cruisers Without Knowing What's Wrong With Them

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

For twenty-seven months, Toyota's engineers tried to break a plastic clip the size of a thumbnail: they pulled it, vibrated it, pressurized it, and the clip held every time. Out in the world, the clips kept lifting, the fuel tubes kept separating, and gasoline kept finding its way onto the ground. On October 8, Toyota recalled 105,794 Land Cruiser Hybrids for a defect it admits it cannot explain.[1][5] None of those numbers are the story here; the confession is.

1,058
roughly how many of the 105,794 trucks carry the defect, if Toyota's filed 1% estimate is taken at face value; it is a number the company had to type, not one it measured

Simple is the problem, and the joint proves it. A resin tube leaves the tank, meets a metal tube heading for the engine, and a small retaining clip holds them together.[3] When the clip lifts or goes missing, the tubes can part, and fuel flows until the hybrid system notices the pressure drop and kills the gas engine. Toyota ran that exact scenario on a parked truck to see what would spill, and the pump ran until the engine quit, which tells you everything about how much fuel a parked truck can dump while nobody is watching.[5] Multiple gallons on the ground. No other Toyota or Lexus sold in America uses this joint, so at least the blast radius has a fence around it.[5]

Dealers first reported lifted clips in July 2024, and Toyota's lab then spent two years failing to reproduce the failure, which is an unusual way to spend two years.[5] A 1 percent defect share appears in the filing because the NHTSA form demands a number, and by the company's own telling, that 1 means "we don't know."[5] One percent of 105,794 is about 1,058 trucks, with the 2025 model year holding the biggest share at 41,955.[2] Against that sit 23 field technical reports and 64 warranty claims, an incidence of roughly eight-hundredths of a percent, with no confirmed injuries or fires.[2][6] Toyota recalled on severity, not frequency, the system working as designed, which is also a little unnerving.

If you own a 2024-2027 Land Cruiser Hybrid: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, not when the letter arrives between November 29 and December 13.[1] Dealers should have the supplemental clamp in December, and the repair is free whether or not your warranty is alive.[3] If you are shopping new: about 800 fresh Land Cruisers sat in dealer inventory under a stop-sale in mid-October, and Toyota will not certify one as used until the clamp is on, so check the VIN before you trust the sticker.[5] The honest version: a clamp restrains a symptom. The root cause is still out there, unidentified, doing whatever it does to plastic clips in the dark. Toyota's lab couldn't catch it in 27 months. You get to drive with it until December.
