The Gap

The All-New RAV4 Flunked IIHS’s Top Safety Award. The Winner Has Triple the Real-World Death Rate.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
December 8, 2027

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this: the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety just ran its crash-test gauntlet on six new vehicles, and Toyota’s redesigned RAV4, the best-selling SUV in America, walked away without a Top Safety Pick award.[1] Not the structure, the airbags, or the headlights: blame the back seat. The updated moderate-overlap test seats a child-sized dummy behind the driver, and in the 2026 RAV4 the shoulder belt rode up too far across the dummy’s torso.[2] Rear head/neck, chest, and restraints all graded Acceptable instead of Good. Under the 2026 criteria, Acceptable in any of the three core crash tests is an automatic disqualification.

RAV4 deaths per 100 million miles, 2014–2023 (FARS): one of the lowest rates on record, yet IIHS flunked it

Worse still: Toyota’s heavier, pricier electrified RAV4 scored Marginal in the same test, with IIHS marking its rear-passenger restraints Poor.[3] Same body, same crash, different kinematics for the family sitting behind you. If you’ve been waiting for the plug-in hybrid as the safe splurge, the lab just vetoed that plan.

And now the part that should make you uncomfortable. Only one vehicle in the batch earned an award: the Nissan Kicks, a $22,000 subcompact whose decade fatality rate is 0.69 per 100 million miles, more than triple the RAV4’s 0.19.[5] In the lab, the Kicks is the safest car in the group. On the road, it’s the deadliest, and somebody’s wrong about the gap.

That win comes with an asterisk: Nissan’s redesigned Kicks initially scored Poor on the same rear-seat test, then revised the rear belts mid-year; IIHS says only Kicks built after June 2026 qualify for the award.[4] A 2026 Kicks built in May and a 2026 Kicks built in July are different cars, safety-wise. Fix it yourself: open the driver’s door, read the certification label on the jamb, and check the month. This is the second build-month asterisk in two months: September’s Telluride award had one too.

Fairness requires the counterargument at full strength: Acceptable is not Poor, Toyota didn’t build an unsafe car, and the award bar moved underneath it. And the FARS numbers above span every model year of each nameplate: the 2026 RAV4 and the belt-fixed Kicks barely exist in that data. You’re comparing generations, not vehicles. But when the cheapest car in the test wins the lab and loses the decade, the verdict deserves a raised eyebrow.

What you actually do: if you’re buying a Kicks, read the jamb sticker and walk away from anything built before July 2026. If you’re buying a RAV4, know the rear seat is the weak link, and the plug-in hybrid is weaker than the standard hybrid. Nobody expected to write that sentence. If the award itself matters to you, the Hyundai Tucson holds Top Safety Pick+ and beat the RAV4 on the test that mattered.[3] And whatever you buy, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before the test drive, not after.
