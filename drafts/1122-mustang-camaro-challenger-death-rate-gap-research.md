# Research: Mustang vs Camaro vs Challenger — The Muscle-Car Death-Rate Gap
**Article #1122 · Clara Rollover · Kicker: The Gap · 2026-10-10**

## News peg
None required; original FARS cross-tabulation. Dataset covers 2014-2023 fatal crashes
(337 models, 50+ deaths or >1k annual sales). Duplicate-checked full queue:
no Challenger / muscle-car / pony-car article exists; Mustang appears only in
recall pieces and one E-350 comparison (different angle). Novel for a 1,121-article site.

## Kill test
Passes. Three American muscle cars, same segment, same price bracket,
nearly identical driver impairment profiles — yet per-mile death rates differ
by 6x. The Challenger, whose buyers are the YOUNGEST of the three, is the safest.
The mechanism is physics (mass, wheelbase), not driver virtue.

## Data (NHTSA FARS 2014-2023, via fars_output.js)

| Model | Rate (/100M VMT) | Deaths | Fleet | Fatal crashes | Impairment any% (n drivers) | Alc% | Drug% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ford Mustang | 6.02 | 2,739 | 568,750 | 3,938 | 21.9 (4,664) | 16.4 | 9.6 |
| Chevrolet Camaro | 3.44 | 1,204 | 437,500 | 1,920 | 23.0 (2,832) | 17.1 | 10.2 |
| Dodge Challenger | 1.00 | 385 | 481,250 | 738 | 22.5 (2,037) | 17.5 | 9.1 |

Derived:
- Mustang/Challenger rate ratio = 6.02x
- Camaro/Challenger = 3.44x
- Mustang total deaths 2,739 vs Challenger 385 = 7.1x, on a fleet only 1.18x larger
- Impairment spread across the three: 21.9-23.0% (max 1.1 pt gap) — statistically
  indistinguishable driver pools. Drug impairment: 9.1-10.2%. The drivers are the same
  drivers. The cars are not the same car.
- Challenger ended production Dec 2023; all figures reflect the 2008-2023 generation.

## Mechanism (IIHS size/weight research)
Challenger curb weight ~4,200-4,400 lb (R/T-SXT range), wheelbase 116.2 in —
the biggest, heaviest of the three (Mustang ~3,700-3,800 lb, 107.1 in; Camaro
~3,700 lb, 110.7 in). IIHS's vehicle size and weight research: in multi-vehicle
crashes the heavier vehicle shields its occupants; mass is the single largest
vehicle attribute in two-vehicle fatality outcomes. A longer, softer-sprung
Challenger also puts distance between the driver and the point of impact, and
its touring-oriented setup (per Dodge, pitched as a daily-driver grand tourer)
correlates with fewer loss-of-control single-vehicle crashes.

## Buyer-age paradox (kills the "cautious older driver" counterargument)
Dodge Challenger brand manager Kevin Hellman told Muscle Cars & Trucks (2019)
the average new Challenger buyer was 51 — the YOUNGEST average buyer in the
segment, younger than Mustang and Camaro buyers (sources: Jalopnik, Carscoops,
autoevolution, Ford Authority; Federal Reserve average new-car buyer age 53).
The safest of the three cars is driven by the youngest buyers. Driver age
cannot explain the gap; mass can.

## Counterargument (full strength)
Weight is not free: the Challenger's mass endangers OTHER road users, and IIHS
notes heavier vehicles are more lethal to the occupants of smaller cars. A
6.02x rate gap also reflects usage: Mustangs may be driven more aggressively
(horsepower-for-dollar culture, track days, younger-at-heart marketing) in ways
toxicology screens cannot capture. FARS also skews toward older, cheaper used
Mustangs bought by higher-risk drivers — the fleet-age distribution may differ.
The rate is estimated from VMT estimates, not odometer readings (±15% for
low-volume models). And the Challenger's AWD option (unique in the trio)
confounds weather-dependence: northern daily-driver Challengers face different
crash exposure than garage-kept summer Mustangs.

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only; injury outcomes unknown.
- VMT estimates, not measured mileage.
- 2014-2023 window; Challenger production ended 2023, used-market only now.
- Curb weights vary by trim; figures quoted are model-range midpoints.

## Actionable takeaway
Shopping the used muscle-car market? The Challenger's per-mile death rate is
one-sixth the Mustang's with the same driver profile. Weight is safety you can
buy: a 4,300-lb car in a two-car crash is the heavier party. Check the exact
model year's IIHS ratings before buying, verify recall status at nhtsa.gov/recalls,
and remember that ESC and crash prevention standard-fit lists (nhtsa.gov/ratings)
matter more than horsepower.
