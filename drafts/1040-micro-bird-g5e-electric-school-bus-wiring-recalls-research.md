# Research: 1040 — Micro Bird G5e Electric School Buses: Five Electrical Recalls in Nine Months

## Angle
The Micro Bird G5e, the Type A electric school bus districts bought with EPA Clean School Bus money, has logged FIVE electrical recall campaigns in 2026. The latest two (September 2026, NHTSA 26V591 and 26V594) cover 278 buses whose wiring harness was routed so it can chafe against a metal bracket, damaging the wire and killing drive power mid-route. In January 2026, the same platform was recalled because its power steering pump can die without warning (486 buses) and because a loose air-conditioning power cable can melt its control board (2,428 school buses + 513 transit buses, fire risk). The structural point: these are not five random defects. They are all the same subsystem family (vehicle electrical) on the same platform, in one calendar year, on the vehicle that carries children. A wire routed against a sharp bracket is a day-one design-review failure.

## The novel calculations
- Cross-campaign tally: nobody has aggregated Micro Bird's 2026 electrical recalls. Five campaigns, one platform, one subsystem family (wiring harness, power steering pump, A/C power cable), nine months, 3,705 buses.
- The two-filing observation: 26V591 (Micro Bird USA LLC, 81 units) and 26V594 (Corp. Micro Bird Inc., 197 units) describe the identical defect in the identical week. Micro Bird is a Canadian company (Drummondville, Quebec; Blue Bird/Girardin joint venture) with a US entity; this reads as the same defect filed through both corporate identities. NHTSA paperwork, nobody connecting the dots.
- The irony math: EPA's program has delivered ~1,100 of 2,600 ordered EV buses while publicly fighting reliability complaints; Micro Bird, one of the early Type A electric builders, has spent 2026 recalling its electricals.

## Strongest counterargument
Five campaigns sounds damning, but the per-campaign numbers are small and the two September filings may describe the same 278 (or fewer) buses counted twice through two corporate entities. Zero injuries or crashes are reported across the September recalls, and there is no Do-Not-Drive advisory. Recalls are the system working: a small manufacturer found the chafing harness and filed proactively. Electric school buses still displace diesel exhaust, and the health case is real: one industry analysis (cited via ChargedEVs/Highland) estimates replacing five diesel buses cuts pediatric asthma risk for ~1,500 students. A wire chafing on a bracket is a manufacturing-routing fix, not an indictment of electrification. And school buses remain, by every statistical measure, the safest way to move children.

## Limitations
- Unit counts per recall come from secondary aggregators (oemdtc, Trucks Parts Service, FleetOwner) citing the filings; I did not pull the Part 573 PDFs. Counts are "potentially involved," not confirmed defects.
- 26V591 and 26V594 may cover overlapping populations (US entity vs. Canadian parent filing the same defect); treat the 278 and the 3,705 as upper bounds and say so.
- No confirmed field incidents, crashes, or injuries are published for the September recalls. Do not imply a bus has died mid-route; the risk is documented, the event count is zero.
- Cannot compute a per-vehicle defect rate: total G5e fleet size is not public. Do not claim "X% of G5e buses are defective."
- FARS does not meaningfully cover school bus models; this article relies on recall documents, not crash outcomes. Say so.
- EPA program stats via NY Post; the Post's framing is adversarial to the program. Use the numbers (attributed to EPA via the Post), not the editorializing.

## Actionable takeaways
- District fleet managers running Micro Bird G5e buses (2025-2027): do not wait for the November 10 letters. Check VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls now against 26V591 and 26V594; call Micro Bird at 819-477-2012 (ref 26-122-HUS / 26-122-HVS). A physical inspection of the harness routing near the bracket takes minutes and the fix is free.
- Parents: ask your district whether its electric buses have had the recall work completed. The buses are yellow and quiet; the question is whether a wire is rubbing a bracket.
- Shopping districts evaluating electric bids: the EPA's own September numbers say delivery and reliability are the program's weak points. Ask bidders for their 2026 recall history before signing.
