## 1. General Editor — 8.5
The census format does exactly what the series promises: one brand, one year, one tally, and the tally is the story. The campaign-by-campaign rundown is dense but each entry carries its own defect, count, and date, so nothing repeats. The dock is length. At 1,167 words this is more than double the 300-500 word guide, though well inside the recent census range (1,521 for #1516) and every paragraph earns its space. The middle rundown paragraph is a long data run; a table would have carried it faster, but the prose keeps Axle's voice intact. The lede buries nothing and the pull stat is the thesis number. Ship it.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
Two injuries are handled with care throughout: always "potentially related," always attributed to Harley's filing, never presented as confirmed causation. No victim is named, no gore, no crash porn. Blame lands on supplier manufacturing flaws and on Harley's own defect estimate, never on riders, and the actionable guidance (clutch in, brake covered, check the VIN) protects readers rather than shaming them. The counterargument is given real room and real force. The hero image shows a parked bike in a service bay, no crash imagery. No self-congratulation, no moralizing. Ship it.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5
The headline is built to travel and the pull stat (136,077) is the number people will quote. "Harley recalled 21,643 bikes to fix roughly 22 of them" is the second quotable line and the one most likely to survive a screenshot. The piece loses half a point because the shareable payload is front-loaded: after the 0.1% paradox, the article becomes chronology and caveats that reward reading rather than skimming, and there is no single-sentence pull quote in the back half as strong as the headline. Fine for the site's audience; slightly less so for a drive-by share.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0
Every checkable claim verified against primary documents. Campaign numbers, dates, and populations all match NHTSA filings and the Harleysite compilation: 26V075 (9,401), 26V234 (16,994), 26V270 (88,039 US), 26V730 (21,643). The 0.1% defect estimate is taken from the Part 573 itself, not the looser "<1%" in press coverage, and the article says so. The 25V590 extension is correctly framed as a carryover, not a new defect. Owner-letter dates (Nov 16-23, 2026) match the Part 573 schedule. RQ26004 is not invoked here and nothing is framed as a finding that NHTSA has not made. One nit: the Feb 9 and Apr 13 filing dates for 26V075 and 26V234 are sourced from the Harleysite overview rather than the Part 573 PDFs directly; the overview compiles NHTSA filings and the dates are consistent with the campaign numbers, but the PDFs would be stronger. Arithmetic verified: tally sums to 136,077; shares 64.7/15.9/12.5/6.9 sum to 100.0; 21,643 x 0.001 = 21.6.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0
Original contribution is real and stated: the 136,077 census tally with explicit overlap accounting, the share breakdown showing one recall at 64.7%, the 0.1% paradox (about 22 defective bikes against 2 injuries), the carryover correction (three genuinely new campaigns, not four), and the touring-line-twice finding (four model codes in two campaigns). Limitations are explicit and honest: populations overlap, US vs worldwide counts differ, the defect estimate is Harley's own number, injuries are "potentially related" not confirmed, and no FARS data exists for 2026 Harleys. The counterargument is genuinely steelmanned rather than strawmanned ("recalls are the safety system working"). Methodology is in the disclaimer as well as the body. Five references, two of them primary NHTSA documents plus the live API query.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0
The pull stat is the thesis number and it is correct, with the overlap caveat in the pull-label itself. Campaign data is presented chronologically in prose with each population attached to its defect mode, which reads better than a table for this story, though a table would have helped the length problem the General Editor flagged. All numbers are consistent between body, pull stat, and disclaimer, and the US-vs-worldwide distinction for 26V270 is stated in both body and disclaimer. No chart needed: the story is a count, and the count is the chart. The share percentages are the article's real visualization and they are correct.
