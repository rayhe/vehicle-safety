# Research: Jaywalking Didn't Kill 7,080 Pedestrians. Drivers Who Didn't Yield Did.

## Peg
October is Pedestrian Safety Month. NHTSA's final 2024 FARS data is out, including the
pedestrian related-factors table that lets us test the jaywalking story against the record.

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA FARS Encyclopedia, People: Pedestrians, 2024** (www-fars.nhtsa.dot.gov/People/PeoplePedestrians.aspx)
   - Total pedestrians killed 2024: 7,080
   - Failure to yield right of way: 3,690 (52.1%)
   - Improper crossing of roadway or intersection: 1,484 (21.0%)
   - Not visible (dark clothing, no lighting, etc.): 977 (13.8%)
   - In roadway improperly (standing, lying, working, playing): 795 (11.2%)
   - Darting or running into road: 696 (9.8%)
   - Under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or medication: 596 (8.4%)
   - Unknown: 1,348 (19.0%)
   - Note on the table itself: sums exceed 100% because more than one factor may be present per pedestrian.
2. **NHTSA Pedestrian Safety page** (nhtsa.gov/road-safety/pedestrian-safety)
   - 7,080 pedestrians killed in 2024; 71,000+ injured
   - A pedestrian was killed every 74 minutes in 2024
3. **NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts Research Note, Overview of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes in 2024**
   (crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov, publication 813791)
   - Pedestrian fatalities fell 287 (3.9%) vs 2023; total fatalities 39,345 (est.)
   - VMT rose 1.5% to 3,294 billion in 2024
4. **IIHS, Pedestrians fatality statistics** (iihs.org/research-areas/fatality-statistics/detail/pedestrians, posted June 2026)
   - 7,080 pedestrian deaths in 2024 = 18% of all crash fatalities
   - Pedestrian deaths up 72% since their 2009 low point
   - Vehicles with pedestrian-detecting automatic braking had a 27% lower rate of pedestrian crashes (Cicchino, 2022)

## Original calculations (the novel contribution)
- Failure-to-yield (3,690) is **2.49x** improper crossing (1,484). The single biggest
  coded factor in pedestrian deaths is a driver behavior, not a pedestrian behavior.
- Failure-to-yield alone accounts for **52.1%** of all pedestrian deaths; improper crossing
  plus darting combined (2,180) still trail it by 1,510 deaths.
- Per-day arithmetic (2024 was a leap year, 366 days): 7,080 / 366 = **19.3 pedestrian
  deaths per day**; failure-to-yield deaths alone = **10.1 per day**, more than one every
  2.5 hours.
- The "not visible" factor (977, 13.8%) is the third-largest factor, which points at
  lighting and conspicuity, not at crossing location: a pedestrian can be in a crosswalk
  and still be coded not-visible.
- Framing check: jaywalking enforcement targets the 21.0% (improper crossing) and 9.8%
  (darting) buckets. Even perfect elimination of both would leave the majority factor
  untouched. Yield enforcement, by contrast, targets the 52.1% bucket.

## Strongest counterargument
- FARS related factors are police-reported at the scene, not adjudicated findings; a
  "failure to yield" code does not prove the driver was legally at fault, and factors
  overlap (a darting pedestrian in dark clothing can also be coded failure-to-yield).
- 19.0% of cases carry an unknown factor, so the ranking rests on the 81% with coded
  factors; the true shares could shift.
- Some "failure to yield" cases involve pedestrians crossing outside crosswalks where
  drivers had little time to react; the code describes the sequence, not the blame.
- The honest read: the table measures what police recorded about the crash sequence,
  and on that record the dominant sequence is a driver not yielding, not a pedestrian
  crossing badly. That is still a meaningful rebuke to the jaywalking-first story.

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only; the 71,000+ injured pedestrians are outside this
  table, and injury crashes may have a different factor mix.
- Factor percentages sum above 100% (multiple factors per crash); do not present the
  buckets as mutually exclusive slices of a pie.
- 2024 is the latest final FARS year; 2025-2026 patterns may differ.
- "Not visible" conflates dark clothing, no lighting, and other visibility failures;
  it cannot by itself justify a clothing prescription.

## Actionable takeaways
- For drivers: yielding is the single highest-leverage behavior in the pedestrian death
  record. Treat every unmarked crossing as a yield zone, especially after dark.
- For pedestrians: conspicuity matters (13.8% not-visible), but no amount of reflective
  gear fixes a driver who does not yield; crossing location alone is not the risk.
- For buyers: pedestrian-detecting AEB cut pedestrian crash rates 27% in IIHS's study;
  check whether a used car's AEB actually detects pedestrians, not just vehicles.
- For cities: the factor table argues for yield enforcement and lighting over
  jaywalking crackdowns.
