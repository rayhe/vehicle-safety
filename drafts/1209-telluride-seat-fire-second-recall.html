Kia Fixed the Telluride’s Fire-Prone Seats. Seven of Them Burned Anyway.

By Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 3, 2027

Before you trust the recall sticker on a used Telluride, read what Kia admitted this summer. Kia recalled approximately 462,869 model-year 2020–2024 Tellurides because the front power seat motors can overheat and start fires. This is the second recall for the same defect: the 2024 fix, a reinforcing bracket Kia dealers were supposed to install, was installed wrong often enough that Kia is ordering even the “repaired” cars back into the shop.[1]

The failure starts with something absurdly ordinary. A passenger climbing in bumps the seat’s side cover or slide knob with enough force to dislodge the power seat switch inside. The motor keeps running after you let go. It runs and runs, overheating until it melts or ignites, and it can do this while you are driving or while the car sits parked in your garage with the ignition off.[2] The warning signs read like a haunted car checklist: a slide knob that sticks, a seat that keeps gliding after you release the switch, a burning smell, smoke curling from beneath the cushion.

Kia’s first answer, recall 24V407 in June 2024, was mechanical: dealers would bolt a reinforcing bracket onto the switch housing and swap the slide knob. What happened next is documented in Kia’s own chronology filing, and it is damning. In July 2025, Kia’s safety office inspected a burned Telluride, designated Vehicle #3, and confirmed the fire started at the driver-side tilt motor with no sign the switch’s back cover had ever come loose.[3] Translation: the bracket was there, the fire happened anyway. X-ray imaging and a full tear-down that August found the tilt motor’s control shaft misaligned in a way no bracket could prevent. Kia had spent a year reinforcing the wrong thing.

7
Seat fires on Tellurides Kia had already “repaired” under the 2024 recall, plus 11 melted seat motors, October 2024–April 2026

Between the failed fix and the replacement recall lie 25 months and 18 incidents: seven fires, eleven melted motors, zero injuries, zero fatalities, and a growing file of field data that finally forced Kia’s hand in June 2026.[3] The replacement recall, 26V430, abandons the mechanical approach entirely. Dealers now install an electronic fuse assembly that cuts power to the seat motor if it runs too long, which is the fix the problem always needed: an electrical kill-switch for a runaway electrical motor, not a bracket hoping the switch stays put. It is free, owner letters went out in August 2026, and until it is installed Kia says to park outside and away from structures.[1]

Now the part that should embarrass everyone involved. The cheapest Telluride, the LX trim with manual front seats, was never at risk, because there is no power motor to run away. Tellurides built from May 30, 2024 got a reinforced switch at the factory and are excluded too. The fire-prone population is everyone in between: buyers who paid extra for power seats got a fire feature, buyers who took their cars in for the 2024 recall got a fix that didn’t fix, and both groups are now told to park on the street until further notice. A census I ran of 2026 NHTSA campaigns found exactly two park-outside advisories in the sample, and this is the only one where the manufacturer’s own prior repair is named in the defect description as a failure mode.[4]

Steel yourself for the defense, because it has numbers on its side. Eighteen incidents across 462,869 vehicles is an incident rate around four one-thousandths of one percent, nobody was hurt, and Kia did eventually re-recall with a genuinely better-engineered remedy instead of doubling down on brackets. The system worked, in the sense that a slow, expensive, fire-documented system eventually produced the right fuse. That defense is real. It does not change the fact that for two years, the official answer to “is my car fixed” was yes, and the correct answer was nobody knew.

Here is what you actually do about it. If you own a 2020–2024 Telluride that is not the LX trim and was built before May 30, 2024, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now, even if, especially if, you already had the 2024 seat recall performed. That old repair is void; the new one supersedes it. Park outside until the fuse assembly is in. Memorize the warning signs, because they are specific and they precede the fire: the sticking knob, the seat that will not stop moving, the melting smell, the smoke. Call Kia at 1-800-333-4542 if your dealer plays dumb about SC374. And the next time a service advisor hands you a “recall completed” slip, remember the Telluride: completed is a claim, not a fact, and sometimes you have to come back for the recall of the recall.
