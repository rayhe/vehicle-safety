### 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Lede reframes the drunk-driving victim in two sentences, then lands the 117 with the cadence (one every 3.1 days) before any table appears. Structure is tight: breakdown, ratio, context, counterargument, action. ### 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.7/10
No individual victim is mined for color; every figure is aggregate from the federal census. The counterargument is stated at full strength, including that FARS does not verify the driver was a parent or guardian, and the limitations name the imputation and causation caveats honestly. The headline is the one place the piece overreaches: "His Own Back Seat" implies the driver's own car and a parental role the data does not verify, and the body has to walk it back in the counterargument paragraph. The walk-back is present and explicit, which is why this is 8.7 and not lower, but the headline still does work the evidence cannot fully support.

### 4. Social / Shareability — 9.0/10
Headline is the strongest share trigger in this batch: a single sentence that reframes a familiar crime. Pull stat 117 with the 3.1-day cadence is the number people will repeat. "They never got a vote" is a ready-made pull quote. The 3.0x overrepresentation ratio gives the piece a second hook for readers who want the math. Slightly weaker on the card alone, where the mechanism (passengers, not victims of the other lane) needs the lede to land.

### 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10

The actionable section correctly frames child endangerment enhancements as state-dependent ("check your state") rather than asserting a uniform rule. The VIN-check closer points to nhtsa.gov/recalls with no overstatement. The one pre-panel fix (adults vs all-ages 12%) was caught before scoring.

### 6. Research Rigor — 8.9/10
Genuine original contribution: the child-passenger decomposition (117 of 221, 53%), the 3.0x overrepresentation ratio, and the 3.1-day cadence are computed here from two fact sheets nobody on the site had cross-tabulated. Methodology is transparent (formulas and denominators shown in the research notes and reproducible from the references). Limitations are explicit: BAC imputation, involvement vs causation, the unverified driver-child relationship, ARF revision risk, the descriptive nature of the 3.0x ratio. The counterargument is stated at full strength, including that there is no new statute hiding in the table. Docked slightly because the 117-passenger finding rests on a single fact-sheet table and the piece does not test it against a second year of data.

### 7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10
Every claim carries both the count and the share, and ratios are always anchored to a baseline a reader can feel (2.6% vs 7.9%, 12% vs 53%). The pull stat is the most surprising number in the piece. The role breakdown is presented as a clean four-line decomposition. The state totals (Texas 1,676, California 1,311, Florida 769) are the one unanchored element: raw counts with no population or VMT denominator, so they read as "big states are big" rather than information.
