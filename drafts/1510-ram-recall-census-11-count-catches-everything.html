Ram Recalled Its 2026 Trucks 11 Times. The Count Misses Almost Nothing

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again. They didn't get better, and Ram's eight 2026 nameplates carry exactly 11 recall campaigns filed in 2026, verified across 37 NHTSA API queries and cross-checked against the Stellantis-wide census this site ran two days ago. Eleven is a real number. This time it is also close to the whole number, because Ram filed exactly one more recall in 2026 that touches no 2026 model at all, and that one covers 16 vehicles spread across four model lines.[1][13]

16
Ram vehicles recalled in 2026 under campaigns the 11-count misses. Jeep's excluded set held 1.47 million

Start with the eleven that count, biggest first: the seat-belt recall 26V495 covers 1,271,294 Ram 1500s in the United States, model years 2019 through 2026, whose second-row buckle anchors may never have been attached to the body. Next, the rearview-camera blackout 26V531 covers 844,027 vehicles across four Stellantis brands, and Ram's share is 319,921, from 211,114 Ram 1500s down to three ProMaster EVs. Nineteen days later came a second camera campaign, 26V560, another 239,131 Ram 1500s for the same defect, which puts the two camera recalls at 1,083,158 American vehicles with screens that can go dark. A trailer-tow module failure, 26V059, covers 456,287 vehicles, and Ram's slice is 455,909 of them, while Jeep's is 378. An instrument-cluster software error, 26V225, covers 65,348 trucks whose 3.5-inch display can go blank at startup, taking the BRAKE, ESC, and TPMS warning lights with it. Six more sit underneath that floor: a Ram 2500 that can outrun its own tires, a 2026 Ram 1500 whose premium headlamps can quietly switch off, a TPMS that cannot tell a driver the tires are low, two filings for one ESC defect, and a side-airbag recall covering exactly five trucks.[2][3][4][5][6]

Per model, the Ram 1500 carries eight campaigns and the Ram 2500 carries six, while the 3500, 4500, and 5500 chassis cabs share the same two, and three ProMaster vans carry zero, on paper. Four of the 1500's eight campaigns are electrical: the instrument cluster, the headlamps, and the radio and camera twice. Count a December 2025 instrument-panel recall for the same truck, filed just outside the 2026 window, and the 1500's wiring has now failed badly enough for a federal recall five times in under a year. Suppliers keep appearing in the fine print: Marelli North America built the headlamp assemblies whose connector terminals were never fully seated, and Harman Becker wrote the radio software that blanks the camera. Wiring is the story here, not any single campaign.[1][6][7][12]

One campaign in the count is really two filings for one defect. Campaign 26V244, filed in April, covered 6,605 Ram 2500s whose steering column control module can kill electronic stability control. Campaign 26V651, filed in October, covers 8,329 of the same trucks for the same module. FCA's first scope missed 1,724 trucks, a 26 percent undercount, and the correction took six months. Owners cleared under the April recall need to check again. At the other end of the scale sits 26V622: exactly five 2026 Ram 1500 crew cabs, built between February 28 and May 18, 2026, whose side-airbag connectors may not be secured. Five VINs, out of hundreds of thousands of trucks built, and the federal system counts it as a full campaign, same as the 1.27-million-truck seat-belt recall.[9][10]

Back to the big one, because 1.27 million deserves a closer look. FCA estimates only 0.1 percent of the 26V495 population actually carries the loose anchor, about 1,271 trucks, and the company knows of one potentially related injury and no fatalities. That estimate is cold comfort to an owner, because the defect has no symptom: no noise, no warning light, nothing a driver can feel. A buckle anchor either holds in a crash or it does not. Canada flagged the concern first, in January 2026, after a 2025 Ram 1500 surfaced with a loose anchor; FCA's regulations committee did not determine noncompliance until July 23. Six months passed between the flag and the filing. Check the VIN, get the free inspection, and do not assume a truck that looks fine is fine.[2][11]

Now the one the count misses is campaign 26V327, filed May 21, covering 16 vehicles across four model lines, a rearview-camera defect spread over Pacifica, Ram, and Wrangler, and Ram's share is a subset of that 16. That is the entire excluded set, all of it. Put the two sides together and the honest headline is not just “Ram: 11.” Counted campaigns cover at least 2,401,650 Ram campaign-units, on the ten campaigns with verified Ram splits, while excluded campaigns cover at most 16 Rams. For every Ram the census misses, it catches at least 150,000. Jeep's census ran the other way, with the excluded set 2.63 times the counted one, and the difference is the story: Ram's biggest recall of the year is inside the count, Jeep's biggest was outside it. Zero of the 11 is flagged over-the-air in NHTSA's API, the same zero-for-everything result every census in this series has returned, even though the 26V531 and 26V560 radio fixes are delivered over the air. Zero park-outside flags, zero do-not-drive flags, in the counted or the excluded Ram picture.[1][13]

Fairness requires the other side, stated at full strength, because 2.4 million sounds alarming and the truth is quieter. Campaign counts are not severity scores, and one seat-belt recall dominates the counted total, with a defect estimate of 0.1 percent, about 1,271 trucks out of 1,271,294. Two of the eleven are the same camera defect filed twice, and two more are the same ESC defect filed twice, so the count contains four filings for two defects. Ram sells five pickup nameplates plus three vans, so it files more campaigns than a brand selling three, and the per-model numbers are the honest comparison, not the brand total. Compare the ratio with care: the 2,401,650 figure is a campaign-unit sum, not a count of distinct trucks, because a 2026 Ram 1500 can appear in both camera campaigns, and the excluded total is a ceiling, not a measurement. All of that is true, and none of it changes the part that matters: a reader who takes “Ram: 11” as the brand's 2026 safety record is wrong by at most 16 trucks, and the biggest one, the seat-belt recall that covers eight model years, is inside the count.

What this census does not prove: the counted Ram-vehicle total is a campaign-unit sum, not a distinct-truck total, because a truck can appear in two campaigns. 26V244's 6,605 trucks sit inside 26V651's 8,329 and are counted once. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates for all model years in each campaign, not 2026-only counts, so no 2026-only Ram vehicle total can be computed from the filings. NHTSA's API can still hide a campaign under an untested spelling, and the ProMaster hole is confirmed, not hypothetical: 26V531's summary names the ProMaster, yet every ProMaster query returns zero rows. 26V059's Ram total is the campaign total minus the Jeep share, because the filing's per-model Ram split was not broken out in the sources consulted. 26V531's 844,027 versus 848,511 is the Part 573 population against Stellantis's announced figure. FARS data is not part of this census: these are recall counts, not crash outcomes. And the 2026 model year is still in progress, so late filings will move every number here.

Here is what to do with all of this, starting with the truck in your driveway. If you own a 2019 to 2026 Ram 1500, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V495 now, because a loose buckle anchor has no symptom and the fix is a free dealer inspection. If you own a 2026 Ram 2500, check for 26V651 and know it is an expansion: if your truck was cleared under the April recall, check again, because the first scope missed 1,724 trucks. If you own a 2025 to 2026 Ram 1500, check for 26V560 and 26V531, the two camera recalls, both free radio software updates. If you tow with a 2025 to 2026 Ram, check for 26V059, because the trailer tow module can kill your trailer's lights and brakes with no dashboard warning. If you own a 2026 Ram 1500 with premium headlamps, 26V421 is a stop-sale recall with an interim inspection only, and the suspect window is October 29, 2025 to February 16, 2026. If you are shopping a 2026 Ram, the 1500 carries eight campaigns, the 2500 carries six, and the ProMaster carries zero on paper and one in the fine print. A truck whose electrical system needed four recall campaigns in one year is a reason to check the VIN twice, or to negotiate like you know.
