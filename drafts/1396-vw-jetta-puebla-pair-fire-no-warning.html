Volkswagen Recalled the Same Jetta Twice in One Month. Once Because It Could Catch Fire. Once Because It Couldn’t Tell You.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 4, 2026

Volkswagen recalled the 2025 Jetta twice in March 2026, fifteen days apart, from the same factory in Puebla, Mexico. One recall covers a ground wire a worker forgot to connect, which can start an engine fire without any warning at all.[1] Another covers an instrument cluster that can go completely dark, so it cannot show you a warning.[2] Same car. Same plant. Same month. Opposite defects.

61,483
2025–2026 Jettas recalled for a fire defect Volkswagen estimates affects fewer than 1% of them

Start with the fire. NHTSA campaign 26V138 covers 48,165 Jettas in the US and 13,318 in Canada, all built between March 3, 2025 and February 17, 2026.[1] A transmission ground wire may not have been connected during assembly, leaving an open electrical circuit that draws excessive current. Volkswagen's own filing names the root cause in two words: human error. Somebody on the line forgot a wire, and the company knows of six confirmed claims from the result: three melted wiring harnesses and three engine-compartment fires.[4] No crashes, no injuries, and no deaths so far. And read the fine print, because it is the whole story: there are no warning indicators for drivers, nothing lights up, nothing beeps, and your first sign can be melting plastic or smoke from the engine bay.

Now the cluster: NHTSA campaign 26V185 covers 75,323 more cars — 39,081 Jettas and 36,242 Taos, all 2025 model year — for a software error in the 8-inch digital instrument cluster supplied by Visteon.[2] A memory error can blank the display at startup: no speed, no fuel, no temperature, no warning telltales, a federal violation of FMVSS 101. Volkswagen estimates that 100% of the recalled population has this defect, which means every single car built in those windows. Warranty claims about dead clusters started arriving in October 2024, and Volkswagen sat on them until February 5, 2026, when its safety committee finally opened an investigation.[6] Sixteen months of warranty claims, then a recall filed in March, fifteen days after the fire recall.

Put the two together and the warning architecture collapses twice over. One defect starts a fire and gives no warning. Another defect removes the display that would show a warning. Production windows overlap, so 2025 Jettas built between March 3 and July 1, 2025 sit in both recalls: a car that can catch fire silently and go dark at the same time. Recall math makes the pair stranger still, because Volkswagen recalled more cars for the defect every car has (75,323) than for the defect that actually burned three cars (61,483). Compare the Tiguan BCM recall from the same year, covered in #951: 49,958 cars, 100% defective, one module killing four federal standards at once. Same manufacturer, same year, opposite ends of the defect-rate spectrum, and the rare defect is the one that set cars on fire. FARS adds a longer shadow to the same story: over 2014–2023 the Jetta nameplate piled up 1,375 deaths at 1.71 per 100 million miles, while the Tiguan managed 0.14 — twelve times safer per mile under the same badge.[8]

Fairness requires the other side. Volkswagen found both defects through its own field monitoring and recalled voluntarily, both fixes are free, and nobody has been hurt in either file. A cluster that blanks at startup is visible before you drive off, and a sub-1% defect rate means most of those 61,483 Jettas never had a loose wire. Still, none of that changes what a buyer is actually being told: check two recall numbers, book two dealer visits, and understand that one of the defects announces itself with fire and the other by deleting the announcements. If you own a 2025–2026 Jetta, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and check for both 26V138 and 26V185 — a 2025 built March through July 2025 can carry both.[9] Buying a used 2025 Jetta or Taos? Assume the cluster defect is present until a dealer record proves the software update was done, because Volkswagen says every car has it. And remember the cluster fix is not over-the-air: it needs a dealer visit and a VW diagnostic tool, so no update will ever arrive in your driveway on its own.
