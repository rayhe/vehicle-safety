One Corroded Cable Can Kill Your Maverick's Steering, Brakes, and Lights at Once

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

Ford is recalling 8,650 2022 Maverick Hybrids, and the defect is a solder joint the size of a thumbnail. The positive 12V battery cable terminal, eyelet C1617E, sits at the high-current Battery Junction Box. It can corrode, loosen, and detach. When it lets go, the truck loses drive power, power steering assist, power brake assist, headlamps, and the ability to shift into Neutral. Four safety systems ride on one weld, with no warning you can feel coming, just a dashboard full of red.[1][2]

4
Safety systems that die when one soldered eyelet lets go: steering assist, brake assist, headlights, and the ability to shift into Neutral.

Blame chemistry, not wear: hydroxy acetic acid, left over from the dip solder application on the eyelet joint, corrodes the ultrasonic weld that attaches the terminal to its cable. The acid was supposed to help the solder flow. Instead it ate the joint it was meant to seal. Ford traced the suspect eyelets through supplier process and maintenance records, which is how a recall of 8,650 trucks gets built around a single weld. Supplier Yazaki Inc. made the cable assembly, part number NZ6T-14300-CA, if you enjoy that kind of detail.[1]

Ford already knew this eyelet was trouble. Trucks repaired under customer satisfaction program 22B39 are excluded from the recall, which means the company ran a program on this same terminal before it became a federal filing. The Field Review Committee approved the recall on October 27, 2026, Ford filed with NHTSA on November 2, VINs became searchable November 9, and owner letters go out the week of November 23. Four years after the first of these trucks rolled off the line in Hermosillo, the fix is a whole new cable run from the battery under the rear seat to the junction box under the hood, not a repair of the joint but an overlay laid alongside the rotten one.[1][3]

Fairness first: no crashes or injuries appear in the public record for this campaign, and 8,650 trucks is a small recall by Ford standards. A skeptic could call it paperwork for a part that mostly holds. That skeptic would be right about the count and wrong about the stakes. Losing steering assist, brake assist, and headlights in the same instant, at highway speed, with no way to shift into Neutral, is not a paperwork problem but a blackout with a steering wheel, in a small population with a catastrophic failure mode.[2]

What to do: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, since the lookup has been live since November 9 and the letter does not arrive until next week. If your truck flashes “Stop Safely Now” or “Shift System Fault” with a red battery icon, pull over. That cluster of warnings is the tell, and the fix is free at any Ford dealer. And if your truck already got the 22B39 customer satisfaction repair, you are not in this recall at all.[2][3]
