## Story in one line
NHTSA opened a federal probe on November 13, 2026 into the magnetic chest clip on 2023-2024 Chicco Fit360 car seats after five reports that the clip breaks into small pieces — including one child who put a piece in their mouth while the car was moving — and the public complaint record shows parents had been reporting the exact same failure since October 2023, three years before the agency acted.

## Verified facts

1. **PE26009 opened November 13, 2026** (NHTSA ODI, quoted in full via govinfonow.com from the NHTSA record). "The Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) has received five reports alleging that the magnetic chest clips on subject 2023-2024 Chicco Fit360 car seats broke into small pieces. Complaints reported conditions that included magnetic chest clips breaking into multiple pieces with at least one instance in which a child placed a small piece into their mouth while the vehicle was in operation. ODI is opening this Preliminary Evaluation to assess the scope, root cause, and risk associated with unexpected disassembly of the magnetic chest clip in 2023-2024 Chicco Fit360 Car Seats." Component: Child Seat: Harness Retainer/Chest Clip. Status: Open, no closing date. No recall, no defect finding, no count of affected seats, no model numbers in the record.
2. **The first report is from October 2023** (NHTSA complaint 11685775, incident Oct 2, 2023, Bartlesville, OK; filed Sep 8, 2025). "My daughter disassembled the magnetic chest clip while seated in the car. She placed a small part in her mouth, creating a choking hazard. I was stranded until a replacement car seat could be brought." The complainant says Chicco provided a plastic replacement clip. The same complaint reports two further hazards on the same seat: the child could unlatch the seat from its base while rear-facing, repeatedly, mid-drive; and the silicone cup holders could be removed by the child and placed over mouth and nose, creating suction. The complaint is one consumer's account, not an NHTSA finding, and the article says so.
3. **November 2025: the clip that will not stay shut** (NHTSA complaint 11701052, incident Nov 19, 2025; filed Nov 23, 2025). "The magnetic chest clip failed. It will clip together but then won't remain magnetized/secure. It's misleading because you strap your child into the seat and then when you arrive at your destination the chest clip has come undone. It's extremely dangerous. We've only had the car seat 15 months." No crash, no injuries reported in this complaint.
4. **June 2026: the false latch** (NHTSA complaints, Plainfield, IL, Jun 8, 2026; Madison, MS, Jun 5, 2026). Plainfield: a 2.5-year-old's clip "very easily popped back open WITHOUT pressing the buttons" and "with light pressure it opens back up." Madison: "If the two halves of the magnetic chest clip are brought together lightly/loosely, the magnets pull the clip together and it appears closed, but the latch can separate without pressing the release buttons." The Madison complainant says Chicco stated it was sending a replacement magnetic chest clip and "indicated that the replacement should fix this issue," without inspecting the seat or the original clip. No crash or injury in either complaint.
5. **Chicco sells the replacement clip for $20** (Chicco USA parts store, read Nov 17, 2026). Magnetic chest clip for the Fit360 and OneFit Max, $20.00, item number 28796900895, in stock. Compatible with six model families (codes 79690, 87009, 87073, 87112, 87209, 87216). Chicco charges $10.00 for the chest clip of its KeyFit infant seat on the same store. Neither Chicco's page nor NHTSA's record says whether the replacement clip differs from the clip under review, so buying one does not settle the question. The listing does not mention NHTSA or the investigation.
6. **No Fit360 recall exists** (Chicco USA product recalls page, read Nov 17, 2026). Chicco's page counts one recall for 2021 to the present, and it is not a Fit360: the MyFit Zip Air, model numbers 07079561970070 and 04079561190070, built September 2020 to August 2024. Chicco's own guidance: a seat with any damaged harness part is out of service, and a seat that has been in an accident is out of service even with no visible damage. Chicco customer service: 1-877-424-4226. NHTSA hotline: 888-327-4236.
7. **Second child-seat probe of 2026** (NHTSA ODI PE26005, opened Jul 6, 2026). Unexpected handle detachment on 2020-2024 Doona car seat and stroller units; ODI wrote a detached handle could let the seat fall while a caregiver loads it into the car or lifts it out. ODI informed the Consumer Product Safety Commission on opening day and called the product one of shared interest; the PE26009 record does not carry that line. The two probes opened 130 days apart. Both are open; the latest activity date on each is its opening day.
8. **What a Preliminary Evaluation is** (NHTSA). A PE is the first formal stage of a defect investigation, not a recall. ODI gathers information to decide whether to close the case or take it further. A recall follows a finding that a product poses an unreasonable safety risk or misses minimum safety standards; most recall decisions are voluntary and come from manufacturers. If a recall does follow, the manufacturer has 60 days from notifying NHTSA to send registered owners a first-class notice. An unregistered car seat is off the mailing list.

## Original contribution: the lag, the false latch, the quiet replacement economy

**The three-year lag.** ODI's record says five reports opened the case. The public complaint file shows the failure was being reported for three years before that: October 2023 (disassembly, child put a piece in mouth), November 2025 (clip will not stay shut), June 2026 (two false-latch reports). The article's original contribution is putting the dates in one timeline and asking the question the record invites: the agency's threshold is five matching reports, and the fifth report evidently arrived in 2026 — but the first four sat in the public database for years while the seats stayed on shelves and in cars. The article does not claim ODI ignored the complaints; it states the dates and lets the gap speak.

**Two failure modes, not one.** The probe's subject line is "unexpected disassembly," and the coverage treats the clip as something that breaks. The June 2026 complaints describe a different and arguably worse failure: the clip does not break, it pretends. The magnet pulls the halves together so the clip looks closed, the mechanical latch never engages, and it pops open under light pressure — a 2.5-year-old's fingers, a turn, a bump. A broken clip you can see. A false-latched clip you cannot. The chest clip's job is keeping the harness straps at armpit level so they stay on the shoulders in a crash; a clip that opens on its own is a restraint that quietly stops restraining.

**The quiet replacement economy.** Chicco was already sending replacement clips to individual complainants — October 2023 (a plastic replacement), June 2026 (a replacement "should fix this issue") — while the product stayed on sale, no recall was filed, and the same magnetic clip was listed for sale at $20 on Chicco's own store. A per-customer fix with no public notice is the cheapest way to handle a defect and the worst way for every other owner to learn about it. The article states the facts and asks whether a replacement part sold alongside the seat under investigation is a remedy or a second sale.

## FARS cross-check
Not applicable in the usual sense: FARS tracks vehicle occupants in fatal crashes, and there is no FARS row for a child-seat component failure with zero reported deaths. No fatality-rate claim is made. The article notes the gap explicitly: the surveillance system that catches vehicle defects counts bodies, and a defect whose worst reported outcome so far is a toddler with a piece of plastic in their mouth does not register in it — which is exactly why a complaint-driven probe, not a crash-driven one, is the mechanism that matters here.

## Counterargument (full strength)
Five reports. That is the strongest fact on the other side, and the article leads with it rather than burying it. No injuries are reported in the ODI record; the mouth incident involved a child placing a piece in their mouth, with no stated injury, and no age given. A Preliminary Evaluation is the earliest stage, most do not become recalls, and the clip may turn out to have a small affected population or a use pattern that explains the failures. The June 2026 complaints describe clips that pop open, which is frightening but is also the clip doing the opposite of trapping a child — and a chest clip is a positioning device, not the primary restraint; the buckle and the harness carry the load in a crash. Chicco's replacement program, whatever its optics, got new clips to the parents who asked. The article states all of that, then states the rebuttal: the October 2023 complaint describes the same disassembly the probe names, three years earlier, and a positioning device that opens by itself is a restraint that stops positioning — the distinction between "retainer" and "restraint" is a regulatory one, not one a toddler in a crash will respect.

## Limitations
- The probe is at the earliest stage: ODI has only agreed to investigate whether a defect exists. NHTSA may close it with no finding. No defect has been found and no recall exists.
- The five reports are allegations in a public complaint database, not NHTSA findings. The mouth incident states no injury and no child age. The October 2023 complaint's base-unlatching and cup-holder claims are the same consumer's account, not findings, and are presented as such.
- The article does not know how many 2023-2024 Fit360 seats are in use; neither NHTSA's record nor Chicco's pages give a count. No population or rate claim is made.
- The $20 replacement clip's relationship to the probe is unknown: neither Chicco nor NHTSA says whether it differs from the original. The article does not claim it is a fix or a re-sale of the same part.
- The three-year lag is a timeline of public complaint dates against the probe's opening date; it is not evidence ODI saw or ignored any specific complaint. The article states the dates and does not claim causation.
- No FARS data exists for this product; no fatality-rate claim is made.

## What to do (actionable)
- If you own a 2023 or 2024 Chicco Fit360: check the chest clip before the next ride. Squeeze the halves together lightly, then pull them apart without pressing the release buttons. If they separate, or if the clip looks closed but the latch never clicked, stop using the seat and call Chicco at 1-877-424-4226 with the model number and date of manufacture in hand. A damaged harness part means the seat is out of service by Chicco's own guidance.
- If a clip has broken or come apart: file a complaint at nhtsa.gov through Report a Safety Problem. Five reports opened this case. Yours is how the count grows.
- Register the seat on Chicco's product registration page. A recall notice goes to registered owners first class within 60 days of a recall decision; an unregistered seat is off the list.
- Check NHTSA's Check for Recalls tool, Car Seat tab, by brand and model. A VIN search does not apply to a car seat.
- If you are shopping for a convertible seat: ask the store to show you the chest clip's latch, not just the magnet. A clip that holds itself together magnetically and latches mechanically is two mechanisms; test both, separately.
