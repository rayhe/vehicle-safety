# Research — #1182: Toronto deleted its speed cameras. Its own report measured what happened next (Oct 15, 2026)

**Journalist:** Axle McScatter | **Kicker:** By The Numbers
**Slug:** `1182-toronto-speed-camera-removal-deaths`
**Headline:** "Toronto Pulled Its Speed Cameras in November. By June, 101 of 104 Camera Sites Were Faster."

## What happened

- **November 2025:** Ontario's provincial government under Premier Doug Ford repealed municipal automated speed enforcement (ASE) cameras, calling them a "cash grab." Toronto deactivated its cameras Nov. 2025 (Ottawa: Nov. 14, 2025).
- **June 23, 2026:** Toronto's General Manager of Transportation Services delivered a supplemental report to city council measuring the fallout across **104 monitored former camera sites**:
  - Average **85th-percentile speed rose 4.8 km/h** (~3 mph).
  - Share of vehicles traveling **11+ km/h over the limit: 2.0% to 8.1%** (a 305% relative increase).
  - In **30 km/h zones**, drivers exceeding the limit by **16+ km/h: 1.4% to 7.2%**.
  - On roads posted 50+ km/h: drivers 16+ km/h over rose **0.5% to 2.9%** (~480% relative).
  - **Speeding increased at 101 of 104 sites.**
  - **Two fatal collisions occurred within 100 metres of former camera locations.**
  - Fatal collisions in the December-to-May window returned to **25, the highest figure in five years.**
- **June 2, 2026 (CBC/Toronto Police):** total fatal collisions in the city hit 23 year-to-date, a **28% increase** over 2025.
- **Oct. 5, 2026 (Toronto Police data):** pedestrian traffic deaths on track for a **five-year high**; motorcyclist deaths at **11, up 57%** year-over-year.
- **2025 baseline (context):** Toronto recorded 39 traffic fatalities in 2025 — the **lowest number in a decade** (19 pedestrians, 12 motorists, 5 motorcyclists, 2 cyclists, 1 micromobility).
- **Ottawa corroboration (June 2026):** at 8 former camera sites, speed-limit compliance was above 85% with cameras; after removal, **fewer than half of drivers complied**.
- **CP24 (Sept. 16, 2026):** on Parkside Drive, vehicles traveling 20+ km/h over the limit rose **more than 500% year-over-year**; residents blame camera removal.
- **The lawsuit:** Toronto faces litigation over Ontario's Bill 56 (the camera-repeal legislation). The city's own June 23 report is, as theautowire put it, a "quantified, self-authored causation exhibit" delivered ten weeks before the claim.
- **The official response:** Ontario Ministry of Transportation says speed bumps and roundabouts are better; gave Toronto **$10M** for traffic calming that "won't change vehicle lanes." A February city staff report estimated full traffic calming would take **13 years and ~$52M**. At that pace, the math: $52M/13y = ~$4M/yr vs $10M one-time.

## Original contribution (the Axle move: run the numbers)

1. **The percentage-vs-points honesty check:** "480% increase" is true AND misleading — it is 0.5% to 2.9%, i.e., +2.4 percentage points. The topcarnews/carscoops critique is right about the big percentages and misses the important number: **+4.8 km/h on the 85th percentile across 101 of 104 sites**, and **25 fatal collisions** back to a five-year high. Report both, with the denominators.
2. **The 4.8 km/h on the injury curve:** AAA Foundation impact-speed research: pedestrian risk of **severe injury ~10% at 16 mph, 25% at 23 mph, 50% at 31 mph**; death risk ~10% at 23 mph, **25% at 32 mph**. The curve is steepest exactly in the urban band (20–35 mph / 30–50 km/h). A 4.8 km/h (3 mph) population-wide shift sits ON the steep part of the curve. Also: the risk to a 70-year-old pedestrian at 25 mph equals the risk to a 30-year-old at 35 mph — age is worth 10 mph.
3. **The fatality trajectory:** 2025: 39 deaths (decade low). 2026 through June: on pace for ~28% above that ≈ 50 deaths — erasing a decade of Vision Zero gains in one legislative session.
4. **The budget ratio:** the province's $10M traffic-calming fund vs the city's own $52M/13-year estimate = enough for about 19% of the job (and calming that doesn't change lanes is slower, gentler, and cheaper-feeling by design).

## Actionable insights (hard gate)

- If you drive in Toronto: 101 of 104 former camera sites are now faster. The cameras that used to ticket you in school zones are gone; the speeders came back.
- For US readers: this is a natural experiment in what happens when automated enforcement is removed. Speeding rose at essentially every site, compliance in Ottawa fell from >85% to <50%, and fatalities are up 28%. Your state legislator banning red-light/speed cameras is voting for these numbers.
- Pedestrians: the steepest part of the injury curve is the 30–50 km/h band most urban streets live in. Three mph across everyone matters more than ten mph from one driver.

## Limitations (required)

- **Correlation, not proven causation:** the report measures speed changes at former camera sites and counts fatalities city-wide; it does not prove camera removal caused any specific death. Other 2026 factors (traffic volumes, weather, enforcement levels) are uncontrolled.
- **Percentage hype:** the 305%/480% figures come from tiny denominators; report absolute points alongside.
- **Canadian data:** Toronto police/city data, not NHTSA FARS; definitions (85th percentile speeds, fatal-collision counts) differ from US conventions.
- **The injury curve** (AAA Foundation) is a meta-estimate across studies, and age-comorbidity effects (70 vs 30) are population-level.

## Strongest counterargument (required)

The skeptics (topcarnews/carscoops) are right that the headline percentages are denominator tricks: 0.5% to 2.9% is a 2.4-point absolute shift. And 4.8 km/h is 3 mph — most drivers wouldn't notice. The defense: 101 of 104 sites moved the SAME direction (that is not noise), the 85th-percentile is the engineering standard for setting limits, and the fatal-collision count — 25 in the Dec–May window, a five-year high — doesn't care about your opinion of percentages.
