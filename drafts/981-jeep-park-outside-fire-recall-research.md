# Research: #981 — Jeep park-outside fire recall (26V363)

**Slug:** 981-jeep-park-outside-fire-recall
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date:** 2026-09-23

## Angle

On June 4, 2026, Stellantis ordered **1,076,999** Jeep Wrangler and Gladiator owners to park outside and away from structures — their Jeeps can catch fire with the ignition off. A company spokesman said a remedy was expected by the end of July. It is now late September, and per the Sept 21 recall roundup, **there is still no fix**. The story is not the recall announcement (June news). The story is: three and a half months of driveway exile with a missed deadline, on top of a three-year investigation Stellantis closed in April 2024 for "low rate of occurrence" before NHTSA forced the issue.

## Novel contribution (original analysis)

1. **Timeline reconstruction** showing ~3 years from first fire reports (2023) to recall (June 2026), including the closed-then-reopened internal investigation and the NHTSA ODI probe that broke the stalemate.
2. **The 0.1% vs 100% contrast:** Stellantis estimates ~0.1% of the 1,076,999 actually carry the defect (~1,077 vehicles), yet all 1.08M owners live under a park-outside order — a 1000x safety margin imposed on owners' driveways.
3. **Fire-rate math:** ~72 reported fires / 1,076,999 vehicles = ~6.7 fires per 100,000 vehicles (0.0067%). Small rate, catastrophic failure mode (ignition-off fire).
4. **The missed July deadline** as the news hook: "end of July" remedy promise vs. Sept 21 "no repair available yet."

## Kill test

- Newsworthy: Yes — 1.08M vehicles under an active park-outside order is one of the largest such orders in recent NHTSA history, and the missed remedy deadline is live news.
- Novel: Yes — no site coverage (grep clean). The closed-investigation reversal + missed deadline framing is not in the wire coverage, which treated the June announcement as the whole story.
- Actionable: Yes — VIN check (NHTSA 26V363 / FCA 21D), warning signs ("Service Power Steering", loss of assist), park-outside guidance, dealer contact.

## Strongest counterargument

Stellantis' estimate that only ~0.1% of the recalled population carries the defect means ~99.9% of displaced owners are parking outside for a connector their Jeep never had. The recall's population was drawn from the suspect production window (build-date range), not from confirmed-defective parts — a deliberately broad net. One could argue the company is over-warning: the fire rate is 6.7 per 100,000 vehicles, no fatalities, no crashes, and the broad net is caution, not confirmed danger to every Jeep. The counterweight: ignition-off fires are the failure mode NHTSA takes most seriously, because the owner has no chance to intervene, and the park-outside instruction is the only mitigation available while no remedy exists. The company does not get to call its own caution excessive.

## Limitations

- Fire counts vary by source (51 vs 72); both are Stellantis/NHTSA-reported figures from different points in time. Treated as "~50-70."
- The root-cause description (terminal spread from insertion force) comes from regulatory filings via secondary reporting; the full Part 573 chronology was not independently pulled from NHTSA's document server.
- Whether the end-of-July remedy target slipped or was re-scoped is unknown — Stellantis has not published an updated remedy timeline as of Sept 21, 2026.
- The recall covers 2021–2025 model years built through Dec 2, 2024; vehicles built after that date reportedly got production changes.
