The Gap

Ford Says Its Recall Crisis Is Old Cars and Software Updates. I Ran the Numbers on the 2026s.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 4, 2026

This week Ford filed three new recalls covering nearly 159,000 vehicles. That makes 67 federal recall campaigns so far in 2026, and the fifth straight year Ford has led the entire auto industry in recalls.[1] Ford has an answer ready. Spokesman Mike Levine says campaigns are down 38 percent from last year, more than 80 percent are fixed with software updates, and the problem is mostly older vehicles. The new metal, he says, is caught by Ford's own proactive testing before customers ever notice. The JD Power Initial Quality Study just ranked Ford first among mainstream brands.[1][2] It is a good story. So I checked it against the 2026s.

25 vs. 5 vs. 2
Federal recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching 2026-model-year vehicles: Ford vs. Toyota vs. Honda

I pulled NHTSA's public recall database for every current Ford, Toyota, and Honda nameplate, model years 2024 through 2026, and counted the campaigns filed this calendar year. Ford: 25 campaigns touching 2026-model-year vehicles, against 5 for Toyota and 2 for Honda.[3] Per nameplate, that is 2.5 for Ford against 0.3 for Toyota and 0.2 for Honda. Roughly an eightfold gap, on the newest cars each company sells. The database is public; go look. Then the software defense collapses on contact: zero of Ford's 25 are flagged as over-the-air fixes in NHTSA's data. Two involve software or labels; the other 23 are hardware. Wiring harnesses. Pistons. Transmissions. Seat fasteners. Ball joints. You cannot push a ball joint over the air. Ford knows this.

Now the defense, stated fairly, because it deserves it. Ford really does appear to be finding a lot of these itself. Levine says the late-model recalls come from internal testing, not customer complaints, and that is a healthier failure mode than the alternative, a point worth granting in full: a company that finds its own defects before the tow trucks do is behaving better than one that waits for the field reports to pile up. Campaigns really are down 38 percent from the all-time record of 152 that Ford set in 2025, and the JD Power win is real. And there is a serious argument that raw recall counts punish transparency: Ford operates under a 2024 NHTSA consent order with an independent monitor reviewing its recall process, which creates every incentive to file early and file often.[4] Toyota and Honda may simply be quieter, not better. Takata taught this industry that silence is the expensive option.

But "we file more because we're honest" only works if the underlying defect rate is comparable, and the 2026 census says it is not, not by a factor of eight. And here is the part that should anger you as a buyer: the remedy for the biggest of this week's recalls, the 148,663-Mustang ground-eyelet defect that can kill the headlights and the engine cooling fan, is not expected until March 2027. Ford mailed the warning letters on August 31, which means Mustang owners get to drive through winter with a known defect and a letter.[1] If you own a 2024–2026 Mustang, or a 2026 Explorer, Bronco, Bronco Sport, Ranger, Maverick, or F-150: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today instead of waiting for the mail, and ask your dealer whether an interim inspection is available while the official remedy is pending.
