# Research: Honda Recalled Its Own Tire-Kit Fix (26V663 / Honda PPW)

## The story
Honda filed NHTSA recall 26V663 (Honda recall PPW) on Oct 8, 2026, recalling 4,804 vehicles whose tire repair kits were "fixed" under an earlier recall (26V366) with the same defective part. The supplier, TEK Corporation of Troy, Michigan, mixed pre-countermeasure nozzles with countermeasure nozzles in its warehouse because it never implemented adequate change-point management. Bad parts shipped out as the remedy. Honda's fix for the fix: delete the offending part entirely. The new nozzle has no one-way valve at all.

## Key facts (all from primary sources)
- **NHTSA campaign:** 26V663; **Honda recall number:** PPW. Filed Oct 8, 2026 (Part 573 report).
- **Population:** 4,804 vehicles: 2,745 CR-V Hybrid (2023-2026), 2,058 Accord Hybrid (2023-2026), 1 CR-V FCEV (2025).
- **Estimated with defect:** 1% (~48 vehicles). Honda cannot identify which ones, so all 4,804 owners return to dealers.
- **The defect:** The tire repair kit nozzle has a one-way valve. If the kit is run with the nozzle improperly connected to the tire valve, pressure builds in the sealant bottle; if the relief valve fails to vent, the bottle cap can detach, raising injury risk.
- **The cause (Part 573, verbatim mechanism):** "During preparation of countermeasure parts for the remedy of NHTSA Recall ID 26V-366, the supplier did not implement adequate change-point management which resulted in the absence of appropriate disposal controls for pre-countermeasure parts, allowing those parts to be mixed with countermeasure parts in the supplier's warehouse."
- **Supplier named:** TEK CORPORATION, Tier 1 OEM supplier, 450 Robbins Drive, Troy MI, 48083.
- **Chronology:** July 3, 2026 — Honda notified of incorrect countermeasure parts at a dealership. July-August — Honda + supplier inspect inventory and shipments. Sept 2 — population analysis. Oct 1 — Honda determines defect exists, decides to recall. As of Oct 1: zero warranty claims, zero injuries, zero deaths.
- **Remedy:** Dealer inspects and replaces the nozzle as needed. "How remedy component differs from recalled component: The remedy component does not have a one-way valve." Supplier will "implement system-controlled processes to prevent parts with different configuration or disposition statuses from being mixed."
- **Notification schedule:** Dealers notified Oct 9, 2026. Owner letters ~Nov 16, 2026 (upcoming).
- **Related recall:** 26V366 (the original tire repair kit recall whose remedy failed).

## Angle / why it is newsworthy
This is a recall of a recall — the remedy itself was the defect. The novel contribution: TEK Corporation's warehouse held two bins of nozzles (old and new) with no system to distinguish them, and Honda's Part 573 names the supplier and the exact process failure (no change-point management, no disposal controls for pre-countermeasure parts). The fix is delicious: remove the one-way valve entirely. A "safety feature" that made the product dangerous, deleted. And the recall math is pure Crash Report: 4,804 vehicles recalled to find ~48 bad nozzles — a 100-to-1 recall ratio — because the supplier could not tell its own parts apart.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. Fresh (filed Oct 8, announced Oct 14, owner letters still upcoming Nov 16). Named supplier, named process failure, concrete mechanism.
- Novel angle? Yes. Prior coverage of "remedy failures" (#997 thicker-oil, #1089 ridgeline-exonerated) never involved a supplier mixing fixed and unfixed parts in one warehouse and shipping the wrong bin. The "delete the part" remedy is also new.
- Dup check: no queue entry or story covers 26V663, PPW, TEK Corporation, or the 26V366 re-recall.

## Limitations (to carry into the article)
- Honda estimates only ~1% of the 4,804 have the defect; the other ~4,756 owners get a dealer visit for nothing — the cost of the supplier's sloppiness.
- Zero injuries/warranty claims as of Oct 1, 2026. This is a caught-in-time failure, not a body count story.
- The original 26V366 defect mechanism (relief valve failing to vent) is asserted by Honda, not independently verified here.

## Strongest counterargument
A skeptic says: this is the system working. A dealership spotted wrong parts July 3; Honda investigated; recall filed Oct 1; zero injuries. The 1% estimate means the actual risk pool is tiny. Recalling 4,804 cars to fix 48 nozzles is expensive theater. The counter to the counter: the theater is the point — Honda could not narrow the population because TEK could not account for its own bins. The recall is big because the supplier's inventory control was nonexistent.

## Actionable insight (required)
If you own a 2023-2026 Accord Hybrid, 2023-2026 CR-V Hybrid, or 2025 CR-V FCEV and already had the tire repair kit "fixed" under the first recall: you are not done. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or recalls.honda.com. And if you ever actually use the repair kit, connect the nozzle to the tire valve FIRST, before running the pump — the defect only bites when the nozzle is improperly connected.
