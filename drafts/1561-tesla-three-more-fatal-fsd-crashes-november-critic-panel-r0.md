## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.7/10
No victim is named; ages and cities come from the public record and FHP withheld names. Causation is never alleged: the article says engagement is not causation twice, in the body and the disclaimer. The counterargument is stated at full strength, including the point that Tesla complied with the law. Deductions: the headline "The Public Was Told Nothing" overstates by a hair, since the public was told about the crash, just not about the system; the lede corrects the impression immediately. The Katy contrast implies selective disclosure from a single example, which is fair but thin as a pattern claim.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.8/10
Three shareable hooks travel without the article attached: "Seven more dead," the 264 record, and the UTC timestamp filing a September crash under October. Headline is long but compresses to "Tesla's driver-assist was on in three more fatal crashes." Deduction: the strongest line, the UTC quirk, is buried mid-article; a reader skimming the first two paragraphs never meets it. The pull stat is the record, not the deaths, which is the right call for credibility but the weaker call for virality.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
SGO requirements stated correctly: report when a Level 2 system was engaged within 30 seconds and the crash meets injury, fatality, airbag, or hospital-transport criteria. The three CBI fields are named correctly: narrative, software version, operational design domain. "Verified Engaged" is Tesla's own term from the filing, quoted as such. No invented URLs: the one drafted NHTSA link 404'd in verification and was replaced with the live Standing General Order page before critique. Deduction: "Florida Highway Patrol called it a car that veered off the road" attributes Electrek's characterization of the public account to FHP; FHP's release said the SUV left the road. Substance is right, attribution is slightly loose.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.9/10
Original contribution is real and derived, not asserted: September's 264 beats July's 236 by 28 (+11.9% in two months); the Naples pair puts two fatal driver-assist crashes in one city in a single release; the UTC-midnight filing quirk is a second hiding mechanism beyond redaction, and it implies September's record may itself undercount September. Limitations are stated in the research file and the disclaimer: engagement is not causation, counts are raw reports not rates, crash dates for two of the three crashes are not pinned to a day. Deductions: the 39 mph pre-crash speed from the filing is omitted; the piece relies on Electrek's SGO matching rather than re-matching the data itself, which the research file acknowledges but the article does not.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.7/10
Every number arrives with its denominator: 264 vs 236, 12 crashes / 16 lives, 1 + 3 + 3 = 7 deaths, 99.9% of reports redacted the same way. Pull stat 264 is labeled with its context. Deductions: no small table for the three crashes, so the reader carries dates, places, and death counts in prose; the +11.9% rate increase computed in the research file never appears in the article, which states only the 28-report gap.
