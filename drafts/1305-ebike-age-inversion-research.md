## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
1. **ERideHero, "Electric Bike Accident Report 2026" (Aug 2026), built from
   CPSC NEISS + CPSRMS:** an estimated 102,777 US e-bike ER visits in 2025,
   up 71% in one year and 19x since 2020 (5,358). Injuries rose 78% in 2024
   and 71% in 2025; the curve is steepening. Method note: e-bikes got their
   own federal product code only in 2024, so 2020-2023 records were
   re-classified from narrative text; the report's 2024 estimate (59,965)
   lands within 1.3% of CPSC's published 2024 estimate (59,200). All figures
   are estimates, and floors: several hundred records a year coded as
   ordinary bicycles were excluded.
2. **Kids' series (same report):** riders 14 and under went from an estimated
   276 injuries in 2020 to 17,206 in 2025, a 62-fold rise, including
   6,444 to 17,206 (+167%) in the last year alone. Kids' share of all
   e-bike injuries tripled from 5.2% (2020) to 16.7% (2025). Riders 15-24 are
   the largest single share (21.9%); more than half of injured riders were
   34 or younger.
3. **Severity (same report + CPSC follow-up study):** 17.6% of 2025 e-bike
   injuries led to hospitalization, transfer, or observation, vs 15.1%
   e-scooters and 14.1% regular bicycles, up every year from 10.1% in 2020.
   Fractures are the top diagnosis (29%, 30,253); head injuries 19,001
   (18.5%) plus face 10,770 (10.8%) mean 29% hit above the neck. CPSC's
   follow-up study found only 40% of injured riders wore helmets; 57% of
   2025 narratives mentioning helmets report riding without one.
4. **Deaths skew old (CPSC CPSRMS via same report; baseline from #998's
   CPSC 2017-2024 report):** CPSC recorded zero e-bike deaths in 2017 and 97
   in 2024, 310 across 2017-2024. E-bikes are 58% of all micromobility
   deaths, ahead of e-scooters (39%). 170 of 310 deaths (55%) were
   collisions with motor vehicles. Of micromobility deaths among riders
   65+, 82% involved e-bikes; adding ages 45-64, older adults are over half
   of all e-bike fatalities. July 2025 peaked at 13,140 injuries vs 3,102
   in February; 81% of 2025 injuries with a recorded location happened on
   a street or highway.
5. **Corroboration — AAP 2026 studies (#1206):** CHLA 2019-2025, 379 kids:
   e-bike injuries were ~3x as likely to need surgery or inpatient stay as
   e-scooter injuries; potentially life-threatening in 8.8% vs 2.8%.
   AAP guidance: no e-bikes under 12, Class 1 only for ages 12-15.
6. **Regulatory context:** CPSC proposed America's first mandatory
   lithium-ion battery safety standard for micromobility products in June
   2026, after 19 battery-fire deaths in the 2017-2024 series. Children are
   allowed to operate devices capable of 20-28 mph, and when illegally
   modified over 50 mph, without formal training (American College of
   Surgeons Bulletin, via report).

## What NOT to claim
- 2025 figures are NEISS-based estimates from a secondary analysis, not
  CPSC-published finals; say so once, plainly. Do not present 102,777 as
  an exact count.
- Do not claim a 2025 e-bike death count; the report's death series ends
  at 2024 (97). The 58% share is a 2017-2024 cumulative share.
- Do not mix the GHSA pedestrian series in here; unrelated.
- Kids' "62-fold" uses the report's 2020 estimate (276), which the report
  itself flags as approximate (small sample). Say "an estimated 62-fold."

## Actionable takeaways
- Parents: AAP says no e-bikes under 12, Class 1 only at 12-15. A Class 3
  (28 mph) bike is not a child's bicycle, whatever the box says.
- Every rider: helmet, always. Head/face injuries are 29% of the total
  and only 40% of injured riders wore one.
- Older riders: the machine that gives mobility back also delivers the
  worst outcomes when it goes wrong; practice braking at speed before
  traffic forces the lesson.
- Buyers: check whether the bike is a legal e-bike or a modified moped
  in disguise, and charge only with the supplied charger, never a damaged
  battery indoors.
