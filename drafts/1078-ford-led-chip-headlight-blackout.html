Ford’s Brand-New Trucks Can Lose Every Headlight at Once

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 5, 2026

Ford is recalling 41,748 brand-new Expeditions and Super Duty trucks because a contaminated LED chip inside the headlight assemblies can kill the daytime running lights, the parking lights, the low beams, and the high beams.[1] All four lighting modes. One chip. According to the NHTSA notice, these vehicles fail Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 108, which is the federal government’s polite way of saying your 2026 work truck does not legally qualify as a vehicle with working headlights.[3]

41,748
2025–2027 Expeditions and 2026 Super Duty trucks recalled under 26C43 for total forward-lighting loss

Pause on the failure mode, because it is the interesting part. Not a wiring harness rubbing through, not a software glitch, not a relay that gave up after 150,000 miles. This is a semiconductor defect: a contaminated chip on the LED assembly. LED headlights are sold as sealed, maintenance-free, lifetime lighting — the kind of component engineers describe as lasting longer than the truck. Ford’s notice says one bad chip can take out every forward-facing light simultaneously. Your only warning is a dashboard indicator that the low or high beams have died, which means you learn about the blackout when you needed the lights.[1]

Now consider which nameplates are on the list, because the FARS data has something to say about one of them. Over 2014–2023, the Expedition accumulated 1,515 deaths at a rate of 2.31 per 100 million VMT; the F-250, its work-truck sibling, accumulated 909 deaths at a rate of 0.43.[4] Run the division: 2.31 ÷ 0.43 = 5.37. The family-hauling SUV built from Ford’s truck DNA already kills 5.4 times more often per mile than the actual truck, and now both share the recalled headlight assemblies. Darkness is not where the Expedition’s risk comes from, but it is not where the risk gets smaller either.

Fairness requires the counterargument at full strength. FARS covers 2014–2023, so the Expedition rate describes older trucks, not the 2025–2027 vehicles under recall; Ford identified this defect and defined the remedy before anyone reported an injury tied to 26C43, which is how recalls are supposed to work; and the dashboard warning gives drivers notice before total darkness. The 5.4x figure also reflects driver mix and use patterns, not the vehicle alone. What I am not claiming: that a single crash has been caused by this chip, because no public count of field failures exists for this recall.

What you actually do with this. If you own a 2025–2027 Expedition or a 2026 F-250, F-350, F-450, or F-550, do not wait for the notification letter — Ford is not mailing those until October 26.[2] Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today; the remedy is a free dealer inspection and replacement of the headlight assemblies. If your beams die in the meantime, that dashboard indicator is telling you about recall 26C43, not a blown fuse — say the number to the service writer. And if you are shopping used Expeditions: the outgoing generation’s 2.31 rate sits far above the Explorer (1.54) and the Edge (0.46), which should be reflected in the price.[5]
