GM's Grille Recall Didn't Stick. Literally.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
Oct 30, 2026

Here's a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: every recall you have ever trusted rests on two silent promises, and both of them just got a letter from the feds. On October 9, NHTSA opened a formal recall query into GM's 2025 recall of GMC Sierra 1500 grille deflectors, because 21 owners reported the part flew off their trucks after the recall repair had already been performed.[1]

7.6x
The new probe population (535,639 trucks) vs. the original recall (70,768)

The two promises, since you asked: that the recall covered every vehicle at risk, and that the fix actually fixed it. NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation is now publicly questioning both. The query, numbered RQ26003, names 2022 through 2024 Sierra 1500s, more than seven times the population GM recalled in February 2025.[2] It's worth savoring the poetry here. GM told regulators that only certain 2022 trucks with chrome deflectors were at risk. Meanwhile, the feds' complaint file now includes 2023s, 2024s, and non-chrome 2022s. Either the supplier's "suspect production window" was wider than claimed, or a whole lot of unrelated trucks are losing trim at suspiciously similar rates.

Let's talk about the part itself, because a grille deflector sounds cosmetic right up until it leaves the vehicle at 70 mph. It's a wide trim bar across the top of the grille, held on by eight attachment points that GM admitted can fracture over time. When all eight let go, roughly 2 kilograms of plastic become a free-range projectile. Run the physics: at 70 mph that's about 1,000 joules of kinetic energy, comparable to a brick off an overpass, aimed at whoever is unfortunate enough to be behind you. The complaint file backs up the physics with anecdotes: cracked windshields, drivers swerving around airborne trim.[3]

GM's 2025 remedy, campaign 25V-060, had dealers "reinforce or repair" the attachment points in about an hour, with explicit instructions not to replace the grille unless necessary. GM estimated only 3% of the recalled trucks actually had the defect, roughly 2,100 trucks.[4] Twenty-one post-repair failures against that estimate is small. It is also 18% of the complaints in the federal record, and complaints are the only data anyone has. Both of those facts are true at once. That's the thing about regulatory math: it's turtles all the way down, and every turtle is missing a denominator.

Now the mandatory splash of cold water, because the strongest case against this article is also the correct one. A recall query is not a finding of a defect. 118 complaints across 535,639 trucks is 0.022%, a rounding error by any statistical standard, and complaint data skews toward the angry. The 21 post-repair reports cannot yield a failure rate because nobody knows how many trucks were actually repaired. No crashes, no injuries, no fatalities appear in the record. If ODI closes this with no action, the story will have been: a regulator checked GM's homework and the homework was fine. That happens. This paperwork is the system working exactly as designed.

But here's what keeps me up at night, and you should let it keep you up too. The reason recalls work is that people believe the repair ends the problem. 21 people watched the problem end twice. And the only reason we know is that they filed paperwork with the government, which means for every one of them there are owners who just shrugged at a rattle and drove on. A loose deflector makes a noise first, GM said so in its own filing. Listen to your grille. The universe is trying to tell you something, and unlike most existential threats, this one gives you a warning rattle.

What to do: Own a 2022 Sierra 1500 with the chrome grille? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; if 25V-060 shows open, get it done (GM: 1-800-462-8782). Had the repair and hear a rattle at the top of the grille, or the deflector looks loose? Report it to your dealer and file a complaint with NHTSA; owner complaints are what opened this query. Own a 2023 or 2024 Sierra? No recall covers your truck yet, but have the dealer inspect any rattle and file with NHTSA anyway. Shopping used? Check the recall history, then physically look at the deflector. A completed-recall record says a repair was recorded. It doesn't say the part is still attached.

What this didn't prove: This desk has not read the actual ODI opening resume; the quoted language comes through six press outlets reporting identical figures. The 2 kg deflector mass is an estimate, so the 1,000-joule figure is illustrative, not measured. The true repair-completion count (the denominator for any failure rate) is not public. GM's position beyond the original 2025 filing was not available in press as of mid-October.
