## Key Findings from Today's IIHS Release

### Highest Driver Death Rates (2023 model year, deaths per million registered vehicle years):
1. Kia Rio — 170 (HIGHEST ever for modern car in IIHS series)
2. Nissan Versa — (2nd, exact figure TBD from full IIHS data)
3. Chrysler 300 — 132
4. Dodge Challenger Hemi — 127
5. Dodge Challenger non-Hemi — 125
6. Nissan Altima — in top 10
7. Chevrolet Malibu — 116
8-10. Nissan Maxima + Hyundai models

### Critical finding: HALF the top 10 are CANCELLED vehicles
- Kia Rio: discontinued after 2024 MY
- Chrysler 300: discontinued after 2023 MY
- Dodge Challenger: discontinued after 2023 MY (Hornet replaced some Stellantis sedan volume)
- Chevrolet Malibu: discontinued after 2024 MY
- Nissan Maxima: discontinued after 2023 MY

### Zero death rate vehicles (2023 MY):
- Audi Q7, BMW X7, Chevrolet Tahoe 4WD, Mercedes-Benz GLB, Mercedes-Benz GLE, Toyota Tundra CrewMax 4WD
- 18 of 20 lowest death rate vehicles are SUVs
- All zero-death vehicles are luxury or high-end

### Other-driver death rates (how dangerous to OTHER people):
- Ram 3500 Crew Cab Long Bed 4WD: 204 (HIGHEST — up from 189 in previous study)
- Dodge Charger Hemi: 161
- Non-Hemi Charger: 136
- Chevrolet Silverado 3500 Crew Cab: 126
- Ram 1500 Classic: 124
- Stellantis holds 7 of top 10 spots

### IIHS President David Harkey quote:
"Even the safest small cars don't fare well in crashes with large pickups and heavy SUVs. Powerful engines and marketing that attracts aggressive drivers is the issue for sports cars and muscle cars. They're dangerous mainly because of how they're driven."

### Novel calculation: Shadow body count
These five models have a combined fleet of ~2.8 million vehicles still on US roads.
Using their IIHS death rates and typical 12-15 year fleet attrition:
- Combined: ~100+ additional driver deaths per year from models no longer manufactured
- Over remaining fleet life: potentially 800-1,200 more deaths from "ghost cars"

### Historical comparison (IIHS Kia Rio across studies):
- 2011 model year study: 149 deaths/million reg years
- 2023 model year study: 170 deaths/million reg years
- INCREASE of 14% despite 12 years of safety improvement

### Original contribution:
The novel finding: the market killed these cars before regulators did. The cancellation was driven by low margins, electrification pressure, and consumer shift to SUVs — NOT by safety concerns. The safety data arrived AFTER the obituary. This raises the question: did market forces accidentally save lives, and what does that mean for the remaining 2.8 million ghost cars?
