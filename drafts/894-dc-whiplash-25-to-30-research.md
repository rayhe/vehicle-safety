# Research notes — #894: DC's traffic-death whiplash (25 → 30+ and counting)

Slug: `894-dc-whiplash-25-to-30`
Journalist: Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; kicker: The Gap)
Date: 2026-09-12

## Kill test
PASS. Fresh (all data from Sept 6–11, 2026), novel, not covered. #799 (Sep 1) covered the national decline + state divergence, but no site story has ever covered DC specifically. The whiplash is the angle: DC had its best traffic year in over a decade (25 deaths in all of 2025), then posted the largest increase of ANY US jurisdiction in H1 2026 (+133% per NSC), and MPD's own dashboard shows 30 deaths by Sept 11 — already past the entire 2025 total, +88% year-over-year. One jurisdiction went from the biggest winner to the biggest loser in six months. The Sept 6 triple-fatality on I-66 (family of three, stopped oxygen-tank truck in an express lane, two reckless-driving charges) gives it a human anchor in the same metro.

## Key calculations (show the math)
- DC 2026 pace: 30 deaths over Jan 1–Sep 11 = 254 days → 0.118/day → annualized ≈ 43 deaths. That is 72% above 2025's 25 and closing in on 2023–24's 52.
- Monthly: 30 / 8.36 mo ≈ 3.6 deaths/month in 2026 vs 2.1/month in 2025.
- NSC consistency check: if DC H1 2025 ≈ 12–13 (roughly half of 25), then +133% → H1 2026 ≈ 28–30, matching MPD's 30 by Sept 11. The two sources agree.
- Context: nationally the rate fell 10.4% since 2024; DC moved the opposite direction.

## Novel contribution
Nobody has put the MPD dashboard next to the NSC state tables: DC is the only jurisdiction to go from largest decrease (2025, -52%) to largest increase (H1 2026, +133%), and its own police data shows it has already blown past its full record-low year by September. The whiplash framing + pace math is original to this piece.

## Limitations (for the article)
- MPD counts are preliminary and subject to revision; some Park Police-jurisdiction deaths are counted separately (table footnotes).
- NSC estimates use a different methodology than NHTSA; NSC predicted 37,810 US deaths for 2025 vs NHTSA's 36,640. Treat both as estimates.
- DC is tiny (25–52 deaths/year): percentages amplify small absolute moves. Fourteen extra deaths = +88%.
- The Sept 6 Liddell crash happened in Fairfax County, VA — it is NOT in DC's count. It illustrates the metro, not the statistic.
- We do not know the causal mix (speeding/impairment/pedestrian) behind DC's 2026 increase from these sources.

## Strongest counterargument
2025's 25 was the anomaly, not 2026. The 20-year table shows DC bouncing between 19 and 54; 2023–24 were both 52. Regression to the mean explains most of the "surge," and small-N volatility means a few multi-fatality crashes swing the percentage wildly. The national trend (16 straight quarterly declines) is the real story; DC is noise.

## Actionable takeaway
Never stop in an express-lane travel lane — the I-66 crash started with two stopped vehicles in a live lane and ended with three deaths and a fire. If your car is disabled: hazards on, get as far off the roadway as possible, get out and behind a barrier. In the DMV specifically, expect aggressive lane discipline in bollard-separated express lanes with no shoulder to escape to.
