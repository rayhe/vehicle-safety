## 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Structure follows the house template cleanly: kicker, headline, lede with bolded key stat, pull-stat, then a logical descent from decade context to cross-tab to mechanism to action to counterargument to limitations. The 66.2% reveal lands in paragraph three with the math shown, which is exactly where a data story's payload belongs. Minor deduction: the final two paragraphs (limitations, original contribution) are necessary but read slightly boilerplate-adjacent to the prior article's closing formula; the content is honest, the rhythm just sags a touch after the strong counterargument.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.8/10
Axle is audible throughout: the catchphrase opener ("I ran the numbers, then I ran them again"), the division joke ("a crisis that evaporates on contact with division"), the table-dump paragraph that reads like someone who genuinely enjoys cross-tabs. No banned phrases, no em dashes, The-starters at 11.3%, rhythm metrics all pass. Sentence shapes vary from 3-word-adjacent punches to a 104-word counterargument build. Deduction: "physics problem wearing a birthday" is doing a lot of metaphor work for a numbers columnist; it lands, but it's the one line that could have been written by Vin Wreckage.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
The piece treats a vulnerable population with unusual care for this genre: it explicitly refuses the ageist frame, states the CDC/NHTSA caution against age-based decisions, and redirects the policy lever from "take grandma's keys" to fragility-first vehicle design. The "grandpa is overwhelmingly endangering grandpa" line is blunt but statistically grounded, not mocking. No self-congratulation, no punching down. Actionable guidance is respectful (conversation, not ambush).

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.7/10
The 66.2% pull-stat is the share trigger: counterintuitive, single-number, argument-ending. Headline creates a clear in-group/out-group curiosity gap ("everyone else" vs "drivers over 65"). The "3.5% per-capita" deflation of the crisis narrative is the second shareable beat, the kind of thing people quote to win arguments. Deduction: the og:description is solid but long; and the piece's strongest asset (the cross-tab) is proprietary arithmetic, which is great for credibility but slightly harder to excerpt than a named study finding.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.9/10
All eight references are real, linked, and correctly attributed: DOT HS numbers cited properly (813 791, 813 762, 813 341), the CDC/NHTSA methodological gap disclosed in both body and disclaimer, secondary sources (NSC, AAA, AboutLawsuits, WPST) labeled as what they are. No legal claims are made; the state-licensing paragraph is framed as "look up your state's rules" rather than legal advice. Deduction: the 2025 early-estimate "lone flat line" claim traces to the repo's own prior coverage of publication 813800 rather than a directly linked primary table; the press release link is provided, but a reader cannot one-click verify that specific subcategory figure.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Two genuine original contributions: (a) the 66.2% own-cohort cross-tab computed from NHTSA's published category breakdown, shown with inputs; (b) the destiny arithmetic (33% deaths vs 28% population → ~3.5% per-capita) that reframes a decade of coverage. Limitations are a dedicated, specific section (CDC/NHTSA gap, CRSS sampling, arbitrary 65 cutoff, exposure estimates, preliminary 2025). The counterargument is stated at full strength and partially concedes the thesis. Methodology is transparent: "author's own arithmetic from published category totals" in the disclaimer. Deduction: the fragility 60-95% range is wide enough to be nearly unfalsifiable, and the article leans on it without noting the width.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10
Every percentage arrives with its absolute: 39,254 deaths (-4.3%, 1,771 fewer), 9,587→9,727 (+1.5%), 4,917/794/217/1,742/956 with percentages summing correctly to 8,626. The 66.2% is derived in prose with the addition shown. Denominators are never hidden. Deduction: the 2022-era 42% figure and the 2024-era 33% figure sit in adjacent paragraphs without an explicit note that they cover different windows and sources; a careful reader can infer it, but the article should say it.

## Verdict
All 7 critics ≥ 8.5 (range 8.7–9.0, average 8.86). All hard gates pass. **SHIP.**
