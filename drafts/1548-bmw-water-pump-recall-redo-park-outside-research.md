# Research — #1548 BMW Water Pump Recall Redo (26V733)

## Story
BMW recalled 720,796 cars and SUVs a second time for the same water-pump fire risk. The 2024 fix (a splash shield) failed. There is no remedy yet, and owners are told to park outside until one exists.

## Verified facts
- Campaign 26V733; manufacturer report date Nov 4, 2026; recall decision Oct 28, 2026; NHTSA consumer alert Nov 13, 2026.
- 720,796 vehicles, model years 2012–2018, N20/N26 2.0L four-cylinder engines, 14 model lines: 2012–2016 3 Series, 5 Series, Z4; 2012–2015 X1; 2013–2017 X3; 2014–2016 2 Series coupe/convertible, 4 Series coupe/convertible/Gran Coupe, 3 Series Sportswagon, 328xi Gran Turismo; 2015–2018 X4; 2016–2018 X5 xDrive40e.
- Defect: improperly sealed electrical connector on the engine water pump; material degradation lets moisture in, short circuit, thermal event, in rare cases fire. Can happen parked and switched off.
- Replaces 24V608 (filed Aug 13–14, 2024), same 720,796 vehicles. 2024 remedy: inspect pump/plug, replace if damaged, install a shield to divert blow-by fluid from the crankcase ventilation hose.
- Why the shield failed: April 2025, a 5 Series outside the U.S. overheated at the pump connector despite the 2024 remedy. Early 2026 review found more incidents in repaired cars. Lab work: PBT (polybutylene terephthalate) connector plastic severely embrittled, CT scans found cracks; hydrolysis suspected. Root cause still officially undetermined. A shield keeps drips off; it cannot undo plastic that degrades on its own.
- 2024 origin: March 2024 inquiry from South Korea's transport ministry about a thermal event; ~18 U.S. complaints/field reports at the time, no crashes or injuries.
- 2026: BMW aware of ~10 U.S. incidents possibly related; no accidents or injuries known. BMW estimates the defect in fewer than 0.1% of the population.
- 2024 completion: 211,553 vehicles repaired by end of 2025 (quarterly completion reports), about 29% of the population.
- Remedy: none. "Under development." Owners advised to park outside and away from structures. No do-not-drive warning; owners may keep driving. Dealers told not to sell/lease/deliver covered vehicles until repaired.
- Timing: dealers notified Nov 4, 2026; owner letters mailed beginning Dec 25, 2026; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning Dec 25, 2026. BMW hotline 800-525-7417.
- Supplier Pierburg; three water pump part numbers listed in the filing. 2024 service bulletin said the pump itself was not part of that recall, so owners who paid for pump replacement paid out of pocket.
- Population mix (Part 573): 3 Series sedans 236,702; X3 163,728; 5 Series 110,102.

## Original math (computed for this article)
- Recall of a recall: 720,796 vehicles recalled twice for the same component, 27 months apart (Aug 2024 → Nov 2026).
- 2024 fix completion: 211,553 / 720,796 = 29.4% repaired; 509,243 vehicles (70.6%) never got the shield at all — and the shield did not work on the ones that did.
- Incident rate: 10 known U.S. incidents / 720,796 = 1.4 per 100,000 vehicles. BMW's <0.1% defect estimate caps the truly defective population at ~721 vehicles, yet all 720,796 must park outside with no remedy and no date.
- Top three models: 3 Series 236,702 (32.8%) + X3 163,728 (22.7%) + 5 Series 110,102 (15.3%) = 510,532 vehicles, 70.8% of the recall.
- Notification gap: dealers told Nov 4, owners told Dec 25 — 51 days. VIN lookup blackout until Dec 25 — 42 days after the public alert (Nov 13) during which owners cannot confirm whether their car is covered.
- Park-outside context: this is the third park-outside recall in The Crash Report's queue (#981 Jeep, #1173 Genesis G80), and the only one that is a redo of a failed prior recall.

## Kill test
PASS. 720,796 vehicles, a park-outside order with no remedy, a failed 2024 fix, and a materials-science root cause (PBT hydrolysis) that explains why the first repair was doomed. Novel angle: the recall that failed — BMW's first fix was a hypothesis with a shield bolted to it.
