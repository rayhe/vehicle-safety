GMC Recalled Its 2026 Lineup 11 Times. The Van Did Four of Them.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. GMC filed 11 recall campaigns touching its 2026 models this year, covering 59,606 vehicles on paper, and the distribution is the story. A work van most buyers never think about carried four of the eleven. Five other GMC nameplates carry exactly one campaign each, and it is the same one: a missing owner's manual. Meanwhile the defect that defines GMC ownership in 2026, an engine that seizes without warning, sits outside the count entirely, at 297,404 GMC trucks.[1][11]

297,404
GMC trucks in the L87 engine recall the census excludes, five times the entire counted total

Method first, because a count is only as honest as its rules. I pulled the canonical 2026 model list from NHTSA's VPIC database, ten models, then queried the recalls API for every nameplate plus the Sierra 1500, 2500, 3500, and EV splits the agency files separately. Fourteen queries. Two came back empty with HTTP 400s, which the API uses to mean nothing filed under that exact name. Twelve nameplates returned results, and every one of them carries at least one 2026 filing. That last sentence sounds impressive until you read the table.[1]

Start with the van. Savana carries four campaigns, more than any other GMC nameplate, and three of them are steering defects on a platform GM has built unchanged since 1996, the oldest single-generation vehicle still sold new in America. In May, 232 vans got recalled because the gear shifter could tell a driver the truck was in Park when it was not. In June, 26,541 vans got recalled because a nut in the steering gear assembly may not have been tightened properly, after a malfunctioning sensor at supplier Nexteer let assemblies leave the factory with nuts only hand-tight. Then in October, GM filed the same nut again: 12,715 vans, because 108 suspect steering gear serial numbers were left off the first recall's inspection list, and a dealer found a loose nut in a van that had already been inspected and cleared under the June campaign. A customer did everything right, brought the van in, drove away cleared, and got recalled again for the same nut, 128 days later.[5][6]

Now the other end of the table, where the count gets flattering. Terrain, Canyon, Sierra 2500, Hummer EV Pickup, and Hummer EV SUV each carry exactly one campaign, and it is 26V114, a radio that was not set to download the electronic owner's manual. GM's own filing puts the whole campaign at 4,054 vehicles across roughly forty GM nameplates, with per-model GMC entries as small as 50 Acadias and 54 Yukon XLs. Strip that one paperwork filing and GMC's count falls to ten, with five nameplates at zero, which is the honest picture of how many distinct things went wrong with a 2026 Terrain or Canyon this year. One, and it was a manual.[2]

Between those extremes sit the campaigns that actually bite. Campaign 26V289 is the only do-not-drive in the census: 66 trucks and SUVs whose transfer cases left a Magna Powertrain plant in Mexico missing an oil pickup tube, which means bearings run dry and a wheel can lock up without warning, and GM told owners not to drive them at all. Twelve of the 66 are 2026 Sierra 1500s. Campaign 26V129 covers 11,787 Sierra and Silverado 3500 trucks with gas engines and dual tanks that can stall with fuel still onboard, because a bad engine-control calibration disables the rear-to-front fuel transfer. Campaign 26V696 covers four crossovers in the United States, three Traverses and three Acadias across North America, whose front seat frames may not have enough welds where the recliner pivot attaches. GM lists all six VINs in its bulletin. Six vehicles. One recall. That is how specific a safety filing can get.[3][4][9][10]

And now the filing the count leaves out, which is where the story actually lives for anyone who already owns a GMC. Campaign 25V274, filed in April 2025, covers 597,571 vehicles with the 6.2-liter L87 V8, and 297,404 of them are GMCs: 153,637 Sierra 1500s, 82,841 Yukons, 60,926 Yukon XLs. GM had logged 28,102 engine failure reports before it filed. In August 2026, NHTSA opened Engineering Analysis EA26-005, covering 997,743 vehicles, because the remedy may not hold. That filing is excluded from this census twice over: it was filed in 2025, and it covers model years 2021 through 2024. Add the three 2025-filed campaigns that do touch 2026 GMCs, an ESC warning light on 1,191 Sierra EVs, a bad airbag crimp on 354 Hummer EVs, and a wrong airbag calibration on 1,120 Canyon AT4X AEVs, and the excluded total reaches 600,236 vehicles. For every GMC the census counts, five more were recalled under filings the method excludes.[11][12]

Fairness requires the other side, stated at full strength, because the 11-count flatters GMC four ways and a reader shopping a Sierra deserves to hear it before the comments say it. First, the count's breadth is mostly one paperwork filing, and without 26V114 five nameplates drop to zero. Second, the count's volume is mostly vans: the two steering-nut campaigns alone are 39,256 of the 59,606 counted mentions, and the Savana is a low-volume fleet van, with GMC the minority twin in its own biggest recall. Third, the count excludes the filing that defines GMC ownership in 2026, and anyone who reads "GMC: 11" as the brand's safety record is wrong about 297,404 trucks. Fourth, the count partly measures a small catalog, not improving quality: GMC sells ten nameplates, and the Sierra EV racking up three campaigns on a truck that barely sells is a rate story the count hides. Strip the manual, the van, and the nut filed twice, and the 2026 GMC record is thinner than eleven suggests.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 59,606 total counts every vehicle in each filing across all model years, not 2026-only cars. That total also double-counts, because 26V718 re-inspects vans already counted in 26V399, so the no-double-count total is 46,891. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates, not 2026-model-year counts; the 2026-only GMC total cannot be computed from the filings, because the per-model split for 26V114 is not fully broken out in the Part 573 read. NHTSA's over-the-air flag undercounts software remedies, as it has in every census in this series: zero of eleven campaigns carry a true flag, even though the ESC recall the method excludes is explicitly fixed by dealer or over-the-air update. Late filings will move every number in this article, and the 2026 model year is still in progress.

What to do with all of this. If you own a 2025 or 2026 Express or Savana, check your VIN again even if a dealer cleared your van in the summer, because 26V718 exists for exactly that van, and owner letters go out December 14, 2026. If you own a 2025 or 2026 Sierra 3500 with a gas engine and dual tanks, the free fix for a stall-with-fuel-onboard is an ECM software update, and letters went out April 8, 2026. If you own a 2026 Sierra EV, check for the steel spare wheel recall and the ESC warning-light recall filed last September, which the census excludes only because of the calendar. If you own a 2021 through 2024 Sierra 1500, Yukon, or Yukon XL with the 6.2-liter V8, you are in the excluded filing, and NHTSA is now asking whether the remedy holds, so check your VIN now and do not assume the recall repair settled it. If you are shopping a 2026 GMC, the census is accurate for new metal, but shop by campaign, not by count: the Savana carries four real defects, the Sierra EV three, and five nameplates carry exactly one recall between them, a missing owner's manual. Check any GMC VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you sign, and check a used Sierra or Yukon twice.
