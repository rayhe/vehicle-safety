GM Is Recalling 306,511 SUVs Because the Glue Holding Their Backup Cameras Together Gave Up

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
September 23, 2026

Let's talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds after you shift into reverse. Your brain expects a video feed. On 306,511 GM SUVs, it may get a blank screen instead, because a manufacturing step designed to make the camera housing's adhesive bond stronger made it weaker.[1] NHTSA campaign 26V539000, issued September 17, covers the 2025 Chevrolet Trax and Trailblazer, the 2024-2025 Buick Envista, and the 2024-2026 Buick Encore GX.

33,000
Owner complaints tied to the affected build window — against 306,511 recalled vehicles, a 10.8% complaint rate

Plasma cleaning is standard practice in camera assembly. You ionize the housing surface so the adhesive grips better. Here it damaged the bond instead, so moisture and grime creep inside over months until the video feed warps, flickers, or dies.[2] GM started investigating dead-camera complaints last year, found no clear pattern, and reopened the case this year as reports kept arriving. Affected vehicles were built April through December 2024, plus some early 2026 models, and the suspect assemblies carry part numbers 86554977 and 86554978.

More than half the population is one vehicle. The 2025 Chevrolet Trax accounts for 159,821 of the 306,511 recalled units, 52.1 percent.[1] GM's cheapest SUV carries the most defective cameras. If you drive a 2025 Trax, your odds of being in this recall are not a rounding error.

This matters more than a convenience feature. Backup cameras have been mandatory on every new vehicle sold in the US since May 1, 2018, under FMVSS 111, the rule Congress ordered after a pediatrician's two-year-old son was killed in a driveway backover.[3] NHTSA's rulemaking estimated an average of 292 backover deaths a year, with 44 percent of the light-vehicle victims under five and 33 percent over seventy.[4] The camera exists to watch a 10-by-20-foot kill zone behind the bumper. A blank screen is not a glitch in your infotainment. It is mandated safety equipment failing on roughly one of every nine cars in the recall population.

A blank camera may be worse than no camera. You glance at the screen, see nothing threatening, and your brain reads the empty frame as a clear rear. With a mirror-only car, at least you know you're doing the work yourself. Until your dealer confirms the fix, back up like the camera doesn't exist.

What to do: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not when the letter arrives. Owner notifications went out September 16, but if your camera flickers or drops in reverse, that symptom is this defect, so book the dealer now.[5] Dealers inspect and replace the camera free. Chevrolet: 1-800-222-1020. Buick: 1-800-521-7300. Shopping used? Confirm the actual dealer repair record, not just that the recall shows as closed.

GM's defense deserves its full weight. Thirty-three thousand complaints, zero reported crashes, zero reported injuries. That ratio is the failure announcing itself, loudly and visibly, instead of hiding. GM opened the investigation last year, reopened it when the pattern solidified, traced it to a specific manufacturing step, and recalled 306,511 vehicles before anyone got hurt. That is the safety system working as designed. And the recall population is a production window, not a failure count; the great majority of these cameras will never fail.

And here is what this doesn't prove. The 33,000 figure is GM's own count of complaints tied to the build window, not an independently audited defect rate. Moisture ingress is gradual, so some cameras may be partially degraded in ways owners shrug off as "just a glitch," which means the true failure count could exceed the complaint count. No injury or crash data exists because GM reports none, so this article cannot quantify real-world backover risk from these failures, only the mechanism. A failed camera is failed mandated safety equipment. Whether any of these specific failures cost a life is a question the data cannot answer.
