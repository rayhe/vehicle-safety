# Research: 1393 — The Pacifica Airbag Recall Reached the Parts Bin

**Slug:** 1393-pacifica-curtain-airbag-parts-recall
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** 2026-11-04

## Angle
Chrysler's Pacifica side curtain airbag defect has now been recalled four times, and the fourth filing is the one that stings: NHTSA campaign 26E-014 recalls 105 loose Mopar replacement curtain airbags, the very service parts meant to fix the defect, because they carry the same defect. Same supplier (Autoliv ASP, Auburn Hills). Same failure (improperly sealed seams, insufficient pressure retention during deployment). Same ejection risk through a side window. The only difference is where the bad airbags were sitting: not in vans, but on dealer shelves and in body shops, with some possibly already installed in vehicles. FCA's remedy splits on that question: buy the part back if it is still in the box, replace it if it is already in a van.

## Kill Test
- Newsworthy? Yes. A defect that survived three vehicle recalls and reappeared in the replacement-parts channel is the recall system chasing its own tail, in public, with paperwork.
- Novel for this site? Yes. Coverage check 2026-11-04: the vehicle recalls are covered (pacifica-airbag-paradox covers the 178,246-vehicle 26V189 round; #808 covers Toyota's separate replacement-airbag recall, different brand and supplier). No draft or story covers 26E-014, the 105-part Mopar equipment recall, or the four-filing chain as a single story. Nearest neighbors are different stories, not this filing.
- Novel contribution: the chain assembled end to end from the Part 573 chronology, plus the arithmetic that makes the scale visceral (105 parts against 178,246 vehicles; a 30% defect estimate on the parts vs 14% on the first vehicle population).

## The four-filing chain (from the 26E-014 Part 573 chronology)
- **May 8, 2025:** FCA files 25V302 (vehicles, 06C) and 25E033 (equipment, 07C) for 2022-2025 Pacifica and Voyager, based on supplier testing and production records available at the time.
- **June 27 and 30, 2025:** NHTSA runs ejection-mitigation testing on a 2025 Pacifica; left and right curtain airbags fail an excursion-limit requirement.
- **July 31, 2025:** FCA's Technical Safety and Regulatory Compliance team opens an investigation into 2025 Pacificas and Voyagers that may carry airbags that do not retain sufficient pressure.
- **September 4, 2025:** FCA files 25V573 (82C) for 2025 models, population set by curtain-airbag material-lot traceability and vehicle production records.
- **September 2025 through February 2026:** FCA meets regularly with the supplier, engineering, and supplier-quality teams to review data and check whether more vehicles are affected.
- **March 19, 2026:** FCA's Vehicle Regulations Committee determines a safety defect potentially exists in the affected equipment.
- **March 26, 2026:** Part 573 filed for 26E-014 (07D), the equipment recall of 105 loose Mopar replacement airbags. Dealers notified around March 27; owner final letters around May 14, 2026.
- **Parallel vehicle round:** 26V189 (FCA 10D/11D) recalls 178,246 Pacifica and Voyager vehicles (2022-2026) for the same seam defect, per USA Today.

## Novel calculations
- **30% of 105 is about 32 airbags.** FCA's own estimate says roughly 32 of the 105 loose parts carry the defect. That is a higher defect share than the first vehicle population (about 14%, or 35,091 of 250,651).
- **105 parts vs 178,246 vehicles:** the parts recall is 0.059% the size of the March 2026 vehicle recall, roughly 1 part for every 1,698 vehicles. Small count, same failure mode, and the only filing in the chain where the defective item was never installed in a car at all.
- **322 days, four filings:** May 8, 2025 to March 26, 2026. The defect was found, expanded twice, and then found again in the parts channel.
- **The seam is the whole story:** every filing in the chain names the same manufacturing fault, improperly sealed airbag seams, from the same Tier 1 supplier, Autoliv ASP of Auburn Hills. This is not four defects. It is one defect with four mailing addresses.

## Counterarguments
- 105 parts is a tiny population, and zero injuries, claims, or field reports were tied to these parts as of March 5, 2026. Nothing here shows a bad replacement airbag actually failed in a crash.
- The system arguably worked: supplier manufacturing records identified a suspect production window (October 25, 2024 to July 14, 2025), and the parts were pulled before most could be installed. Traceability, not luck, found them.
- The remedy is simple and free: buy-back off the shelf, replacement in the van, with new parts carrying properly sealed seams.
- The vehicle recalls dwarf the parts recall, and owners of 2022-2026 Pacificas and Voyagers should check the vehicle campaigns (26V189, 10D/11D) first, since those cover the vans actually on the road.

## Limitations
- The four vehicle-recall populations overlap (26V189 covers vehicles with incomplete status under earlier recalls plus 2026 models). Do NOT add 250,651 and 178,246 into a single "total vehicles" figure; the filings do not support that sum.
- Whether any of the 105 parts were actually installed in a van before the recall is not stated in the Part 573; the dealer instructions cover both cases, which proves FCA could not rule it out, not that it happened.
- The 30% defect estimate is FCA's filed estimate, not a measured failure rate from testing all 105 parts.
- The 06D cross-reference in the dealer notice is not fully explained in the documents read; the article states only that dealer instructions route vans already under a vehicle recall through that campaign's instructions, without asserting what 06D covers beyond that.

## Actionable takeaways (for the article)
- Pacifica/Voyager owners (2022-2026): check VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V189 (10D/11D) and any earlier open curtain-airbag campaign; the repair is free and takes about two hours per side on the vehicle recalls.
- Anyone who had a Pacifica/Voyager curtain airbag replaced at a dealer or body shop: ask whether the installed part was Mopar 68224526AJ/68224527AH from the suspect window, and whether it falls under 07D; FCA will replace an installed recalled part free.
- Dealers and shops: recalled parts in inventory may not legally be delivered or sold; FCA repurchases uninstalled units.
