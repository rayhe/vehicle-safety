## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10
Structure is sound: kicker, headline, lede with the key stat bolded, pull stat, five body paragraphs, references, disclaimer. The headline carries the whole story and the lede delivers the 1,284-vs-228 contrast in the first paragraph. Body is ~560 words, inside the guide range. ## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victims, no children, no individual shaming. The "probably not the best-maintained example of its breed" line judges the condition of old trucks, not their owners, and it sits inside the counterargument paragraph that concedes the point's force. No self-congratulation, no moralizing about buying decisions. The piece does not overclaim: the causal language stays at "does most of the explaining" for ESC, not "caused the drop."

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.5/10
The headline is built to travel ("Death Rate Fell 85% Overnight") and the 85% pull stat is a clean share trigger that survives screenshotting without context. The 106-to-29 cliff is the second hook. Slightly data-heavy for viral spread: the exposure-adjustment explanation is necessary but not punchy, and the piece has no second pull quote for social cards. Good enough to ship; not the site's most viral frame.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Every figure traces to a primary source: FARS 2014–2023 model-year counts via the site dataset (1,284 pre-2007, 228 post-2007, peak MY2004 198, MY2006 106, MY2007 29); Edmunds and MotorTrend for the 2007 hardware changes (10% stiffer frame, standard AdvanceTrac with RSC, three-row curtains, larger brakes); Ford launch material via VehicleVoice for the "class-exclusive" claim and Stephen Kozak quote. No legal claims of consequence are made. The "300 horsepower" carryover engine figure matches Edmunds. The 3-death gap between the BY_MODEL total (1,515) and the model-year table sum (1,512) is a source-table rounding artifact; the article uses the model-year series consistently (1,284 + 228 = 1,512).

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution, not synthesis: the exposure-adjusted per-model-year death series for the Expedition, the 85.3% drop, the 106-to-29 cliff, and the worst-post-below-best-pre comparison — none of it published anywhere on this site before. Limitations are honest and specific (window-exposure adjustment is not a fleet or VMT adjustment; the 2007 refresh changed five things at once, so no single component can claim the drop; FARS captures fatal crashes only). The strongest counterargument (survivorship/attrition plus truncated exposure) is stated at full strength and the piece concedes both effects are real before answering with the step-vs-slope distinction. One gap: the exposure-adjustment method is described in the disclaimer but the worked example (a 2021 model year has 3 observable years) never appears in the body — flagged, not hidden.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
The pull stat (85%) is the right number and the math is shown with inputs in the body (1,284 vs 228; 106 to 29; 198 peak; 88 best pre-2007 year). Deduction: the exposure adjustment itself is a black box in the article — the reader is told the rate is "adjusted for how long each model year was actually on the road" but never sees the divisor. The research file documents the method (deaths divided by min(10, 2023 − model_year + 1)); the article should carry one worked sentence. Minor at this length, and the disclaimer states the method's limits, so this is noted rather than fixed.
