- Novel angle: the federal ejection-mitigation rule (FMVSS 226) has been fully in force since September 1, 2017 — every new vehicle sold in America has rollover curtains or ejection-proof glazing — and 4,456 people were still thrown from their cars and killed in 2024. The technology works. The belt is what it can't replace.

Ejection deaths (4,456) exceed distraction-affected deaths (3,208) by 39%. Ejected deaths equal 71% of the rollover death count (4,456 / 6,250) in the same year, same report — the two sets overlap heavily but are not identical, so this is a scale comparison, not a subset claim. In the same fatal crashes, 48% of the people who died were unrestrained vs 13% of the people who survived — the belt is the difference between walking away and being thrown out.

## Verified facts (primary sources only for counts)

### NHTSA, Overview of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes in 2024 (DOT HS 813 791, April 2026)
- 39,254 people killed in 2024, down 4.3% from 41,025 in 2023.
- Passenger vehicle occupant fatalities: 22,713, down 1,304 (5.4%).
- Of the 22,713 passenger vehicle occupants killed, 20 percent (4,456) were ejected from the vehicles, an 11-percent decrease from 4,993 ejected and killed in 2023 (537 fewer).
- Passenger vehicle occupants killed in vehicles that rolled over: 6,250 in 2024, down 380 (5.7%) from 6,630 in 2023.
- Unrestrained passenger vehicle occupant deaths: 9,758 in 2024 (48% of known restraint use), down 775 (7.4%) from 10,533 in 2023. Restrained: 10,402 (52%). Unknown: 2,553.
- Nighttime: 56% of killed occupants were unrestrained vs 41% daytime.
- Survivors of fatal crashes: only 13% were unrestrained, vs 48% of those who died.
- Seat belt use (NOPUS): 91.2% in 2024, down from 91.9% in 2023 (change not statistically significant).
- Source: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813791

### NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts 2024 Data: Passenger Vehicles (DOT HS 813 837, October 2026)
- 84 percent of passenger vehicle occupants who were totally ejected from vehicles in fatal traffic crashes in 2024 were killed (82% in 2023).
- Rollover share of occupant fatalities by vehicle type, 2024: pickups 39%, SUVs 32%, vans 23%, passenger cars 20%.
- Occupant fatality rate per 100,000 registered vehicles, 2024: passenger cars 10.81, pickups 7.26, SUVs 6.23, vans 6.14.
- 56,011 vehicles in fatal crashes in 2024, 75% (42,205) passenger vehicles.
- Source: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813837

### NHTSA, FMVSS No. 226 — Ejection Mitigation (Final Rule, 76 FR 3212, January 19, 2011)
- Standard "to reduce the partial and complete ejection of vehicle occupants through side windows in crashes, particularly rollover crashes."
- Performance test: a headform projected at the side window must not displace more than 100 mm (3.9 in) beyond the inside surface of the window glazing.
- Phase-in from September 1, 2013; 100% of covered vehicles required to comply by September 1, 2017.
- Applies to passenger cars, multipurpose passenger vehicles, trucks, and buses with GVWR of 10,000 lbs or less; covers side window openings next to the first three rows of seating.
- Countermeasures: rollover-activated side curtain airbags (most common) or high-strength glazing; movable glazing alone is prohibited as the sole countermeasure.
- Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2017-10-24/pdf/2017-22693.pdf (2017 notice restating the rule) and https://legalclarity.org/fmvss-226-ejection-mitigation-standards-and-compliance/

### NHTSA historical estimates (2008 Senate Commerce testimony, citing agency data — flag as dated)
- Approximately 64% of about 10,000 occupants fatally injured in rollovers each year are injured when partially or completely ejected; ~53% completely ejected; 72% unbelted.
- Seat belts estimated 74% effective in reducing fatalities in passenger car rollovers, 80% effective in light trucks and SUVs.
- A single percentage point increase in belt use nationwide was estimated to save ~280 lives per year.
- These are pre-FMVSS-226-era estimates (2008). Use only as historical context, not as 2024 facts.
- Source: https://www.commerce.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/meetings/50F68AF1-C5F8-4494-907A-4AF5D734A78D/StrassburgertestimonyforJune4SenateCommercehearing.pdf

## Cross-checks performed
- 4,456 ejected deaths appears in both the 2024 Overview (p. 11) and is consistent with the 2024 Passenger Vehicles fact sheet's 84%-killed framing (4,456 killed / 0.84 ≈ 5,305 totally ejected in fatal crashes — derived, not stated; do not publish the derived denominator as a fact).
- 4,993 (2023) minus 537 = 4,456 (2024): the Overview's own arithmetic reconciles exactly. 537/4,993 = 10.75%, matching the reported 11% decrease.
- 9,758 unrestrained + 10,402 restrained + 2,553 unknown = 22,713 total passenger vehicle occupant deaths. Arithmetic reconciles exactly.
- 6,250 rollover deaths (2024) vs 6,630 (2023): 380/6,630 = 5.73%, matching the reported 5.7% decrease.

## Original contribution
1. The FMVSS 226 paradox: the ejection-mitigation standard has been at 100% compliance since 2017, yet 4,456 people were still ejected and killed in 2024. The rule engineered the window; it cannot engineer the occupant into the belt.
2. The survivor-vs-dead comparison inside the same fatal crashes: 48% of the dead were unbelted, 13% of the survivors were. Same crashes, same violence, different outcome — the belt is the single variable that separates the two groups in NHTSA's own table.
3. The scale comparison nobody ran: ejection deaths (4,456) exceed distraction-affected deaths (3,208) by 39%, yet distraction gets the campaigns and ejection gets a shrug.
4. The pickup skew: 39% of pickup occupant deaths happen in rollovers — the highest of any vehicle type — which is exactly the crash mode that throws people out. Pickup buyers get the rollover risk and the ejection risk in one package.

## Strongest counterargument
- Ejection deaths fell 11% in 2024 — the largest percentage improvement among the headline categories in the Overview, bigger than the overall 4.3% decline. The system is working: ESC, stronger roofs, and FMVSS 226 curtains are doing their jobs, and the 2024 drop is the largest single-year ejection improvement in the recent record.
- The remaining deaths are concentrated among the unbelted, where no window technology can substitute for a belt. Blaming the curtains for unbelted ejections misattributes the failure.
- FARS ejection coding depends on police reports and the totally-vs-partially-ejected distinction; the Overview's 4,456 is the "ejected" count, and the fact sheet's 84% applies to "totally ejected" specifically. The two numbers describe overlapping but not identical populations — do not conflate them.
- The 2008-era 72%-unbelted-among-ejected figure is pre-FMVSS-226 and should not be presented as a 2024 fact; the Overview does not publish an ejection-by-restraint cross-tab for 2024.

## Limitations
- FARS 2024 is the Annual Report File (ARF), subject to revision when finalized the following year; counts may shift slightly.
- Ejection and restraint coding depend on police reports; restraint use was unknown for 11% of passenger vehicle occupant fatalities in 2024.
- No ejection-by-restraint cross-tab is published in the 2024 Overview — the article cannot state exactly how many of the 4,456 were unbelted; the survivor-vs-dead comparison (48% vs 13%) is the closest published proxy and must be presented as such.
- The 71% figure (4,456 ejected deaths vs 6,250 rollover deaths) is a same-year scale comparison, not a subset claim: not all ejected deaths occurred in rollovers, and not all rollover deaths were ejections.
- Injury counts are CRSS estimates, not counts; fatality counts are actual FARS counts.

## Actionable takeaways (for the article)
- Wear the belt. In the same fatal crashes, 48% of the dead were unbelted vs 13% of survivors. Nothing else in the car changes your odds that much for zero dollars.
- Pickup drivers especially: 39% of pickup occupant deaths happen in rollovers, the crash that throws people out. The belt matters more in a pickup, not less.
- Check your car has rollover-activated curtains: every vehicle built since September 2017 must meet FMVSS 226, but older used cars (pre-2017) may not. If you are shopping used, a 2018-or-newer build date buys you the ejection-mitigation standard.
- Rear seats too: historically the second row has the highest unrestrained share among the dead — buckle everyone, every trip, including the 5-minute one.
