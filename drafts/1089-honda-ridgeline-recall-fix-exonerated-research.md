# Research: NHTSA Spent Two Years Trying to Prove Honda's Recall Fix Was Broken. The Fix Won.

**Slug:** 1089-honda-ridgeline-recall-fix-exonerated
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; "The Gap"/"Investigation")
**Kicker:** Investigation
**News peg:** NHTSA closed Engineering Analysis EA25-003 on September 22, 2026 (Reuters), with no further action. Carscoops follow-up Oct 6, 2026.
**Kill test:** PASS. A federal investigation opened on zero complaints, run for two years, ending in exoneration is genuinely unusual on this beat. Novel angle: the proactive "recall remedy review" mechanism + contrast with recall fixes that did fail (GM L87, under EA26005 right now).

## Verified facts (primary sources)

1. **Original recall 22V-867** (NHTSA recalls API, ReportReceivedDate 11/23/2022): Honda recalled certain 2017-2019 Ridgeline vehicles. "Opening and closing the tailgate may break the rearview camera wiring harness, causing the rearview camera to fail." Honda campaign 6RW. Population ~129,000 (ODI resume RQ24-011: 129,266 estimated; Reuters: 129,092).
2. **Defect mechanism** (ODI Resume RQ24-011, primary NHTSA document): protective corrugated tubing insufficient against bending wear; zip ties insufficiently tightened and would not keep tubing in place; harness fatigues and breaks after repeated tailgate open/close; RVC image fails to display. Remedy: replacement harness with longer protective corrugated tubing and sufficiently tightened zip ties.
3. **Scale of original problem** (AutoBodyNews, citing NHTSA): 3,437 backup-camera warranty claims Dec 2017-Oct 2022; zero injuries or deaths.
4. **Second recall 24V-321** (ODI resume; autoevolution/carbuzz): May 3, 2024, Honda recalled 187,290 model-year 2020-2024 Ridgelines (Honda YI7) for RVC wire harness breakage. Supplier Aptiv Services US (Troy, Michigan); woven twisted tube susceptible to moisture; freezing water + road salt + tailgate cycling = fatigue and breakage. Remedy: new-supplier harness with improved materials. 402 warranty claims, no accidents/injuries.
5. **RQ24-011 opened 06/26/2024** (ODI Resume, primary): "Prompted By: Recall Remedy Review." Investigator Caroline Woody, reviewer Sharon Yukevich, approver Tanya Topka. The money quote: at opening, "ODI has not received any allegations of RVC failure post recall 22V-867 remedy." The query was opened because the 22V-867 remedy parts and the 24V-321 recalled parts "use the same supplier and materials for critical components in the RVC wire harness." Pure proactive suspicion. Zero crashes/fires/injuries/fatalities in the failure report summary.
6. **Upgraded to EA25-003, Feb 2025** (Reuters 9/22/2026): after NHTSA received reports of wire-harness failures in vehicles that had received the original recall remedy. Engineering Analysis is the last step before a recall demand.
7. **Closed Sept 22, 2026, no further action** (Reuters 9/22/2026): NHTSA's Vehicle Research and Test Center surveyed owners, inspected selected vehicles, and ran durability testing of the wiring harness. Findings: no RVC failures caused by wire-harness damage; the part "not expected to fail during a reasonable vehicle lifespan"; most reports received involved other RVC or wire-harness failure modes not linked to the durability issue under investigation.
8. **Fix details** (Carscoops 10/6/2026): dealers replaced the harness with one with more protective tubing and tighter zip ties. VRTC concluded the impacted part should not fail "within a reasonable vehicle life span."
9. **Why it matters legally** (TheAutoWire 9/22/2026; autoevolution): FMVSS 111 treats the rearview display as required safety equipment. S5.5.3 requires the image within 2 seconds of the start of the backing event; applies to light vehicles built on/after May 1, 2018 (covers 2019 Ridgeline, part of 2018). Out of warranty, harness+camera+labor is an owner-paid bill, and a dead display can fail state safety inspection.

## Novel contribution
- Reconstruction of the full RQ -> EA -> closure arc from primary ODI documents, surfacing that NHTSA opened the probe with literally zero post-remedy complaints (proactive "recall remedy review").
- The contrast frame: two recall remedies under the federal microscope in 2026. Honda's Ridgeline harness fix: exonerated. GM's L87 V8 fix (EA26005, ~997,743 trucks/SUVs, ~7,000 failure complaints, failures reported AFTER the 25V-274 recall repair): still open, early read negative. Same regulatory tool, opposite trajectories.
- Actionable owner guidance: if you own a 2017-2019 Ridgeline and the 22V-867 remedy was performed, your harness is almost certainly fine per federal testing. If your camera is dead now, NHTSA's own data says it's probably the camera module or another failure mode, not the harness. Check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for both 22V-867 and (2020-2024 trucks) 24V-321.

## Limitations
- NHTSA never defined "reasonable vehicle life span"; VRTC sample sizes and test-cycle counts were not disclosed in public summaries.
- "Most reports were other failure modes" leaves room for a low background rate of genuine harness failures the sample didn't catch.
- The closure covers only the 2017-2019 remedy durability question; it says nothing about 2020-2024 trucks under 24V-321.
- The GM L87 contrast is a live investigation; its outcome is unknown. Framed as trajectory comparison, not verdict.

## Strongest counterargument
Owners who paid out of pocket for post-remedy camera repairs have a legitimate grievance regardless of the federal verdict; NHTSA's suspicion was well-founded (same supplier/materials as a genuinely defective part), and an EA closure is a finding of "insufficient evidence," not a certificate of perfection. The 2018-2019 trucks are now 7-8 years old; "reasonable vehicle life span" is doing quiet work in that sentence.
