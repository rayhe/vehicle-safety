Same Supplier, Same Skipped Furnace: Now Toyota’s Steering Knuckles Are Cracking

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 1, 2026

In July, Toyota recalled about 5,400 Grand Highlanders and Lexus TXs because the rear axle carriers could crack at their suspension mounts. On September 11, Toyota recalled 617 more 2026 SUVs because the front steering knuckles can crack and break. Both parts came from the same North Carolina forge. Both skipped the same aging furnace.

6,025
Vehicles recalled across two recalls in two months, one supplier, one root cause

The recipe for a forged aluminum part includes a trip through an aging furnace, which is not decoration. Aging is the heat-treatment step that brings the metal up to its specified strength, and when a batch of parts leaves the forge without it, you get aluminum that looks right and behaves wrong. Supplier Bharat Forge Aluminum USA caught the escape during a routine quality audit in late June, by which point the batch had already shipped and been bolted onto trucks built between June 19 and 26, 2026: 196 Lexus RX350s, 127 Grand Highlander Hybrids, 126 Grand Highlanders, 83 RX350hs, 61 TX350s, 23 TX500hs, and one lonely RX500h.[1][2]

What makes this one worth a column is the failure mode. A steering knuckle is one of the few components on a car with no redundancy whatsoever: it carries the wheel hub, anchors the suspension arms, and takes the tie rod that actually turns the wheel. If a camera glitches, you get a warning; if a knuckle fractures, you get nothing, no warning light, no limp-home mode. Toyota’s own description says the trigger is a large impact, like striking a pothole or a curb, which means the driver finds out about the defect at the exact moment the part was already failing.[3] That’s a hell of a way to learn metallurgy.

Fair is fair: this is the recall system working the way the brochure says it should. The supplier’s own audit caught the escape, and Toyota spent roughly ten weeks investigating before deciding. The recall went out with zero warranty claims and zero field reports, which is to say Toyota is recalling 617 vehicles for a defect that has hurt nobody yet.[2] Against the industry standard of recalling parts after the body count arrives, that’s almost noble. Almost.

Because here is the question Toyota has not answered, and it’s the one that keeps me up: the same forge, the same skipped furnace, two different parts, two months apart. A furnace that failed to age one batch of rear carriers and one batch of front knuckles was running the whole time in between, aging everything else it was asked to age. Presumably. Toyota has disclosed no broader audit of that supplier’s other parts, and until it does, every owner of a 2026 Grand Highlander, RX, or TX is left doing the math on which end of their suspension gets recalled next.[4]

So check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls against 26V598 (Toyota 26TA19, Lexus 26LA09) and 26V437, and do it now instead of waiting for the November 2 letter. Dealers will inspect and replace the knuckles free, but unlike the software recalls stacking up this year, this one involves a physical part that is quietly weakening under your front wheels. There is no dashboard light for under-aged aluminum.[1]
