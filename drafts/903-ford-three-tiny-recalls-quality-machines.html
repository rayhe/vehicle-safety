37 Trucks, 17 Raptors, 1,945 Vans: Ford’s Three Tiniest Recalls Were All About the Machines That Check the Machines

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
September 14, 2026

I ran the numbers on Ford’s September 9 recall filing. Then I ran them again, because the arithmetic looked too clean. 223,472 plus 1,945 plus 37 plus 17 equals exactly 225,471, the total the Detroit Free Press reported, which means every vehicle in that day’s filing is accounted for in exactly four campaigns.[1] Three of those four campaigns cover 1,999 vehicles, just 0.887 percent of the filing, and all three trace back to the factory machinery whose entire job is catching defects.

1,999
Vehicles across Ford’s three smallest Sept. 9 recall campaigns — 0.887% of that day’s filing

Start with the Raptor, because it is the funniest failure of the three. Seventeen 2026 F-150 Raptors, built between late December 2025 and late January 2026, shipped with rear Panhard rod fasteners that may not have been tightened to specification. The root cause, per Ford’s own chronology: the torque-checking device was in bypass mode.[2] Its one job was confirming the bolts are tight; with checking bypassed, the trucks rolled on. Ford only found out when a dealer discovered a customer’s truck with a loose Panhard rod, and the company has logged two warranty claims since. A loose Panhard rod can shift under load, which degrades handling, which raises crash risk, though nobody has crashed yet.

Of the three, the Transit is the cruelest irony: 1,945 2026-and-2027 Transits recalled because the wireless seat belt reminder, the system that warns you when rear passengers ride unbuckled, may never warn you. But the defect was not in the seat belt system. It was in the factory tester: a Magna end-of-line test device ran a status-check command that overlapped the reminder’s “pre-activated polling signal” and bricked the system into a permanent error state.[2] Put plainly, the machine that tests the safety system broke the safety system, with two field reports logged so far. There is no fix available yet, so the affected vans sit under a delivery hold while Ford figures out how to un-brick 1,945 dashboards.

9.7%
How much smaller the wrongly installed 8.8-inch axle bearings are than the 9.75-inch spec — on Max Tow trucks built to haul the heaviest loads

The axle bearings are the almost-success story, which makes them the most interesting of the three. Thirty-seven F-150s, built over just two days in May 2026, received 8.8-inch rear axle bearings instead of the 9.75-inch units specified for the Max Tow package.[2] Somebody installed bearings almost ten percent smaller than the design calls for in trucks engineered to haul the heaviest loads. Sterling Axle Plant caught the mistake mostly, though thirty-seven trucks escaped. Consequences of a wrong bearing are not subtle: seizure, locked rear wheels at speed, a separated driveshaft, a truck rolling away if the parking brake isn’t set.

Zoom out for the industry picture. Ford has filed 71 recall campaigns in 2026 through September 9, down from 108 in the same stretch of 2025, and it still leads the industry for the fifth straight year. Chrysler sits at 33, Toyota at 23, General Motors at 22.[1] Filing pace is down 34 percent, but the composition is what interests me. On the same day Ford recalled a quarter-million trucks because gas tanks can detach, it also recalled 37 trucks because somebody grabbed the wrong bearings off a shelf. One apparatus, two modes: firehose and pipette.

Now the full-strength case against my own thesis, because these three failures share a root cause the way three rainy days share weather: superficially. Against the thesis, the axle case actively contradicts the framing: the checking system worked, caught the error at the plant, and contained the damage to 37 trucks over two production days, which is precisely what quality control is supposed to do. Ford self-reported all four campaigns, recalled as few as 17 vehicles without a single reported injury, and put 1,945 Transits on a delivery hold rather than ship them broken. A company that recalls 37 trucks because it counted its axle bearings is running a functioning quality system, bypass-mode embarrassment notwithstanding.

What to do with all this: if you own a 2026 F-150 with the Max Tow package built May 27 or 28, 2026, or a 2026 F-150 Raptor built between late December 2025 and late January 2026, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now.[4] Raptor owners: an under-torqued Panhard rod telegraphs itself as vague rear-end steering or a clunk over bumps, so take it to the dealer before the notification letters land. Transit fleet buyers: the affected vans are on a delivery hold, so confirm with your dealer before scheduling upfits, since every remedy here is free.
