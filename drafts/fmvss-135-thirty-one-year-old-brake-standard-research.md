# Research: The Brake Standard Written Before ABS Was Mandatory

## Hook
NHTSA published an NPRM on June 26, 2026, proposing to amend FMVSS 135 ("Light vehicle brake systems") to accommodate ADS-equipped vehicles. The comment period was extended to August 26, 2026. On the same day, NHTSA withdrew the AV STEP voluntary safety program.

The irony: FMVSS 135 was finalized in 1995. Electronic Stability Control wasn't mandated until 2012. ABS wasn't required on all light vehicles until 2013 (via FMVSS 126/135 update). The brake standard being "modernized" for robots was written when Netscape Navigator was cutting-edge.

## Key Facts (Primary Sources)
1. **FMVSS 135** published 1995, governs light vehicle brake systems
   - Source: Federal Register, NHTSA press release June 25, 2026 (https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/fmvss-updates-brake-pedal-requirements)
2. **NPRM** proposed June 26, 2026: remove hand/foot brake controls for ADS-exclusive vehicles, retain stopping distance performance
   - Source: Federal Register 91 FR 38593
   - Comment period extended to August 26, 2026
3. **AV STEP withdrawal**: Voluntary safety program withdrawn same day
   - Source: Crowell & Moring analysis (https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/nhtsa-proposes-updates-to-federal-brake-standards-for-autonomous-vehicles-and-withdraws-av-step-program)
4. **ABS mandate timeline**: FMVSS 126 (ESC, which requires ABS) finalized 2007, effective 2012 for all vehicles
   - Source: NHTSA ESC final rule (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2007-06-22/html/E7-11965.htm)
5. **NHTSA Q1 2026**: Fatality rate dropped to 0.99/100M VMT, 7,770 deaths, down 4.3%
   - Source: NHTSA press release
6. **Speeding fatalities 2025**: Down 11% to 10,035 (pre-pandemic levels)
   - Source: NHTSA press release July 2, 2026

## FARS Data Context
- Total deaths in FARS dataset (2014-2023): ~490,000+
- All from human-driven vehicles governed by FMVSS 135 as-is
- Zero ADS-equipped vehicles in the FARS fatal crash dataset
- The standard being modernized has never killed a single person in an ADS context

## Novel Contribution
Cross-reference NHTSA rulemaking priority (ADS braking modification) with the actual death toll from the existing standard's fleet. Every death in the FARS dataset happened under the 1995 FMVSS 135 framework, yet NHTSA's modernization effort targets the zero-death ADS fleet, not the 38,000-death-per-year human fleet.

The strongest counterargument: ADS modernization is prospective (enabling future vehicles), not retrospective. You can't fix 1995-vintage vehicles with new rules. And ADS vehicles may reduce future deaths. Fair. But the regulatory attention allocation reveals priorities: one NPRM for robots, zero for the fleet that kills 38,000 per year.

## Limitations
- FMVSS 135 HAS been amended multiple times since 1995 (ESC addition, etc.)
- The ADS rulemaking is about removing unnecessary physical controls, not weakening brake performance
- Stopping distance requirements are being RETAINED for ADS vehicles
- FARS data doesn't code "brake failure" as a crash cause
