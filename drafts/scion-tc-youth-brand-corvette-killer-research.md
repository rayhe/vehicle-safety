# Research: Scion tC — Toyota's Youth Sports Car Kills at Higher Rates Than the Corvette

## Angle
Toyota created Scion as a youth brand in 2003, targeting 18-25 year olds. The tC was its best-selling model — an affordable coupe designed to be a first sports car. FARS data shows it kills at 2.30 per 100M VMT, a higher death rate than the Chevrolet Corvette (1.52). The paradox: a car marketed to the least experienced drivers is more lethal per mile than a car with twice the horsepower. Scion was discontinued in 2016, but 131,250 tCs are still in the US fleet.

## Primary Sources

### FARS Data (2014-2023)
- Toyota tC: deaths=242, rate=2.30/100M VMT, fleet=131,250, class=Sports Car
- Corvette: deaths=320, rate=1.52/100M VMT, fleet=262,500, class=Sports Car
- tC impairment: 21.9% any, 17.0% alcohol, 8.4% drug (894 drivers in FARS)
- Corvette impairment: 26.2% any, 21.3% alcohol, 10.4% drug (1,147 drivers)
- Despite LOWER impairment, the tC has a HIGHER death rate than the Corvette

### Key Findings (Original Contribution)
1. The tC has a LOWER impairment rate (21.9%) than the Corvette (26.2%) but a HIGHER death rate (2.30 vs 1.52). The car is killing sober young drivers.
2. Sober-adjusted death rate: tC = 2.30 × (1-0.219) = 1.80. Corvette = 1.52 × (1-0.262) = 1.12. The sober gap is even WIDER — the tC kills sober drivers at 1.6x the Corvette rate.
3. The tC's death rate is higher than the Camaro (3.44) but with half the horsepower. Wait, Camaro is 3.44 which is higher than tC's 2.30. Let me recalculate.

Actually: tC (2.30) is LOWER than Camaro (3.44) and Mustang (6.02). But it's HIGHER than Corvette (1.52), Challenger (1.0), MX-5 (1.79), 350Z (1.61). 

Revised finding: Among sports cars, the tC has the 4th highest death rate after Veloster (8.54), Mustang (6.02), and Camaro (3.44). But the tC has the LOWEST horsepower and lowest price of any car on that list. 

The real story: You'd expect death rate to track with horsepower/speed. It doesn't. The tC (180 hp) kills at a higher rate than the Corvette (455 hp), the 350Z (287 hp), or the Challenger (305+ hp). The variable isn't the car's power — it's the driver's experience level and the car's crash protection.

## Limitations
- Fleet size of 131,250 introduces more variance than higher-volume models
- FARS data is 2014-2023; the tC was produced 2004-2016, so most data captures aging vehicles
- VMT estimates for the tC may be less precise than for mass-market sedans
- Scion demographics are assumed from brand marketing, not directly measured in FARS
