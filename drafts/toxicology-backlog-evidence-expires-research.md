# Research: Toxicology Lab Backlogs — DUI Evidence Expires Before It's Tested

## Angle
America's forensic toxicology labs are so backlogged that DUI blood evidence takes up to a year (370 days in Washington state) to process. Meanwhile, drugs metabolize out of the blood in hours to days. The system designed to catch impaired drivers can't keep up — cases get dismissed on speedy trial grounds, prosecutors drop charges, and victims wait months for justice that may never come. The technology exists to test the blood. The lab capacity doesn't.

## Why This Matters (Kill Test)
- This is NOT a rehash of "drugs are bad" or "DUI is increasing." It's about a **systemic infrastructure failure** in the evidence chain.
- Novel finding: the evidence *literally degrades* while waiting to be tested (BAC and THC concentrations negatively correlate with time-to-collection per Wisconsin study), but the lab backlog means even properly collected samples sit for 370 days.
- The paradox: as drug-impaired driving enforcement increases (good), labs can't process the evidence (bad), so enforcement functionally fails (ugly).

## Primary Sources

### 1. Washington State Patrol Toxicology Lab (KIRO 7 Investigation)
- Turnaround time: **370 days** (over 1 year) — confirmed by Lab Manager Brian Capron
- 17,000+ cases submitted per year, 13% annual increase
- Projecting 18,000+ cases in 2024
- 60-65% of cases are DUI-related
- New Federal Way lab opened, but only 6 authorized scientists, needs 4 more
- Training new scientists: 12-18 months each
- Washington passed SB 5880 to allow private lab contracting as stopgap

### 2. Colorado Task Force on Drunk and Impaired Driving (Jan 2026 Meeting Minutes)
- Blood test results currently **~140 days delayed** (Crystal Soderman, DOR)
- 42.4% refusal rate for express consent BAC testing (563 refusals in September alone)
- ~6,000 express consent refusals in 2025
- Only ~45% of offenders request administrative hearings
- Toxicology delays impact: court case timelines, victim compensation claims, dram shop liability cases
- Fatal crashes in Colorado up 14.2% YoY

### 3. National Institute of Justice (2019)
- State and local forensic laboratories **understaffed by more than 900 positions**
- Shortfalls resulted in growing backlogs

### 4. AAA Issue Brief: Toxicology Labs (Dec 2025)
- "Chronic backlogs and limited resources mean delayed results, dismissed cases, and missed opportunities to hold impaired drivers accountable"
- Stop-limit testing: BAC ≥0.10 cases tested ONLY for alcohol — 17.3% were also drug-positive but never identified
- BAC testing rates for fatal crash persons decreasing from 2012 to 2021
- National problem, not isolated to individual states

### 5. Wisconsin Specimen Collection Delay Study (TRB/TRID)
- 8,923 crash-involved drivers arrested for DUI, 2019-2021
- Mean time-to-collection: 1.80 hours
- Fatal crashes: 2.35 hours to collection
- BAC negatively correlated with delay (r = −0.11)
- THC negatively correlated with delay (r = −0.16)
- Translation: the longer you wait to draw blood, the lower the measured impairment — evidence degrades in real time

### 6. North Carolina Speedy Trial Issues (UNC School of Government)
- Blood test results can take "up to a year to come back"
- Court of appeals found speedy trial violation from 14-month delay in impaired driving case (State v. Sheppard)
- Legal framework: delays attributed to state negligence weigh against prosecution

## Key Numbers for Article
- **370 days**: Washington State tox lab turnaround
- **140 days**: Colorado blood test backlog
- **72 hours**: THC half-life in blood (active metabolite), most gone within days
- **17.3%**: DUI cases with hidden drug positivity never tested (stop-limit)
- **900+**: Forensic lab staff shortfall nationwide
- **42.4%**: Colorado express consent refusal rate
- **17,000+**: Annual tox cases in Washington alone
- **14.2%**: Colorado fatal crash increase YoY

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing the Wisconsin specimen collection degradation data (BAC/THC negatively correlate with time) against the 370-day lab backlog creates an original observation: even if blood is drawn promptly, the *analysis* delay means cases are decided months after the biological reality. The evidence is preserved in a vial, but the legal window for prosecution narrows every day the lab sits on it. This is a compounding failure — biological degradation at collection + institutional delay at processing.

## Limitations
- Washington's 370-day figure is from a 2024 KIRO investigation; current numbers may differ after SB 5880 private lab authorization
- Colorado's 140-day figure is from January 2026 meeting minutes
- NIJ's 900-position shortfall is from 2019; could be better or worse now
- Stop-limit testing (17.3%) is from one study, may not be nationally representative
- Wisconsin degradation data measures time-to-collection, not time-to-analysis — the lab storage conditions may partially preserve concentrations

## Strongest Counterargument
Blood samples, once properly drawn and refrigerated, don't degrade the way active metabolism does. The THC/BAC in the vial on day 370 is likely close to what it was on day 1. The real problem isn't evidence degradation in the lab — it's that a 370-day wait creates legal exposure (speedy trial), prosecutorial fatigue (cases deprioritized), and victim abandonment (compensation delayed). The evidence is fine; the justice system around it isn't.
