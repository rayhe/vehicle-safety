# Research: Land Rover Recalls Exactly One Range Rover Sport (and 873 With Pre-Cracked Subframes)

## The story
In late September 2026, Jaguar Land Rover filed two wildly different safety recalls for the same model line in the same week:

1. **The smallest recall ever filed (26V607000):** a single 2026 Range Rover Sport, built February 16, 2026, recalled because a deformed/misaligned circuit board inside its Bosch-supplied Powertrain Control Module could cause engine stall without prior warning. NHTSA affected-vehicle population: 1. The owner gets a letter November 20 and a free PCM replacement.
2. **The 100%-defect recall (26V613, JLR D165/D166):** 873 Range Rover Sport SVs (2024-2026, built Oct 3 2023 - May 22 2026 at Solihull) with rear subframes that left the factory with cracks from a forging defect at suppliers Linamar Casting and Gestamp Assembly. Final machining was expected to remove material from the affected node but routinely didn't; cracks hid from manual inspection. Some owners' vehicles could not even be confirmed affected because production image history had storage gaps.

## Key facts / numbers
- Recall 26V607000: "Powertrain Control Module May Cause a Loss of Drive Power," 2026 Range Rover Sport, 1 vehicle. Built Feb 16, 2026. Supplier Bosch identified modules with "anomalous production parameters"; JLR quarantine notice; investigation found FOUR customer-delivered vehicles with suspect modules, but only ONE was included in the campaign (filing gives no reason for the other three). No injuries/incidents reported. Owner notified Nov 20, 2026.
- Recall 26V613 / D165 (cracked, confirmed by photo) and D166 (photo missing, inspect and replace if crack found): 873 SUVs in US + territories. Forging defect detected June 4, 2026 at supplier; PSCC engineering assessment concluded Sep 11 that impact "could not be ruled out"; PSCC Decision Forum ordered urgent recall Sep 17; filed with NHTSA Sep 24. No accidents, injuries, fires, claims, or field reports in the US. Dealers notified from Oct 8; owner letters by Nov 20.
- Context: 2026 Range Rover Sport carries five active NHTSA recalls; the power-loss family (48V mild-hybrid DCDC, 26V599000) expanded to ~194,000 vehicles in Sep 2026.
- Engine stall while driving is one of the highest-severity single-vehicle failure modes: NHTSA treats loss of motive power at speed as a crash risk (no steering assist, no brake boost).

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy: yes. A recall population of exactly one is nearly unheard of; a recall where the defect rate is effectively 100% (built cracked) is the structural opposite. The paradox pair is the hook: one car recalled for a defect nobody can find without imaging, 873 recalled for defects hidden FROM imaging. NHTSA's own oversight apparatus mobilized for one SUV.
- Novel angle after 70+ Crash Report articles: no prior piece has covered the single-vehicle recall. The angle is the bureaucracy of precision: a federal safety campaign with a population of one is both a joke and proof the system works.

## Actionable takeaways (for article)
- If you own a 2026 Range Rover Sport, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls even if you never got a letter; the single-car recall owner is notified Nov 20, 2026.
- 2024-2026 Range Rover Sport SV owners: inspect the rear subframe for cracks; JLR replaces confirmed cracked subframes free under D165/D166.
- Buying used: a recall population of one still means the defect existed in the fleet; Bosch's "anomalous production parameters" affected more modules than the recalled set.
