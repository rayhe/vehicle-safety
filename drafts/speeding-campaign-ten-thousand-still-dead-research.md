# Research: "Speeding Catches Up with You" Campaign vs. FARS Reality

## Primary Sources

### 1. NHTSA Q1 2026 + 2025 Full-Year Data (Released July 8, 2026)
- **Source:** NHTSA press release, DOT HS 813 833
- **Key data:**
  - Q1 2026: ~7,770 traffic deaths, down 4.3% from Q1 2025
  - Q1 2026 fatality rate: 0.99 per 100M VMT — lowest Q1 since 2014
  - 2025 full year fatality rate: 1.10/100M VMT — second-lowest ever
  - 2025 full year: lowest death count since 2019
  - Americans drove 11 billion MORE miles in Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025

### 2. NHTSA Speeding Campaign (July 6-31, 2026)
- **Source:** NHTSA press release
- **Campaign:** "Speeding Catches Up with You" — launched in Nashville July 6
- **NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison quote:** "Unless you're a racecar driver on the track, there's no excuse for speeding."
- **Launched with:** NASCAR team owner Justin Marks, Nashville PD, victim advocate

### 3. NHTSA 2024-2025 Speeding Data (from same press release)
- **2024:** 11,288 speeding-related deaths = 29% of ALL traffic fatalities
- **2024:** 316,757 speeding-related injuries
- **2025:** Estimated 10,035 speeding deaths — DOWN 11%
- **Context:** "A return to pre-pandemic numbers last seen in 2019"

### 4. NHTSA 2024 Subcategory Report (DOT HS 813 729)
- **Speeding fatalities:** Down 6% from 2023 to 2024
- **Overall fatalities:** Down 3.8% from 2023 to 2024
- **Speeding declined FASTER than overall rate** — speeding improvement outpacing total improvement

### 5. FARS Model-Level Death Rate Data (2014-2023)
- Sports car death rates per 100M VMT:
  - Hyundai Veloster: 8.54 (highest in dataset)
  - Ford Mustang: 6.02 
  - Chevrolet Camaro: 3.44
  - Toyota tC: 2.30
  - Corvette: 1.52
  - Dodge Challenger: 1.00
- Total sports car deaths: 6,134 (3.2% of all deaths, 1.7% of fleet)
- Sports car death/fleet ratio: 1.93x overrepresented

### 6. NHTSA Crash Cost Study (2023)
- Direct taxpayer cost: $30 billion/year
- Direct societal cost: $340 billion/year  
- Total including quality-of-life: $1.37 trillion/year

## Angle
The NHTSA is simultaneously:
1. Running an anti-speeding campaign (July 6-31)
2. Releasing data showing speeding killed 10,035 people in 2025
3. Celebrating the declining fatality rate

The paradox: the campaign treats speeding as a BEHAVIORAL problem ("there's no excuse"). But the 11% decline in speeding deaths is outpacing the overall decline (3-5%), suggesting TECHNOLOGY (AEB, ISA, lane keeping in newer vehicles) is doing the work, not driver behavior. If the campaign works because cars are getting better, the campaign is taking credit for engineering.

## Original Contribution
Cross-reference the speeding death trajectory with FARS model-level data to show:
1. Most speeding deaths aren't in sports cars — they're in Civics, Accords, Silverados
2. The vehicles with the highest death rates are ALSO the oldest vehicles (Cobalt, S-10, Cavalier) — fleet turnover is removing both the most dangerous and the most speed-prone
3. The speeding decline will continue not because of campaigns but because old cars are retiring
