# Research: The Beater Effect — #805, Dale Impactor III

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Cross-tabbing FARS toxicology data (307 models) against each model's newest model year in fatal crashes reveals the "beater effect": the older the car, the more impaired its dead drivers. The single most drug-positive car in America is the Oldsmobile Alero, a brand GM killed in 2004.

## Kill test
Genuinely novel: nobody has run impairment-rate vs. fleet-age correlation on FARS. Not a data dump — it's a discovered relationship (r=-0.32, n=303) with a hilarious mascot (a dead GM sedan leading the league in drug positives). Dale's sardonic sports-stats voice is built for exactly this. PASS.

## Original findings (computed from fars_output.js, 2026-09-03)
- n=303 models with 50+ drivers in FARS_TOXICOLOGY and model-year data.
- Pearson r between newest model year in fatal crashes and any-impairment rate: **-0.323**. Drug-only: -0.246. Alcohol-only: -0.263.
- Bucket means (anyPct): newestMY 1980-2004: 22.7% (n=20) / 2005-2009: 21.7% (n=51) / 2010-2014: 20.9% (n=33) / 2015-2026: 19.7% (n=199). Monotonic staircase.
- 13 of the top 20 most-impaired models have newestMY <= 2010.
- Oldsmobile Alero: drugPct **18.4%** — highest of all 307 models. alcPct 18.4%, anyPct 29.1%, n=141 drivers. Newest MY in fatal crashes: 2004 (final production year; brand discontinued).
- Runner-up drinkers are also fossils: Park Avenue (2005, 31.7% any — already covered in park-avenue-impairment-king.html, do not re-litigate), C/K Pickup, Astro Van (2005), Five Hundred (2007), Tiburon (2008), Rodeo (2002), Escort (1999).

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
The Corvette's newest model year is 2023 and it sits at 26.2% any-impairment, 8th worst overall. The Audi A3 (2017) is 4th at 27.1%. The Cadillac CTS (2019) is 10th at 25.9%. New, expensive, high-performance cars driven by self-selected risk-takers blow up the "it's the age" story. The honest reading: vehicle age is a proxy for *who can afford the car* — broke high-risk drivers buy $3,000 Aleros; thrill-seeking high-risk drivers buy Corvettes. The car doesn't cause the impairment; the price tag sorts the drivers. Correlation -0.32 is moderate, not destiny.

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only (~40k deaths/yr vs ~6.7M total crashes). Non-fatal impaired driving is invisible here.
- Drug-positive != impaired. THC is detectable days/weeks after use; NHTSA and researcher Jim Hedlund explicitly warn "drivers who are drug-positive may not necessarily be impaired." The Alero's "drug king" crown is really a "detectable-drug" crown.
- Testing is inconsistent: not every dead driver is tested for drugs in every state; testing bias could skew model-level rates.
- newestMY is derived from model years *appearing in fatal crashes*, a proxy for fleet recency, not registration data. (C/K Pickup shows 1986 — likely a FARS coding quirk as later trucks got coded Silverado; don't lean on it.)
- n=141 for the Alero is modest; confidence intervals are wide versus n=1,147 Corvette.

## Methodology
1. Parsed FARS_TOXICOLOGY (307 models; fields drivers, alcPct, drugPct, anyPct) and FARS_MODEL_YEAR (323 models; deaths by model year) from fars_output.js.
2. newestMY(model) = max model year with >0 deaths. Joined on (make, model); kept n=303 with drivers>=50.
3. Pearson correlations and bucket means computed in Python (statistics module).

## Actionable takeaways
- Shopping the bottom of the used market (sub-$5k sedans)? You're buying into the highest-impairment driver cohort on the road — drive like everyone around you in a 2004 Alero is impaired, because nearly 1 in 3 of its fatal-crash drivers were.
- For teen first cars: skip anything 15+ years old (pediatric researchers' explicit cutoff); check IIHS's affordable safe-vehicle list; ESC is non-negotiable (37% fatality-odds difference per IIHS).
- Check any used VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before purchase.
