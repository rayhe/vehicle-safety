# Research Notes: #1153 — Chery Omoda 5 Rear-Axle Detachment (Malaysia, Oct 2026)

## Story
On Oct 6, 2026, a Chery Omoda 5 in Johor Bahru, Malaysia lost its entire rear suspension assembly — torsion beam, trailing arms, shocks, brake lines, both rear wheels — while driving on Jalan Pandan near AEON Mall Tebrau City. Photos show the assembly resting behind the car. No injuries. On Oct 11, Malaysia's Road Transport Department (JPJ) suspended the model's vehicle type approval and halted new registrations, and Chery Auto Malaysia recalled 8,878 Omoda 5s.

The catch: this is the SECOND time. On April 28, 2024, an Omoda 5 lost its rear axle in slow traffic in Malaysia. Chery blamed a welding-tip replacement done without recalibration (incomplete weld penetration), recalled 600 vehicles (components made Aug 14-17, 2023), confirmed 60 with bad parts, replaced the axles, and closed the recall in June 2024. Twenty-nine months later, the wheels came off again.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: viral footage, government suspended the model's certification, 8,878-vehicle recall, all within the last 72 hours.
- Novel angle? Yes: the recall-that-didn't-end-it. 2024 campaign covered 600 vehicles from a 4-day production window; the 2026 recall covers 8,878 — roughly 15x. Was the first scope too narrow, or is this a different failure? Plus: the model got a new rear suspension in Sept 2025, so the Oct 2026 car may not even carry the old design.
- Full-queue dup check: chery/omoda/malaysia/welding defect/rear axle detach all return zero hits across 359 queue entries. First Chinese-automaker story on the site.

## Original data contribution
- Scope ratio: 8,878 / 600 = 14.8x. The 2026 recall is nearly fifteen times the 2024 campaign.
- 2024 hit rate: 60 confirmed defective of 600 inspected = 10%. The precautionary campaign found a 1-in-10 defect rate in a 4-day production window.
- Timeline: Apr 28, 2024 incident -> Apr 30 recall announced -> closed June 2024 -> Oct 6, 2026 recurrence. 29 months between failures; the first recall was "closed."
- Suspension-generation question: model updated Sept 2025 with new rear suspension. If the Oct 2026 vehicle has the new design, the weld theory from 2024 doesn't transfer.

## Numbers for the article
- 8,878 vehicles recalled (Oct 2026, C and H variants)
- 600 vehicles in 2024 precautionary campaign; 60 confirmed with bad axles (10%)
- 4-day production window (Aug 14-17, 2023) scoped in 2024
- 29 months between incidents
- 0 injuries in both incidents
- 14.8x scope ratio

## Limitations (must state in article)
- Root cause of the Oct 2026 failure is NOT confirmed; JPJ's technical investigation is ongoing. The 2024 weld explanation "has not been confirmed as the cause of the October 2026 failure."
- Unknown whether the Oct 2026 vehicle carried the pre- or post-Sept-2025 suspension.
- This is a Malaysia-market story; Chery does not sell the Omoda 5 in the US. No FARS/IIHS/NHTSA data applies.
- Chery's claim that "some welds... lack sufficient strength" is the company's characterization, not an independent finding.

## Strongest counterargument
Chery moved fast: statement and 8,878-vehicle recall within 5 days of the incident (2024: 2 days from incident to recall announcement). The 2024 campaign's 10% hit rate shows they did find and replace bad parts, and the recall was closed with JPJ's involvement. The 2026 failure may have a different root cause entirely — same symptom, different disease. JPJ's suspension is precautionary, not a finding of fault.

## Actionable insights
- Omoda 5 owners (C/H variants, Malaysia): check the recall, get the inspection.
- Broader: a "closed" recall is a claim about a production window, not a guarantee the root cause is extinct. When the same failure recurs, the first question is whether the scope was right.
- Watch for JPJ's technical investigation findings — that determines whether this is a weld-process repeat or a new-design problem.
