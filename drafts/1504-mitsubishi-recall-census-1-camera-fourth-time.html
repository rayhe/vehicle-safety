Mitsubishi Filed One Recall in 2026. It Was the Same Backup Camera, for the Fourth Time.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Mitsubishi sells three passenger cars in America. Three. An Outlander, an Outlander Sport, and an Eclipse Cross, a lineup you could memorize in the time it takes to reverse out of a driveway. This year Mitsubishi filed exactly one recall campaign touching a 2026 model, and it was a backup camera that does not show up. But here is the number that changes the story: 67,146 vehicles sit in that one campaign, and the same defect has now been filed four times in four years, covering 322,166 vehicles once you stop double-counting the overlaps. One campaign. Four filings. Same screen going dark.[1][2]

4
NHTSA filings for the same A-IVI backup-camera defect, 2022 through 2026

Method first, because I run the numbers and then I run them again. I pulled the canonical 2026 model list from NHTSA's VPIC database, three passenger models plus the Outlander PHEV variant, and queried the recalls API for every one, keeping only campaigns filed in calendar 2026. Four queries. Two came back empty with HTTP 400s, which the API uses to mean nothing here. Two came back with the same single campaign. That is the whole census: three models, four queries, one campaign, and the campaign is a screen that goes black in reverse.[1]

Campaign 26V549 arrived August 25, and it concerns the Alliance In-Vehicle Infotainment system, the Bosch-supplied touchscreen that also shows the rearview camera image. Improper software programming can leave the screen frozen, black, rebooting, or scrolling on its own, and if any of that happens while the driver is backing up, the camera image is gone and the truck fails Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111. Mitsubishi estimates 1 percent of the 67,146 actually carry the defect, about 671 trucks. NHTSA's record cites no crashes or injuries. Remedy is a dealer software flash, free, and the API's over-the-air flag says false, same as every other campaign in this series, even though the fix is software a dealer installs in minutes.[2][3]

One of three nameplates carries the entire census. Outlander Sport and Eclipse Cross carry zero filings, not just in 2026 but across the whole 2024-2026 sweep. Now the filing the count leaves out, which is where the story actually lives: 25V369, filed June 2, 2025, covers 198,940 Outlanders from model years 2022-2024 plus 2023-2025 PHEVs. Same A-IVI system. Same frozen-or-black screen. Same FMVSS 111. Nearly three times the counted campaign, excluded only because its newest model year is 2025, and the 2025 PHEV in that filing is the same vehicle as the 2026 PHEV in the counted one, one model year apart, split across two filings by the calendar.[4]

Add the counted and excluded filings and Mitsubishi's 2025-2026 recall record totals 266,086 vehicles, of which the census counts 67,146, or 25.2 percent. For every Mitsubishi the count includes, 2.96 more were recalled under the filing it excludes. Now run the thread back to the start, because this defect has a longer resume than most of Mitsubishi's models: 22V470 in July 2022 recalled 56,080 Outlanders for the same camera failure, 23V345 in May 2023 recalled 89,907 more, and 25V369 in June 2025 replaced and expanded 23V345 outright, telling owners repaired under the old campaign to come back for the new remedy. Sum all four filings and you get 412,073, but that double-counts, since 25V369 swallowed 23V345 whole and covers 22V470's model years too. Count each vehicle once and the thread totals 322,166, against 2025 US Outlander and PHEV sales of 42,189. Mitsubishi has now recalled more Outlanders for this one defect than it sold in an entire year, seven times over.[4][5]

Now the other side, stated at full strength, because the 1-count flatters Mitsubishi three ways and the comments will say so if I do not. First, the method excludes the filing that covers more Mitsubishis, 198,940 of them, so anyone who reads "Mitsubishi: 1" as the brand's safety record is wrong about nearly two hundred thousand vehicles. Second, the one counted campaign is not a new defect. It is the fourth filing for a software bug Mitsubishi has been fixing since July 2022, and the 1 percent defect estimate in both filings means the overwhelming majority of recalled vehicles were never going to go blank. Third, the count partly measures a shrinking catalog, not improving quality: Mitsubishi sells three passenger models, down from six a few years ago, with the Mirage and Mirage G4 gone after 2024, so there are fewer nameplates to recall. Strip the shared defect thread and Mitsubishi's own 2026 record is one campaign for one software bug its supplier has now shipped four times.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 266,086 total counts every vehicle in each filing across all model years, not 2026-only cars. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates, not 2026-model-year counts; the 2026-only Mitsubishi total cannot be computed from the filings, because the per-model split for 26V549 covers 2025-2026 Outlanders together. NHTSA's over-the-air flag undercounts software remedies, as it has in every census in this series, and a campaign hiding under an untested model spelling would be missed, which biases the count down, not up. Late filings will move every number in this article, and the 2026 model year is still in progress.

If you own a 2025-2026 Outlander or a 2026 Outlander PHEV, the camera recall is a free dealer software update, and owner letters went out September 24, 2026, so check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. If you own a 2022-2024 Outlander or a 2023-2025 PHEV, you are in the bigger, excluded filing, and if your car was repaired under 23V345, the fix did not hold, so check your VIN and get the new remedy. If you are shopping a 2026 Mitsubishi, the census is accurate for new metal: one recall across three nameplates, with the Outlander Sport and Eclipse Cross carrying zero. But shop used Outlanders by campaign, not by badge, because every 2022-2026 Outlander has now been recalled at least once for the same camera defect, some three times. Before you trust any used Outlander, shift into reverse and watch the screen. If it hesitates, freezes, or stays black, that is the defect, and the fix is free.
