## Angle (1 sentence)
America spent 2026 celebrating a 4.3% drop in traffic deaths, but the decade view reverses the story: 39,254 people died in 2024, nearly 20% more than in 2014, and only 7 of 50 states are safer than they were ten years ago.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy? YES.** The 2024 FARS Annual Report File (released April 2026) is the newest final-ish national data, and every outlet led with the one-year decline. Nobody ran the decade comparison at the state level.
- **Novel angle on data? YES.** Three original findings: (1) the one-year improvement (-1,771) erased only 27% of the decade's growth (+6,510); (2) the five largest decade increases are ALL western states (AZ, WA, OR, NV, CO) and three of the next four are New England (NH, ME, VT) — two geographic clusters, not a national blur; (3) deaths rose faster than miles or people — the per-mile rate went from 1.08 (2014) to 1.19 (2024), so it is not just more driving.
- **Distinct from queue coverage? YES.** #1399 covered the SAME 2024 data but only the one-year state change (33 safer, 17 deadlier in 2024 vs 2023). This piece uses a different window (2014 vs 2024), a different finding (43 worse, 7 better), and a different frame (the decade, not the year). #1469 covered the 2024/2025 national totals, not the state decade. No prior piece ran a 2014-to-2024 state comparison (grep: no hits for "Arizona +59%", "only 7 states", "Washington +58%").

## Facts (all traced)
- **National, 2024:** 39,254 deaths, down 1,771 (-4.3%) from 41,025 in 2023. Rate 1.19 per 100M VMT. (NHTSA Overview of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes in 2024, DOT HS 813 791, April 2026; FARS 2024 Annual Report File.)
- **National, 2014:** 32,744 deaths (final file; 32,675 in the Annual Report File). Rate 1.08 per 100M VMT. (NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts 2014 Data: State Traffic Data, DOT HS 812 293, June 2016.)
- **Decade change:** 39,254 - 32,744 = +6,510 deaths, +19.9% (about +20%). Rate 1.08 to 1.19 = +10.2% per mile.
- **States worse than 2014: 43 of 50. States better: 7.** (NHTSA FARS state tables 1994-2024, tabulated in the wheelsaccidentadvice state dataset, cross-checked below.)
- **Largest decade increases:** Arizona +59% (773 to 1,229), Washington +58% (462 to 730), Oregon +51% (357 to 538), Nevada +43% (290 to 417), Colorado +41% (488 to 689), New Hampshire +40% (95 to 133), Maine +35% (131 to 177), Vermont +34% (44 to 59).
- **Only 7 decreases:** North Dakota -33% (135 to 90), Wyoming -29% (150 to 107), Kansas -12% (385 to 339), Pennsylvania -6% (1,195 to 1,127), West Virginia -6% (272 to 256), Alaska -4% (73 to 70), Oklahoma -4% (669 to 645).
- **2024 deadliest roads per mile:** Mississippi 1.81, Arizona 1.74, South Carolina 1.67 per 100M VMT. Safest: Massachusetts 0.59. National 1.19.
- **2024 per-capita:** Mississippi 25.6 deaths per 100,000 residents (highest), Rhode Island 4.7 (lowest). National 11.5.
- **2024 one-year context (from #1399):** 33 states + Puerto Rico down, 17 states + DC up in 2024 vs 2023. Maine had the biggest one-year jump (+31%) and is also +35% over the decade — its 2024 spike is not a blip, it is an acceleration.
- **VMT context:** 2024 VMT 3,294 billion miles, up 1.5% on 2023; 2014 VMT about 3,026 billion. Miles grew ~9% over the decade while deaths grew ~20% — deaths outran miles by roughly 2-to-1.

## Original calculations (computed here, not quoted)
1. **The recovery arithmetic.** The 2024 improvement (-1,771) erased 27.2% of the decade's growth (+6,510). At that pace, three more years of identical declines would be needed just to return to 2014 levels — and the decline has not held at that pace historically (2022 and 2023 fell by smaller amounts).
2. **The two clusters.** The 8 largest decade increases split into two geographic blocks: 5 western states (AZ, WA, OR, NV, CO — all +41% or more) and 3 northern New England states (NH, ME, VT — all +34% or more). No southern or mid-Atlantic state is in the top 8. The decade's deterioration has a map, and the map is the coasts' edges.
3. **Deaths outran miles.** VMT rose ~9% (3,026B to 3,294B) while deaths rose ~20%. The fatality rate per 100M VMT rose 10.2% (1.08 to 1.19). Population grew ~7% over the same span. Deaths grew faster than miles and faster than people — the roads got more dangerous per mile, not just busier.
4. **Maine, both windows.** Maine is the only state in the top 5 of BOTH windows: +31% in one year (135 to 177, the largest one-year jump in the country) and +35% over the decade (131 to 177). A one-year spike can be noise; a spike on top of a decade trend is a direction.

## Actionable takeaways
- The one-year decline is real but small against the decade: if you live in Arizona, Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Colorado, New Hampshire, Maine, or Vermont, your roads are 34-59% deadlier than in 2014. The national headline does not describe your state.
- The seven states that improved (ND, WY, KS, PA, WV, AK, OK) are the exception, not the rule — and four of the seven are Great Plains states. Ask your state DOT what the other 43 are doing differently; the answer is not "driving less."
- For buyers: the state you drive in matters as much as the car you drive. Mississippi's per-mile death rate (1.81) is triple Massachusetts' (0.59). Same country, same cars, three times the risk per mile.
- Per-mile risk rose 10% over the decade even as cars got safer — the danger is in exposure and behavior (speed, impairment, distraction), not in the sheet metal. That is where the next decade of improvement has to come from.

## Limitations (what we do NOT know)
- 2024 figures are the Annual Report File, subject to revision when NHTSA publishes the final file (2023's count rose from 40,901 to 41,025 between vintages). The decade percentages could shift by a point or two; the ranking and the direction will not flip.
- 2014 counts differ slightly between vintages (ARF 32,675 vs final 32,744 nationally; AZ 770 vs 773). Percentages here use final-file counts where available; using ARF counts moves Arizona from +59.0% to +59.6% — immaterial.
- State counts are not adjusted for population or VMT growth in the headline percentages; the per-mile rate comparison (1.08 to 1.19) is the adjusted version, and it still rises 10%.
- This analysis cannot say WHY each state moved: enforcement, speed limits, road design, drug and alcohol trends, and post-pandemic driving behavior all vary by state and are not separated in FARS state totals. The clusters (West, New England) are descriptive, not causal.
- FARS counts deaths within 30 days of a crash on a public road; it does not capture long-term injury burden.

## Counterargument
The strongest case against the alarm: 2024 is genuinely good news — three straight years of declines, the rate at 1.10 in the 2025 estimate, and the 2021 pandemic peak (42,939) more than half unwound. A decade comparison anchors on 2014, one of the safest years on record (the post-recession low was 2011's 32,479), so "20% above 2014" partly measures how unusually safe 2014 was, not how unusually dangerous 2024 is. Fair — and the rate comparison (1.08 to 1.19) is the honest version of the same point, because it strips out the miles. But the counterargument concedes the core finding: even on the rate, the roads are 10% deadlier per mile than a decade ago, and 43 states are worse on the raw count. A three-year decline that has erased only a quarter of a decade's growth is a recovery in progress, not a recovery complete.
