Volkswagen Recalled 22,524 ID.4s for a Fire Risk It Can't Explain. The Fix Is a Sticker and 80%.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 19, 2027

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better. On September 30, Volkswagen filed a federal safety recall covering 22,524 ID.4 electric SUVs for a high-voltage battery failure that can start a fire, and the two fields that normally make a recall a recall are both blank: root cause, unknown; remedy, under development.[1]

80%
The maximum charge VW asks owners to use, enforced by a mailed sticker, until a fix exists that currently does not

NHTSA campaign 26V630, VW code 93EX, covers 2023-2026 ID.4s built January 2023 through February 2025, the window after assembly moved to Chattanooga. The filing says certain charging profiles plus a state of charge above 80% may trigger thermal propagation, the chain reaction where one overheating cell cooks its neighbors until the pack burns. The cells come from SK Battery America in Georgia, and VW's Product Safety Committee has asked the supplier for "additional clarifications" on production and a clean date. The investigation is still open. The population could grow.[2]

The interim remedy is a behavioral prescription with federal paperwork. Owners are asked to cap charging at 80%. VW is mailing warning stickers for in-car placement, a visual reminder not to use the top fifth of the battery you paid for. Connected vehicles get a remote software configuration that caps the limit automatically. Interim letters go out November 27, 2026, with a second mailing promised whenever a remedy materializes.[3]

Let's price the interim. A 2024 ID.4 Pro S with the 82 kWh pack carries a 291-mile EPA rating.[4] Eighty percent of 291 is 233. That is 58 miles of advertised range deleted by a sticker. On the 62 kWh trims rated at 206 miles, the cap drops the car to about 165 miles, commuter-car territory in a vehicle sold as a family crossover.

Read the last two rows together. In one calendar year, roughly half of every ID.4 ever sold in America has been swept into a battery-fire recall, and this wave was filed without a diagnosis or a cure. Europe gets its own wave: a VW spokesperson told Handelsblatt "several thousand" EVs, including the ID.3, ID.7, ID.Buzz and Cupra Born, are affected after an internal quality check found substandard cells.[5]

The counterargument deserves full strength. Filing early with a mitigation is arguably the correct call: 22 claims in two and a half years, a conservative cap, zero attributed injuries, and paperwork honest enough to say "unknown" instead of inventing a root cause to satisfy a form. Most EV makers already recommend 80% for daily charging, so many owners lose nothing they weren't already giving up. An OTA cap plus mailed stickers reaches every car faster than 22,524 dealer visits.

What this means for you

Own a 2023-2026 ID.4? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls under campaign 26V630, VW code 93EX. Do not wait for the November 27 letter. Set your charge limit to 80% in the infotainment menu today, even if your car gets the over-the-air cap. Shopping used? Any Chattanooga-built ID.4 needs a recall-status check before money changes hands. Two battery-fire recalls in one year is this car's new normal.
