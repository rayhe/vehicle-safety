## 1. General Editor — 8.7/10
Structure holds: lede names the number, pull stat lands it, ratio paragraph widens it, rider-fault paragraph complicates it, helmet experiment and FMVSS hook give it policy legs, action paragraph and honest counterargument close it. Two nits: the FMVSS 127 point appears twice (paragraph 4 and the action paragraph) without new information the second time, and the helmet paragraph could open the piece instead of sitting fifth, since the 4.4x gap is the second-strongest number in the article. Neither is fatal. Headline is the best on the site this month.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Distinctly Rex: noir opener ("Every rider knows the car"), deadpan asides, no throat-clearing. Fragment punches ("Not a drunk rider. Not a speeding rider.") earn their keep. No banned phrases, no AI tells in the scan: no "delve," no "crucially," no "landscape," no "testament." Sentence rhythm passes with real variance (631.3) and the short/long split reads human. One flat line: "Run the comparisons and the shape gets worse" is a throat-clear before a good paragraph. Minor.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.8/10
The moral reasoning is the article's spine and it is handled straight: it refuses the easy story (reckless rider) and the easy opposite story (innocent rider), giving riders their own fault paragraph in full. No victim-blaming of the dead, no self-congratulation, no policy prescription dressed as data. The helmet-law paragraph correctly frames the gap as a state-line artifact rather than a personal failing. No issues.

## 4. Social Shareability — 8.7/10
Headline is built to travel: a number, a villain maneuver, and a punchline ("The bike was going straight"). The pull stat (1,532) is self-contained and quotable. The 4.4x helmet gap and the 2.2x left-turn-vs-straight ratio are both tweetable. Weakness: no single sentence in the body as sharp as the headline; the closest ("Wait for a gap you can actually see, not one you hope is there") is good but buried in paragraph six.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
All figures check against DOT HS 813 824: 1,532/3,396 (45%), 689 straight-through, 78% frontal, 35% unlicensed vs 14%, 37% speeding, 26% impaired, 48% vs 11% unhelmeted, 17 states + DC + PR. The "33 states" implication (50 minus 17) is arithmetically correct. FMVSS 127 motorcycle-detection gap is attributed to the October 2026 rider petition and the site's own #1426 rather than asserted as NHTSA text. One fix applied pre-panel: the lede now carries the full NHTSA phrasing "going straight, passing, or overtaking" instead of compressing to "going straight"; the headline keeps the compression as headline shorthand, and the body and references state the full definition. FARS maneuver-not-fault is stated explicitly. No invented citations.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution: the 24.6% floor (left-turn crashes as a share of all rider deaths), the 2.22x left-turn-vs-straight ratio, the 4.4x helmet-law gap, and the 2.5x license gap are all computed from the fact sheet, none published in it. The Teoh 26% vs NHTSA 45% definition difference is confronted head-on instead of smoothed over, and the conservative floor is named. Counterargument is stated at full strength (fault unknown, exposure, rider fault slices). Limitations are in the disclaimer and honest: ARF provisional status, CRSS sampling, maneuver-not-fault. Methodology is shown, not gestured at.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.6/10
Pull stat plus inline ratios carry the numbers well, and the references section reproduces the full fact-sheet figures with a working link. A small comparison table (left-turn 1,532 vs straight 689 vs other 1,175) would have made the configuration ranking scannable at a glance; prose does the job but the reader has to hold three numbers in their head. Percentages are always paired with counts, which is the right discipline.
