## The crash (primary source 1: NTSB preliminary report HWY26MH022, released Oct 15, 2026)

- Wednesday, September 23, 2026, ~6:11 p.m. CDT. 2017 Mack LR633 garbage truck, operated by Waste Management of Texas, southbound in the center lane of US Highway 287 near Erath Street, Fort Worth, Tarrant County, Texas.
- US 287 there: divided six-lane, 60 mph limit. Erath Street overpass crossed US 287, supported by 12 bridge columns; four stood on the right side of the southbound lanes.
- The four right-side columns were protected by a concrete traffic barrier with an integrated crash cushion. A concrete embankment, sloping upward, sat beyond the columns.
- The truck had finished its pickup route and was returning to the Fort Worth Waste Management facility. Driver + one passenger aboard.
- Sequence: truck crossed from the center lane over the right lane and shoulder, continued across the shoulder, traveled along the concrete embankment **behind the traffic barrier**, and struck the first bridge column.
- Driver fatally injured. Passenger seriously injured, hospitalized. Very light rain had just begun; roadway still dry.
- Damage to the column was so extensive TxDOT removed the section of Erath Street crossing southbound US 287. As of the Oct 15 preliminary report, Erath Street remained closed for new overpass construction.
- Parties: FMCSA, TxDOT, Fort Worth Police Department. Probable cause pending; NTSB intends safety recommendations.
- URL: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY26MH022.aspx

## The mechanism nobody covers (original contribution)

The barrier did its job exactly as designed and the column still got hit. The truck never touched the crash cushion: it rode the upward-sloping concrete embankment *behind* the barrier and arrived at the column from the protected side. The shield was a wall with an open back door. The embankment, built to hold up the overpass approach, doubled as a ramp past the protection. This is a design-pattern failure, not a maintenance failure: any shielded column sitting behind a sloped embankment has the same gap.

## The lethality math (primary source 2: FHWA/TX-10/9-4973-1, "Analysis of Large Truck Collisions with Bridge Piers, Phase 1")

Texas heavy-truck run-off-road data, divided highways:

| | All truck ROR crashes | ROR + hit bridge pier |
|---|---|---|
| Crashes | 32,326 | 159 |
| Fatal (K) | 601 (1.9%) | 14 (8.8%) |
| Incapacitating (A) | 2,239 (6.9%) | 24 (15.1%) |

- **Bridge-pier strikes kill at 8.8% vs 1.9% for the average truck run-off-road crash: ~4.6x more lethal.** (8.81 / 1.86 = 4.74.)
- Tangents only: pier strikes 5 fatal of 46 (10.9%) vs all ROR 167 of 8,664 (1.9%): **5.6x**.
- Minnesota divided highways, same study: pier strikes 1 fatal of 28 (3.6%) vs all ROR 5 of 421 (1.2%): 3.0x. Direction consistent across states.
- Garbage trucks in the Texas divided-highway table: 48 run-off-road crashes, **0** hit a bridge pier. The Fort Worth crash is the rare case in the exact cell the data says almost never happens.

## Fixed-object context (primary sources 3–4)

- ~20% of motor-vehicle crash deaths result from a vehicle leaving the roadway and striking a fixed object (National Safety Council, via SafetyNow summary). Fixed-object collisions were ~27% of deaths in 2018 (NSC Injury Facts).
- 2004 national fixed-object deaths by object struck (AASHTO Roadside Design Guide data): embankment 525 deaths; trees 4,520; utility poles 1,191; boulders 1,131; guardrail 434; bridge rail 77; concrete barriers 186. Embankments alone killed 525 people in one year, and the Fort Worth truck used one as its route to the column.
- National Academies / TRB report on bridge-pier occupant protection (NCHRP): 239 reported bridge-pier first-and-only-object-struck cases across NJ, NC, OH, WA, WY (2000–2013 windows); estimated 520 including unreported. https://nap.nationalacademies.org/skim.php?record_id=25313&chap=54-67

## Actionable takeaways (for draft)

- If you drive a heavy vehicle for work, run-off-road is survivable ~98% of the time until the roadside puts concrete in your path; bridge piers are the worst object in the dataset.
- For road agencies: a crash cushion protects the column only from the travel-lane side. Where a sloped embankment runs behind the barrier, the barrier is a fence with a gate. TxDOT closed Erath Street; the fix is extending the barrier or regrading the embankment, not replacing the cushion.
- For drivers: a garbage truck finishing its route at 6:11 p.m. is a tired driver in a 30-ton truck with a high center of gravity and a cab built for visibility, not crashworthiness. Give work trucks room.
