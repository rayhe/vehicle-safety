Takata’s Second Wave Just Doubled: Honda Recalls 810,592 More

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

One week. Two automakers. 1,124,621 cars added back to the Takata ledger. Honda filed campaign 26V765 on November 19, recalling 810,592 Acuras and Hondas over airbag inflators that can rupture, eight days after Toyota announced 314,029 of its own. Both batches share the same story: propellant that degrades in heat and humidity, in inflators that were supposed to be the safe kind.[1][3]

1,124,621
US vehicles added to the Takata count in eight days: Toyota’s 314,029 plus Honda’s 810,592.

Honda did not recall a car; it recalled a climate. The recall covers 2013 Accords, 2012–2013 Civics and CR-Vs, and 2013 Acura ILX and RDX models, but only if the car was originally sold or ever registered in 14 hot, humid states and territories, from Florida to Guam, which means a 2013 Civic in Miami is recalled and the identical car in Minneapolis is not, because the defect lives in the air the car has been breathing for 13 years, not in the car itself. Check the registration history, not just the badge.[2]

Both recalls name the same chemistry: desiccated, phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate, PSAN, with a desiccant packed in to absorb moisture. Desiccant was the fix for the original moisture problem. Honda watched these inflators for years and concluded the propellant can degrade anyway. Toyota reached the same conclusion through a TK Global service-life study and decided on November 6. Zero ruptures, zero injuries, zero crashes in either batch, which is the system working. It is also the system admitting the fix has a shelf life, and the shelf life is expiring on 2012–2013 cars right now.[2][4]

Fairness check, because nothing has actually gone wrong here: no inflator in either batch has ruptured, no one has been hurt, and both companies moved on models and simulations before the first failure, which is what a functioning safety system is supposed to do. Against 67 million inflators and 4.7 million cars still unrepaired, 1.1 million is a rounding error. All true. Also true: the repaired count the industry has quoted since 2019 is now overstated by at least 1.1 million cars, and NHTSA says other automakers are still running their own inflator analysis and testing. Proactive is good news for the owners inside the recall. It is bad news for every completion statistic quoted since the “final phase.”[5]

If you own one of these, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Honda’s interim letters go out December 18, Toyota’s December 28 to January 11, but neither requires waiting. Honda’s recall numbers are BQK, MQJ, KQI and QQH; Toyota’s are 26TB25, 26TA25, 26LB11 and 26LA11, and the repair is free at the dealer in both cases. A rupturing inflator sends metal fragments into the cabin. A two-minute VIN check is the whole price of knowing.[2][4][6]
