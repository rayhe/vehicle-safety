# Research: Hyundai Kona — Cheapest TSP+ Winner, Three Recalls in 2026

## Hook
Hyundai Kona recalled for the third time in 2026 (July). Latest: rear center seatbelt buckle may fail. Hyundai tells owners not to use one seat. This is the cheapest vehicle ($25,500) to earn IIHS Top Safety Pick+ in 2026.

## Sources (3+ primary)

### 1. NHTSA Recall Notice — Kona Seatbelt (July 2026)
- 47,733 2026 Hyundai Konas + 16 2025 Kona Electrics = 47,749 total
- Rear center seat belt buckle assemblies manufactured outside of specification
- Root cause: supplier stamping die not properly maintained (wear from overuse)
- Remedy: inspect + replace buckle assembly if necessary, free
- Third recall in 2026: steering knuckles (Feb), [another], seatbelt (July)
- Source: NHTSA via Autoblog (https://www.autoblog.com/news/hyundai-kona-seat-belt-recall)

### 2. IIHS 2026 Award Results
- Kona earned Top Safety Pick+ (lowest MSRP vehicle to do so at $25,500)
- Only 2 of 16 newly tested vehicles earned TSP+ in May 2026 batch (Palisade + Prius)
- Source: IIHS (https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/two-additional-models-earn-awards-in-latest-iihs-ratings), AutoEvolution report

### 3. FARS Data (2014-2023)
- Kona: 73 deaths, 136 crashes, 481,250 fleet, rate 0.12 per 100M VMT
- Deaths/crash ratio: 0.537 (close to average 0.579)
- Impairment: data not separately broken out (model may be too new for robust tox sample)
- For comparison:
  - Hyundai Palisade: 38 deaths, rate 0.06 (the OTHER TSP+ winner)
  - Hyundai Tucson: 669 deaths, rate 0.34
  - Honda HR-V (segment competitor): 142 deaths, rate 0.13
  - Subaru Crosstrek: 86 deaths, rate 0.08
  - Toyota C-HR: 109 deaths, rate 0.40

### 4. Hyundai Kona 2026 Recall History
- Recall #1: February 2026 — steering knuckles faulty
- Recall #2: [earlier 2026 — need to verify]
- Recall #3: July 2026 — rear center seatbelt buckle

## Original Contribution
Cross-tabulation: IIHS TSP+ status vs recall count in same calendar year. The Kona proves that crash test ratings and real-world quality are measuring different things entirely. IIHS tests one car on one day. Recalls reveal systemic manufacturing defects across tens of thousands of vehicles.

Novel finding: the Kona has a FARS death rate of 0.12 — genuinely low. But with 3 recalls in 7 months affecting steering, structural integrity, and occupant restraint, the IIHS gold star is a snapshot, not a warranty.

## Limitations
- FARS data covers 2014-2023; Kona only went on sale in the US in 2018, so the dataset skews toward earlier years with lower sales volume
- The 73 FARS deaths may include both first-gen (2018-2023) and second-gen (2024+) Konas — can't separate by generation
- IIHS TSP+ was earned by the 2026 model; FARS data doesn't include the 2026 model year at all
- Recall count alone doesn't indicate severity — the seatbelt buckle recall affects one specific seat position

## Strongest Counterargument
The Kona's three recalls might actually prove the SAFETY system is working. Hyundai identified issues (supplier die wear, steering knuckles) and issued recalls before any injuries occurred. Zero injuries from any of these recalls. A manufacturer that quickly recalls is better than one that doesn't. IIHS tests structural safety in crashes; recalls address manufacturing defects. They're complementary, not contradictory.

## Actionable Takeaway
- If you own a 2026 Kona: do NOT use the rear center seat until the buckle is inspected/replaced
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for all three open campaigns
- When shopping: TSP+ means the DESIGN passed a crash test. It doesn't mean your specific vehicle was built correctly. Check recall history at nhtsa.gov before trusting the badge.
