## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The federal government keeps a body count for all-terrain vehicles: 15,250 dead since the early 1980s. The newest full-year data shows 63% of ATV deaths happen ON roads, the exact surface the machines are engineered against, while small towns keep legalizing road use for tourism dollars and the official census refuses to count the fastest-growing segment.

## Original calculations (the novel contribution)
- 209 on-road vs 75 off-road deaths in 2025 = 2.8:1 ratio. The vehicle named for "all terrain" dies almost 3x more often on pavement.
- 18% of 2025 deaths were children under 16 (58/331); 27 of them were driving. Cumulative: ~1/5 of 15,250 = 3,000+ children since the 1980s.
- Weekend + summer concentration: 49% of deaths on Sat/Sun; 27% in July+August alone.
- The undercount gap: CFA's news-based 331 vs CPSC's ~550-650 census means the headline number captures roughly half the real toll. And ROVs (which now outsell ATVs) are in neither count: CFA knows of 646 ROV deaths since 2013 from news reports alone.
- Wisconsin 2026: 30 deaths by Aug 8 with 26 unhelmeted = 87% unhelmeted among known cases.

## Limitations (must state in article)
- CFA's 331 is assembled from news reports: a documented undercount (CPSC's death-certificate census runs ~2x). Treat as floor, not total.
- ROV/UTV deaths are excluded from the 15,250 cumulative count and from CPSC's annual ATV census by design (3-2 vote, Oct 2025).
- Helmet use unknown in 82% of 2025 cases; road type unknown in 14%.
- No exposure denominator (hours ridden): cannot compute per-mile risk vs passenger cars. Comparisons are counts and shares, not rates.

## Strongest counterargument (must state at full strength)
Most ATV deaths are behavioral, not mechanical: no helmet, alcohol, children on adult-size machines, passengers on single-rider ATVs. These are work vehicles and rural transport, not toys; per hour of use the risk picture could look very different, and nobody measures the hours. Towns opening roads argue it channels riding onto supervised routes and keeps tourism dollars local. The CPSC commissioner who oversees this docket is himself an ATV owner with two young riders.

## Actionable insights (hard gate)
- Never ride an ATV on pavement: low-pressure tires and a high center of gravity make roads the deadliest surface (63% of 2025 deaths). This is also the manufacturers' own rule.
- Kids under 16: only age-rated youth models, never adult ATVs (27 child drivers died in 2025). Check the April 2026 recall: 4,900 Rex110/Sierra110 youth ATVs (Lil Pick Up) — throttle can stick.
- One rider per machine; helmet every ride (87% of Wisconsin's 2026 dead with known status were unhelmeted); take a hands-on safety course before riding.
- If your county is considering an OHV road ordinance, the data says the road is where these machines kill.
