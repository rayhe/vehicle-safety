Stellantis Recalled the Backup Camera. Then It Recalled the Backup Camera Again.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 6, 2026

On August 17, Stellantis recalled 844,027 vehicles because the radio could kill the backup camera. On September 1, it recalled 239,131 more Ram 1500s because the radio could kill the backup camera. Same supplier, same failure, same federal violation, five weeks apart, for a combined 1,083,158 vehicles.[1][2]

1,083,158
US vehicles recalled in five weeks for backup cameras that can go dark — NHTSA campaigns 26V531 and 26V560

Start with August. Campaign 26V531, Stellantis number 78D, covered 2026 and 2027 models across all four brands: Chrysler Pacifica and Voyager, Dodge Charger, a long list of Jeeps, Ram 1500 and 2500 pickups, and the ProMaster vans. Radio software from Harman Becker Automotive Systems may prevent the rearview camera image from appearing when the driver shifts into reverse, which puts the trucks out of compliance with FMVSS 111, the federal rear-visibility standard in force since May 2018. NHTSA estimates that 100 percent of the recalled vehicles carry the defect, because software is deterministic that way: every unit ships the same code, so the recall population is the defect population.[3]

Then September arrived with a second campaign for the same failure. Campaign 26V560, Stellantis number 83D, covers 239,131 Ram 1500s from model years 2025 and 2026, built between October 6, 2023 and February 23, 2026. Stellantis opened its investigation on March 17, 2026, after reports of cameras failing to display on both model years, and its Vehicle Regulations Committee approved the recall on August 26, with owner letters starting September 24. Remedy in both campaigns is a free radio software update, delivered over the air or at a dealer, and nobody has been hurt in either one.[4]

Now the part that makes the second recall more interesting than the first: August covered 2026 and 2027 models, while September covers trucks built as far back as October 2023, which means the defect was already in the field for nearly three years when Stellantis announced the first recall, and the first recall missed the oldest and largest cohort of affected trucks. Meanwhile, the 2026 Ram 1500 sits in both campaigns: 211,114 of them in August, plus an unknown share of the 239,131 in September, because the build windows overlap from April 2025 through February 2026. Some trucks need the radio fixed twice, under two campaign numbers, for the same failure. NHTSA has not said the two populations are identical, and the build windows suggest they are not, which is worse, not better: it means the defect spans two radio configurations, and Stellantis needed two campaigns to catch both.

Cross-referencing the Ram 1500 against a decade of FARS data cuts against the scary framing in a useful way. In the repo dataset, the Ram 1500 carries a fatality rate of 0.13 deaths per 100M VMT across 714 deaths from 2014 through 2023, the lowest rate of any nameplate in the file.[5] This truck is not a deathtrap. What is dangerous is the screen going dark at the exact moment the driver is reversing toward something the camera was mandated to show, because NHTSA has estimated that backover crashes kill about 210 people and injure about 15,000 every year in the United States, with young children a significant share of the fatalities. That is why the camera is federal law, and why a blank screen is a noncompliance rather than a glitch.

One more recall arrived in the same month, because the Ram 1500 was having a season. Campaign 26V623 covers 16,385 model-year 2026 trucks whose tire pressure sensors, supplied by Sensata Technologies, may report false pressure or fail to light the warning lamp, a FMVSS 138 noncompliance with a 100 percent defect estimate of its own, and owner letters went out October 27. The September camera recall and the TPMS recall together cover 255,516 Ram 1500s, and the August recall swept in another 211,114 of them, and every one of these trucks is fixed free. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls rather than waiting for the mail, and check for all three campaigns, since one VIN can carry more than one. If your backup camera ever goes blank, stop, get out, and look. Mirrors are not a substitute for the system federal law requires. And when shopping, ask whether the camera image runs through the infotainment radio or a dedicated display path, because a safety system that shares a computer with the radio fails when the radio does.[6]
