Porsche Found 548 More Cars With Wheels That Could Fall Off

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2026

A wheel bolt has exactly one job. Porsche has now filed two federal recalls for a batch of bolts that cannot do it.[1][2] On October 16, 2026, the company filed NHTSA campaign 26V682, adding 548 more 2024 sports cars to a do-not-drive order first issued in October 2024, because the original recall's parts list was drawn from incomplete supplier paperwork.[1] The center-lock wheel bolt may fracture and let a wheel detach at speed. The filing's remedy date is December 15, 2026; until then, Porsche says, don't drive them.

In 2024, Porsche told regulators the cause was still under investigation. The 2026 filing names it: a batch of center-lock bolts from German supplier Hirschvogel, part number 992601119B, tested with lead and bismuth levels outside specification.[3] Not loose nuts. Not an assembly slip. The alloy chemistry was wrong. Those bolts went into cars that do 190 mph.

How did 548 cars slip through the 2024 net? Two more field fractures, in May and August 2026, with no injuries reported, reopened the case.[4] The supply-chain review that followed found unrecorded parts transfers inside the supplier's manufacturing and logistics facilities. Defective bolts had moved off the books, so the 2024 recall boundary was drawn around paperwork that didn't match reality.[3] Matching supplier delivery records to component and vehicle build dates put the bad parts in 911s and 718s built June 7 through July 19, 2024. A recall is only as good as its supplier's ledger, and this one had gaps.

Look at which cars made the list: 2024 911 GT3, GT3 RS, Turbo S, Turbo, S/T, Carrera GTS, plus the 718 Cayman GT4 RS and Spyder RS.[1][5] That's Porsche's track-grade halo fleet, the machines customers buy specifically to flog at redline. Porsche estimates 1.4% of the 548 carry the defect, which is roughly eight cars. The other 540 park theirs anyway, because a dealer inspection is the only way to find the eight. That is the correct call. It is also an admission that Porsche could not audit one supplier's logistics well enough to scope its own 2024 recall.

Drive a 2024 GT3, GT3 RS, Turbo S, Turbo, S/T, Carrera GTS, Cayman GT4 RS, or Spyder RS? Run your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; VINs in the expanded population have been searchable since October 20, 2026.[1] If yours is in: do not drive it until a Porsche dealer inspects the center-lock fastening system, and call Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243 citing campaign ATA1.[6] Owner letters go out December 15. Shopping a used 2024 GT3 or GT3 RS? Verify the recall is closed on that specific VIN before money moves, because for two years these cars sat for sale inside the gap.

What this article did not prove: Porsche's 1.4% defect estimate is the company's own engineering figure, unverified by any regulator, and the two 2026 field fractures establish that bad bolts exist, not how many. Nothing here clears your specific car; only the dealer inspection does that.

Fair point, stated plainly: an expanding recall can be read as the system working. Porsche found new field fractures, reopened the supplier books, traced the chemistry to lead and bismuth, and widened the net, with no injuries reported to date.[3][4] It is a fair defense. It is also a second attempt at closing a recall for the same bolts.
