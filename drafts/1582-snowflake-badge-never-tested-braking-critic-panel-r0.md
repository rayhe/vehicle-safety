## 1. General Editor — 8.5
Lede lands in the first sentence and the structure is clean: badge definition, badge-gap proof, WeatherPeak surprise, ice, inversion, Wintrac upset, AWD, defense, action. The pull stat (15.1 feet) is the article's thesis in one number. Deductions: 970 words runs long against the 300-500 word guide, though in line with recent house norm; the AWD paragraph restates a point the test data already makes, and the WeatherPeak paragraph carries two "Tyre Reviews checked" sentences that could be one.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0
Mia is unmistakable here: engineering detail, visible excitement about the test design, and open contempt for a badge standard that never measures braking. "Four patches of rubber, and the drivetrain is a footnote" is the line of the piece. No banned phrases, rhythm passes on all three metrics. Deduction: "Give the other side its full due" is a solid formulation but lands as a throat-clear before the counterargument rather than part of it.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
No victim-blaming, no invented harm, no crash or injury claims anywhere in the piece. Risk is stated as stopping distance, never as deaths. The remedy and owner action steps are concrete and cheap. Nothing to flag. Deduction: the lede's "is the whole crash" is dramatic, but it is defensible: 25 feet at neighborhood speed genuinely decides whether a crash happens.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5
The headline is the share: "never tested braking" is a complete claim that also happens to be true, and the pull stat is quotable. The badge-gap number travels well in a single screenshot. Deduction: tire tests are a narrower share topic than recalls or verdicts, and there is no human-stakes story to carry it beyond the data. The ceiling is the topic, not the writing.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0
No statutes, verdicts, or regulations to misstate, and the piece never pretends otherwise. The 3PMSF definition is sourced to Kinja and Tire Rack, the test is described with its real parameters (2025 Camry, 215/55R17, near-freezing), and every number carries a source. Deduction: the "1999" date for the snowflake standard rests on a secondary source (Kinja); the ASTM text itself was not consulted, and the research file says so honestly.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0
Genuine original contribution on four counts: the badge-gap arithmetic (same badge, 15.1 ft apart), the inversion table (snow/ice winner is the wet-weather loser, 36 ft swing), the Wintrac Pro+ upset inside one manufacturer's lineup, and the residual-speed translation (73/75/69 km/h still being carried when the better tire has stopped). Limitations are stated in the disclaimer, and the counterargument is given at full strength with its own paragraph. Deductions: no FARS crash data links tire type to deaths, so braking distance is a proxy, stated in the disclaimer; Tire Rack's commercial interest is named but the piece leans on Tire Rack as the primary source for most numbers, with Tyre Reviews as the independent cross-check.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0
Numbers are consistent across two independent sources and cross-checked in both feet and metres: 56.1/57.3/72.4/81.1/84.5 snow, 38.7/39.4/57.7 ice, 93/129 wet. The pull stat (15.1 ft) is the right number to lead with. Deduction: the "won snow and ice" overstatement slipped into the draft and had to be caught in critique. It was fixed before scoring, but the ice ranking should have been verified against the table during drafting, not during review.
