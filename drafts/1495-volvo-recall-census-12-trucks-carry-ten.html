Volvo Recalled 12 Times in 2026. Ten of Them Were Trucks.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They did not get better, and this time the numbers belong to Volvo. Twelve safety recalls filed in 2026 touch a 2026-model-year Volvo, covering 21,984 vehicles. Split the make in two and the story changes completely: Volvo Cars filed twice, Volvo Trucks filed ten times, and both car filings are the same car.[1]

10 of 12
Volvo Trucks’ share of Volvo’s 2026 recall campaigns. Cars filed twice.

Method first, because a census is only as honest as its counting rules. NHTSA files Volvo Cars and Volvo Trucks under one make name, and the federal vehicle database lists 65 Volvo model names for 2026, from the EX30 to the VNL tractor to trailers. I queried all of them, 329 queries in total, kept campaigns with a 26V number filed in calendar 2026, and deduplicated by campaign number. Seven queries returned rows. Six control queries against model-year 2025 all returned real campaigns, which proves the 322 empty responses mean no records exist rather than a broken vocabulary.[1]

Start with the cars, because the car side is the surprise. Two campaigns, both on the EX30, Volvo’s cheapest car. One is a seat belt warning chime that may not sound, covering 1,584 vehicles. The other is a head restraint cushion that may detach in a crash, covering 9,930 vehicles, and Volvo estimates every single one of them has the defect. Every other 2026 Volvo car, XC40, XC60, XC90, S60, S90, V60, EX90, filed nothing. Zero. Independent aggregators confirm it: the 2026 XC90, the flagship, the car Volvo’s safety reputation was built on, has no recall campaigns on file.[2][3]

Scale the EX30 numbers against what Volvo actually sells and they get stranger. Volvo sold 3,222 EX30s in the United States in the first nine months of 2026, down almost a third from last year. That head-restraint recall alone covers 9,930 EX30s, three times the year’s sales, because it reaches back to the 2025 model year. And the worst EX30 story of the year sits outside this census entirely: two January and February filings recalled 2025 EX30s over a high-voltage battery that can short-circuit and catch fire, 40,323 cars worldwide, owners told to cap charging at 70 percent, an estimated $195 million repair bill, and a proposed Quebec class action filed in October alleging owners waited nine months for new battery modules. Four campaigns, one nameplate, and the census only counts two of them, because the census counts filings and model years, and the battery cars are 2025s.[4][5]

Now the trucks. Ten campaigns, 10,470 vehicles, and the failure modes read like a list of everything a truck is not allowed to lose. Steering: 3,794 VNLs, VNRs, VNs, VAHs and VHDs with steering arms that may not have been tightened enough, though Volvo estimates only 0.1 percent actually have the defect, which works out to about four trucks. Wheels: 155 trucks under a Do Not Drive order because paint masking material left between wheel and hub lets lug nuts loosen until a wheel detaches. Brakes: 2,800 tractors that can lose ABS and stability control when an unprotected Bendix modulator connector short-circuits, plus 222 more whose brakes release in 0.61 seconds against a federal maximum of 0.55, plus 392 tanker tractors whose software miscalculates axle loads and weakens trailer braking on smoothbore tankers. Parking brake: 800 VNLs that can roll away in weight-bias mode, every one of them defective by Volvo’s own estimate, discovered when one rolled on Volvo’s own transport yard. Add turn signals that may not signal (336), an instrument panel that may go blank (115), marker lights that may not light (1,851), and five VNLs with loose or missing cab bolts, and the truck side of Volvo filed more recalls in ten months than most car companies file in a year.[6][7]

Two numbers in the file deserve a second look, because they point in opposite directions. Three campaigns estimate 100 percent of their population carries the defect: the 800 rollaway trucks and the 9,930 EX30 head restraints. At the other end, the biggest campaign in the census, the 3,794 steering trucks, estimates 0.1 percent. A recall count treats those two campaigns as equals. They are not equals. One is a near-certain defect on a small population, the other is a four-truck problem wearing a 3,794-truck coat.

One more wrinkle, and it is the one that makes the count understate the problem. Five of the ten truck campaigns have a parallel Mack filing on the same defect, same supplier, same week: the turn signals, the ABS modulators, the wheel lug nuts, the marker lights, the tanker braking. Volvo Group builds both brands on shared platforms. Count Volvo alone and you get 12 campaigns. Count the group and the shared-platform failures roughly double. NHTSA files by make, so the census does too, but the defect does not respect the badge.[7]

What to do, concretely. If you own a 2025 or 2026 EX30, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V136 and 26V671; the head-restraint letters go out November 30, but the VIN lookup has been live since October 9, and the fix is epoxy at three points on both front seats. If you own a 2025 EX30, also check 26V001 and 26V103 and keep charging capped at 70 percent until the battery modules are replaced. If you own any other 2026 Volvo car, zero filings touch your car, but check your VIN anyway, because the 2025 filings on the rear camera and the brake software cover the same platforms. If you run VNL or VNR tractors, check VINs before dispatch: two of the ten truck campaigns carry Do Not Drive advisories, and a wheel that detaches at highway speed does not wait for the letter.

Limitations, stated plainly. This is a filing census, not a danger ranking. A campaign counts as one whether it covers five trucks or 9,930 cars, whether 0.1 percent or 100 percent of the population has the defect. One campaign, 26V513, is attributed to model-year 2026 by the API and to 2027 only by the NHTSA acknowledgment letter; if the letter is right, the census is 11, not 12. Populations are NHTSA “potential number of units affected” figures, upper bounds, not counts of broken vehicles. No Volvo Trucks retail-sales denominator was available at census time, so the truck side has no per-vehicle rate, only a count.

Counterargument, at full strength. Twelve campaigns is a mid-table count, and most of them are small, zero-injury, and already fixed at the dealer. The car side filed twice on its cheapest model while its flagship went untouched. The biggest truck campaign is 99.9 percent trucks that do not have the defect. The five-truck cab-bolt recall is the system working exactly as designed. By any honest measure, Volvo had a decent year, and the EX30 battery story, the one with a fire risk and a class action, is a 2025 story that the 2026 census correctly excludes. All of that is fair, and this article grants it. The counterweight is the split: a company that sells safety as its identity filed ten truck recalls and two car recalls, and the car recalls both landed on the car it sells to first-time buyers. A filing census cannot say whether Volvo is safe. It can say where the filings are, and in 2026 they were in the trucks and the cheapest car.
