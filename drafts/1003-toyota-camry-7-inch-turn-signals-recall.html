Toyota’s $28,700 Camry Can Lose Its Turn Signals. The $33,700 One Can’t.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 28, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. Toyota just recalled 508,354 Camry Hybrids in the United States, with the global count now around 655,000, because a software glitch can blank the 7-inch instrument cluster at startup. When the screen goes dark, the turn signal lamps, the hazard lights, and the seatbelt warning buzzers stop working too. That is not a display bug. It is a control bug.

508,354
US Camry Hybrids carrying the buggy software — Toyota’s own estimate says 100% of them

Here is the part that should make you angry. Per the NHTSA Part 573 filing, the 7-inch “combination meter,” built by Visteon in Mexico, does not just show information. It controls vehicle functions, including the lamps your car is federally required to have. The LE ($28,700), SE ($31,000), and Nightshade ($32,300) all carry the 7-inch unit. The XLE ($33,700) and XSE ($34,900) get a 12.3-inch cluster with different software, and they are not recalled. Spend $2,700 stepping from SE to XLE, and your turn signals are suddenly immune. That is a trim perk nobody listed on the window sticker.

One screen bug breaks four separate federal motor vehicle safety standards: 101 for controls and displays, 108 for lamps, 114 for theft and rollaway protection (the key reminder), and 208 for occupant crash protection (the seatbelt reminder). Four standards. One part. And it took two years to reproduce — Toyota first heard complaints in July 2024, could not replicate the failure on a test bench for 24 months, finally caught it on a complete vehicle in late July 2026, and the recall decision landed four days later. Universal exposure, random occurrence, two years invisible to testing. That is the scariest combination a software defect can have.

Fairness check. The failure only strikes at startup, when the screen is visibly dead in your driveway and a restart likely brings everything back. Similar Toyota and Lexus meters running different software were never affected. The fix is a free dealer software flash, already rolling out with owner letters through October 5. So: if you own a 2025-2026 Camry LE, SE, or Nightshade, punch your VIN into nhtsa.gov/recalls — Toyota’s numbers are 26TA16 or 26TB16 — and get the flash. Shopping used? Make the seller show the recall is done before you sign. And if your cluster ever greets you with a blank screen, drive like nobody can see your intentions. Because nobody can.
