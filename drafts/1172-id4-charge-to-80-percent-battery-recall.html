Volkswagen Told 22,524 ID.4 Owners to Stop Charging. It Still Doesn't Know Why.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 15, 2026

On September 30, 2026, Volkswagen filed a defect report with NHTSA that reads like a status update from a mystery novel. Campaign 26V-630 covers 22,524 ID.4 electric SUVs whose batteries can suffer thermal propagation, a fire, when charged above 80 percent. The filing lists the root cause as unknown, the remedy as under development, and the interim fix as a warning sticker on the dashboard.[1][2]

80%
Highest charge VW currently trusts its own battery packs with

Credit where it is due: the timeline shows a safety system working. Volkswagen began reviewing high-voltage battery thermal events in the field in June 2026. Preliminary data on June 17 pointed at a correlation between specific isolation-resistance values and DC fast-charging ratios. A field study of complete battery systems followed, covering vehicles left out of earlier battery recalls, and in September VW identified the overheating packs. The Part 573 report landed September 30, was amended October 1, and the announcement came October 8.[4] Identification to federal filing in under a month is, by recall standards, brisk.

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again, and they did not get better. Twenty-two U.S. field claims were logged between January 18, 2024 and August 7, 2026: roughly 31 months, or about 0.7 claims per month.[2][3] Volkswagen estimates 0.1 percent of the recall population is actually affected.[8] Multiply it out: one-tenth of one percent of 22,524 is about 22 vehicles. Observed failures and estimated defective packs are the same number, which means the recall population is sized at roughly 1,000 times the number of known bad packs. That is not a criticism; it is how recalls are supposed to work. Cast the net wide, catch the fish you cannot see. Still: roughly 22,500 owners are under an 80 percent charge cap to protect about 22 cars.

Two details deserve their own line items. To scope the recall population, Volkswagen read the cars' own charging data, combined with factory and supplier records.[3] Your charging habits became evidence in your own recall. That is a first for this desk, and it will happen more often. The second line item is the range math. A 2024 ID.4 Pro S is rated at 291 miles of EPA range.[7] At an 80 percent cap, usable range drops to about 233 miles. Fifty-eight miles of advertised range are off-limits until a fix exists that nobody has designed, for a defect nobody has explained, on a date nobody has set.

Then there is the supplier. Cells come from SK Battery America in Commerce, Georgia, and VW's product safety committee has asked the supplier for clarifications on production and a possible clean date.[4][5] Regulars will recognize the name: SK cells were the through-line of our September investigation into misaligned battery electrodes that hit two automakers at once.[6] This is also VW's second major battery action of 2026; earlier the company recalled nearly 100,000 EVs globally, including about 44,000 ID.4s in the United States.[2] None of this means SK is a bad supplier. It means SK is a big one, and big suppliers have big denominators. The honest counterargument to the alarm above: the recall record lists no injuries, the failure rate is genuinely low, and VW moved from field signal to federal filing in weeks. If the eventual remedy is a software update, and some vehicles are already getting a software-enforced 80 percent cap, this will read in hindsight as the recall system working exactly as designed.

What this does not prove: the mechanism is correlation, not causation. Isolation resistance and DC-charging ratios tracked with the failures, but with the root cause officially unknown, the 80 percent threshold is a fence around a hole nobody has seen the bottom of. That 0.1 percent defect rate is VW's estimate, not an independent measurement. Twenty-two claims are not necessarily twenty-two fires. This story is built on recall filings, not fatality data. Nobody has died in these events, and that distinction matters.

If you own one of these cars, the to-do list is short. Set your maximum charge to 80 percent in the touchscreen charging menu now. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, where the campaign has been searchable since early October.[1] Expect a risk letter around November 27, with a second letter when the remedy exists.[2] VW's customer line is 1-800-893-5298; the recall code is 93EX. Shopping used? Any 2023-to-2025 ID.4 from the Chattanooga build window should have the remedy applied before you trust a 100 percent charge.
