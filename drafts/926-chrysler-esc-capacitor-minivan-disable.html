A Cracked Capacitor Just Demoted 2,017 Chrysler Minivans to 2011

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 17, 2026

Chrysler has recalled 2,017 minivans because a cracked capacitor in the brake pedal position sensor can disable electronic stability control, the single most important safety technology of the last thirty years.[1] The only warning the driver gets is a dashboard light that most people have never decoded. Your 2026 minivan could be driving around with 2011-level stability protection, and the only signal is a glyph.

4,100
Estimated lives saved by ESC in 2010–2014, per NHTSA analysis cited by IIHS[2]

Recall 26V561, filed September 1, covers 2025–2026 Chrysler Voyagers (357 vehicles), Pacificas (1,652), and eight 2026 Pacifica plug-in hybrids. The suspect sensor, part 68236598AA, comes from CTS Corporation in Elkhart, Indiana. When its capacitor cracks, the ESC warning light illuminates and the system shuts itself off.[1] That is the entire symptom list. There is no shudder, no chime, no performance change you can feel. Just the light.

This recall is legally distinct from the usual defect actions. It is an FMVSS 126 noncompliance. Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 126 has required electronic stability control on every new passenger vehicle since the 2012 model year, operational during all phases of driving.[3] These 2026 vehicles were built in violation of a 2012 rule. It is a strange species of recall: not "something might break" but "your car already fails a fourteen-year-old federal standard."

The stakes are why the standard exists. IIHS studies indicate ESC cuts fatal single-vehicle crash risk by about half and fatal multiple-vehicle crash risk by 20 percent. For rollovers, the deadliest single-vehicle outcome, the reduction is 72 percent for cars and 75 percent for SUVs.[2] NHTSA's own analysis credits ESC with 1,580 lives saved in 2014 alone. Losing that coverage is not a minor trim-level deletion. It is a rollback of the biggest single safety gain of the modern era.

FCA's own risk language is refreshingly blunt: "Failure of the ESC system when intervention is expected and/or relied upon can cause a vehicle crash without prior warning."[1] No warning before the failure. The company opened its investigation on March 25, 2026, identified the cracked-capacitor root cause by August, and determined the noncompliance on August 26. Owner notification letters begin September 29. Anyone driving with the light on right now is relying on luck through every rain-slicked curve.

One detail worth sitting with: these are minivans. Voyagers and Pacificas are purchased by people with children, car seats, and the particular anxiety that comes with both. Chrysler estimates 10 percent of the recalled population actually carries the defect, which works out to roughly 200 vehicles.[1] Small fleet. Large irony. The family hauler is the one rolling without rollover prevention.

The strongest counterargument

The real exposure here is small, and the system worked. Roughly 200 vehicles with actual failed sensors is a rounding error against a U.S. fleet of 280 million, and unlike most defects, this one announces itself with a warning light. Drivers who treat dashboard lights as instructions, rather than ambient decor, will catch it. FCA investigated voluntarily over five months and recalled without a fight or a federal order. The minivan rollover objection also has teeth: IIHS's most dramatic numbers skew toward SUVs, and minivans ride lower, so the actual fatality delta for these specific vehicles is smaller than the 75 percent headline suggests. This is a recall behaving exactly the way recalls are supposed to behave.

Limitations

The 10 percent figure is Chrysler's engineering estimate, not a counted population, so the true number of failed sensors on the road is unknown in both directions. The IIHS effectiveness statistics compare ESC-equipped versus non-equipped vehicles across the general fleet; applying them to specific minivan drivers assumes comparable driving conditions, which may not hold. The 573 report cites no field crashes, so FCA's "crash without prior warning" language describes a stated risk, not a documented outcome. We have not seen the remedy sensor's design-change documentation beyond Chrysler's one-line description: a sensor "produced without a cracked capacitor."

What to do

If you own a 2025–2026 Voyager or Pacifica, or a 2026 Pacifica plug-in hybrid: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. VINs became searchable September 9, so there is no reason to wait for the September 29 letter. If your ESC light is illuminated, drive the vehicle like it has no stability control, because it has none. Slow down for curves, widen your following distance in rain, and get the sensor inspected under the recall. And consider learning what the ESC light looks like. It is the squiggly car with skid marks. When it lights up, your car is telling you it just became a 2011.
