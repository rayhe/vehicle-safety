# Research: Dodge Avenger — America's Druggiest Drivers Drive a Dead Sedan

**Slug:** 1112-dodge-avenger-druggiest-drivers
**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk Chief)
**Date:** 2026-10-09

## Core finding

The Dodge Avenger has the highest drug-positive rate of any model with 500+ drivers in FARS toxicology data (2014–2023):

- **drug_pct: 11.3%** (99 of 877 drivers) — #1 among all models with n>500
- alcohol_pct: 14.9% (131 drivers)
- any_pct: 22.0% (193 drivers)
- deaths: 192 total, rate 0.38/100M VMT (LOW lethality — the car itself is not deadly)
- Model years in FARS: 2008–2014, peak 2013 (54 deaths)

The paradox Dale gets to chew on: the most drug-positive driver pool in America drives one of the least lethal cars per mile. Impairment without lethality — the Avenger is a slow, cheap, survivable sedan full of wrecked drivers.

## The dead-car hook

- Avenger sedan production: 2007–2014; 2014 was the final model year (Wikipedia, Carvana). Killed by Marchionne's brand restructuring; Dodge became the muscle brand, Chrysler the mainstream brand; the midsize sedan had no seat at the table.
- Consumer Reports: discontinued after 2014, "lived on in some daily-rental fleets." Rental-fleet afterlife = high-turnover, low-attachment drivers.
- Sales peaked 2007 (~106,000 units), collapsed to ~43,000 by 2013 (kevinsautos.com).
- Successor: Chrysler 200 (also dead now).

## The honest limitation (the story's spine)

NHTSA's own research note "Understanding the Limitations of Drug Test Information, Reporting, and Testing Practices in Fatal Crashes" (rosap.ntl.bts.gov):
- "knowing that a driver tested positive for cannabinoids does not necessarily indicate that the person was impaired by the drug at the time of the crash" — THC metabolites detectable weeks after use.
- Testing is inconsistent across jurisdictions; labs are costly; a jurisdiction that tests more will show higher drug-positive percentages. Selection bias is baked in.
- NHTSA Report to Congress on Marijuana-Impaired Driving: THC immunoassay screens can respond to non-impairing metabolites; "impossible to predict specific effects based on THC-COOH concentrations."

So the headline number (11.3%) measures *exposure to testing + drug prevalence in the driver pool*, not impairment at the wheel. Dale's take: the number is still signal — it tells you who is driving Avengers — but it is not a conviction.

## Counterargument (full strength)

1. Drug-positive ≠ impaired. A daily cannabis user can test positive weeks sober. The 11.3% overstates true impairment, possibly a lot.
2. Testing bias: if Avenger crashes happen in jurisdictions that test aggressively, the rate inflates. FARS drug data is not a randomized trial.
3. The Avenger's low death rate (0.38) suggests these crashes are mostly survivable — or that Avengers are driven fewer high-risk miles. The drug finding may say more about *who buys a $4,500 used sedan* than about the car.
4. Confounders: age, income, rental-fleet exposure, geography — none controlled.

## Actionable takeaways

1. Used-car shoppers: orphaned cheap sedans (Avenger, ~$4,500 used per CR) concentrate the highest-risk driver pools. Your crash risk isn't just your car — it's the cars around you. Insurance actuaries already price this; check quotes before you buy the bargain.
2. Drug-impaired driving enforcement needs testing standards, not just more testing — NHTSA's own research says current data can't distinguish impairment from last week's joint.
3. If you drive an Avenger: the car is fine (rate 0.38 is low). The statistic is about the fleet's drivers, not your sedan. But check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — Takata-era airbags touched this generation.
