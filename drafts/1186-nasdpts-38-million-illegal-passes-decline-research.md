## News peg
**National School Bus Safety Week runs October 19-23, 2026** (starts Monday; this year's theme "Safe Rides, Everyday Heroes"). The freshest national data on the week's core issue — drivers illegally blowing past stopped school buses — comes from the **NASDPTS 2026 Illegal Passing Survey**, presented at the National School Transportation Association meeting in Denver (summer 2026) and re-presented at the NAPT 2026 conference (October 2026), where NTSB and FMCSA officials also laid out federal school-transportation priorities.

## Verified facts

**NASDPTS 2026 Illegal Passing Survey** (School Transportation News, via NASDPTS Executive Director Ronna Weber):
- 101,334 school bus drivers participated — 26.2% of the nation's school bus drivers — across 39 states + DC, in a voluntary one-day count during the 2025-2026 school year.
- They reported **56,024 vehicles illegally passing** their stopped buses in that single day.
- NASDPTS extrapolated to 100% of U.S. school bus drivers: **~213,800 illegal passings per school day**.
- Projected across a 180-day school year: **38.4 million illegal passings per year**.
- **The trend is down two years running:** 45.2 million (2023-2024) → 39.3 million (2024-2025) → 38.4 million (2025-2026). That's a 15% decline over two school years. NASDPTS: "we are noticing a trend in the reduction" — but "38.4 million illegal passings is still entirely too many" (NASDPTS President Mike Stier).

**Scale context** (GHSA + BusPatrol, "A National Action Plan for School Bus Safety," March 2026):
- ~20 million children ride ~500,000 school buses each day in the U.S.
- The plan: first comprehensive 50-state roadmap; 69 recommendations for highway safety offices, law enforcement, districts, tech companies, AV providers, judges.
- At 213,800 illegal passes per school day across ~500,000 buses: roughly **one illegal pass per bus every 2.3 school days**.

**Federal/policy movement:**
- **Brake for Kids Act** (H.R.2348 / S.2812): would direct NHTSA to conduct a nationwide public-safety campaign on the dangers of illegal school bus passing. NSTA continues its #KidsDeserveABrake campaign and is lobbying Congress for it.
- **NAPT 2026 Annual Conference** (October 2026): NTSB and FMCSA officials discussed school transportation safety priorities; NASDPTS presented the survey; NSTA proposed a "Break for Kids Act" public-service campaign.
- NTSB has a **preliminary report out on a fatal Tennessee school bus crash** (details pending final report).

**State-level illustration — Delaware** (SafetySense, October 2026):
- In Delaware's one-day stop-arm survey, **1,108 participating bus drivers reported 572 illegal passing violations in a single day** — roughly one violation for every two participating buses. Nearly 300 involved vehicles approaching from behind the bus, where children are least visible.

**The death data (for honest calibration)** (NHTSA, via Idealease summary of federal data):
- 2014-2023: **169 pedestrian deaths vs 111 school-bus occupant deaths** in school-bus-related crashes — the danger is outside the bus.
- 2000-2022: **55 fatalities in crashes involving a driver illegally passing a stopped school bus**; nearly half (25) were pedestrians age 18 or younger.
- Calibration: ~55 illegal-passing deaths over 22 years against ~40 million annual violations → the per-violation death risk is on the order of 1 in 16 million. The violation count measures noncompliance, not carnage.

## Original calculations (the novel contribution)
- **The 15% decline:** (45.2 − 38.4) / 45.2 = 15.0% over two school years; year-over-year 2024-25 → 2025-26: (39.3 − 38.4) / 39.3 = 2.3%.
- **Per-bus rate:** 213,800 illegal passes/day ÷ ~500,000 buses ≈ 0.43 per bus per day → **one illegal pass per bus every ~2.3 school days**.
- **Delaware intensity:** 572 violations / 1,108 participating drivers = 0.52 per participating bus per day — above the national extrapolated average (0.43), consistent with a small dense state.
- **Violation-to-death calibration:** 55 illegal-passing deaths / 22 years = 2.5/year vs ~40M annual violations → ~1 death per 16 million violations. (Order-of-magnitude illustration, clearly labeled.)

## Limitations (must state in article)
- **Voluntary survey, varying sample:** participation is voluntary and changes year to year (26.2% of drivers this year; different states/drivers than prior years). The extrapolation assumes non-participating drivers see the same violation rate as participants — unproven. The "decline" may partly reflect sample composition, not behavior change.
- **Violations ≠ deaths:** the census counts noncompliance. With ~2.5 illegal-passing deaths/year nationally against ~40M annual violations, the count is a measure of lawlessness around children, not of carnage. Do not present 38.4M as 38.4M near-deaths.
- **Cause of decline unproven:** stop-arm camera expansion (e.g., Kentucky's 2026 camera law), awareness campaigns, or survey noise could each explain part of the drop. The data cannot apportion credit.
- **One-day snapshot:** a single school day's count, projected across 180 days, assumes the survey day is representative. Weather, local events, and enforcement blitzes move the number.
- **NTSB Tennessee crash:** preliminary report only; no findings cited beyond its existence.

## Strongest counterargument (must state at full strength)
38.4 million is still an astronomical number, and the celebrated "decline" is 2.3% year-over-year — well within the noise of a voluntary survey whose participating drivers and states change every year. NASDPTS itself says the problem is "far from resolved," and its own president calls 38.4 million "entirely too many." Worse, the violation count may measure enforcement theater more than safety: cameras that mail $225 civil tickets to registered owners (no points, often not the driver) could suppress *counted* violations near equipped buses while changing nothing about the unobserved millions. And the death data is the coldest water of all — about 2.5 illegal-passing deaths a year. If the goal is saving children's lives rather than counting scofflaws, the 38.4-million figure is a measure of disrespect for the law, not of danger to kids. The buses themselves remain the safest vehicles on the road; the crisis framing serves the camera vendors and the associations selling the solution.

## Actionable insights (hard gate)
- **Drivers:** red flashing lights + extended stop arm = stop, both directions, in all 50 states (divided-highway exceptions vary by state — know yours). Yellow flashing = the bus is about to stop; slow down, don't accelerate past. The Delaware data shows nearly half of violators come from behind the bus, where children are least visible — the rear approach is the kill zone.
- **Parents:** teach the 10-foot rule (wait at least 10 feet from the curb), wait for the driver's hand signal before crossing, make eye contact with the bus driver. The NHTSA data is unambiguous: the child is safest inside the bus; the walk to and from it is where 169 pedestrians died in a decade.
- **Districts/communities:** ask whether your buses carry stop-arm cameras — camera programs show >90% non-recidivism among cited drivers (Lee County, FL). Support the Brake for Kids Act (H.R.2348/S.2812) if you want a federally funded national campaign.
- **What the decline means for you:** illegal passing is falling, but at 213,800 violations per school day, your child's bus is still passed illegally about twice a week. Act like the law is a suggestion to everyone else — because for 38.4 million driver-days a year, it is.
