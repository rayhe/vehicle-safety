# Research: #1255 — The Car Seats That Passed the Law and Failed the Crash

**Slug:** `1255-axkid-britax-car-seat-test-failures`
**Assigned journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — child restraint design, restraint-system failure analysis)
**Date:** 2026-10-23

## The peg

October 2026: European consumer-testing consortium (ADAC, Stiftung Warentest, Which?, ANWB, Consumentenbond, Que Choisir, Råd & Rön) ran its second 2026 child-seat test round. Two seats from leading brands failed crash tests catastrophically enough that the consortium broke protocol and published an early warning on Oct 6, before the full results were due. Both brands have since issued voluntary recalls.

## What failed

### Axkid Minikid Core (extended rearward-facing seat, Sweden)
- Frontal impact, rearward-facing install, Q6 dummy (6-year-old): the support leg RETRACTED mid-crash in both upright and reclined positions.
- The support leg braces the seat against the vehicle floor. Once it collapses, the seat and child move far further forward than designed.
- In the reclined position test, the harness also broke away from the seat shell. The dummy was no longer fully restrained.
- Which? recommendation: stop using the seat immediately.
- Axkid response (Daniel Lundgren, Safety and Compliance Manager): attributed the leg failure to the base striking the leg with enough force to release its adjustment mechanism. Said the finding is "inconsistent with its own internal tests" but the company takes it seriously.
- **Key tension:** Axkid initiated a voluntary recall, replacing all Minikid Core seats free of charge (Consumentenbond: replaced with the Minikid 4 Pro) — while simultaneously telling parents they may keep using the seats while waiting for the replacement, because the seats "still perform well in legal requirements and other crash test scenarios." Which? says stop now; the maker says use while you wait.
- Company line: "Minikid Core has successfully completed all required approvals" and its own standards "go beyond the legal requirements." It passed the law. It failed Which?.

### Britax Römer Baby-Safe Pro + Vario Base 5Z (infant carrier + ISOFIX base)
- Frontal impact, rearward-facing + RECLINED on the Vario Base 5Z, Q1½ dummy (18-month-old): the harness buckle FAILED and opened. The crash-test dummy came loose and was thrown.
- **Mode-specific failure:** upright on the base, it held. Installed with the car's adult seatbelt (no base), it held. Only the reclined-on-base configuration exploded the buckle. Same seat, same harness, same child — one recline click is the difference between restrained and ejected.
- Britax response: investigating; said the result is unexpected given its own full-sized vehicle and development tests under the same conditions showed no abnormalities.
- **Contagion angle:** Britax points out the failed buckle is "a widely used industry-standard part sourced from a common supplier and used by multiple manufacturers." If the buckle is the fault, it may not be confined to Britax Römer.
- **UPDATE 12/10/2026 (Which? updated the article):** Britax issued a voluntary recall of the Baby-Safe Pro for all products manufactured 26 December 2025 to 30 September 2026 (item numbers 2000039636–2000042125 and set combos with the Vario Base 5Z 2000039740–2000042127, colors across LUX/Style/Classic ranges). Production date is printed on the product label on the bottom of the seat shell. A dedicated recall site is under development.

## The regulation-vs-reality gap (the novel angle)

- Both seats were fully legal for sale. R129 approval requires passing standardized tests. Which? tests are derived from Euro NCAP crash scenarios, using "higher — but realistic — loads on the child-restraint system," tougher than the standard R129 regulations.
- In the US, FMVSS 213 requires only a 30 mph frontal test for certification; new side-impact and updated frontal standards are due by the end of 2026 (still being phased in). Everything legal can still fail a realistic crash.
- The ADAC's broader 2026 results: 16 models tested (8 baby seats); "almost every second seat" rated good, but one baby seat and one toddler seat rated "poor" — the two under early warning. Three best were baby seats (Maxi-Cosi Coral Slide Pro + Familyfix Slide Pro test winner at ~570 euros, Recaro Toron 1, Cybex Sirona G3 + Base G3).

## Actionable takeaways

1. Own a Baby-Safe Pro? Flip the shell, read the production-date label. If it's between 26 Dec 2025 and 30 Sep 2026, it's in the Britax voluntary recall — register on Britax's recall site (being built) and do not use it reclined on the Vario Base 5Z until fixed.
2. Interims that the testing says are safe for the Britax: upright on the base, or belt-installed with no base. Those configurations passed.
3. Own a Minikid Core? Axkid's recall replaces all affected seats free. Register via Axkid's online form. Which? says stop using it now; the maker says you may wait. Mia's take: the side that ran the test and watched the harness leave the shell gets the benefit of the doubt.
4. General: the buckle you trust most is an industry-standard part — ask whether any of this crosses into US Britax models, and re-check your seat's install mode (recline vs upright changes what the harness experiences).
