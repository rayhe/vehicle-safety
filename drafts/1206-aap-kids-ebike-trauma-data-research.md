## News peg (October 2026)

Two studies presented at the **American Academy of Pediatrics 2026 National Conference & Exhibition** (San Diego, Oct 2-6, 2026):

1. **Weir et al.** — "Comparison of Bikes, E-Bikes, and Gas-Powered Cycle Injuries in the United States: A National Review from 2005–2024." NEISS data, patients age 17 and younger: **134,426 pedal-assist bicycle cases, 305 e-bike cases, 1,721 gas-powered cycle cases.**
   - **E-bikes had the highest percentage of trauma-related deaths** of the three categories. E-bikes also had the second-highest trauma-related admission rate.
   - Gas-powered bikes led in hospital admissions, followed closely by e-bikes.
   - Fractures more common in e-bike crashes (vs. contusions/abrasions for pedal bikes).
   - Head injuries most common body region in all three: **20% e-bikes**, 16.6% gas-powered, 15.8% pedal-assist.
   - Author Seth Weir, DO (third-year general surgery resident): goal was "to better inform parents when buying electric-assist bicycles."
2. **Edison et al. (Children's Hospital Los Angeles, 2019-2025)** — e-bikes vs e-scooters in kids: e-bikes produced higher rates of concussions, fractures, surgeries, and inpatient admissions. **E-bike patients were ~3x as likely to require surgery and ~3x as likely to need an inpatient stay** as e-scooter patients. Author: Bianca Edison, MD, MS, orthopedic surgeon.

## The novel angle (original contribution)

The intuition most parents hold is a ladder: pedal bikes (safe) < e-bikes (a bit faster, a bit riskier) < gas-powered bikes/motorcycles (dangerous). The Weir data breaks the ladder: **in pediatric trauma outcomes, e-bikes sit at the TOP of the death-share ranking, above gas-powered bikes.** The likely mechanism is exposure + protection mismatch: e-bikes put motorcycle-ish speeds (up to 28 mph, Class 3) under riders with bicycle-ish protective behavior — and the head-injury share (20%, highest of the three) plus fracture-over-abrasion pattern says these are higher-energy crashes, not just more crashes. Edison's 3x surgery finding completes the reframe: the vehicle parents fear less (the e-bike, parked next to the scooter in the garage) hospitalizes kids at triple the rate of the one that gets the headlines.

## Limitations

- NEISS captures emergency-department visits, not a complete fatality census; trauma-death percentages are shares within each category's ED cases, not population death rates.
- Only 305 e-bike cases over 20 years (the category barely existed before ~2017); small-sample noise is real, though the direction is consistent across both studies.
- Conference abstracts, not yet peer-reviewed full papers; methods detail is limited.
- German and NYC figures come from different taxonomies (police reports, council data); not directly comparable to NEISS.
- The denominator problem (#1191): e-bike ridership exploded, so rising case counts partly reflect exposure growth. The studies' contribution is about SEVERITY per incident, which is less denominator-sensitive.

## Strongest counterargument

E-bikes may be replacing something more dangerous: a teen on an e-bike is a teen not learning to drive a 4,000-pound SUV at 16, and not riding in cars with teen drivers — the deadliest thing adolescents do. Absolute pediatric e-bike deaths remain small next to the ~2,000+ annual teen motor-vehicle deaths. And 134,426 pedal-bike cases dwarf 305 e-bike cases precisely because pedal bikes are everywhere; per-trip risk comparisons need exposure denominators nobody has. Banning or fearing e-bikes could push kids back into cars, which is worse.

## Actionable insights

- **Helmet, every ride, no exceptions.** Head injuries were the #1 body region in all three categories and highest (20%) for e-bikes. A helmet is the cheapest severity-reduction device in this entire story.
- **Don't buy your kid a Class 3 e-bike.** 28 mph pedal-assist is motorcycle speed with bicycle brakes and bicycle judgment. Class 1 (20 mph, pedal-assist only) is the saner ceiling for riders under 16.
- **Check your state and local age laws** — many riders in the AAP data were violating age/helmet rules already on the books.
- **Buy from reputable brands.** The CPSC's parallel track is battery fires (hundreds of fires, dozens of deaths); cheap no-name e-bikes fail on two axes at once.
- **Supervise the transition.** Fractures-not-abrasions means higher-energy crashes; new riders should practice away from traffic, and parents should know the actual top speed of the device (many "e-bikes" sold to kids exceed legal limits).
