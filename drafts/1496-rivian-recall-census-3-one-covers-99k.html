Rivian Recalled 3 Times in 2026. One of Them Covered 99,000 Vehicles.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

Rivian filed three safety recalls in 2026 that touch a 2026-model-year vehicle. Three campaigns, 103,526 vehicles, and one of those campaigns accounts for 98,828 of them. A census counts filings, which are what a regulator sees. This census says Rivian had the second-quietest recall year in the series and one of the loudest, in the same breath.[1]

95.5%
Share of Rivian’s 2026 census population in a single campaign: 26V597, the rearview camera notification.

Method first, because a census is only as honest as its counting rules. NHTSA’s recalls API returns a 400 when no recall records exist for a model year, and rows when they do. I queried every model the federal vehicle database lists for Rivian in 2026, four names, 25 queries with suffix variants, and two returned rows. Three controls against model-year 2025 all returned real campaigns, which proves the empty responses mean no records exist rather than a vocabulary failure, and I kept campaigns with a 26V number filed in calendar 2026, deduplicated by campaign number. Three survived.[1]

Start with the big one, because it is almost the whole census. On September 16 Rivian filed 26V597: a drive-mode notification can cover the rearview camera image when a driver shifts into reverse without answering a stability-control prompt, which fails FMVSS 111, the rear-visibility rule. Population: 98,828 vehicles, 2022–2026 R1T, 2022–2027 R1S, and 2,481 model-year 2027 R2s. Rivian estimates 100 percent of them carry the defect, because the suspect software is in all of them. And here is the part that makes the filing read like paperwork for a fix that already happened: the over-the-air remedy was already installed on more than 95 percent of the fleet when NHTSA published the campaign, with no customer reports, no crashes, and no injuries attached. Rivian found it in internal testing, decided on September 10 that a noncompliance could not be ruled out, and filed six days later.[2]

Buried in that population is the census’s strangest line. R2 is a 2027 model year. VPIC lists no 2026 R2 at all, which means a model-year query would never find it. Yet 2,481 R2s built between April 23 and August 10, 2026 are inside 26V597. Rivian’s best-selling vehicle in the third quarter, in a quarter when the company set a delivery record of 19,248 vehicles, was recalled before it had a full quarter of sales behind it. It entered the census through the campaign record, not the model list. Count the campaign once and the population is 98,828. Subtract the R2 slice and the strictly-2026 population is 101,045. Either way, a single notification covered more than twenty times the other two campaigns combined.[2][3]

Then the recall of a recall. In June 2025 Rivian recalled 27,882 R1s because front turn-signal lamps from supplier Myotek could fail to light. That should have ended it, because a recall is supposed to end a defect. It did not, because Myotek failed to quarantine the suspect parts and they kept going into new cars for another fourteen months. Rivian found 17 more failures between June 2025 and July 2026, then filed 26V508 on August 6, adding 3,829 more vehicles, 3,204 R1S and 625 R1T. Fewer than 1 percent are actually affected, by Rivian’s estimate, and no crashes are attached to the campaign. Nobody discovered the defect twice; it was never contained once.[4]

Smallest campaign in the census, best origin story: on September 16, 2025, a service technician noticed a loose second-row seat belt retractor during routine work on an R1T. Rivian traced the suspect installation window back to September 15, 2021, four years of production, and found 869 vehicles, 434 R1Ts and 435 R1Ss. Bolts holding the second-row seat belt retractors may not have been tightened to spec, with about 1 percent actually affected and no crashes or injuries reported, and a person caught it, not a sensor.[5]

Two more stories belong outside the count, and the census is honest enough to name them. In January Rivian recalled 19,641 R1s, model years 2022 to 2025, because rear toe links had been reassembled wrong during service under an old procedure, with one crash and minor injuries attached. In May NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation on 114,922 R1s over the same joint, after two owners described toe links separating while driving. That investigation is still open, and this site covered it in May. And the delivery vans: 34,824 EDVs, model years 2022 to 2025, recalled over a seat belt pretensioner cable damaged by drivers sitting on the belt while it was buckled underneath them. Filed in November 2025, letters in January 2026, 100 percent defect estimate. Together, the outside-census pair totals 54,465 vehicles. It contains the only crash in Rivian’s recall year. A census counts filings on 2026 cars. Danger, such as it is, lives one model year back.[6][7]

What to do, concretely, starts with the VIN lookup. If you own a 2022–2026 R1T or 2022–2027 R1S, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V597. An over-the-air update to software 2026.23 or later fixes it, and if your truck has updated since September it is probably already fixed, but the VIN lookup is the confirmation, not the version number. If you own a 2025 or 2026 R1S or R1T, check for 26V508, and do not assume a turn-signal replacement under the June 2025 recall clears you, because the expansion covers vehicles that got the same bad lamps after the original recall. If your R1 had rear suspension service before March 10, 2025, confirm the 26V003 toe-link remedy was applied. If you drive an Amazon delivery van, the EDV recall is 25V816, and the advice is free: do not sit on a buckled seat belt.

Limitations, stated plainly, before anyone mistakes this census for a ranking. This is a filing census, not a danger ranking. A campaign counts as one whether it covers 869 vehicles or 98,828, whether under 1 percent or 100 percent of the population has the defect. Populations are NHTSA “potential number of units affected” figures, upper bounds, not counts of broken vehicles. That R2 slice means the census population mixes model years by design: the campaign touches 2026 vehicles, so it counts once, R2s included. Rivian does not break out R1, R2 and EDV deliveries, so there is no per-model denominator, only the nine-month company total of 41,807 deliveries for scale.[3]

Counterargument, at full strength, because the honest reading deserves a hearing. Rivian had an excellent year. Three campaigns is the second-fewest of any brand in the series that filed anything, behind only Audi’s one. Biggest of the three was a software notification Rivian had already fixed on 95 percent of the fleet before the filing. Other two cover fewer than 5,000 vehicles combined, with sub-1-percent defect estimates and zero crashes or injuries between them. That toe-link investigation that looks alarming is an investigation, not a recall, and Rivian’s position is that the two owner complaints do not implicate the joint itself. All of that is fair, and this article grants it. Counterweight is the shape of the count. A single software version, present in every car the company ever built, turned into a 98,828-vehicle recall. A supplier that did not quarantine its bad parts turned one recall into two. And the company’s best-selling new car was recalled before it had a full quarter of sales. Rivian’s safety problems are increasingly software problems, and software problems scale the way software scales: everywhere, at once, and fixed before the letter arrives.
