## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.8/10
Fair to every party where fairness is owed: the counterargument paragraph is at full strength (best half-year in a decade, noise/seasonality/regression to the mean, arithmetic destiny) and the limitations are explicit in the article itself. Neither NHTSA nor NSC is treated as wrong; the 1,710 gap is presented as a measurement difference, not a disagreement about reality. No blame is assigned to any driver group; cyclists are described as unprotected by the trend, not as a cause of it. Deduction: "Drive like it ends this quarter" is prescriptive toward readers in a way that borders on editorializing risk behavior, and it lands in the action section where it reads as advice rather than analysis. Minor.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.6/10

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.9/10
Original contribution is real and shown with math: the within-half-year deceleration (Q1 -4.3% to Q2 -0.2%, a ~95% collapse in the improvement rate), the 983-death counterfactual against the H1 2025 pace, the 17-quarter streak count derived from the Q1 report's "16th consecutive" language, and the NSC-vs-NHTSA 1,710-death gap quantified on identical months. Limitations are honest (preliminary projections, revision risk, seasonality, Q2 base revision) and the counterargument is stated at full strength in the article itself. Verifiability: 6 references, all hyperlinked, with the primary CrashStats note and the NSC release linked directly. Deduction: the Q2 implied count of 8,980 is derived arithmetic, not a published figure, and the tension between the reported -0.2% and the raw -0.8% arithmetic is explained in one clause; a reader doing the subtraction themselves could think the article's math is wrong before reaching the base-revision explanation.
