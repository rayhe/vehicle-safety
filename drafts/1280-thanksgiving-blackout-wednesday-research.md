# Research: #1280 Thanksgiving Is America's Deadliest Holiday. The Killer Isn't the Drive Home.

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Thanksgiving is the deadliest holiday on American roads, but the fatal mechanism is not the long drive home. It is the local nights: Blackout Wednesday homecoming drinking and the Saturday after, when impaired drivers aged 21-34 kill at rates far above the annual baseline.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. NHTSA counts 868 drunk-driving deaths across five Thanksgiving periods (2019-2023), more than one-third of all deaths in those windows, and the 2026 AAA forecast (81.8M travelers, a record) means this year's exposure is the largest ever measured.

## Primary sources

Holiday period defined as 6 p.m. Wednesday to 5:59 a.m. Monday. Source: NHTSA press materials, restated by Connecticut DOT, Nov 2025: https://portal.ct.gov/dot/ctdot-press-releases/2025/protect-what-youre-thankful-for-never-drive-impaired

Same CTDOT release above. Corroborated by NHTSA's 2023 Thanksgiving Eve figure: 33% of the 92 drivers killed on Thanksgiving Eve 2023 were drunk (NHTSA via Arash Law summary): https://arashlaw.com/the-dangers-of-driving-on-thanksgiving/

4. **NHTSA, Thanksgiving Eve 2017-2021:** alcohol impairment played a role in fatal crashes involving 137 drivers on Thanksgiving Eve alone; largest share ages 21-24. ## Key facts (all traced above)
- 868 drunk-driving deaths, Thanksgiving periods 2019-2023; 35% share vs 29% annual average.
- 174 drunk-driving deaths, 2023 Thanksgiving weekend alone; largest share ages 21-34.
- Thanksgiving Eve 2023: 33% of 92 drivers killed were drunk. Thanksgiving Eve 2017-2021: 137 alcohol-involved fatal-crash drivers, largest share 21-24.
- NSC: 502 estimated deaths 2024 period; 439 projected 2025; 2026 exposure record: 81.8M travelers.
- Saturday after Thanksgiving is the deadliest drunk-driving day of the holiday week, per five-year analysis, not Wednesday.
- Holiday window definitions differ: NSC uses Wed 6 p.m. to Sun 11:59 p.m.; NHTSA uses Wed 6 p.m. to Mon 5:59 a.m. Totals are not directly comparable across sources.
- Seatbelts: 45% effectiveness for front-seat occupants; 111 additional lives per Thanksgiving period if universal belt use.

## Original contribution
1. **The local-trip inversion:** the travel press covers Thanksgiving as an interstate story (81.8M travelers, congestion windows), but the fatal mechanism is local: short, familiar, nighttime trips by 21-34-year-olds home for the holiday, drinking with people they grew up with. The highway miles are the exposure; the hometown bar is the cause.
2. **The Wednesday/Saturday split:** Blackout Wednesday has the reputation, but the five-year data puts the deadliest drunk-driving day on the Saturday after. Reputation tracks the drinking; deaths track the driving home after two more days of it, when the reunion crowd disperses and the designated-driver plan has expired.
3. **Excess-death math:** 868 deaths over five periods averages ~174 drunk-driving deaths per Thanksgiving. At the 29% annual baseline, a ~500-death holiday period would expect ~145 alcohol deaths; the observed 35% share implies roughly 30 excess alcohol deaths per Thanksgiving attributable to the holiday elevation alone. Labeled as an estimate from published shares, not a FARS finding.

## Limitations
- NSC figures are estimates and projections, not final FARS counts; the 2025 number (439) is a projection made before the holiday.
- NHTSA and NSC use different holiday windows (Sunday midnight vs Monday 5:59 a.m.), so their totals cannot be stacked or directly compared.
- The Saturday-after finding comes from a law-firm analysis of NHTSA data, not a peer-reviewed study; it is attributed as such.
- State-level variation is large (New Jersey +156% during Thanksgiving week); national shares do not describe any particular state's risk.
- The excess-death calculation assumes a ~500-death period total and applies published percentage shares; it is arithmetic on estimates, not a causal finding.

## Strongest counterargument
Thanksgiving travel itself may be safer per mile than an ordinary week: the holiday surge is mostly daytime, family, highway driving in good conditions, and the deadliest-holiday label counts bodies, not rates. A critic could fairly say the holiday's death toll is high because 81.8 million people are moving, not because any individual trip is especially dangerous, and that singling out Thanksgiving stigmatizes a family holiday while ordinary Saturdays kill quietly all year. The reply is in the share, not the count: alcohol's share of deaths rises from 29% to 35% during the holiday week, which means something about the week itself, not just its volume, is changing the risk.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Do not drive Wednesday night or Saturday night of Thanksgiving week; if you must travel Wednesday, drive in daylight.
- Set the sober ride before the first drink, not after the last one; the plan that matters is Wednesday's and Saturday's, not just Thursday's.
- If you are hosting: the 21-34 crowd home for the holiday is the highest-risk group; offer the couch, not the keys.
- Buckle up on every trip, including the two-mile drive to a childhood friend's house; 111 Thanksgiving lives a year ride on that click.
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before the holiday drive; a recall fix is free and faster than a tow on I-95.
