## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** NHTSA opened preliminary evaluation PE26005 on July 6, 2026 into the Doona + Car Seat & Stroller — the $550 infant seat that converts to a stroller in one click — after reports that the carry handle can separate from the seat. The population is an estimated 735,734 units (2020–2024 model years). The probe is still open with no finding, no recall, and zero incidents in the opening resume's failure table. A second, distinct failure mode sits in the same file: NHTSA consumer complaint 11706176 (Cape Coral, FL, incident Dec 19, 2025) says the wheel-deployment mechanism tightened the harness around an infant by itself during conversion from car mode to stroller mode, leaving visible bruising. The calendar hook: on December 5, 2026, nine days from now, FMVSS 213b becomes mandatory for newly manufactured child restraints and FMVSS 213a side-impact compliance arrives — the investigated Doona + was certified under the old FMVSS 213, while the new Doona X (launched July 2026, $850) is certified to 213a and 213b.

**Challenge — is this genuinely new after 1,588 articles?** Yes, four ways. (1) Zero prior coverage: full-content grep for "doona" across stories/ and drafts/ returns nothing; queue slug grep for doona/fit360-adjacent terms returns only #1572 (Chicco Fit360, a different seat and a different defect). (2) The handle is the product. The Doona's entire pitch is one part doing three safety-critical jobs: it is the carry handle, the stroller push handle, and — locked in position C against the vehicle seatback — the anti-rebound bar in a crash. A handle problem is not a trim problem. (3) The jurisdictional wrinkle: ODI told CPSC about the probe at opening because the product is of shared interest — a car seat is NHTSA's, a stroller is CPSC's, and the Doona is both at once. Neither regulator owns the whole product. (4) Novel computations: the per-model-year population (~147,000), the 143 days the probe has sat open with no update, and the 213b timing (investigated seat certified to the outgoing standard; replacement certified to the incoming one).

## Verified facts

### Primary: NHTSA ODI Resume, PE26005, opened July 6, 2026
https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2026/INOA-PE26005-14415.pdf
1. **Investigation PE26005, opened 07/06/2026**, prompted by field report review. Investigator Taylor Collins, reviewer Bruce York, approver Tanya Topka.
2. **Manufacturer: SP Distribution, LLC. Products: 2020–2024 Doona + Car Seat & Stroller. Population: 735,734 (estimated).**
3. **Problem: "The carry handle may separate from the child seat and stroller."** ODI summary: "ODI is aware of reports alleging unexpected handle detachment on 2020-2024 Doona + Car Seat & Stroller model car seats. If the handle unexpectedly detaches from the car seat, the car seat could be dropped when placing it in or out of the vehicle, potentially injuring the child."
4. **Failure report summary in the resume: all zeros.** 0 incidents, 0 crashes/fires, 0 injury incidents, 0 injuries, 0 fatality incidents, 0 fatalities. The probe rests on field reports of alleged detachment, not on counted ODI incidents.
5. **"ODI will inform the Consumer Product Safety Commission of this investigation at the time of opening as this product is of shared interest."** The Doona is a car seat (NHTSA) and a stroller (CPSC) in one product.
6. **Purpose: "assess the scope, root cause, and risk associated with handle detachments in 2020-2024 Doona Car Seats."** A preliminary evaluation is an investigation, not a recall and not a defect finding.
7. **Still open:** OEMDTC's NHTSA investigations mirror (updated Nov 17, 2026) lists PE26005 as open, latest activity its opening day.

### Cross-check: NHTSA consumer complaint 11706176 (Doona, Cape Coral, FL)
https://oemdtc.com/complaint/11706176/ (mirror of the NHTSA complaint file; page updated 2026-11-23)
8. **Incident Dec 19, 2025; received by NHTSA Dec 19, 2025.** While converting the seat from vehicle mode to stroller mode, "the wheel deployment mechanism caused the harness straps to tighten automatically and forcefully around the infant." The caregiver activated the wheel-deployment handle after removing the seat from the vehicle; harness tension "increased suddenly without any adjustment being made to the harness." The infant screamed; "visible bruising was later observed consistent with excessive strap compression." 1 injury, 0 deaths, medical attention required, medical evaluation being pursued.
9. **The caveat, in the complainant's own words:** the seat "was received second-hand. It is suspected that the soft goods (padding) may have been removed for cleaning by a prior owner and reinstalled incorrectly, allowing the harness webbing to be routed over a moving component of the wheel deployment mechanism rather than under it." The complainant's argument: removing and reinstalling padding "is a foreseeable and expected user action" and the design "should not allow a foreseeable reassembly error to result in automatic tightening of the harness during normal operation." The wheel-deployment mechanism "should be mechanically isolated from the harness system."
10. **This is a different failure mode from the PE.** The probe is about the carry handle separating; the complaint is about the wheel-deployment mechanism tightening the harness. Same product, same signature moving parts, different alleged defect. Do not merge them into one claim.

### Cross-check: FMVSS 213b / 213a compliance date, December 5, 2026
Federal Register Vol. 91, No. 173, Sept 9, 2026 (Docket NHTSA-2025-0046): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-09/html/2026-18380.htm
11. **FMVSS 213b becomes mandatory for child restraints manufactured on or after December 5, 2026.** FMVSS 213a side-impact compliance date is also December 5, 2026 (delayed from June 30, 2025). FMVSS 213 is replaced by 213b for newly manufactured restraints on that date.
12. **New labeling rules:** harnessed seats may not be recommended for forward-facing use by children under 26.5 lbs; booster seats may not be recommended under 40 lbs; an infant seat recommended for children over 30 lbs must pass testing with the larger three-year-old-size dummy.
13. **Site cross-reference:** the draft `child-seat-side-impact-12-year-delay-research.md` already covers the 14-year MAP-21-to-213a delay and its ~52 children/year side-impact body count. Cite it as background; do not re-derive it.

### Cross-check: Doona product facts
14. **Doona + is a rear-facing infant seat for 4–35 lbs, up to 32 in, FAA accepted for aircraft use, converts car seat to stroller without a separate frame.** Certified to FMVSS 213, ASTM F833, ASTM F2050 (Velivolo review, citing the manual). The handlebar in position C (closest to the vehicle seatback) acts as an anti-rebound bar in vehicle mode.
15. **Doona X launched July 2026.** Babylist's July 9, 2026 launch post lists the X at $850 vs $550 for the original; the X adds an adjustable handlebar, suspension, a no-rethread harness, a larger canopy, and three stroller recline positions. BestCarSeatHub's review of the X manual says it is certified to FMVSS 213a and 213b. The PE covers 2020–2024 Doona + only; it does not cover the Doona X.
16. **Second 2026 child-seat probe for context:** PE26009, opened Nov 13, 2026, over magnetic chest clips breaking on 2023–2024 Chicco Fit360 seats (5 reports). Covered by #1572; one line of context here only.

## Computations (original to this piece)
- **Per-model-year population:** 735,734 units / 5 model years (2020–2024) = ~147,000 seats per model year. That is roughly one Doona + for every 25 babies born in the US in a given year (about 3.6M births/year), a remarkable penetration for a $550 seat.
- **Probe age:** opened July 6, 2026; 143 days open as of Nov 26, 2026, with no posted update since opening day. A preliminary evaluation with no movement in nearly five months is a probe in limbo, not a probe racing toward a recall.
- **The zero-incident paradox:** the resume's failure table lists 0 incidents, 0 injuries, 0 deaths — and ODI opened the investigation anyway, on field reports. The honest reading: NHTSA is investigating a failure it has not yet counted, because the failure mode (dropping a baby) is the kind you investigate before the count exists.
- **One part, three jobs:** the handle is the carry handle, the stroller push handle, and the anti-rebound bar. No other infant seat asks one component to be the carrying device, the steering device, and a crash-structure element. That is the design story underneath the probe.
- **The $300 standard gap:** the investigated Doona + ($550, FMVSS 213) and the replacement Doona X ($850, FMVSS 213a/213b) differ by $300 and by a full generation of federal standards. Parents shopping this week are choosing between the investigated seat and its successor at the exact moment the rulebook flips.
- **Two regulators, one product:** the Doona is the only infant seat that is simultaneously a car seat and a stroller, which is why ODI had to notify CPSC. The car-seat half and the stroller half answer to different agencies with different recall powers.

## Strongest counterargument
There is no finding here, and the file is thin. The PE resume counts zero incidents, zero injuries, zero deaths. The probe was prompted by field reports, not by a crash. The harness complaint — the only injury in the record — involves a second-hand seat whose padding may have been reinstalled wrong by a prior owner; that is arguably owner error, not a design defect, and the complaint is a single self-reported VOQ, unverified by NHTSA. Doona has no recall, the new Doona X is already certified to the incoming standards, and the 735,734 population is an estimate that includes seats long expired, crashed, or retired. A skeptic can fairly say this is a routine preliminary evaluation doing what preliminary evaluations do: asking questions. All true — which is why the article calls it a probe, not a danger, and why the actionable advice is inspection and registration, not disposal.

## Limitations
- PE26005 is a preliminary evaluation: no defect finding, no recall, no remedy. It can close with no action.
- The resume discloses no count of handle-detachment reports, only that ODI is "aware of reports alleging" them. No failure rate can be computed.
- Complaint 11706176 is a single unverified consumer report; the seat was second-hand and the suspected cause is owner misrouting, not a confirmed defect.
- Doona X certification to 213a/213b comes from a review site reading the manual, not from NHTSA.
- Prices ($550 / $850) are from retailer and review sources as of July–November 2026 and may have changed.

## Rejected claims (do not use)
- **"Doona recalled 104,000 seats in 2022 over a chest clip."** One low-quality source (accidentsupport.org) asserts this. TeachToddler's Oct 24, 2026 check of NHTSA records found no Doona recall at all, and Baby Bargains says the brand has had no safety recalls. Unverified and contradicted; dropped.
- **"NHTSA counted X handle detachments."** The resume lists 0 incidents in its table and gives no report count. Do not invent a numerator.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Owners of a 2020–2024 Doona +: before every carry, tug the handle and confirm it locks in position. The manual's handle check takes five seconds and the alleged failure is a handle that lets go without warning.
- After washing or removing the padding, re-check the harness routing against the manual diagrams. The Dec 2025 complaint says misrouted webbing over a moving part is what let the wheel mechanism tighten the harness. If the straps feel different after reassembly, stop and re-route.
- Register the seat at doona.com/register and check nhtsa.gov/recalls by model and manufacture date. The probe can become a recall; registration is how the notice reaches you.
- Buying used: get the model and serial numbers and check NHTSA before paying. The one injury in the file was a second-hand seat.
- Shopping now: ask which standard the box claims. Seats manufactured on or after Dec 5, 2026 must meet FMVSS 213b and 213a; the Doona X is certified to both, the Doona + to the outgoing FMVSS 213.
- Report a handle issue to NHTSA (a VOQ). Field reports are literally what opened this probe.
