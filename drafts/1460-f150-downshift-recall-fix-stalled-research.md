# Research — #1460: Ford's F-150 Downshift Recall Fix Has Stalled

**Slug:** `1460-f150-downshift-recall-fix-stalled`
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (investigation beat)
**Date:** November 10, 2026
**Status:** RESEARCH complete — proceed to DRAFT

## The story in one line
Ford recalled 1.39 million F-150s in April 2026 because their transmissions can slam from sixth gear to second at highway speed and lock the rear wheels. Seven months later, owners say dealers have stopped applying the fix, Ford's own recall page still claims the remedy is available, and NHTSA's investigation file is still open. The fix exists. It just isn't reaching the trucks.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. 1.39M trucks, a safety recall remedy reportedly paused, fresh reporting Nov 10, 2026 (today). A recall that can't get fixed is a distinct story from a recall.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. Not "Ford recalls trucks" (April news) but "the recall fix has stalled and nobody will say why" — plus NHTSA found a second failure mode (reverse-uphill rollaway) the recall paperwork doesn't address at all.
- **Not a duplicate?** Verified: no drafts or queue entries for 26S28, 26V237, downshift, or lead frame. The site's June 17 piece ("Ford Recalled Your Car, Fixed It, Filed the Paperwork. The Fix Was Fake.", 26V369/26V378 re-recalls) is a different campaign and serves as context: Ford has a documented pattern of recall remedies failing to land.

## Verified facts (primary sources)

### The recall
- **NHTSA campaign 26V237, Ford number 26S28.** Ford's Part 573 defect report filed April 14, 2026.
- **1,392,935 trucks.** 2015–2017 Ford F-150 with the 6R80 six-speed automatic, built March 12, 2014 – August 18, 2017. (2017s with the 10R80 ten-speed are NOT included.)
- **Defect:** the transmission lead frame, a molded wiring carrier mounted on the valve body, degrades from thermal cycling and vibration. Electrical connections fail, the transmission range sensor (TRS) signal drops, and the powertrain computer reads a garbage gear position and commands a downshift.
- **Worst case (Ford's own words, via NHTSA):** sixth gear to second while traveling 35–64 mph.
- **Remedy:** free powertrain-control-module software recalibration at a dealer. Lead frame replacement only for trucks that have already stored the matching diagnostic trouble codes, under an extended warranty. Ford estimates ~1% of the recalled population actually carries the defect, but every owner in the range must check because trucks weren't built in VIN order.
- VINs searchable from April 15, 2026. Remedy letters mailed mid-July 2026.

### The investigation
- **PE25-002** opened March 21, 2025 (after NHTSA sent Ford a batch of owner complaints Oct 30, 2024). Upgraded to **EA26-001** January 30, 2026. Ford's field review committee approved the recall April 7, 2026.
- **329 owner complaints (VOQs)** in NHTSA's pool; ~60% interviewed. **43% reported at least one rear-wheel lockup** — tires locked, screeched, or skidded during the downshift.
- **114 of 329** had already replaced a lead frame or valve body before the recall; 80 confirmed by submitted repair invoices.
- Ford's counts as of April 2, 2026: **444 warranty claims, 121 field reports, 105 customer-service reports, 316 NHTSA owner complaints, 2 injuries, 1 crash** potentially related. 891 vehicles behind the reports.
- **Second failure mode:** NHTSA's test center found that if the range-sensor signal drops while the truck reverses up an incline, the transmission can go to neutral and the truck can roll forward. The recall paperwork for 26S28 addresses unintended downshifts, not this reverse-rollaway scenario.
- **EA26-001 still open** as of October 2026. NHTSA keeps cases open while a remedy reaches owners, to see whether it works.
- Ford's chronology lists related reports back to **April 24, 2015** — eleven years from first report to recall.

### The stall (the news hook)
- **Oct 29, 2026 — The Autowire:** "F-150 Downshift Recall Stalls." F-150 Forum users report dealers saying Ford put recall 26S28 on hold. Ford's public recall page still said a remedy became available July 9, with no restart date.
- **Nov 10, 2026 — Medium/Aerospace and Mechanical Insider:** owners say dealers have stopped applying the transmission software update. Atlanta News First reported Ford paused the update because of a parts issue; after the station contacted Ford about one affected owner whose truck was at a dealership, Ford agreed to provide that owner a loaner until the update could be completed.
- **Neither Ford nor NHTSA has published confirmation** that recall 26S28 is suspended nationwide. Geographic scope and expected duration are unresolved. Ford's public page reportedly continued to show the remedy as available.

## Original contribution (not just synthesis)
1. **The 1% math:** Ford's own estimate says ~1% of 1,392,935 trucks carry the defect — roughly 13,900 trucks. But the software remedy is the only protection for all 1.39M, and the hardware fix goes only to trucks that already logged fault codes. A paused software rollout leaves the full population exposed to a defect Ford says affects ~14,000 of them, with no way for an owner to know which side of that line their truck is on.
2. **The open-file paradox:** EA26-001 stays open precisely so NHTSA can watch whether the remedy works. A remedy that stops moving is the exact condition the open file exists to catch — and the file has no published update on the stall.
3. **The unaddressed second defect:** the reverse-uphill rollaway NHTSA's own test center found is not in the recall paperwork at all. Even a perfectly executed 26S28 leaves that failure mode untouched.
4. **Pattern, not accident:** this is Ford's second recall-remedy failure in six months on this site's record (June 17: 26V369/26V378, dealer tool silently failing to install updates). Two different mechanisms, same outcome: owners told their truck is fixed, driving trucks that aren't.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The pause may be a narrow logistics hiccup, not a suspension. No owner report has been independently verified by a regulator; forum posts and a single local-TV report are thin evidence for a nationwide claim. Ford has not confirmed anything, and NHTSA has not confirmed anything — the absence of a confirmation cuts both ways, and a parts shortage that delays a software update by weeks is a supply-chain story, not a safety scandal. Only ~1% of the trucks carry the defect, so the overwhelming majority of owners face no real risk even while the rollout pauses. The software remedy, when it resumes, addresses the downshift; the reverse-rollaway scenario requires a specific combination (signal loss + reversing uphill) that may be vanishingly rare in practice. And NHTSA keeping EA26-001 open is routine procedure, not a signal of alarm.

## Limitations (honest accounting)
- Owner reports of the pause are unverified; Ford and NHTSA have published no confirmation of a suspension.
- Complaint counts are self-reported; NHTSA's 329-VOQ pool is a complaint sample, not a measured failure rate.
- The 1% defect estimate is Ford's, from its own population analysis, not independently verified.
- No FARS attribution: fatality data cannot tie deaths to this specific defect, so no death count is claimed for it.
- The reverse-rollaway finding comes from NHTSA test-center replication, not from field incidents.

## What readers should DO
- 2015–2017 F-150 with the 6R80 six-speed (check your door sticker/transmission code; 2017s with the 10-speed are exempt): run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. If 26V237/26S28 is open, call your dealer and ask specifically whether the PCM recalibration is available this week — don't accept "the recall is open" as an answer, ask whether the update can actually be installed.
- If your truck has already downshifted on its own, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov (VOQ) — the 329 complaints are what forced this recall.
- If you tow or reverse up inclines, know the recall does not cover the reverse-rollaway mode: use the parking brake before shifting out of reverse on a slope.
