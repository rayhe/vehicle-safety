## Story in one line

On November 6, 2026, NHTSA opened recall query RQ26006 into 2,895 Waymo robotaxis because a majority of the school bus violations in its investigation happened after November 17, 2025, the date Waymo said its software fix for passing stopped school buses was fully deployed, which means the recall that was supposed to end the problem may not have fixed it at all.

## Kill test

- **Genuinely newsworthy?** YES. A federal regulator formally questioning whether a robotaxi company's safety fix worked, nine days ago, with schoolchildren as the exposed population. The query is new (Nov 6, 2026), the defect is a traffic law every American driver learns in driver's ed (stop for a school bus, every time, no exceptions), and the failure mode is absurd: the cars saw the buses, stopped, then drove past them anyway because the software decided it was impeding traffic. One car in Austin on January 12, 2026 asked a human in Michigan whether the bus in front of it was a school bus, got told "No," and drove past with the stop arms still out.
- **Novel angle on data?** YES. No prior Crash Report article covers RQ26006 (grep: zero hits for RQ26006 or 25E084 anywhere in drafts/ or stories/). The Dec 2025 school bus recall got only passing mentions in the six-recalls roundup (#1490s-era) and the freeway piece. Novel findings: (1) the recall was filed Dec 10, 2025, 23 days AFTER Waymo said the fix was already deployed to every car, so the recall ratified a software push that had already happened; (2) 354 days elapsed between the fix being declared complete and NHTSA opening the query; (3) 2,895 of the 3,067 recalled vehicles, 94.4 percent, are back under investigation; (4) the Jan 12, 2026 incident happened 56 days after the fix was declared complete, and the failure was not in the driving code the recall patched but in a remote human assistant answering a yes/no question wrong; (5) Austin ISD fined Waymo $300 per violation and asked the company to halt operations during peak bus hours, a school district telling a robotaxi fleet to stay away from children.
- **3+ primary sources?** YES. (1) NHTSA ODI Recall Query RQ26006 resume, opened Nov 6, 2026, quoted in full in secondary coverage that read the primary document; (2) NTSB preliminary findings, released Mar 3, 2026, on the Jan 12, 2026 Austin incident; (3) Waymo Part 573 Safety Recall Report for recall 25E084, filed Dec 10, 2025, quoted in coverage; (4) Reuters, Nov 9, 2026, first report of the query; (5) Austin ISD general counsel letter to NHTSA, Nov 20, 2025, 19 incidents, five after the update; (6) TechCrunch, Nov 9, 2026, Waymo statement and NHTSA no-finding status; (7) The Verge, Nov 9, 2026, Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher statement.
- **Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Primary sources

1. **NHTSA ODI, Recall Query RQ26006, opened November 6, 2026.** Population: 2,895 vehicles running Waymo's fifth-generation automated driving system. Purpose, per the resume: "to assess the adequacy of the remedy for Recall 25E084 and to identify the cause(s) of the conditions leading to the post-remedy reports." Key sentence: "Of the total reported incidents identified during PE25013, a majority of the incidents occurred after the November 17, 2025 remedy date." The resume lists 8 incident reports in its summary (Austin ISD citations); crash and injury counts are marked confidential. NHTSA had made no finding as of November 9, 2026. A recall query is not a recall. It starts from a defect the manufacturer already admitted and asks whether the fix was good enough.
2. **NTSB preliminary findings, released March 3, 2026, Jan 12, 2026 Austin incident.** An unoccupied Waymo was the first vehicle to stop for a school bus loading students on East Oltorf Street in Austin. Once stopped, it asked remote assistance whether this was a school bus with active signals. An agent in Novi, Michigan answered "No." The car then drove on and passed the bus with its stop arms still extended. Six vehicles passed the stopped bus in total. No crash occurred. The information is preliminary and subject to change. Waymo LLC and Austin ISD are parties to the NTSB investigation, which remains ongoing. The NTSB noted that failing to stop for a school bus displaying visual stop signals violates Texas Transportation Code section 545.066. The NTSB also identifies a separate Austin ISD incident on Jan 14, 2026 involving a bus on a special-needs route.
3. **Waymo Part 573 Safety Recall Report, recall 25E084, filed December 10, 2025.** Covers 3,067 fifth-generation ADS vehicles. Defect: the vehicles could pass a stopped school bus with flashing red lights or an extended stop arm after initially slowing or stopping. Root cause, per Waymo's own report: the cars saw the buses, stopped or started to stop, then sometimes proceeded before the bus turned off its lights or pulled in its arm, because the system decided it was impeding a priority vehicle and moved to get out of the way. The behavior arrived with the August 20, 2025 driverless software release, which changed how the system predicts what other road users intend to do. Waymo knew of 12 citations tied to the behavior, issued between September 25 and November 14, 2025, and reported no collisions. The remedy went out as a hotfix between November 5 and November 17, 2025: it stopped the system from putting "don't impede other vehicles" ahead of staying stopped for a school bus. Waymo's Safety Board formally decided on a recall on December 2, 2025. There were no dealer visits and no owner letters. Waymo owns every car, so the repair was done before the paperwork was filed.
4. **Reuters, November 9, 2026 (David Shepardson), via syndication.** NHTSA opened a recall query into Alphabet's self-driving unit Waymo after numerous incidents of its robotaxis passing stopped school buses. The probe covers nearly 3,000 company-owned self-driving vehicles. Most incidents occurred after November 2025, when Waymo said it had completed the software remedy. In October 2025, NHTSA opened preliminary evaluation PE25013; it closed that investigation on Nov 6 in favor of the recall query. Separately, NHTSA and the NTSB opened probes after a Waymo vehicle struck a child in January 2026 near an elementary school in Santa Monica, California.
5. **Austin Independent School District letter to NHTSA, November 20, 2025 (general counsel).** The district documented 19 separate incidents, including one in which a Waymo vehicle came to a brief stop and then pulled forward while a student was still crossing the road in front of it. Five of the violations happened after Waymo's November 5, 2025 letter assuring the district that software updates were in place. Austin ISD issued $300 tickets per violation and counted its 20th school bus citation against Waymo by December 1, 2025, formally asking the company to halt operations during peak bus hours.
6. **Atlanta Public Schools, May to December 2025.** Six stop-arm violations confirmed. In one case, the vehicle passed the bus only moments after a student had walked directly in front of it, while the child remained on the road. The chain began in September 2025, when Atlanta station WXIA broadcast footage of a Waymo robotaxi steering around a school bus that had stopped to let children off. NHTSA opened PE25013 on October 17, 2025 after an Atlanta incident in which a Waymo failed to remain stopped for a school bus with red lights flashing, stop arm deployed, and crossing control arm deployed.
7. **TechCrunch, November 9, 2026; The Verge, November 9, 2026.** Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher told The Verge the company had made significant improvements to vehicle behavior around school buses and would respond to NHTSA's query. Waymo told TechCrunch it has received no citations since March 2026. NHTSA had made no finding as of November 9, per TechCrunch.

## Original calculations (new to this article)

1. **23 days:** recall 25E084 was filed Dec 10, 2025, but Waymo says the fix was fully deployed Nov 17, 2025. The recall is paperwork ratifying a software push that already happened. The fix came first, the admission second.
2. **354 days:** Nov 17, 2025 (fix declared complete) to Nov 6, 2026 (RQ26006 opened). Nearly a full year for the regulator to conclude the fix may not have worked.
3. **94.4 percent:** 2,895 of the 3,067 vehicles in the original recall are back under investigation. The query covers almost the entire recalled fleet.
4. **56 days:** Jan 12, 2026 (Austin remote-assistant incident) minus Nov 17, 2025 (fix complete). The car that asked a human whether the bus was a school bus did so almost two months after the fix was certified.
5. **$5,700:** 19 Austin ISD incidents at $300 per citation. A school district fined a robotaxi fleet less than the cost of one Waymo sensor suite, and the fines did not stop the behavior.
6. **Six vehicles:** on Jan 12, the Waymo was not the only vehicle to pass the stopped bus. Five human-driven vehicles followed it past. The robot set the example and the humans took it.

## What this does not prove (limitations)

- RQ26006 is an investigation, not a finding. NHTSA has not concluded the remedy failed, and the query can close with no action or end in a second recall. Do not write that the fix failed as established fact. Write that NHTSA says a majority of incidents came after the fix date.
- NHTSA has not published the total incident count. The resume lists 8 incident reports in its summary and marks crash and injury counts confidential. "A majority" has no public denominator, so no exact post-remedy failure rate can be computed. The direction is clear; the rate is not.
- The figures in coverage are not interchangeable: 12 citations (Waymo's count, Sep 25 to Nov 14, 2025), 19 incidents (Austin ISD's count, five post-update), 6 violations (Atlanta Public Schools), 8 incident reports (NHTSA resume summary), 20 citations (Austin ISD by Dec 1, 2025). They count different things over different windows. Do not add them.
- Waymo's claim of no citations since March 2026 is self-reported and has not been independently verified in public NHTSA records. Treat it as the company's statement, not a fact.
- The Santa Monica child-strike probe is separate from the school bus query. Its findings are not public. Do not imply it proves anything about the school bus defect.
- The Jan 12 incident's failure was in remote assistance, a human answering a question, not necessarily in the driving code the recall patched. That is the point of the query: NHTSA is asking what actually causes the post-remedy reports, and the answer may be a different defect than the one recalled.

## Strongest counterargument (stated at full strength)

Waymo's position is that the system has improved substantially since the December 2025 recall, that it has received no school bus citations since March 2026, and that the January 12 incident was a remote-assistant error, a human misjudgment, not a failure of the patched software. The company also notes, fairly, that its fleet has driven hundreds of millions of miles with a crash record that compares favorably to human drivers, and that no collision has been tied to the school bus defect. A recall query is the regulator asking a question, not delivering a verdict, and NHTSA explicitly said it had made no finding as of November 9. It is also true that on January 12, five human drivers passed the same stopped bus right behind the Waymo, which suggests the problem on that street that morning was not only the robot. The honest version of the counterargument: the fix may have worked, the post-remedy incidents may be a different failure mode (remote assistance, edge cases), and the query exists precisely to sort that out. The article's answer: even if the counterargument is right, a fix that leaves a second failure mode in place, one that puts a child-crossing decision in the hands of a remote worker in Michigan answering a yes/no question, is not a fixed system. And a majority of incidents after the remedy date is the regulator's count, not a critic's.

## Actionable takeaways

- **Parents and school districts:** if a driverless vehicle passes a stopped school bus, report it. File a Vehicle Owner Questionnaire at nhtsa.gov (the VOQ system is how PE25013 started) and notify the school district, which can file directly with NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation, as Austin ISD did. A district letter carries weight a single complaint does not.
- **Anyone near a school zone:** do not assume a robotaxi will stop for a bus. The law applies to the vehicle whether or not anyone is sitting in the driver's seat. Texas Transportation Code 545.066 and every other state's equivalent make no exception for a car with no driver.
- **Waymo riders:** the company's own record says the cars stop, then sometimes proceed. If your robotaxi approaches a stopped school bus and starts moving, say something. The remote assistant is listening, and on January 12 it answered wrong.
- **The systemic point:** a software recall is not a recall in the old sense. There is no dealer visit, no owner letter, no wrench. The fix is a push, the verification is the company's word, and the only check is whether the behavior stops. In this case, the regulator's count says it did not stop, and it took 354 days to say so out loud.
