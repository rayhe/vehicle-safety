# Research: #1154 — Truck Deaths Fell 2.5% in 2024. Injuries Rose 5%.

## Angle
NHTSA's finalized 2024 large-truck data (DOT HS 813 816, June 2026) tells two opposite stories. Deaths in truck-involved crashes fell to 5,340 (-2.5% vs 2023, down from a 2022 high of ~5,959). But estimated injuries ROSE 5% to 161,201 — and the divergence is sharpest for the people who matter most to this site's readers: occupants of OTHER vehicles. Their deaths fell 90 (-2.3%); their injuries jumped 8,809 (+8.2%). The crash that kills you is getting rarer. The crash that hospitalizes you is not.

## Verified numbers (primary: NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts "Large Trucks: 2024 Data", DOT HS 813 816, June 2026; https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/91904/dot_91904_DS1.pdf)

Killed 2024 vs 2023:
- Total: 5,340 vs 5,478 (-2.5%); 2022 was 5,959/5,969 (Table 2 total 5,969)
- Large-truck occupants: 920 vs 962 (-4.4%)
  - single-vehicle crashes: 560 vs 595 (-35, -5.9%)
  - multivehicle crashes: 360 vs 367 (-7, -1.9%)
- Occupants of other vehicles: 3,753 vs 3,843 (-90, -2.3%) = 70% of all truck-crash deaths
- Nonoccupants: 667 vs 673 (-6, -0.9%)

Injured (CRSS estimates) 2024 vs 2023:
- Total: 161,201 vs 153,486 (+5.0%)
- Occupants of other vehicles: 116,475 vs 107,666 (+8,809, +8.2%) = 72% of all truck-crash injuries
- Large-truck occupants: 41,454 vs 41,738 (-284, -0.7%)
  - single-vehicle: 14,652 vs 13,137 (+1,515, +11.5%)
  - multivehicle: 26,802 vs 28,601 (-1,799, -6.3%)
- Nonoccupants: 3,273 vs 4,082 (-809, -20%)

Original calculations (mine, from Table 2):
- Injuries per death (all): 2024: 161,201/5,340 = 30.2; 2023: 153,486/5,478 = 28.0 → +7.7%
- Injuries per death (other-vehicle occupants): 2024: 116,475/3,753 = 31.0; 2023: 107,666/3,843 = 28.0 → +10.8%

Medium-truck surge (Table 1, my cross-tab):
- Medium trucks (10,001–26,000 lb GVWR) share of trucks in FATAL crashes: 29% (1,540) → 31% (1,618)
- Share of trucks in INJURY crashes: 39% (44,540) → 44% (53,603) — +9,063 trucks in one year
- Share of trucks in PDO crashes: 39% (160,651) → 41% (173,182)
- Heavy trucks (>26,000 lb) share of injury-crash trucks fell 53% → 47%
- Reading: the 18-wheeler's little brother (delivery vans, box trucks, straight trucks) is doing a growing share of the hurting.

Exposure paradox (Table 3):
- Registered large trucks: 14.89M → 16.08M (+8.0%) while large-truck VMT flat: 329.9B → 329.6B miles
- Fatal involvement rate per 100M VMT: 1.63 → 1.58 (-3%)
- Injury involvement rate per 100M VMT: 35 → 37 (+5.7%)
- Per mile, trucks are less likely to kill and MORE likely to injure.

Rear-end detail (Table 4): in two-vehicle fatal crashes involving a large truck, the initial impact point on the truck was the REAR in 22.1% of cases (21.2% = other vehicle's front into truck's rear). Cars running into the back of trucks is the single largest impact-point category after frontal-frontal (30.7%).

## Limitations (for the article)
- Injury figures are CRSS estimates from a national sample, NOT counts — sampling error applies; FARS deaths are census counts.
- 2024 data is the Annual Report File (ARF), subject to small revisions in the final file.
- Cannot prove causation: the divergence could reflect more crashes at lower severity, reporting changes, or safety tech (AEB/ESC) shifting fatal crashes into injury crashes. State the hypotheses, pick none as proven.
- 2020+ vehicle-classification change doesn't affect the 2023→2024 comparison.

## Strongest counterargument (for the article)
More injuries with fewer deaths could simply mean MORE crashes happening at survivable speeds — i.e., the roads got busier, not more violent. Registered trucks rose 8% while VMT stayed flat, meaning more trucks driving fewer miles each (urban delivery growth), and urban crashes injure more than they kill. The "more violent" reading is not the only one; the boring reading (exposure mix shift toward cities) fits the medium-truck surge too.

## Actionable takeaways (hard gate)
- 72% of truck-crash injuries are people in OTHER vehicles (116,475). Leave 4+ seconds behind trucks; the crash you survive is increasingly the one you're in.
- Don't cut in front of a truck and brake: in 22.1% of two-vehicle fatal truck crashes, the truck was struck in the rear — cars rear-ending trucks.
- Fleet buyers: IIHS's Oct 8 ratings show which cargo vans actually brake for motorcycles/pedestrians — check before buying work vehicles.
- Check VIN recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls (standard).
