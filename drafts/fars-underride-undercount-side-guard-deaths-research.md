# Research: FARS Underride Undercount → Side Guard Deaths

## Angle
NHTSA's own crash database (FARS) systematically undercounts truck underride deaths. The agency then uses that undercounted number in cost-benefit analysis to conclude side guards aren't worth mandating. IIHS says the real number is 10× higher — which flips the cost-benefit math. The data error is the policy error.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Hein (2023) — FARS side underride crash reporting accuracy
- **Source:** Eric Hein, "An Evaluation of NHTSA Side Underride Crash Reporting Accuracy in FARS Case Listings" (May 2023)
- **URL:** https://annaleahmary.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Hein-ANPRM-Enclosure-5-Inaccurate-FARS-Reporting-side-underride-records.pdf
- **Key findings:**
  - 40 independently verified fatal side underride crashes examined
  - 39 of 40 found in FARS — but 16 of 39 (41%) coded as "No Underride or Override Noted"
  - 1 crash with 3 fatalities entirely missing from FARS
  - Total: 17 of 40 crashes (43%) and 28 of 61 fatalities (46%) inaccurately coded or missing

### 2. IIHS — Brumbelow (2023) cost-benefit rebuttal
- **Source:** Matthew Brumbelow, IIHS Senior Research Engineer, regulatory comment on NHTSA ANPRM
- **URL:** https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/nhtsa-study-underestimates-benefits-of-side-underride-guards-for-trucks
- **Key findings:**
  - NHTSA estimated side guards prevent 17.2 fatalities/year
  - IIHS alternative methodology: 159-217 fatalities/year preventable
  - NHTSA excluded multi-vehicle crashes, speeds >40 mph, used speed limits instead of EDR data
  - 10× underestimate of benefits

### 3. NHTSA ANPRM — Side Underride Guards (2022-2023)
- **Source:** NHTSA, Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, RIN 2127-AM54
- **URL:** https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/anprm-side-underride-guards
- **Key findings:**
  - Cost: $973M-$1.2B to equip all new trailers
  - Benefits: Only 17.2 lives saved, 69 serious injuries prevented per year
  - Cost per equivalent life saved: $73.5M-$103.7M
  - NHTSA threshold for mandating: ~$12.5M per life
  - Conclusion: Not economically feasible

### 4. ProPublica/FRONTLINE investigation (2023)
- **Source:** ProPublica, "How Regulators Failed to Act to Prevent Underride Crashes"
- **URL:** https://www.propublica.org/article/underride-crashes-nhtsa-dot-iihs-safety-cars-trucks
- **Key findings:**
  - Only 17 states have a police report field for underride
  - GAO (2019) found underride fatalities "likely underreported"
  - ProPublica compared two NHTSA databases: 27 fatal underride crashes in detailed DB, only 3 coded as underrides in FARS (89% miss)
  - Joshua Brown Tesla Autopilot side underride crash (2016) — coded "no underride noted" in FARS

### 5. Stop Underrides Act 2.0 (2026)
- **Source:** S. 3775 / H.R. 7354, introduced Feb 4, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.freightwaves.com/news/new-legislation-would-require-crash-protection-on-new-trucks
- **Key findings:**
  - 4th time introduced
  - Requires side underride guards on new trucks (up to 40 mph impact)
  - Includes provision to correct misclassified underride crashes in FARS
  - Requires law enforcement training on identifying underride incidents
  - Bipartisan Senate sponsors (Gillibrand, Luján) + House (Cohen, DeSaulnier, Ross)

### 6. FreightWaves analysis (2026)
- **Source:** FreightWaves, "A Billion-Dollar Fix for a Distraction Problem"
- **URL:** https://www.freightwaves.com/news/a-billion-dollar-fix-for-a-distraction-problem-disguised-as-an-underride-problem
- **Key findings:**
  - At least 300 underride deaths per year (cited by Rep. DeSaulnier)
  - Marianne Karth discovered Joshua Brown FARS miscoding
  - Owner-Operator Independent Drivers Association calls it "one of the costliest federal trucking mandates in history"
  - The 2.0 bill tacitly admits FARS data is "fundamentally unreliable" for underride analysis

## Original Contribution
The novel finding: NHTSA's cost-benefit threshold is $12.5M per life saved. At 17.2 lives (NHTSA's FARS-based number), the cost is $73.5-103.7M per life — 6-8× over threshold. At IIHS's 159-217 lives, the cost drops to $4.5-7.5M per life — UNDER the threshold. The database error doesn't just distort the count. It mathematically inverts the policy conclusion.

## Counterargument
The trucking industry's strongest case: even if FARS undercounts, ADAS technology (automatic emergency braking, lane-keeping) will reduce underride crashes independently of guard mandates. NHTSA itself noted this in its ANPRM (Question 5). If ADAS reduces the target population by 50%, even IIHS's higher number might not justify the mandate cost. However: ADAS adoption in the existing fleet is decades from saturation, and the people dying now won't benefit from future technology.

## Limitations
- FARS covers fatal crashes only; non-fatal underride injuries (severe TBI, paralysis) are uncounted in both FARS and this analysis
- The 40-crash Hein sample is small; the true miscoding rate across all FARS underride records could be higher or lower
- IIHS's 159-217 estimate uses different crash inclusion criteria than NHTSA; neither has been independently validated
- Side guards have variable effectiveness above 40 mph; the real-world speed distribution of fatal underrides is poorly characterized
