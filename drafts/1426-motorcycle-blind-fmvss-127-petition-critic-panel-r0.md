## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10

Lede lands in two sentences: the paradox (a test that brakes for everything except the likeliest fatality) and the news (Oct 29 filing). Structure is clean: filing, the rule, the test result, the arithmetic, the counterargument, the limitations, the actions. The 17x arithmetic is the article's spine and it is stated twice, once as a division and once as a ratio, which is once too often; the second occurrence could have been cut. The headline is long but it is the whole story in one line, which is what this site's headlines are for. Docked half a point for the repeated arithmetic and for length: the piece earns its words, but barely.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10

Unmistakably Vin Wreckage: "a rounding error with a rider on it," "Somewhere in Washington, a federal test is teaching cars to brake for everything except the vehicle most likely to die in front of them." No other byline on this site would write that lede. Banned phrases zero. Rhythm gate passes after the pre-panel fragment merges; the surviving fragments ("Two targets, a car and a person on foot, and that is the whole cast") read as deliberate pacing now rather than metronome ticks. One tell remains: three consecutive paragraphs open with a variation of "here is what X requires/does" ("Here is what the federal mandate actually requires," "Here is the honest version of the claim"). Not a banned phrase, but a repeated scaffold. Noted, not blocking.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

The piece advocates for riders without caricaturing regulators or automakers. NHTSA's rule is described as a real achievement with a real gap, not a conspiracy. The counterargument is given at full strength, including the left-turn statistic that undercuts the article's own thesis, and the incremental-benefit caveat is stated rather than buried. No victim is named, no crash is described in graphic detail, no rider is used as a prop. The 6,228 deaths are a count, not a spectacle. Clean.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.5/10

Pull stat "27x" is the shareable number, and the headline is a complete argument that survives being pasted without context. The Rivian 0-for-6 is the detail people will quote in replies. Weakness: no single pull-quote sentence under 15 words besides the pull stat itself; the lede's second sentence ("when the government writes the test for automatic emergency braking, include a motorcycle") is close but runs long. The docket deadline (Nov 25) gives it a timely hook for sharing this week. Good, not great.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10

FMVSS 127 described correctly: lead vehicle + pedestrian, test procedures, Sept 1, 2029 compliance, 360 lives / 24,000 injuries projection. Docket NHTSA-2026-1519 and the FMVSS 108 NPRM dates (signed Oct 22, FR Oct 26, 30-day comment) check out. The litigation/abeyance history is in the research file and correctly kept out of the article's claims. One caution, handled properly in the text: the Oct 29 filing's exact form is not specified in available reporting, and the article says so explicitly rather than inventing a docket number. The "about November 25" comment deadline is hedged with "about," which is the right call given the 30-day-from-publication arithmetic.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Original contribution is real and checkable: the test-protocol cross-tab (federal test devices = lead car + pedestrian dummy; the only motorcycle target in American testing is IIHS's voluntary V2V 2.0), plus the 17x arithmetic and the 0-for-6 Rivian result. Every number traces to a cited primary source: NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts DOT HS 813 824, the Federal Register final rule, the NHTSA NPRM, IIHS ratings. Limitations are explicit and honest, including the filing-form gap, the ARF revision caveat, and the commercial-vehicle scope of the Rivian result. The left-turn statistic rests on a secondary summary (tvbikerdad citing Traffic Injury Prevention) rather than the study itself; flagged in the references as a summary, which is honest but one step short of ideal. Docked half a point for that and for the MRF agenda being a year old (disclosed, but it means the "news" is the delivery, not the idea).

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10

Numbers are rendered as prose with the math shown: 28.00 / 1.05 = 27x, 6,228 / 360 = 17x, 3% of vehicles / 16% of deaths. No invented precision; "almost 27 times" is NHTSA's own phrasing. The pull stat carries the single most important number. Weakness: the article would be stronger with a small table (test device vs. test device) rather than burying the cross-tab in paragraph 3 prose; a reader skimming will miss that the federal test has exactly two targets. The FARS cross-tab opportunity (site's own dataset) is unused, but the article's data is NHTSA/IIHS primary data, which is the right call for a regulatory story. Good.
