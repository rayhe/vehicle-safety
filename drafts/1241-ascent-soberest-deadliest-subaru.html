Sobriety Report

The Soberest Drivers in America Die at Nearly Three Times the Rate of the Drunkest Hooligans

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
December 5, 2027

According to the toxicology reports. There are a lot of them. Subaru Ascent drivers are the most sober people on American roads: just 8.2% of drivers in fatal Ascent crashes showed any impairment.[1] Across our 307-model dataset, that is the lowest impairment rate on record, and the median sits at 20.3%. Nobody else is even close.

8.2%
Ascent drivers with any impairment in fatal crashes — lowest of all 307 models tracked

Here is the part the toxicology lab did not see coming. Per mile, the Ascent kills its occupants at 0.78 deaths per 100 million vehicle-miles. Compare that with the Subaru WRX, a turbocharged hooligan car with the highest impairment rate in the whole Subaru lineup at 23.4%, and a fatality rate of just 0.29.[1] America's soberest car. Its drunkest drivers. Somehow the drunkest drivers win.

Skeptics will point out the obvious, and they are not wrong. An Ascent seats eight; a WRX seats five. FARS counts occupant fatalities, so one loaded family SUV crashing hard can produce more deaths than a solo WRX driver finding a ditch. Kids are more fragile occupants. With the Ascent launching for model year 2019, the fleet estimates are noisier than a twenty-year nameplate. All fair. But the Forester also hauls families, and it sits at 0.26, a third of the Ascent. The Crosstrek is at 0.08, nearly a tenth. Family hauling explains some of this. It does not explain all of it.

Then there is the hardware story. For 2024 and 2025, the Ascent earned the IIHS Top Safety Pick+ award, the institute's highest honor.[2] NHTSA gave the 2025 model five stars overall. Both ratings share a flaw: the dummies are always alone, always sober, always belted, and never packed with three kids and a dog. Note that the Ascent does not carry a five-star rollover rating. It earned four.[5]

And in July 2026, NHTSA paperwork forced the recall of 384,407 Ascents, every model year from 2019 through 2026, because the axle weight rating on the certification label was wrong. A wrong label lets owners unknowingly overload the vehicle, which increases crash risk.[3] Read that again. Seven model years of the soberest car in America, and nobody knew for sure how much weight the rear axle was rated to carry.

Two things to do. One: if you own a 2019-2026 Ascent, enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and check for recall 26V436000.[3] That fix is a corrected sticker and it is free. Two: if you are shopping for a three-row SUV, ignore the trophies for a day. Crash-test stars describe a lab. Per-mile numbers describe your life. In the end, driver sobriety matters less than the math of the seats.
