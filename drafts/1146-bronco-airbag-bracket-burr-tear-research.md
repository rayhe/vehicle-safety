## Verified facts
- **26V580 / Ford 26S64:** 2,711 model-year 2026 Broncos, built June 27–July 26, 2026. Metal burr on the right-hand-side side air curtain (SAC) mounting bracket can tear the curtain fabric on deployment → faster deflation → increased injury risk in a crash.
- **26V581 / Ford 26S65:** 7 model-year 2026 Explorers; passenger-side active vent may not open as intended → bag may not deploy properly (stays too firm / improper deployment). Filed in the same batch.
- **Cause (26V580):** supplier production-layout change; a deburring step was skipped. Process miss, not a design flaw.
- **Detection:** no dashboard warning; owners only find out via VIN lookup or Ford's letter.
- **Timeline:** interim owner letters mailed September 15, 2026; remedy letters anticipated ~October 30, 2026; dealers increase bracket-to-airbag clearance free of charge.
- **Field data:** as of August 18, 2026, Ford was not aware of any warranty claims, field reports, customer reports, accidents, or injuries tied to 26V580.
- **Curtain physics (why a tear matters more here than on a frontal bag):** side curtains must stay inflated longer — side impacts and rollovers involve multiple contacts over a longer period, and the curtain exists to keep occupants from being ejected through side glass. A curtain that deflates early can cover the window at first impact and be gone by the second.
- **Scale context:** Road Ethos counts this as the 2026 Bronco's ninth recall (attributed claim).

## Original contribution (Crash Report cross-tabs)
1. **Opposite-mode pairing:** 26V580 (curtain deflates too fast) + 26V581 (passenger bag won't vent, too firm) filed in the same batch — Ford's airbags are failing in opposite directions at once. Same defect category (airbag deployment), opposite physics. Nobody led with the pairing.
2. **Process-vs-design distinction:** the defect is a skipped manufacturing step after a layout change, not an engineering error — which reframes the "ninth recall" count: the Bronco's problem is supplier process control, not vehicle design.
3. **Silent-defect quantification:** no dashboard warning exists, so the detection rate for affected owners depends entirely on letter open rates and VIN lookups — the recall's effectiveness is bounded by mail, not by diagnostics.

## Limitations
- No incident, injury, or warranty-claim data exists — the tear is a modeled failure (burr + deployment = tear risk), not an observed one. We don't know the actual tear probability per deployment.
- The supplier is unnamed in public filings; "skipped deburring step" comes from secondary reporting (The Autowire), not the Part 573.
- The "ninth recall" count is Road Ethos's tally, attributed, not independently verified against NHTSA records here.
- We don't know how many of the 2,711 vehicles actually carry a burr — the campaign covers the build window, not confirmed-burr units.

## Strongest counterargument
This is the safety system working as designed: Ford found the burr through its own process controls, self-reported before a single customer complaint, and the remedy is a free dealer visit. 2,711 is a small population; the failure requires an actual side impact or rollover to matter; and even a torn curtain still deploys — the debate is over deflation rate, not whether the bag shows up. Zero known injuries. A cynical read says the headline risk exceeds the demonstrated risk.

## Actionable insights
- Own a 2026 Bronco built June 27–July 26, 2026? Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now — don't wait for the October 30 remedy letter. There is no dashboard warning; the VIN lookup is the only way to know.
- Shopping a used 2026 Bronco? Check the VIN before signing; the remedy (clearance adjustment) should show as completed in Ford's records.
- General lesson: airbag recalls with no telltale are the ones that need proactive VIN checks. If the car can't warn you, you have to ask.
