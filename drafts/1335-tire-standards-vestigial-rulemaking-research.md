# Research — #1335: NHTSA's Tire-Standards Overhaul (the Vestigial Tests)

**Slug:** 1335-tire-standards-vestigial-rulemaking
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Working title:** "Your Tires Are Certified by a Test Built for Tires Nobody Makes Anymore"

## The kill test
- **Newsworthy:** NHTSA announced Oct 26, 2026 it is opening a negotiated rulemaking to modernize federal tire safety standards — the first major overhaul in 20+ years. The Federal Register notice published Oct 27; comments and committee nominations due Nov 27, 2026. Multiple outlets reported Oct 28–29 (TTNews, OTR News, guessingheadlights, CCJ).
- **Novel angle:** The specific tests under review — plunger energy (tire strength), bead unseating resistance, endurance (chunking) — were designed for bias-ply tires, per the American Trucking Associations' own Technology & Maintenance Council director, who called them "vestigial." Nobody in the 1,300-article queue has covered the rulemaking itself; the tire stories (#1204-class recall-completion, the EU worn-tire gap piece) covered adjacent ground, not this.
- **Second angle / original contribution:** The tests measure a failure mode that almost never kills anyone. The plunger test pushes a steel rod into a brand-new tire's tread; bead-unseat pushes a tire off its rim. What actually kills — NHTSA's own data — is underinflation (tires 25%+ underinflated are 3x as likely to be in tire-problem crashes), age, and heat. So the agency's first tire-test rewrite in two decades is aimed at tests that never constrained the real failure modes. The rulemaking's safety payoff depends entirely on whether the new tests address what actually fails.
- **Paradox for the lede:** Tire deaths fell ~30% (738 in 2017 → 511 in 2024, per NHTSA via tiregrades) while the "vestigial" tests stayed frozen. If the standards were broken, why did the body count fall? Either the tests were irrelevant (they certify a floor nobody hits) or something else — TPMS (mandatory since MY2008), better manufacturing, TREAD Act early-warning reporting — did the work. The negotiated rulemaking may be fixing tests that never mattered, or it may finally aim at what does. Nobody knows yet, including the committee.
- **VERDICT: PROCEED.**

## Hard facts (verified across sources)
1. **Announcement:** NHTSA announced Oct 26, 2026 it will pursue negotiated rulemaking to modernize tire safety standards. NHTSA press release (Oct 26): "Trump's Transportation Department Moves to Modernize Tire Safety Standards, Deliver Safer American Roads." (nhtsa.gov press releases page, crawled Oct 30)
2. **Notice:** Federal Register notice of intent published Oct 27, 2026. Committee discussions to focus on "the continued necessity and effectiveness of the tire strength test (plunger energy), the bead unseating resistance test and the tire endurance test (chunking)" plus non-pneumatic tires and modernization of tire markings. (TTNews 10/29, OTR News 10/29, sgibinc 10/29)
3. **Committee:** Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy will appoint 18 experts/stakeholders — tire manufacturers/retailers, vehicle manufacturers, safety organizations, research/testing groups, engineering associations. Single term of two years or until committee activities complete. (TTNews 10/29)
4. **Comment deadline:** Comments and committee membership nominations due Nov 27, 2026; nominations to tiresfac@dot.gov by 5 p.m. Eastern. (OTR News 10/29)
5. **The "vestigial" quote:** Robert Braswell, executive director of ATA's Technology & Maintenance Council: the bead unseat and plunger energy tests were "developed many decades ago when bias tires were popularly used... Some of these tests are really kind of a legacy. They're vestigial, and they're not really necessary with respect to how a radial tire is constructed." (TTNews 10/29)
6. **Scope:** The standard most relevant to trucking is FMVSS 119 (new tires for vehicles over 10,000 lbs GVWR) — performance and marking requirements. Light-vehicle tires fall under FMVSS 139 (our site's EU worn-tire piece cites it). (OTR News 10/29; vehicle-safety.org eu-worn-tire-test-us-gap)
7. **Non-pneumatic:** Kevin Grove, ATA director of safety and technology policy: hasn't seen non-pneumatic tires on commercial trucks (only forklifts); "would need a lot more data on non-pneumatic performance and reliability" plus law-enforcement inspection work before adoption. (TTNews 10/29)
8. **Timeline honesty:** "The rulemaking process likely will not be swift... The process can take years." (TTNews 10/29)
9. **The toll:** NHTSA estimates ~11,000 tire-related crashes/year. Tire-related crash deaths: 738 (2017) → 562 (2022) → 646 (2023) → 511 (2024), per NHTSA via tiregrades.com. Our own site's tire-recall piece cites 646 deaths in 2023; the EU worn-tire piece cites ~622 average annual deaths. Direction: down roughly 30% since 2017.
10. **The real failure modes:** NHTSA research: tires underinflated by 25%+ are ~3x as likely to be involved in tire-problem crashes; only ~19% of consumers keep tires properly inflated. (tiregrades.com citing NHTSA)
11. **History:** The TREAD Act (Nov 2000) — the last big tire-regulatory moment — followed Firestone tread separations on Ford Explorers that killed 271 people and injured 800+. That was 26 years ago. TPMS has been mandatory on light vehicles since MY2008. (vehicle-safety.org tire-recall-12-percent-completion-rate; CED Technologies)
12. **Adjacent:** Same week NHTSA also proposed FMVSS 108 updates with optional cyan ADS marker lamps for autonomous vehicles (Oct 23 press release; CCJ/Ford Authority Oct 29–30) and published the MY2027 5-star testing list (Oct 15). Mention as one-line context only — separate stories.

## FARS grounding
- Tire-specific crash causation is not coded in FARS; tire death counts come from NHTSA's tire-safety publications, not FARS. Say so explicitly (methodology honesty).
- FARS context we can state: the site's EU worn-tire piece already established tall-center-of-gravity vehicles (E-350 at 2.51/100M VMT) turn tire failures into rollovers. Reuse as context with attribution to our own prior analysis, not as new.

## What we did NOT prove (limitations — dedicated accounting)
- The 511/646/738 trend figures come from tiregrades.com's aggregation attributed to NHTSA, not from a NHTSA publication we directly read. Treat as NHTSA-reported-via-secondary; the direction (declining) is corroborated by our own site's prior figures (622 avg, 646 in 2023).
- The rulemaking proposes nothing yet. No test has been changed, removed, or added. The committee doesn't exist yet. Any safety payoff is speculative.
- Negotiated rulemaking committees are consensus bodies dominated by regulated industries; the outcome could weaken as well as strengthen standards. Braswell (ATA/TMC) is an industry voice, not a safety advocate.
- We cannot attribute the 2017→2024 death decline to any single cause (TPMS, tire construction, TREAD Act reporting, fewer miles, better EMS). The decline happened under frozen tests, but correlation is not attribution.
- FMVSS 119 vs 139 scope: OTR News frames the notice around heavy-truck tires (FMVSS 119); the NPRM language about "new tire technologies" and markings may touch 139 as well. Don't over-specify which CFR sections change.

## Counterargument (at full strength)
If the tests are truly "vestigial" and irrelevant, then the 30% drop in tire deaths since 2017 happened without them mattering — which means modernizing them won't save lives either, and this rulemaking is regulatory theater with a two-year committee and a comment period as props. Worse: the actual killers are underinflation and age, and no plunger test, new or old, has ever checked the air pressure in your tires or the DOT date code on your sidewall. The agency is rewriting the test for how strong a new tire is, while the tires that kill people are old and flat — two properties no certification test can ever measure. If you want fewer tire deaths, you'd mandate aging standards and better TPMS, not a better plunger. And there's a darker version: a negotiated rulemaking stacked with tire-manufacturer and trucking representatives could use "modernization" to relax testing burdens and call it simplification. The process is the product.

## Actionable takeaways
- File a comment by Nov 27, 2026 (federalregister.gov; committee nominations to tiresfac@dot.gov). Public comments genuinely shape negotiated-rulemaking agendas.
- Check your tire age now: the DOT date code on the sidewall (week/year, e.g., "3221" = 32nd week of 2021). Rubber degrades regardless of tread; replace at ~6 years.
- The penny test (Lincoln's head upside down in the tread) still beats any federal standard for your actual risk.
- TPMS light on? That is the single highest-leverage tire safety device in your car — take it seriously, since only ~19% of drivers keep tires properly inflated.
