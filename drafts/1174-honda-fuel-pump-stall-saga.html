Honda Has Recalled 3.3 Million Fuel Pumps. The Motorcycles Were Just the Latest Chapter.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
October 15, 2026

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: your engine is a controlled explosion that only keeps exploding because a small electric pump keeps feeding it gasoline, and when the pump quits, the explosion stops. In a car, that is an inconvenience with a steering wheel and four tires to coast on. On a motorcycle at seventy miles an hour, it is a different kind of math entirely.

Honda would like 4,206 motorcycle owners to think about that math. On October 1, the company told NHTSA that the fuel pumps in certain 2023–2025 VT1300CX cruisers — the Fury, the chopper-styled one — and 2024–2025 VT750s — the Shadow Phantom — may malfunction due to a manufacturing defect, and that a dead pump can stall the engine and put the rider on the ground.[1] VINs became searchable October 8, and owner letters go out November 27. Dealers replace the pump free, which is the least a manufacturer can do when the part in question is the only thing standing between you and silence at speed.

3,285,258
Honda fuel pumps recalled since December 2023, across five campaigns — the Fury and Shadow recall is chapter five

This recall is not really about 4,206 motorcycles. It is about a component that will not stop failing.

In December 2023, Honda recalled 2,490,460 cars and SUVs — Accords, Civics, CR-Vs, Ridgelines — because their fuel-pump impellers had been improperly molded, could deform, and could stall the engine.[2] In 2024 the campaign metastasized to motorcycles: 29,301 Goldwings and CBRs in one filing, 40,481 CBRs and VFR1200s in another, same improperly molded impellers, same stall.[3][4] A third 2024 campaign added 720,810 more cars whose high-pressure pumps could crack and leak fuel onto hot engine parts, which is the fire-flavored version of the same story.[5] Add the 4,206 cruisers and Shadows, and Honda has now recalled 3,285,258 fuel pumps in under three years. Of those, 2,564,448 were recalled specifically because the engine might just stop.

Pause on that number, because Honda has recalled enough fuel pumps to empty a mid-size American city of its vehicles, and the defect keeps finding new model families like it is working through a checklist: cars, then sportbikes, then tourers, now cruisers. No pump cares what it is installed in.

Now the case for Honda, stated at full strength, because it deserves better than a strawman. Every one of these campaigns was company-initiated, and the repairs are free. The filings describe the defect and order the fix; the documented consequence is the stall itself. And a recall is, in the strictest sense, the system working: the defect was found, the regulator was told, the owners get letters. You could argue — reasonably — that five fuel-pump recalls in three years is evidence of a manufacturer that reports problems rather than one that hides them.

Sure, but there is a difference between a manufacturer that finds problems and a manufacturer that keeps manufacturing the problem. In 2023 the car recall was about impellers molded wrong at the supplier; in 2024 the bike recalls were about impellers molded wrong at the supplier; and the 2026 cruiser recall is about a fuel pump with a manufacturing defect that Honda has not publicly detailed, which means either the impeller problem survived three years of recalls or there is a second, unrelated way Honda's fuel pumps fail. Neither option is comforting when you are the one on two wheels.

And that is the part worth sitting with. A stalled Civic is a tow truck; a stalled Fury at highway speed is a 670-pound cruiser with no power, no engine braking, and a rider doing sudden unplanned physics. NHTSA's consequence statement puts it in the agency's flattest possible language — “increasing the risk of a crash” — which, translated from regulator, means exactly what you think it means.[1]

If you own a 2023–2025 Fury or a 2024–2025 Shadow Phantom, do not wait for the November letter. Check the VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls — live since October 8 — because the replacement is free and the pump will not send you a warning text before it quits. If your bike has started stuttering at idle or dying at stoplights, that is not character; that is the preview. And if you are shopping used — Goldwing, CBR, VFR, Fury, Shadow, or any Honda built in the last decade — run the VIN first. At this point, asking whether a used Honda has fuel-pump recall history is not paranoia; it is due diligence.
