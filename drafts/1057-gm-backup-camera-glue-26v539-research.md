# Research — #1057: GM Backup Camera Glue Failure (26V539)

**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (recall mechanics / investigation beat)
**Date:** 2026-10-03

## The story
GM is recalling **306,511** compact SUVs because their rearview cameras may display a distorted or blank image. The defect is in the glue: a plasma-cleaning step in camera-housing assembly weakened the adhesive bond sealing the housing, letting moisture and grime creep in over time.

**Campaign:** NHTSA 26V539 / GM A262554900
**Component:** BACK OVER PREVENTION
**Estimated defect rate:** 10% (~30,651 vehicles actually defective)

## Affected models
| Model | Years | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Chevrolet Trax | 2025 | 159,821 |
| Chevrolet Trailblazer | 2024-2026 | 73,665 + 850 additional 2024/early-2026 |
| Buick Envista | 2024-2025 | 41,323 |
| Buick Encore GX | 2024-2026 | 30,852 |

Only **standard** rearview cameras affected; Surround Vision (premium trim) cameras unaffected.

## The chronology (the novel hook)
1. **May 20, 2025** — GM opens investigation into inoperative rearview camera complaints.
2. **Sep 4, 2025** — GM **closes** the investigation: "no defect trend was identified." Continues monitoring field data at six-month intervals.
3. **Mar 25, 2026** — GM **reopens** the investigation based on a rise in claim rates. Root cause found: the plasma-cleaning treatment used to prep the camera housing assembly could weaken the adhesive bond in some assemblies.
4. **Aug 13, 2026** — GM's Safety Field Action Decision Authority (SFADA) approves the recall.

The suspect window: vehicles built **April–December 2024**. The end of the window coincides with a modification the supplier made to the plasma-cleaning fixture.

**33,365 field complaints** received between Jul 3, 2024 and May 30, 2026 tied to the suspect window. GM says it is not aware of any accidents or injuries.

## The irony (angle)
The plasma cleaning existed to *prepare* the housing for bonding. It degraded the bond instead. A manufacturing step meant to make the camera cleaner made it leak. Moisture gets in, the image warps or dies, and the driver shifts into reverse looking at a dead screen. Federal law has required rear cameras on every new car since 2018 — this one fails through its own assembly line.

Second irony: GM closed the investigation. The complaints kept coming. It reopened six months later. The trend that "wasn't identified" was identified by the calendar.

## Remedy
Dealers **inspect and replace as necessary** (not a blanket replacement). Replacement parts were produced outside the suspect window. Dealers notified Aug 20, 2026; owner letters began Sep 16, 2026, phased. VINs searchable on nhtsa.gov since Aug 20, 2026.
