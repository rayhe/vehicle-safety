August 2026 data study by Steven M. Sweat, Personal Injury Lawyers, APC: ranked U.S. metro areas by child (ages 5–18) pedestrian/cyclist deaths near schools over a 12-year period. Published ~Sept 1, 2026. Back-to-school timing.

## Novel angle / kill test
Not another "slow down near schools" PSA. The finding: the posted 25-mph school-zone limit is functionally decorative. 70% of drivers ignore it by 20+ mph, half of schools have no crossing guards (self-enforced), and the deaths cluster where the signage supposedly protects: half of Oxnard's fatalities within 400m of a school; Provo's 8 deaths all pedestrians. The novel consumer math: school bus is statistically the safe part — kids die walking to it, not riding in it (38 in-bus vs 79 pedestrians + 83 in other vehicles over the same decade). Two-thirds of child pedestrians killed weren't at intersections: the crossing guard isn't where the kid dies.

Counterintuitive wrinkle worth honoring: biking looks safer than walking only in raw counts (63/72 pedestrian); exposure-adjusted per-trip risk may differ. Study says so itself — keep it.

## Original contribution (for rigor gate)
- The "enforcement gap" arithmetic: if 70% of drivers exceed by 20+ over a 25-mph school zone, then at 45+ mph the child fatality probability is roughly 8x the posted-limit case (NHTSA/GAO figure: 5% die at ≤20 mph, 40% at 30 mph, 80% at 40 mph). Do the math explicitly.
- Composite the two data sets: study's metro ranking vs NHTSA's 5-to-10 age concentration + non-intersection clustering — the practical takeaway is route selection (mid-block crossings), not mode choice.
- Utah paradox: Provo AND Salt Lake City both in top 4 — the state with the wholesome reputation has two of the four deadliest school zones; honest speculation on wide arterials + winter darkness, flagged as speculation.

## Actionable insights (Clara Rollover beat)
1. Pick the route, not the mode: two-thirds of killed kids weren't at intersections — choose the route with the fewest mid-block arterial crossings, even if longer.
2. The 10-foot danger zone: teach kids to stay 10 ft from the bus and make eye contact with the driver before crossing.
3. Push the school/city for automated enforcement or crossing guards; posted limits alone don't change behavior (70% noncompliance stat).
4. If you drive through a school zone: the speed limit is 25 when children are present (CA CVC §22352); assume the kids, not the sign, are the enforcement.

## Limitations
- Study is from a personal-injury law firm, not a peer-reviewed paper; 12-year FARS window not exposure-adjusted (no walking/biking trip counts).
- "Half of schools have no crossing guards" is an expert assertion in the study, not independently verified — attribute it.
- Pedestrian death-probability-by-speed figures are NHTSA/GAO estimates, approximate.
