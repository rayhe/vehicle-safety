Mazda Recalled Its New Cars Zero Times in 2026. The Only Thing It Recalled Was a Sticker.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again, and they didn't get better, because there was nothing to get better: eleven brands into the recall census, Mazda is the first to file zero. Seven 2026 models, 341,714 cars sold through October, and not one NHTSA safety recall.[1][7]

0
NHTSA safety campaigns filed in 2026 touching a 2026 Mazda. Eleven-brand total is 140, and Mazda's share of it is 0 percent.

Start with the method, because a zero earned by a broken query is worth nothing. I ran 32 queries against the NHTSA recalls API for every 2026 Mazda model name, plus hybrid and plug-in hybrid variants. Every single one came back HTTP 400, not empty results but errors. The API has no vocabulary for a 2026 Mazda at all, which is the same failure mode that cost the BMW census two campaigns, except here it is total.[1] So the zero does not rest on the API. It rests on six independent confirmations: CarBuzz counted zero for every Mazda model in March, and again for the CX-50 in August, the CX-90 in October, and the CX-5 in November. A recall tracker pulling NHTSA's own file dated today lists zero safety recalls for the 2026 CX-5. A safety-rating site found no recalls on record for the 2026 CX-70 PHEV as of October 21.[2][3][4][5][6] Control queries prove the API itself works for Mazda: 2025 models return real campaigns, and a sweep of model years 2022 through 2025 found no 26V Mazda campaign anywhere, which leaves six sources, one control sweep, and zero campaigns.

Put the zero in the table and it looks like a typo. Ford filed 41 campaigns, Stellantis 27, GM, Toyota, and Hyundai 15 each, Nissan and BMW 7 each, and Subaru 5 with Honda and Kia at 4 each, while Mazda filed 0. Eleven-brand average is 12.7 campaigns, which means Mazda sits 12.7 below average, and Ford alone filed 41 times as many campaigns as Mazda did.[8] Every model is clean: CX-30, CX-5, CX-50, CX-70, CX-90, MX-5, Mazda3. Zero across the board, including the volume cars and the all-new third-generation CX-5.

Now the contrast, because a zero means nothing without a baseline. In 2025, Mazda filed three campaigns covering 277,273 vehicles. Campaign 25V357 recalled 171,412 Mazda3s and CX-30s because an airbag sensor could set an error code and switch the airbags off under a low-battery condition. Campaign 25V568 recalled 104,854 CX-90s and CX-70s because a fuel gauge could lie about the fuel level and let the car run dry and stall in traffic. Campaign 25V737 recalled 1,007 CX-50s because front suspension bolts may never have been tightened properly, which can let a suspension component or a wheel detach.[9] One year later, the filings stopped entirely, and the 2026 column in every table reads zero. Airbags switching themselves off, fuel gauges lying, bolts never tightened, then silence. Either Mazda fixed its quality system in twelve months, or it has defects it has not found yet, and a census counts filings, not defects, so it cannot tell you which.

Strictly speaking, Mazda did recall one thing in 2026. Emissions recall 8026C, issued in May, covers 2026 CX-50 Hybrids built between September 8 and September 25, 2025. Wrong battery labels were attached to the B-pillar and the hybrid battery beneath the rear seat, violating California's battery-labeling rule. The fix is a dealer attaching the correct labels, free, in about five minutes. It carries no NHTSA campaign number, no safety defect, and no risk to anyone, which is why it sits outside the census and inside this paragraph: the only recall-shaped thing Mazda did all year was a sticker.[10]

Before anyone frames the zero, read the rest of the file. NHTSA opened recall query RQ26002 on February 6, 2026, after 26 owners reported sudden increases in steering effort in CX-90s that had already received the remedy for the 2024 steering recall, 24V022, which covered 43,754 cars. Mazda's own response says the condition also appears in some 2025 CX-90s and 2025–2026 CX-70s, and no new recall has resulted yet. Separately, the 2026 CX-5 carries 19 owner complaints in NHTSA's file dated today, mostly infotainment failures, and in early October one owner reported her new CX-5 braking hard on its own three or four times on one drive, and no investigation has been opened.[3][11][12] And the brand-new CX-5 has 75 manufacturer communications, technical service bulletins, on file with NHTSA, against 32 for the 2026 RAV4. Bulletins are not recalls; they are the manufacturer's quiet instructions to dealers. A new model with 75 of them and zero recalls is a car being fixed in the service bay rather than in the recall file, and whether that is good or bad is exactly the question a census cannot answer.

Give the skeptics their due, because their case is real. Zero recalls is not zero defects, and a small manufacturer that sold 341,714 cars while Ford sold millions can look clean by finding its problems in the service bay instead of the recall file, and Ford's 41 recalls, for all their embarrassment, are at least public. BMW, a company of similar size and premium ambition, filed 7 campaigns in the same window, including a recall of a single unsold motorcycle over a turn signal, while Mazda filed none. That contrast cuts both ways: either Mazda built a genuinely clean model year, the CX-5's first year on a new generation included, or it has defects it has not found yet, and both readings fit the data. The census proves neither, and the honest version of this story says so out loud.

What this story does not prove matters too. The API could not be queried for any 2026 Mazda, so the zero rests on six outside sources plus control sweeps, and a campaign touching only a 2026 Mazda would have to escape all of them plus NHTSA's own recall file dated November 12, 2026, which is possible but unlikely. The 341,714 sales figure is US sales through October, not 2026-model-year production, so it is a denominator for scale, not a precise population. Complaint counts are unverified owner reports, not findings of defect. RQ26002 is an open query, not a finding, and the 26 steering reports are allegations under investigation.

What to do with this: own a 2026 Mazda of any model? As of today, there is no safety recall on your car, but check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls anyway, because a campaign filed tomorrow would not appear in this census. Own a 2026 CX-50 Hybrid built September 8–25, 2025? You are in the 8026C label recall, which is not a safety defect, and the fix is a dealer sticking the right labels on. Own a 2024–2025 Mazda3, CX-30, CX-90, or CX-70? Your car is in one of the three 2025 campaigns, airbag sensor, fuel gauge, or suspension bolts, and those are the recalls that matter for your VIN. Do not let the zero headline talk you out of checking. Own a 2024 CX-90, and you are also inside the RQ26002 query, so if your steering effort changes suddenly after the remedy, report it to NHTSA, because that is how queries become recalls.[1][9][11]
