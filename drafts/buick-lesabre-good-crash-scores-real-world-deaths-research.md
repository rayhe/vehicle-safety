# RESEARCH — #1285: The Buick LeSabre Aced Its Crash Test. The Real World Gave It an F.

**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate — used-car buying guidance)
**Slug:** buick-lesabre-good-crash-scores-real-world-deaths
**Kill test:** PASS. Novel cross-tab nobody on the site has run: IIHS/NHTSA crash-test ratings vs.
real-world lethality-per-fatal-crash-involvement. The LeSabre is still a live used-car
proposition (~$2,975 resale per KBB, ~$4,247 average used price per CarBuzz), marketed to this
day as a safe, sensible buy on the strength of 25-year-old test scores. The data says the
opposite. This is not another "old car kills" data dump: it is a ratings-vs-reality paradox
with a direct buy/don't-buy takeaway.

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA FARS 2014–2023, via the site's fars_output.js** (deaths, fatal-crash involvements,
   VMT estimates, toxicology). All core numbers below come from this file, computed directly.
2. **IIHS ratings page, 2000 Buick LeSabre** (iihs.org/ratings/vehicle/buick/lesabre-4-door-sedan/2000):
   moderate overlap front, original test, overall evaluation **G (Good)**, structure/safety cage **G**,
   chest **G**, leg/foot **G**, driver restraints **G**, head/neck **A (Acceptable)**. Rating applies to
   2000–05 models built after April 1999. Side impact original test: not tested. Rear crash
   protection / head restraint: **Poor** (per Edmunds' IIHS summary).
3. **NHTSA star ratings as reported for the 2005 LeSabre** (via CarBuzz/Cars & Bids summary):
   five-star passenger front and side, four-star driver front and side.
4. **Market context:** KBB 2005 LeSabre resale ~$2,975 / trade-in ~$1,475; CarBuzz average used
   price ~$4,247; Edmunds trade-in range $226–$2,512. The car is a live sub-$5,000 used buy.
5. **IIHS fatality statistics / FARS background pages** (nhtsa.gov FARS page, iihs.org fatality
   statistics) for methodology context.

## Core numbers (computed from fars_output.js)
- Buick LeSabre: **1,344 occupant deaths** in 2014–2023 FARS, across **1,633 fatal-crash
  involvements** (vehicle records in fatal crashes).
- **Lethality index = 1,344 / 1,633 = 0.82 own-deaths per fatal-crash involvement.** In other
  words: when a LeSabre is in a crash that kills someone, a LeSabre occupant is the victim
  82% of the time. Rank: **4th highest of 163 models** with 300+ fatal-crash involvements.
- Comparators: all-vehicle average 0.57 (191,193 deaths / 336,202 involvements); sedan average
  0.645; Ram 2500 0.20; Ram 1500 0.34; Ford F-250 0.36. The LeSabre loses its own occupants at
  more than 4x the rate of a Ram 2500's occupants.
- **Fatality rate 2.67 deaths per 100M VMT (estimated)** vs. sedan-class average 1.39 (1.9x) and
  all-vehicle average 0.91 (2.9x). Rank 18 of 147 models with 200+ deaths.
- Annual average: 134.4 deaths/year over the decade.
- Model-year deaths (vehicle model year, 2014–2023 crashes): the IIHS-"Good"-rated 2000–05
  generation accounts for **924 of 1,344 deaths (69%)** — 2000: 152, 2001: 128, 2002: 153,
  2003: 153, 2004: 188, 2005: 150. The good test score did not save this generation.
- Sibling check: Buick Century (same era, same GM sedan playbook): 849 deaths, 1,106
  involvements, lethality 0.77. The pattern is not a one-model fluke.
- Replacement check: Buick Lucerne (LeSabre's successor): 163 deaths, 220 involvements,
  lethality 0.74, rate 0.65. Still high lethality, far lower rate — the rate story improved,
  the loses-its-own-occupants story did not.
- Impairment (FARS toxicology, LeSabre drivers in fatal crashes): 1,209 drivers, 284 any
  impairment = **23.5%** (alcohol 17.2%, drugs 10.9%) vs. national 20.0% across all models.
  Modestly above average — impairment does NOT explain a 0.82 lethality index. This is the
  car (and its age, and its occupants' age), not the bottle.

## The paradox, stated plainly
- IIHS moderate-overlap front: Good. NHTSA: five stars for passengers. Contemporary verdict:
  "one of the safest sedans on the road at the time" (CarBuzz).
- Real world, 2014–2023: 4th-deadliest-per-crash-occupant-outcome of any high-volume model,
  death rate nearly double the sedan average.
- Why the gap is plausible (and must be stated, not hidden):
  - The IIHS test is ONE scenario: 40 mph frontal offset into a deformable barrier. Real fatal
    crashes include side impacts (LeSabre side test: never run by IIHS in original form), rear
    impacts (head restraint: Poor), rollovers, and multi-vehicle pileups.
  - The LeSabre never got electronic stability control or side curtain airbags in this
    generation; side airbags were added to the standard list only late in the run.
  - Age confound (STRONGEST COUNTERARGUMENT): LeSabre buyers skewed elderly, and older
    occupants die more easily in any crash. FARS here is not age-adjusted, so part of the
    0.82 could be occupant frailty rather than structure. The article must say this at full
    strength. The rebuttal that survives it: frailty does not explain why the LeSabre's rate
    (2.67) is nearly double the sedan average while its impairment is only modestly elevated,
    nor why its sibling Century shows the same 0.77 pattern — but the confound is real and
    the article will not pretend otherwise.
  - Fleet-age confound: by 2014–2023 every LeSabre on the road was 9–24 years old, with
    degraded restraints, no modern active safety, and unknown maintenance. A 2005 test score
    describes a new car, not a 20-year-old one.

## Actionable takeaways (Clara's beat)
- Shopping under $5,000? A LeSabre's "Good" IIHS score is from a test that never measured
  side impact or small overlap, on a car that has never had ESC or curtain airbags. Cross-shop
  a 2012+ sedan with ESC and side curtains for the same money.
- If you already own one: this is the car to retire first from highway duty, not the one to
  hand to a new teen driver. Check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls either way.
- The general rule the data supports: a crash-test rating is a new-car snapshot in one
  scenario. Real-world lethality is what 10 years of FARS says. Prefer the latter.

## Limitations (to appear in the article)
- FARS counts fatal crashes only; injury-only performance is invisible here.
- VMT and fleet figures are estimates from sales data, not odometer readings (±15%
  uncertainty for low-volume models; LeSabre is high-volume, so tighter).
- DEATHS in the FARS vehicle file counts deaths among that vehicle's occupants; the
  lethality index is own-occupant deaths per fatal-crash involvement, not a probability that
  a given crash kills someone (multi-vehicle crashes can kill occupants of several vehicles).
- No age adjustment for occupants; see counterargument above.
- NHTSA star ratings for 2005 are as reported by secondary summaries; the IIHS "Good"
  rating is verified directly from IIHS's own page.
