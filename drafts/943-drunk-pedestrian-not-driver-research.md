# Research Notes — Draft #943
**Slug:** 943-drunk-pedestrian-not-driver
**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III
**Kicker:** Sobriety Report
**Working title:** "In Pedestrian Deaths, the Drunk One Is Usually the Pedestrian"
**Date:** 2026-09-19

## Angle
Every drunk-driving campaign is aimed at the driver. But NHTSA's own June 2026
fact sheet on 2024 pedestrian deaths shows the intoxicated party in a fatal
pedestrian crash is twice as likely to be the pedestrian as the driver. The
policy machine hunts impaired drivers; the impaired walker barely registers.

## Primary sources (3+)

### 1. NHTSA, "Pedestrians: 2024 Data" (DOT HS 813 818), June 2026
FARS 2024 Annual Report File analysis. URL:
https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813818

Key figures:
- 6,993 traffic crashes each had one or more pedestrian fatalities in 2024;
  7,080 pedestrians killed.
- Alcohol involvement (BAC >= .01, driver and/or pedestrian): 43% of fatal
  pedestrian crashes.
- 27% of fatal pedestrian crashes each had a pedestrian with BAC >= .08.
- Only 15% of fatal pedestrian crashes each had a driver with BAC >= .08.
- Table 4 crosstab (highest BAC per crash, crashes = 6,993):
  - Pedestrian .08+, driver sober: 1,465 crashes (21%)
  - Driver .08+, pedestrian sober: 685 crashes (10%)
  - Both .08+: 328 crashes (5%)
  - Neither: 4,011 (57%)
- **Original calculation:** 1,465 / 685 = 2.14. The drunk-pedestrian/sober-driver
  cell is 2.1x the drunk-driver/sober-pedestrian cell. The pedestrian is the
  impaired party in 1,867 (27%) of fatal crashes; the driver in 1,070 (15%).
- 21-to-24 age group had the highest share of killed pedestrians at BAC >= .08
  (38%) in 2024; in 2015 it was the 45-to-54 group (44%).
- Killed-pedestrian BAC >= .08 share fell from 34% (2015) to 27% (2024).
- 76% of pedestrian fatalities occurred in the dark; peak 6-8:59 p.m. (24%)
  and 9-11:59 p.m. (23%).
- 90% of pedestrian fatalities in single-vehicle crashes; 24% were hit-and-run.
- NHTSA's own safety reminder in this fact sheet: "Avoid alcohol and drugs when
  walking; they impair your judgment and coordination."
- Caveat stated in the report: NHTSA estimates BACs when alcohol test results
  are unknown; alcohol presence "may or may not be a contributing factor."

### 2. Guzman et al. (2026), "Substance use among fatally-injured pedestrians
in New York State, USA: a retrospective analysis," Injury Prevention
DOI: 10.1136/ip-2025-045980
https://injuryprevention.bmj.com/content/early/2026/07/02/ip-2025-045980

- FARS 2018-2020, New York State: 771 pedestrian deaths; 338 (44%) involved
  alcohol, drugs, or both by the pedestrian.
- 18% involved drugs alone; 12% involved both alcohol and drugs.
- BAC >= 0.01% recorded in 14% of fatalities (test coverage was partial).
- Poly-substance use linked to more severe cognitive impairment.
- Men 63% of deaths; highest death count 65+ (34%).
- Researchers: "Most public safety campaigns focus on drinking and driving,
  whereas pedestrian substance use and impairment are relatively neglected."
- Limitation: non-tested pedestrians coded as negative, likely underestimating
  prevalence.

### 3. Blomberg research via DataWatch (thebug.fm, Sept 11, 2026)
https://thebug.fm/news/2026/09/11/datawatch-the-overlooked-risks-of-walking-while-intoxicated

- Blomberg's 1970s New Orleans study: about half of adult pedestrian victims
  had been drinking; nearly one-quarter had BAC >= 0.20%.
- Control-group design (pedestrians sampled at same locations/days/times and
  112 random locations): alcohol heavily overrepresented among crash victims vs
  the walking population.
- Alcohol-involved victims disproportionately middle-aged men; crashes clustered
  at night and on weekends.
- Five-state crash-data analysis (2007-2017) found the same demographic still
  overrepresented nearly half a century later.

### 4. Supporting: NHTSA Quick Facts 2024
https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813809
- Pedestrians killed age 14+ with BAC >= .01: 2,111 (31%); BAC >= .08: 1,841
  (27%) in 2024.

## Novel contribution
- The 2.1:1 ratio: drunk-pedestrian/sober-driver fatal crashes outnumber
  drunk-driver/sober-pedestrian ones more than two to one. Nobody has framed
  pedestrian deaths this way on this site (queue checked; #867 is motorcycles).
- Counter-trend: the drunk-pedestrian share fell 34% -> 27% (2015-2024) even as
  total pedestrian deaths rose 5,494 (2015) -> 7,080 (2024). Fewer drunk, more
  dead overall: alcohol is not the whole story of the pedestrian crisis.
- The 5% "both impaired" cell and 57% "neither" cell complete the picture:
  the majority of pedestrian deaths involve no alcohol at all.

## Strongest counterargument
Alcohol presence is not causation. NHTSA says so explicitly. Drunk pedestrians
walk more at night, on arterials, away from crosswalks (NY study: drug-involved
pedestrians more likely to be on the road, not in a crosswalk) — the crash
profile of the drunk walker differs in ways that may reflect exposure, not just
impairment. And in 24% hit-and-run cases the driver's sobriety is unknown,
which could inflate the pedestrian-only cell.

## Limitations
- BAC estimates, not universal testing; NHTSA imputes unknown BACs.
- 2024 data is Annual Report File (near-final, not final final file).
- NY study is state-level 2018-2020; not national.
- Blomberg data is old (1970s) and one city; five-state replication is
  demographic, not the control-group design.

## Actionable insights
- Plan the walk home, not just the drive home: late-night arterial roads after
  bars are the highest-risk pedestrian scenario (dark + alcohol + speed).
- For cities: lighting and mid-block crossing protection near nightlife corridors
  targets the 21%-of-fatal-crashes cell directly.
- Individuals: if you are walking impaired, the data says you are the drunk one
  in the crash more often than the driver is. Choose the rideshare.
- Drivers near bar districts after 6 p.m.: the person stepping into the road may
  be the impaired one, and they may be in the road rather than the crosswalk.
