# Research — #940: IIHS Tested Seven, Only Two Passed

**Slug:** `940-iihs-seven-tested-two-passed`
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; beat match: car-buying guidance)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date:** 2026-09-19

## The story

IIHS released ratings for seven 2026/2027 models on Sep 3, 2026 (Autoblog; Consumer Affairs). Only two earned Top Safety Pick+: the redesigned 2027 Kia Telluride and the 2026-27 Tesla Model Y. Five models missed: BMW 3 Series, BMW i4, GMC Canyon Crew Cab, Lexus IS, Toyota Crown Signia. Pass rate: 2/7 = 28.6%.

## Novel hooks (kill test: passes)

1. **The build-date lottery.** The 2027 Telluride's TSP+ applies ONLY to vehicles built after June 2026, when Kia quietly upgraded the headlights. Same model year, same badge, same dealer lot: pre-June builds carry Marginal headlights and no award; post-June builds carry the top award. Two identical-looking SUVs, two different safety ratings.
2. **The price paradox.** The failures cluster in luxury badges: BMW 3 Series (Acceptable moderate overlap, Marginal v2v prevention), BMW i4 (Poor v2v crash prevention), Lexus IS (Marginal moderate overlap, Acceptable side). Meanwhile a ~$36K Kia passes. Spending more buys no safety here.
3. **The common killer.** 4 of the 5 failures share one failing muscle: the updated moderate overlap front test (40 mph, 40% offset, rear-seat occupants at risk) and/or crash-prevention 2.0. The moderate overlap test got tougher; many vehicles struggle with rear-seat occupant protection. (Lexus IS: rear-seat vulnerability specifically.)
4. **The reminder irony.** The Model Y earns the top award despite a Marginal seat-belt reminder. Seat-belt reminder quality is not a TSP+ criterion; preventing the crash is. Priorities: crashworthiness + active prevention first.

## Facts (all sourced)

- IIHS 2026 award bar: Good in small overlap front, updated moderate overlap front, side; headlights Acceptable/Good on every trim; TSP+ adds Good pedestrian crash prevention + Acceptable/Good in vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention 2.0, with standard equipment. (Consumer Affairs/Consumer Reports daily roundup, Sep 10 2026)
- 63 vehicles earned IIHS awards in 2026: 45 TSP+, 18 TSP. (IIHS via YouTube roundup)
- Kia has five 2026 TSP+ models: 2027 Telluride (built after June 2026), 2026 K4, 2026 EV9, 2026 Sorento (built after Sept 2025). The Telluride's headlight improvement = mid-cycle silent fix, just like the Sorento's Sept 2025 fix pattern.
- Failure table (Autoblog, Sep 3 2026):
  - BMW 3 Series: Acceptable moderate overlap + Acceptable headlights + Marginal v2v prevention
  - BMW i4: Marginal/Poor headlights + Poor v2v prevention
  - GMC Canyon Crew Cab: Marginal moderate overlap + Poor v2v prevention
  - Lexus IS: Marginal moderate overlap (rear-seat risk) + Acceptable side
  - Toyota Crown Signia: Marginal moderate overlap
- "None of the five models listed here are brand-new releases" — expect better from next-gen versions. (Autoblog)
- IIHS research: ~50,000 lives saved by IIHS safety tests over time. (via Autoblog)

## Limitations (honest accounting)

- All seven vehicles meet federal safety standards; "missed the award" != unsafe. IIHS tests are granular beyond FMVSS.
- FARS 2014-2023 contains no data on 2026/2027 redesigns; no fatality-rate cross-tab is possible for these specific vehicles. The article leans on IIHS crash-test data, not fatality data.
- I could not verify from IIHS directly whether the pre-June-2026 Telluride would earn a lower-tier TSP (non-plus); say only what sources confirm.
- The "lives saved" figure is IIHS's own estimate via Autoblog; treat as the institute's claim.

## Strongest counterargument

The harder 2026 test bar is somewhat arbitrary: a "Marginal" moderate-overlap rating in 2026 can reflect rear-dummy kinematics under a tougher protocol, not real-world injury risk that would show up in FARS. BMW and Toyota would argue their cars are as safe in the real crashes that actually kill people. That's half-right: the crash-prevention failures (BMW i4 Poor v2v) are harder to dismiss, since AEB performance maps directly to avoided collisions.

## What the reader should DO

1. Shopping a 2027 Telluride: check the build-date sticker on the driver's door jamb. Only post-June-2026 builds carry the TSP+ headlights.
2. Shopping any of the five misses: test-drive the crash-prevention first (AEB), and check the latest at iihs.org/ratings before buying.
3. Rule of thumb: price does not predict safety. Check the rating, not the badge.
