Hyundai's Robots Got Reprogrammed. Humans Tightened the Lug Nuts. Now Wheels May Fall Off.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 29, 2026

NHTSA's October 21 filing reads like a factory-floor confession: on seventeen brand-new 2026 Hyundais, the lug nuts may not have been tightened properly, and a loose wheel at highway speed does not politely warn you. Seventeen cars (eight Venues, four Elantras, four Elantra Hybrids, and one Elantra N) could shed a wheel because a human held the torque wrench while the robots were being reprogrammed.[1]

17
2026 Hyundais recalled for lug nuts that may let a wheel detach, with 24 more recalled for loose suspension bolts from the same plant

The recall, campaign 26V693000, names the 2026 Venue, Elantra, Elantra Hybrid, and Elantra N. Hyundai's filing says the lug nuts may loosen until the wheel detaches, which the agency translates as loss of vehicle control or a rolling road hazard for everyone behind you. Both translations end in a crash report.[1]

It did not happen alone. A sibling filing, 26V692, covers 24 more 2026 Hyundais, 20 Elantras and four Tucson Hybrids, whose front strut fasteners may not have been secured to the steering knuckles. Both recalls came out of the same Ulsan plant in South Korea, and both fasteners came from the same supplier, Samjin Nut. Two recalls, one supplier, one plant, one week. Coincidences this tidy usually have paperwork.[2]

The paperwork exists. After a tooling change on the wheel-tightening equipment, the plant ran a temporary manual torquing process to keep production moving. It ended on September 16, and Hyundai learned something was wrong six days later. Nobody told the regulator until October 21, and owners will not hear about it until letters go out on December 19. That is the part that bothers me: the fix is a torque wrench and an afternoon, but the letters wait two months.[4] Canada's version of the strut-bolt recall posted on October 20, one day before the U.S. wheel recall.[6]

A wheel that detaches at speed is not a flat tire. The car drops onto the hub, the driver loses steering authority on that corner, and a 40-pound wheel becomes an unguided projectile with its own travel plans. Hyundai reports zero incidents and zero injuries so far. Zero is a lucky number, not a plan.[2]

Now the case against the story, because it deserves one. Forty-one vehicles is a factory hiccup, not a fleet defect. Hyundai caught it, traced it to a documented process gap, and stopped the manual process before the filing. The supplier is not blamed; the parts are fine. No one has been hurt, no car has lost a wheel, and the remedy is an inspection that takes less time than an oil change. If this is the recall system working, it looks a lot like it.

Still, if you bought a 2026 Elantra or Venue: do not wait for the December letter. The VINs have been searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls since October 23, the remedy is free, and a torque check is same-day work. Ask the dealer whether your car came out of Ulsan during the August window. The recall number is Hyundai 310, NHTSA 26V693000, and the customer line is 1-855-371-9460.[3] A five-minute call beats a wheel you never saw coming.
