Chrysler Filed 5 Recalls for Its 2026 Minivans. One Names a Car That Doesn’t Exist

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 15, 2026

I ran the census on every 2026 Chrysler. Sixteen queries against NHTSA’s recalls API, three canonical models from VPIC, every result deduped by campaign number and filtered to filings from calendar 2026, and five campaigns touch a 2026-model-year Chrysler. One of them names a car that does not exist.[1]

8
Plug-in hybrid vans in recall 26V561, the eight vehicles NHTSA’s summary calls “Voyager Plug-In Hybrids” — a car Chrysler has never built

Start with the biggest number, because it is the least Chrysler-specific. Recall 26V531, filed August 13, covers 844,027 vehicles whose Uconnect5 radios can blank the rearview camera, and every single one of them is defective by the filing’s own math: a 100 percent defect estimate. Chrysler’s share is 131,681 Pacificas, 15.6 percent of a recall that spans 15 model lines across four Stellantis brands.[2] Your minivan shares a radio with a Ram 1500, and the radio can forget the camera exists. Fix is software, dealer or over the air, free, and it is the largest single campaign in the entire census series.

Then the airbags: recall 26V189, filed March 26, covers Pacifica and Voyager vans built from May 2022 through August 2025 whose curtain airbags may not hold pressure in a crash, because the seams were improperly sealed. Autoliv made the bags. NHTSA’s letter announced 178,246 vans; the Part 573 filing that followed lists 293,264, a 64 percent expansion after vehicles migrated out of two earlier recalls, 25V302 and 25V573. Thirty percent are estimated to carry the defect.[3] If your van was already repaired under one of those 2025 recalls, check the VIN again: migrated vehicles get the new remedy, both curtain airbags replaced.

Then the small one, and the strange one. Recall 26V561, filed September 1, covers 2,017 Pacificas and Voyagers built between June and October 2025: a cracked capacitor inside the brake pedal position sensor can knock out electronic stability control, a violation of FMVSS 126. Supplier is CTS Corporation of Elkhart, Indiana. Ten percent are estimated to carry the defect.[4] And here is the part worth reading twice. NHTSA’s summary of the recall lists a “2026 Chrysler Voyager Plug-In Hybrid Electric” among the affected vehicles. No such car exists. Voyager is the gas-only fleet sibling; only the Pacifica comes as a plug-in hybrid. All eight plug-in hybrids in the recall are Pacificas, built September 11 to October 25, 2025. Part 573 is right, and NHTSA’s summary is wrong. Eight vans, 0.4 percent of the recall, and the federal database still managed to invent a model.

Two of the five were not filed by Chrysler at all. BraunAbility, which converts Pacificas and Voyagers into wheelchair-accessible vans, filed both. Recall 26V296, filed May 12, covers 476 converted Pacificas whose Q’Straint wheelchair retractors may not lock, which means a wheelchair can move during transit; Braun estimates about 2 percent carry the defect.[5] Recall 26V358, filed June 3, covers conversions built over fifteen days in May 2026 with the wrong spring inside the front-seat harness connector, a spring that can touch the terminals and impair the airbags and seat-belt pretensioners.[6] Fix is a thirty-minute inspection. Chrysler’s minivan is the default wheelchair van in America, and its recall year is partly a conversion company’s recall year.

One model carries a zero, and the zero comes with an asterisk. VPIC lists the Grand Caravan as a 2026 Chrysler, and it has no recalls at all, because you cannot buy one in the United States. It is a Canada-only nameplate, revived for the Canadian market. Zero campaigns, zero vehicles, zero relevance to an American buyer, and it still counts as a third of Chrysler’s lineup on paper. For scale, Stellantis filed 27 campaigns touching a 2026 model year across its brands: Jeep 12, Ram 11, Chrysler 5, Dodge 2, Alfa Romeo 1, Fiat 0.[7] Chrysler sits in the middle, and three of its five are Chrysler-only.

Fair objection: five recalls is a middling count, and the count mixes three different kinds of thing. A shared radio bug says almost nothing about Chrysler’s minivans. A genuine Chrysler-specific defect with a federal-standard violation attached sits in the same count. And two of the five belong to a conversion company and a few hundred wheelchair vans. That reading is not wrong, but the mix is the point. A 2026 Chrysler buyer faces one airbag recall that grew 64 percent after filing, one camera recall shared with a million trucks, one ESC recall with a phantom car in the paperwork, and two conversion recalls that exist only because the minivan is the default wheelchair van in America. No single number captures that, and five does not either, but five with the table attached comes closer.

What this census does not prove: no FARS cross-tab is meaningful here, because these are 2026 models with no fatal-crash history yet, and a campaign count says nothing about injury rates. For 26V296, the 476-van figure comes from the Part 573 filing via MoparInsiders; one outlet reports 1,571 for the same campaign, and the filing itself was not directly verified this run. No unit count was available for 26V358, only the May 7 to 22 conversion window. Defect percentages are the manufacturers’ estimates. Campaigns filed after November 15 are not counted, and the excluded sweep reached back to model year 2019.

If you own a 2022–2026 Pacifica or Voyager, 26V189 is the one that matters: check the VIN, and if your van was repaired under the 2025 airbag recalls, check it again. If you own a 2026 Pacifica or Voyager, 26V531: shift into reverse and watch the screen, and if it goes black, the fix is a software update, free, over the air or at the dealer. If your van was built between June and October 2025, 26V561: the ESC warning light is the tell, and only 2,017 vans are in it, so the VIN check takes thirty seconds. If you own a BraunAbility conversion, 26V296 and 26V358: call BraunAbility at 1-800-488-0359 with your VIN, because both fixes are free inspections. And if you own a 2020–2022 Pacifica plug-in hybrid, 26V362 is not in this census, because it touches no 2026 car, but it is the one that matters to you: battery fire risk, park outside, do not charge, 17,277 vans.
