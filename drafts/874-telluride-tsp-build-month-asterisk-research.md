# Research Notes — #874: Telluride TSP+ Build-Month Asterisk

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The 2027 Kia Telluride just won IIHS's highest award (Sept 3, 2026) — but the Top Safety Pick+ applies ONLY to vehicles built after June 2026, when Kia fixed the headlights. A shopper buying a "2027 Telluride, IIHS award winner" could drive home a May build with Marginal headlights. The award is on the model, not necessarily on the car on the lot.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes — announced one week ago, concrete consumer trap nobody is explaining at the point of sale.
- **Novel after 800+ articles?** Yes. Build-month-dependent IIHS awards + FARS cross-tab of the outgoing generation + the batch-wide pattern (vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention, not structure, is the new award killer) have not been covered. Prior Telluride stories (fire recalls, belt recalls) covered defects, not awards.
- **Verdict: PROCEED.** Journalist: Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate). Kicker: The Gap.

## Key facts (verified)
- IIHS fourth-wave 2026 testing: 7 models (Kia, Tesla, BMW, GMC, Lexus, Toyota). Only 2 earned TSP+.
- Telluride TSP+ requires post-June-2026 build; pre-June builds have Marginal headlights. Headlights are trim-level equipment — the fix was a running production change, not a redesign.
- Model Y's only blemish: Marginal seat-belt-reminder effectiveness. (Tesla Model Y already covered by this site as FARS-safest; award is consistent with real-world data.)
- The five failures: BMW 3 Series (Acceptable moderate overlap + headlights; Marginal V2V prevention), BMW i4 (Marginal/Poor headlights; Poor V2V prevention), GMC Canyon (Marginal moderate overlap; Poor V2V prevention), Lexus IS (Marginal moderate overlap; Acceptable side), Toyota Crown Signia (Marginal moderate overlap).
- Pattern: 4 of 5 failures involve the UPDATED vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention test or the toughened moderate overlap test. The barrier moved from crash structure to sensors/software and rear-seat protection.
- IIHS: ~50,000 lives saved attributed to its testing program (per Autoblog citing IIHS research).

## Original contribution
1. FARS cross-tab: the outgoing-generation Telluride's real-world 0.04 rate validates the award — the lab and the road agree, which makes the build-month asterisk the actual trap rather than the award itself.
2. The batch-wide audit: V2V prevention failures (not structural crashes) disqualified 3 of 5 models — the award barrier has moved to AEB software.
3. Consumer mechanism: IIHS awards are model-year + build-date specific; dealer listings and window stickers advertise "IIHS Top Safety Pick+" without the build-date qualifier. Actionable: check the door-jamb build-date sticker; look up the specific build on iihs.org/ratings.

## Limitations (for the article)
- FARS data covers 2014-2023: the 0.04 Telluride rate reflects the FIRST-generation Telluride (2020-2025), NOT the redesigned 2027 model that won the award. Real-world performance of the 2027 redesign is unknown; the award is lab-only evidence for the new car.
- FARS rate uses estimated VMT (not odometer readings); ±15% uncertainty for low-volume models.
- Lexus IS, BMW i4, Toyota Crown Signia have no FARS rows in this dataset (low counts/newer), so no real-world cross-check for them.
- Marginal headlights ≠ unsafe car; it means degraded nighttime visibility vs. Good-rated lamps. Say exactly that.

## Strongest counterargument
The build-month qualifier is standard IIHS practice (running production changes get separate ratings all the time), and a May-built Telluride is still a structurally Good-rated SUV that would protect its occupants in a crash. The asterisk is a headlight rating, not a death sentence — and Kia fixed it mid-year, which is the system working. Acknowledge: this is a buyer's-guide nuance, not a safety scandal.

## Actionable takeaways (Clara voice)
- Buying a 2027 Telluride? Open the driver's door, read the build-date sticker on the jamb. After June 2026 = award spec. Before = Marginal headlights.
- Cross-check any "IIHS award winner" claim on iihs.org/ratings with the exact build date, not just the model year.
- For the 5 failed models: the disqualifiers are AEB software and rear-seat dummies, not crumple zones — test-drive the driver-assist systems, not just the crash structure.
- VIN recall check at nhtsa.gov/recalls before purchase regardless.
