# Research — #1327: The Work Trucks That Can't See Motorcycles (IIHS First-Ever Commercial Crash-Avoidance Ratings)

## Angle
IIHS published its first-ever front crash prevention ratings for commercial vehicles on Oct 8, 2026, and America's work trucks face-planted. None of the four Class 3 pickups tested (Ford F-350, Chevy Silverado 3500HD, GMC Sierra 3500HD, Ram 3500) earned a Good rating in either the vehicle-to-vehicle or pedestrian test. The specific failure mode is delicious and terrifying: several systems didn't even issue a warning in motorcycle trials. Meanwhile 6,215 people died in 2024 in crashes involving medium/heavy-duty vehicles or light vans — 16% of all US roadway deaths — and the federal AEB rule that covers passenger cars doesn't touch these trucks. The blind spot is regulatory, not just technological.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? Yes: first-ever IIHS commercial crash-avoidance program (launched June 2026, first ratings Oct 8, 2026, follow-up Oct 22). Novel vs 1,300-article queue: nobody has covered Class 3 commercial AEB. Angle: regulatory gap + motorcycle invisibility + the discontinued BrightDrop irony. Proceed.

## Key facts (verified across 3+ sources)
1. **Program**: IIHS released first vehicle-to-vehicle and pedestrian front crash prevention ratings for cargo vans and Class 3 pickups (GVWR 10,001–14,000 lbs) on Oct 8, 2026. Same protocols and good/acceptable/marginal/poor scale as passenger vehicles. IIHS President David Harkey: "a wake-up call for commercial vehicle makers." (IIHS via Autoblog 10/8, Safety+Health 10/16, Motor.com/IIHS 10/19, EZ Tech 10/9)
2. **Pickups — zero Good ratings**: Ford F-350 (Poor v2v / Acceptable ped), Chevrolet Silverado 3500HD (Poor / Marginal), GMC Sierra 3500HD (Poor / Marginal), Ram 3500 (Marginal / Acceptable). Nine of 11 vans+pickups got substandard v2v ratings: seven Poor, two Marginal. (Safety+Health 10/16, Autoblog 10/8, Automotive-Fleet 10/22)
3. **Motorcycle detection was the toughest challenge**: "Several systems did not issue warnings or slow at all in most of the motorcycle trials." Test includes warnings and braking against car and motorcycle targets at multiple speeds, plus warnings for a semitrailer. (IIHS via ClaimsJournal 10/8, TunedDrive 10/8)
4. **Speed-specific failures**: F-350 could only avoid a passenger-car target at 31 mph; in four higher-speed tests it failed, and at 43 mph it didn't reduce speed at all. Silverado 3500HD failed to avoid the passenger car in all six tests. (Autoblog 10/8)
5. **Pedestrian/child paradox**: BrightDrop 400 and Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 earned Good pedestrian ratings; Sprinter 2500, F-350, Ram 3500 Acceptable. Some trucks had more trouble detecting a child dummy in daylight than an adult at night — possibly the tall front ends vs short dummy. Express 2500 and Savana 2500: Poor/Poor, no AEB (optional forward collision warning only). (IIHS via ClaimsJournal, TunedDrive, Automotive-Fleet)
6. **Scale of the exposure**: 6,215 people killed in 2024 in crashes involving medium- or heavy-duty vehicles or light vans — 16% of US roadway fatalities (NHTSA data via IIHS). Automatic emergency braking requirements for new passenger cars don't apply to these commercial trucks and vans. As of Oct 12, no manufacturer had announced a fix. (IIHS via Automotive-Files/YouTube transcript, ClaimsJournal)
7. **Program trajectory**: IIHS commercial vehicle program launched June 2026 with driver protection; will add headlight and rear AEB ratings for Class 3 this year and next; Class 4–6 trucks phased in later. (IIHS via EZ Tech, Motor.com)
8. **Irony**: Chevrolet ended BrightDrop 400 production in October 2025, per reporting on the ratings — one of the two vehicles to earn a Good pedestrian rating is discontinued. (espresso.cafecito.tech 10/13 — secondary source, attribute or drop)
9. **Adjacent**: Oct 22, 2026 follow-up on small SUVs in the updated moderate-overlap test (rear seat dummy): Nissan Kicks earned Good + Top Safety Pick after June 2026 rear seat belt change; 2026 RAV4 Plug-in Marginal; Audi Q3 and Chevy Trailblazer Poor. (MotorBiscuit 10/26) — background color only, not the lead.

## Limitations / counterarguments (must state at full strength)
- IIHS measured behavior under its own protocol, not field crash rates on public roads; no exposure denominator (crashes per mile) reported for these specific vehicles.
- The 6,215 figure is fleet-wide context (all medium/heavy-duty + light vans), not the tested vehicles specifically.
- Missing AEB on Express/Savana is a feature absence, not a defect; those are archaic fleet specials, not consumer flagships.
- Test vehicles are MY2025–2027; manufacturers may have already changed software/hardware.
- "No manufacturer announced a fix as of Oct 12" — four days after release; fixes may exist now.

## Actionable takeaways
- Fleet buyers: demand the IIHS commercial crash-avoidance ratings before spec'ing Class 3 trucks; the Sprinter 2500 is currently the only Good v2v performer; BrightDrop/Rivian lead pedestrian detection.
- Motorcycle riders: assume heavy pickups cannot see you — the systems sometimes don't even warn the driver.
- Parents near school bus/work zones: these trucks struggle with child-size dummies in daylight; give them extra space.
- Check NHTSA recalls by VIN; AEB is not federally required on these trucks, so don't assume it's there.
