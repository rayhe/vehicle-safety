# Research: #1029 — The Rollover Paradox

**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread)

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The deadliest trick a car can perform is falling over: 6,250 passenger-vehicle occupants died in rollover crashes in 2024, 28% of all occupant deaths, and when a crash involves only one vehicle, the car kills its occupants by rolling over 47% of the time. ESC fixed most of this (72-75% fatal rollover risk reduction), yet pickups' rollover share hasn't moved in 46 years.

## Core data (all IIHS Fatality Facts 2024 unless noted)
- 6,250 passenger-vehicle occupants died in rollover crashes in 2024 (vehicle tips onto side/roof at ANY point in crash).
- Rollover crashes = 28% of all passenger-vehicle occupant deaths (6,250 / 22,713).
- Single-vehicle crashes: 47% of occupant deaths involved rollover (4,547 / 9,685). Multiple-vehicle: 13% (1,703 / 13,028).
- 22% of fatal rollovers had NO prior impact (1,375 / 6,250) — pure loss of control.
- Rollover share of occupant deaths by vehicle type: cars 20% (2,412), pickups 38% (1,553), SUVs 33% (2,200).
- Single-vehicle rollover share: cars 16% (1,831), pickups 29% (1,183), SUVs 22% (1,483).
- Very large pickups: 42% of occupant deaths are single-vehicle rollovers (253/596); drivers 43% (201/470).
- Historical: 2002 peak 10,729 rollover deaths (33% of occupant deaths) -> 2024: 6,250 (28%). Single-vehicle rollover deaths: 2005 peak 8,770 (57%) -> 2024: 4,547 (47%).
- Pickup rollover share: 1978: 39% (1,968/5,066) -> 2024: 38% (1,553/4,034). Flat for 46 years.
- SUV rollover share: 1978: 62% -> 2024: 33%. (ESC worked, mostly on SUVs.)
- ESC: -72% fatal single-vehicle rollover risk (cars), -75% (SUVs). NHTSA: 4,100 lives saved 2010-14. Standard equipment 2012+ per FMVSS 126 (2007 rule).

## Original calculations
1. (10,729 - 6,250) / 10,729 = 41.7% absolute decline in rollover deaths, 2002 -> 2024; share fell only 33% -> 28% (5 points).
2. Pickup rollover share 39% -> 38% over 46 years: the one vehicle class the rollover story forgot.
3. Very-large pickup: 253/596 = 42.4% of occupant deaths are single-vehicle rollovers — the single biggest slice of how the biggest trucks kill.
4. 1,375 no-prior-impact rollovers = 22%: the car fell over all by itself.

## Strongest counterargument
The 28% share partly reflects success elsewhere: frontal airbags, side curtains, and stronger structures made non-rollover crashes far more survivable, so rollover is a bigger slice of a smaller pie. Heavier pickups/SUVs also survive multi-vehicle crashes better, mechanically inflating their rollover share. The improvement is real (ESC), but the denominator moved too.

## Limitations
- IIHS "rollover" = tips onto side/roof at ANY time during the crash, not necessarily the initial impact; overlaps with frontal/side categories.
- FARS is a census of FATAL crashes only; no per-crash lethality rate computable without CRSS exposure data.
- 1978 vs 2024 fleet comparison spans a totally different vehicle mix (SUVs barely existed in 1978).
- Ejection not separated in these tables; belt status not cross-tabulated here.
- Single-year (2024) snapshot for the headline numbers.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Shopping used: 2012+ model years have ESC standard (FMVSS 126). A pre-2012 pickup without ESC is the worst combination in this dataset.
- Check the NHTSA rollover resistance star rating at nhtsa.gov/ratings before buying; it measures exactly this failure mode.
- Very-large pickup shoppers: single-vehicle rollover is your dominant fatal threat (42%), not the crash with the other guy.
- 22% no-impact rollovers = loss of control on curves; speed is the input you control.
