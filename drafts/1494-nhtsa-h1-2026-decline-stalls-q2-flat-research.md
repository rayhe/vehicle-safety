## Story in one line
NHTSA finally published its first-half 2026 early estimate (DOT HS 813 875, Nov 10, 2026): 16,750 deaths, down 2.5%, rate 1.02, the second-lowest half-year rate since 2014. The headline is good news. The quarters underneath it are not: Q1 fell 5.0%, Q2 fell 0.2%. The 16-quarter decline streak survived the second quarter by roughly 20 deaths.

## Key verified facts (from DOT HS 813 875)
- **H1 2026:** estimated 16,750 deaths, down about 2.5% from the 17,175 projected for H1 2025. Lowest H1 death count since 2015 (Reuters).
- **Rate:** 1.02 fatalities per 100 million VMT, down from 1.05 in H1 2025. Second-lowest first-half rate since at least 2014.
- **Miles:** VMT rose an estimated 12.9 billion miles, +0.8%. Deaths fell while driving rose.
- **The quarters:** Q1 2026: 7,715 deaths, down 5.0%. Q2 2026: 9,035 deaths, down 0.2%. The entire half-year improvement came in Q1; Q2 was effectively flat.
- **Breadth:** 8 of 10 NHTSA regions down. 35 states + Puerto Rico down; 15 states + District of Columbia up.
- **Full-year 2025 (revised context):** 36,640 deaths, down 6.7% from 2024, rate 1.10, second-lowest yearly figure ever.
- **Next release:** projections for the first nine months of 2026 due in late December 2026.

## Original calculations (for the article)
- **Streak margin:** Q2 2025 projected deaths were 9,055; Q2 2026 is 9,035. The decline is 20 deaths, 0.2%. The 16-quarter streak of consecutive quarterly declines (running since Q2 2022, per the Q1 2026 report) survived Q2 by 20 deaths out of 9,055.
- **Deceleration within the half:** Q1 improved 5.0%; Q2 improved 0.2%. The improvement rate fell 96% between quarters ((5.0-0.2)/5.0).
- **Revision trail:** the Q1 2026 report (DOT HS 813 833) put Q1 at 7,770 deaths, down 4.3%, rate 0.99. The H1 report revises Q1 to 7,715, down 5.0%. Q1 actually looks slightly better on revision; the stall is entirely a Q2 story. Similarly H1 2025 was 17,140 in the earlier release and is now projected at 17,175. Small revisions, same direction.
- **Cross-series check (do not mix in one sentence):** NSC's H1 2026 estimate (Sept 9, 2026, covered in #1402) was 18,460 deaths, down 2%, rate 1.12. NHTSA says 16,750, down 2.5%, rate 1.02. Different methods (NSC counts deaths within 100 days and includes private roadways; NHTSA/FARS counts 30 days on public roads only), different levels, same verdict: the decline has slowed to roughly 2-2.5% from 8-13% a year ago.
- **Counterfactual (label as rough):** had H1 2025's 8.2% NHTSA pace held, H1 2026 would have come in near 15,767 (17,175 x 0.918). The actual 16,750 is about 983 deaths above that pace. Label explicitly as arithmetic, not a forecast.

## State detail
- Texas had the largest estimated death count: 1,851, essentially flat vs 1,856 in H1 2025, rate 1.19 vs 1.02 nationally. California 1,825, Florida 1,439.
- 15 states + DC rose in H1 2026. NHTSA's state estimates carry 5-10% uncertainty; do not write a single-state verdict off a half-year projection.

## Limitations (must appear in the article)
- All H1 2026 numbers are preliminary statistical projections, not final counts. They will be revised when the FARS Annual Reporting File for 2025 lands later this year and the Final File for 2025 and ARF for 2026 arrive next year.
- NSC and NHTSA count different things (100-day vs 30-day window; private roadways included vs public roads only). Never mix the two series in a single sentence without saying which is which. The deceleration claim is built within the NHTSA series (Q1 -5.0%, Q2 -0.2%), which is methodologically consistent.
- Q2's 0.2% is a projection of a projection: Q2 2025's 9,055 is itself a projected number. The "20 deaths" margin is within the noise of the estimation method. The honest phrasing is "effectively flat," not "exactly 20."
- The streak claim (16 consecutive quarterly declines since Q2 2022) comes from NHTSA's Q1 2026 report; the H1 report does not restate the streak count. Date the claim.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
A 2.5% decline is still a decline, the rate is the second-lowest ever recorded for a first half, deaths fell while miles rose, and the streak is intact. A single quarter of near-flat performance after the largest first-half drop since 2008 (H1 2025, -8.2%) could be regression to the mean: you cannot fall 8% every half-year forever, because the denominator keeps shrinking and the easy wins get used up. The article must grant this fully, then answer it with the within-half math: the improvement did not just slow, it nearly stopped in Q2, and the breadth is narrowing (15 states + DC up). A streak that survives by 20 deaths is a streak on paper; it is not momentum.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- The national trend no longer does your safety thinking for you. Q2 was flat. Check your own state's trend, not the national headline: 15 states + DC got worse in H1 2026.
- If you drive in Texas, the largest death count in the country (1,851) was flat year over year while the national rate fell. A 1.19 fatality rate against a 1.02 national rate is a 17% worse risk per mile.
- The next data point lands in late December (first nine months of 2026). That release, not this one, will say whether Q2 was a pause or the end of the streak.
