# Research: #917 — "The NTSB Says Toll Booths Have to Die"

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — fatality investigations, paradoxes)

**Date:** 2026-09-17

## News peg (1-2 sentences)
On September 16, 2026, the NTSB issued formal recommendations (H-26-10 through H-26-18) telling the Ohio Turnpike and Infrastructure Commission to permanently close low-speed tolling lanes and implement fully electronic tolling at four named toll plazas, and telling the Federal Highway Administration to adopt national guidance on the safety benefits of electronic tolling. The trigger: an August 15, 2024 crash at the Swanton Township toll plaza on I-80 where a Toyota Highlander slowed across the path of a Mack truck-trailer while diving for a manual toll booth, killing two passengers, seriously injuring two more people plus the truck driver, and spilling sulfonic acid across the interstate.

## Key facts (all sourced)
- Aug 15, 2024, ~5:29-5:34 a.m. EDT: 2015 Toyota Highlander (driver + 3 passengers) eastbound on the Ohio Turnpike (I-80), milepost 49, approaching the Swanton Township Toll Plaza. The plaza's eastbound approach: two high-speed toll lanes (70 mph limit) + two lanes designated for toll-booth access, separated by a painted gore area transitioning to a concrete barrier wall. [NTSB docket]
- The SUV decelerated and changed lanes across the path of a 2019 Mack combination vehicle (truck-tractor + 2009 Great Dane van semitrailer). The truck struck the SUV from behind, veered right, hit the concrete barrier, and overturned. The SUV came to rest in a culvert. [NTSB docket]
- Two SUV passengers died; the driver and one passenger were seriously injured; the truck driver was seriously injured. The truck's sulfonic-acid cargo spilled; no exposure injuries were reported. [NTSB press release]
- Video review of the plaza over three weeks showed **1,099 similar last-minute lane changes, an average of 52 per day**, with vehicles illegally traveling through the painted gore between high-speed and low-speed lanes. [NTSB press release]
- NTSB probable cause: the SUV driver's deceleration and lane change. Contributing: driver fatigue (reduced situation awareness); the lack of signage stating that missed tolls could be paid online. Contributing to severity: the large speed differential; the lack of seat belt use by several occupants. [NTSB investigation page]
- Recommendations: H-26-10 (FHWA: update MUTCD toll-facility guidance incl. signage on fully electronic options); H-26-11 (FHWA: guidance on safety benefits of fully electronic tolling); H-26-12/13/14 (Ohio Turnpike + Springfield Township: expedite emergency access point, systemwide evaluation, build them); H-26-15 (safety improvements at Westgate, Swanton Township, Newton Falls, Eastgate toll plazas); H-26-16 (fully electronic high-speed tolling and **permanent closure of low-speed lanes** at those four plazas, with a timeframe); H-26-17 (Springfield Township: expedite access point); H-26-18 (IBTTA: inform members). Reiterated: H-15-42 (Ohio: enact primary-enforcement seat-belt law for all seating positions). [NTSB investigation page]
- "This crash shows the deadly safety consequences of mixing high-speed electronic tolling with low-speed manual tolling lanes." — NTSB Chairwoman Jennifer Homendy. [NTSB press release]
- Illinois and Pennsylvania have already implemented fully electronic tolling on their portions of the I-80 turnpike. [NTSB press release]
- Investigators found emergency responders were delayed by lack of direct access to the toll road (would not likely have changed survivability). [NTSB press release]

## The novel thread (original contribution)
Three pieces of original arithmetic/framing nobody has published on this story:
1. **The near-miss ratio.** 1,099 illegal last-minute lane changes in 21 days = 52/day = roughly one every 27 minutes, day and night. The August 15 crash was not a freak event; it was one fatal outcome drawn from a pool of ~1,100 near-identical maneuvers in three weeks. The plaza was running a lottery where the losing ticket was a Mack truck.
2. **The speed differential as the engineering constant.** The plaza's own geometry builds a 70-mph-to-near-zero differential into the interstate. NTSB's severity finding is not driver-behavior trivia; it is the physics the plaza manufactures: a 70 mph truck has ~2.5 seconds to perceive and react to a vehicle decelerating into its lane at 5:29 a.m.
3. **The cascade.** The initial crash seeded traffic queues in which further crashes occurred (NTSB docket); contemporaneous reporting put that morning's Ohio Turnpike toll at 4 dead across three crashes and 13 vehicles. The death count of the toll-booth system that morning was 4, not 2.

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
The NTSB pinned probable cause on the SUV driver's deceleration and lane change, with fatigue as the likely reason — not on the toll plaza. A tired driver can drift across any lane line at 5:29 a.m. whether or not there is a booth ahead. The 1,099 lane changes are evidence of a lot of drivers who executed the maneuver just fine. The deadliest variable in the cabin may be the one the NTSB has been reiterating since 2014: the passengers who died were not wearing seat belts, and belted occupants in the same SUV survived a collision with an 80,000-pound truck. You can demolish every toll booth in Ohio and still lose unbelted, fatigued drivers to the interstate. And electronic tolling has its own losers: unbanked drivers, out-of-state tourists, and everyone being tracked by plate readers instead of cash.

## Limitations
- NTSB documents only. The Ohio Turnpike and Infrastructure Commission's response to H-26-16 (whether it will comply, and on what timeframe) was not available at research time.
- No FARS rate claim on toll plazas is made: FARS has no toll-plaza exposure denominator, so no per-mile fatality rate is computed here.
- The cascade fatality count (4 across three crashes) comes from contemporaneous cdllife reporting, not the NTSB final report; the docket confirms subsequent queue crashes with injuries/fatalities but does not enumerate them.
- The 2.5-second reaction estimate is illustrative kinematics, not from the NTSB report.
- Injury/death attribution to seat-belt non-use is the NTSB's finding ("could have prevented their injuries and death"), not an independent analysis.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you drive a hybrid toll plaza (Ohio Turnpike, New Jersey Turnpike, anywhere with cash lanes beside high-speed lanes): missing your toll is not an emergency. You can pay online; that is exactly what the missing signage at Swanton failed to tell the Highlander driver. Do not cross a painted gore at speed.
- Buckle every passenger in every seat. The two people who died in this crash were unbelted; the belted occupants survived. Ohio still lacks primary-enforcement seat-belt coverage for all seating positions (NTSB has been asking since 2014).
- The general rule: treat any plaza that mixes 70-mph lanes with 0-mph booths as a construction zone with no warning signs, because geometrically that is what it is.
