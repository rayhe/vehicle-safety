Chrysler Recalled the Airbags. Then It Recalled the Replacement Airbags.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 4, 2026

Start with a parts bin, not a crash. FCA spent ten months recalling Chrysler Pacifica and Voyager minivans for side curtain airbags that lose pressure through badly sealed seams. Then, on March 26, 2026, it filed one more recall, and this one was not for a van at all. It was for 105 loose Mopar replacement curtain airbags, the parts meant to fix the defect, carrying the same defect.[1]

105
Mopar replacement curtain airbags recalled under NHTSA 26E-014, with an estimated 30 percent carrying the same improperly sealed seams as the vans they were meant to repair

Follow the paperwork in order. FCA filed the first two recalls on May 8, 2025: 25V302 for the vehicles and 25E033 for the equipment, both covering 2022-2025 Pacificas and Voyagers.[1] NHTSA then tested a 2025 Pacifica in June 2025, and both curtain airbags failed an excursion-limit requirement in the ejection-mitigation test.[1] FCA opened an investigation on July 31, 2025, and filed a third recall, 25V573, on September 4, 2025, for 2025 models traced through airbag material lots.[1] A fourth vehicle round, 26V189, followed in March 2026 with 178,246 Pacifica and Voyager vehicles from model years 2022-2026.[3] And in that same month, FCA filed 26E-014 for the parts bin.[1]

Autoliv ASP of Auburn Hills built all of them, and every filing names the same fault: seams sealed badly enough that the curtain loses pressure during deployment.[1] A curtain that deflates early protects less and raises the risk of partial or complete ejection through a side window, which is exactly what Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 226 exists to prevent.[5] FCA estimates 30 percent of the 105 parts carry the defect, roughly 32 airbags.[1] That share runs higher than the first vehicle population, where about 14 percent of 250,651 vans were estimated affected.[5] Scale is the only thing that shrank: 105 parts against 178,246 vehicles in the March 2026 vehicle round works out to about one part for every 1,698 vans.[1][3]

Remedy splits on one question: is the part still in the box? FCA will repurchase any recalled airbag that has not been installed, and dealers may not legally deliver or sell parts in the recall.[2] If a recalled part already went into a van, dealers replace it free, 1.4 hours for one side and 1.6 for both, routing vans already under a vehicle recall through that campaign's instructions.[2] FCA knew of zero accidents, injuries, warranty claims, or field reports tied to these 105 parts as of March 5, 2026.[1] Credit belongs where it is due: supplier manufacturing records drew the suspect window, October 25, 2024 through July 14, 2025, and that paper trail is what caught parts that had never been bolted into anything.[1]

Limits matter here. FCA has not said whether any of the 105 parts were actually installed before the recall, only that its instructions cover both cases.[2] Vehicle recall populations overlap across the four filings, so adding 250,651 and 178,246 into one grand total would double-count vans.[1][3] If you own a 2022-2026 Pacifica or Voyager, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V189 and any earlier open curtain-airbag campaign, and get the free repair. If a shop already replaced your curtain airbag, ask whether the installed part was Mopar 68224526AJ or 68224527AH from the suspect window, and whether it falls under 07D. A recall that reaches the parts bin is still a recall, and a fixed van with a defective fix is not fixed.
