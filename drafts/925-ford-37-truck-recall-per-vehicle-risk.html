By The Numbers

37 Trucks Have the Wrong Bearings, and Per Vehicle That's This Week's Worst Recall.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
September 17, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again; they didn't get better. Ford filed four recall campaigns in a single batch this week, and every newsroom covered the big one: 223,472 F-150s with fuel tanks that can leak or detach. The one with the scariest failure mode covers 37 trucks. A seized rear axle bearing can lock your rear wheels at highway speed, or separate the driveshaft and let the truck roll away from its parking spot.

37
2026 F-150s in the axle-bearing recall, campaign 26V576, with the stabilizer-rod recall beside it covering just 17.

Campaign 26V576, Ford number 26S63, is a precision strike of a recall. Every affected truck is a 2026 F-150 with the Max Tow package, built on May 27 or 28, 2026. At Ford's Sterling Axle Plant, 8.8-inch rear axle tail bearings went in where 9.75-inch bearings were required — a 0.95-inch shortfall on the exact package engineered to pull the heaviest loads. That is the part of this story worth reading twice: the axle built for maximum towing got a bearing that didn't meet the standard for the job it exists to do.

A worker on the Sterling line spotted the wrong bearings going in on May 26. Dearborn issued a stop-ship on June 3, by which point 38 trucks had escaped, 37 sold in the United States. Credit where due: that is the quality system working, a human on the line catching a parts-bin error within days. Then the calendar does something less flattering: field action approved September 1, owner notification letters going out September 21. From the build date to the letter: roughly sixteen weeks, for a defect with no remedy ambiguity at all — a dealer swaps the bearing, free of charge. Small recalls have near-100% defect rates in the affected population; the 223,472-truck fuel tank campaign spreads its defect fraction across five model years. Per affected vehicle, 26V576 is the worst bet in the batch.

Its sibling campaign, 26V577 (Ford 26S66), covers 17 trucks whose rear stabilizer rod fasteners were never tightened properly on the line. A loose rod shifts, handling degrades, and you lose a fraction of the control you'd want in a truck this size. Seventeen vehicles that nobody will write the think piece about. That is precisely the pattern worth noticing: recall coverage scales with population, but per-vehicle severity often runs the other way.

Two honest caveats, because the numbers demand them. First, Ford reports zero accidents or injuries from either campaign, and ranking failure modes by worst-case consequence is an editorial judgment, not epidemiology — the big fuel-tank recall may well cause more total harm. Second, this isn't a FARS story. A 54-truck defect population has no fatality data and no model-year trend; it's a recall-data story, and it says nothing about whether your 2024 F-150 is safe. What it does say is that if you bought a 2026 F-150 with the Max Tow package this summer, your VIN has been searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls since September 14 under campaign 26V576. Check it before the September 21 letter finds you. If your truck is one of the 37, park with the parking brake engaged until the dealer swaps the bearing — a separated driveshaft can let a truck roll away from park. And the general rule, for free: small recalls are the ones where nearly every listed vehicle actually has the defect. Check those first, not last.
