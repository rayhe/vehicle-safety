The Drunkest Pickup in America Is the One Nissan Just Killed

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
October 1, 2026

According to the toxicology reports — and there are a lot of them — the drunkest full-size pickup in America is dead. Nissan ended Titan production in 2024 after a 20-year run, and the truck took its title with it: 23.9% of Titan drivers in fatal crashes tested impaired, the highest rate of any modern big pickup in the federal database.[1][3] Nearly one in four. The next closest truck does not even make it within three points.

23.9%
Titan drivers in fatal crashes who tested impaired. Highest rate of any modern big pickup

The standings: GMC Sierra 21.0%, Chevrolet Silverado 20.6%, Toyota Tundra 19.6%, Dodge Ram 19.1%, Ford F-150 18.9%.[1] The F-150 is the one that should interest you. Twenty-one thousand fatal-crash drivers, the largest sample of any truck in the database, and it posts the soberest number of the six. Below the 20% national baseline.[1] The most-crashed truck in America is driven by the least-impaired drivers. Ponder that over your next bar napkin.

Now the part that ruins the punchline. The Titan's fatality rate is 0.57 deaths per 100 million miles, the lowest of the six. At 1.25, the Silverado sits more than double that, with the Sierra at 1.01 and the F-150 at 1.04.[2] So the truck with the most impaired drivers kills the fewest per mile. Drunk drivers, safe truck. Impairment concentration and crash lethality are two different problems that happen to wear the same badge.

Before anyone concludes that impaired drivers should buy Titans, the boring truth: impairment tracks the buyer, not the badge. Titan buyers skewed work-truck, rural, and younger-male, which is exactly the demographic profile that dominates impaired-driving statistics. And “alcohol-positive” in this dataset means any BAC above zero, not above the 0.08 legal limit. Some of these drivers were under the limit. FARS only records fatal crashes, so this is impairment among people who died or killed, not a survey of your neighbor's tailgate.

Since the truck is discontinued, this is a used-market story now. Titan depreciation is brutal: a well-optioned 2021 SV runs around $26,000 against $40,000-plus competitors. The per-mile safety record is genuinely the class's best.[4] Buy one if you want a bargain. Get a pre-purchase inspection, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and if your friend in a Titan offers to drive home at 1 AM, look at his eyes and offer to drive instead.
