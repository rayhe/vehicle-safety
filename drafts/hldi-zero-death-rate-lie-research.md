# Research: The HLDI Zero-Death List Is a Body Count Shell Game

## Hook
IIHS/HLDI published 2023 driver death rates per million registration years (reported by CarBuzz, Jul 30 2026). Six models hit a death rate of ZERO: Audi Q7, BMW X7, Chevy Tahoe 4WD, Mercedes-Benz GLB-Class, Mercedes-Benz GLE-Class, Toyota Tundra CrewMax 4WD. All heavy SUVs/trucks.

But these vehicles didn't stop killing. They stopped dying.

## ORIGINAL ANALYSIS: Cross-tab of "zero-death" vehicles against 10 years of FARS crash data

The HLDI study measures "driver death rate" — deaths of the vehicle's OWN driver per million registration years. It doesn't count the deaths the vehicle causes in OTHER vehicles.

Our FARS data (2014-2023) shows these "zero-death" vehicles were still involved in THOUSANDS of fatal crashes:

| Vehicle | Occupant Deaths | Total Fatal Crashes | "Death Export" Rate |
|---------|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Audi Q7 | 29 | 92 | 68.5% |
| Chevy Tahoe | 2,592 | 5,078 | 49.0% |
| Toyota Tundra | 1,223 | 2,947 | 58.5% |
| Toyota Land Cruiser | 343 | 988 | 65.3% |
| GMC Yukon | 1,114 | 2,339 | 52.4% |
| Chevy Suburban | 593 | 1,151 | 48.5% |

The Audi Q7 was in 92 fatal crashes. In 63 of them (68.5%), someone ELSE died while the Q7 occupant walked away.

Compare to the vehicles HLDI identifies as the DEADLIEST:
| Vehicle | Occupant Deaths | Total Fatal Crashes | "Death Export" Rate |
|---------|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Chevy Cobalt | 1,540 | 1,907 | 19.2% |
| Buick LeSabre | 1,344 | 1,633 | 17.7% |
| Kia Rio | 268 | 367 | 27.0% |

The deadliest vehicles mostly kill their own occupants. The "safest" vehicles mostly kill other people.

## Limitations
- FARS "crashes" counts all fatal crashes involving the vehicle, including multi-vehicle crashes where the vehicle occupant was at fault. The "death export" metric is imprecise — some crashes kill pedestrians, cyclists, or involve more than two vehicles.
- HLDI uses 2023 single-year data; our FARS spans 2014-2023. Single-year data has high variance for low-volume models.
- Registration-year methodology differs from our VMT-based rates.
- "Zero death rate" for single-year data in low-volume luxury models (Q7, X7, GLB) may simply reflect small sample sizes.

## Strongest Counterargument
Heavy vehicles ARE genuinely safer for their occupants. The engineering that creates crashworthiness (mass, structural rigidity, ride height) has real physics behind it. The HLDI study doesn't claim these vehicles are safer for everyone — it specifically measures DRIVER death rate. The problem isn't the study; it's how the headline reads. "Zero death rate" implies nobody dies. Someone does.
