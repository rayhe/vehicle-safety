# Research: The Belt Inversion — Passenger Crossover in 2024

## Core Finding
For the first time in the 30-year FARS record (1995-2024), more fatally injured passengers were wearing seatbelts than not in 2024.

### 2024 Passenger Fatal Data
- Belted: 2,209 (46%)
- Unbelted: 2,023 (42%)
- Unknown: 608 (13%)

### Historical Passenger Belt Trend (selected years)
- 1995: 29% belted, 62% unbelted
- 2000: 33% belted, 59% unbelted
- 2005: 38% belted, 55% unbelted
- 2010: 41% belted, 51% unbelted
- 2015: 44% belted, 46% unbelted
- 2019: 45% belted, 45% unbelted (THE TIE — pre-pandemic)
- 2020: 42% belted, 47% unbelted (PANDEMIC REGRESSION)
- 2021: 44% belted, 45% unbelted
- 2022: 42% belted, 46% unbelted
- 2023: 42% belted, 44% unbelted
- 2024: 46% belted, 42% unbelted (FIRST CROSSOVER)

### Driver Crossover (for comparison)
Drivers crossed over much earlier:
- 2013: 47% belted, 45% unbelted (first driver crossover)
- 2024: 46% belted, 44% unbelted
The driver crossover is ~11 years ahead of the passenger crossover.

### Why This Matters — The Math
- National belt use: 91.2% (2024 daytime observational survey)
- Unbelted fraction of population: ~8.8%
- Unbelted fraction of known-status fatalities: ~48% (excluding unknowns)
- Overrepresentation: 48/8.8 = 5.5x

Being unbelted makes you roughly 5.5x more likely to die per mile driven. Belts still work. But when >91% of people are belted, belted occupants inevitably become the majority of fatalities — even though any individual unbelted occupant is 5.5x more at risk.

### Why Passengers Lagged Drivers by 11 Years
Hypothesis: Passenger belt compliance historically trailed driver compliance. Passengers (especially rear-seat) have lower belt use rates. Rear-seat belt reminders are only now becoming standard (NHTSA proposed rule, estimated to save 50-1,600 lives/year).

### 2024 FARS Overview
- 39,254 total deaths
- 22,713 passenger vehicle occupant deaths (58%)
- 7,080 pedestrians (18%)
- 6,228 motorcyclists (16%)
- 1,075 bicyclists (3%)
- 920 large truck occupants (2%)
- Fatality rate: 1.19 per 100M VMT

### Crash Type Belt Data (2024)
- Frontal: 45% belted
- Side: 55% belted
- Rear: 55% belted
- Other (rollover): 26% belted

## Angle
The belt inversion is a statistical milestone, not a failure. It means belt adoption has finally reached the point where even in the deadliest crashes, most occupants were doing the right thing. The 8.8% still not wearing belts are producing ~48% of deaths. The actionable insight: the last marginal belt adopters will save more lives per conversion than any other single intervention. A 1-percentage-point increase in belt use from 91% to 92% would save ~hundreds of lives annually.
