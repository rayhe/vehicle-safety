One Operator Skipped a Snap Ring. Four Truck Makers Recalled 6,932 Trucks.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

One operator at a driveline assembly station skipped one step for five weeks in the summer of 2026. That step was installing a snap ring, a part that costs less than a dollar and holds the driveline together. By November 12, four truck makers had recalled 6,932 brand-new 2027 trucks because of it.[5]

6,932
2027 trucks recalled across four manufacturers for the same missing snap ring, plus 414 loose service drivelines.

Start with the part, because the whole story hangs on a piece of spring steel you could lose in a couch. A snap ring sits in the slip yoke assembly of a Meritor driveline and keeps the bearing cup from walking out of position. Leave it out and the cup can separate, the driveline can separate, and an 80,000-pound truck loses drive power while its own driveshaft parts become road hazards for everyone behind it.[1] No crash is required, and no warning light fires. A truck that passed every inspection on the line can shed its driveline on the interstate months later, because the defect is an absence, and absences do not show up on a checklist that assumes the part is there.

Now the census, because this is where a single defect turns into a supply-chain story. Cummins, which builds the Meritor drivelines, filed first on October 28 with an equipment recall for 206 loose service drivelines.[4] Daimler Trucks followed on November 6 with 3,894 Freightliner and Western Star trucks, and PACCAR filed November 11 with 982 Kenworths. Volvo and Mack closed the sequence on November 12 with 1,078 and 978 trucks, and each added an equipment recall for 104 service drivelines the same day.[6] Seven campaigns, four brands, and fifteen days from first filing to last. Add them up and the tally is 6,932 trucks and 414 service drivelines, every one of them traceable to drivelines built between July 14 and August 21, 2026.[5]

Per the manufacturers' reports, the cause was one operator at the supplier station who did not follow the documented assembly process and left the ring out, working that station from July 14 to August 21.[5] Cummins told Volvo Group on October 23 that defective parts had reached several plants. Trucks were identified from supplier records, since drivelines are built and labeled per chassis number, which is the only reason the recall populations are this precise instead of a guess with a round number attached.

Fairness requires the other side, and the other side is not weak. This is the recall system working: a supplier caught its own escape, traced it to one station and one operator, and told the truck makers; every OEM filed within fifteen days of the first campaign, and the remedies are free inspections with driveline replacement where the ring is missing. No crashes, injuries, or deaths appear in the public records for any of the seven campaigns. A reasonable reader could call 6,932 trucks a success story with a big number attached.

Reasonable, and incomplete, because the success story and the failure are the same event viewed from opposite ends. One station, one operator, one skipped step, and the blast radius covered four competing brands and nearly seven thousand trucks before anyone noticed, which is what concentration looks like when you draw it as a diagram instead of a press release. Nothing here was designed wrong, and nothing was built from bad steel. A single human did not do a single step, and the system that was supposed to catch that had no second check between the station and the highway. That is not a cover-up; it is a single point of failure with a federal paper trail.

What to do: if your fleet took delivery of new 2027 trucks this fall, check every VIN against all four vehicle recalls, 26V737, 26V743, 26V746, and 26V747, at nhtsa.gov/recalls.[7] A truck carries only one of them, and the VIN lookup is the only way to know which. Do not wait for the owner letters, expected January 5 to January 11, 2027; the fix is a free inspection, and a missing ring will not announce itself before it lets go. If you stock Meritor RPL20 service drivelines, check the part numbers against 26E079 and 26E080 as well. And the next time a supplier tells you a defect is contained to one station, ask how many brands that station feeds, because the answer here was four.
