Last Year It Was the Knuckles. Now the Range Rover Sport’s Subframe Is Cracking.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
September 29, 2026

On September 24, Jaguar Land Rover told federal regulators that the rear subframe on certain 2024 through 2026 Range Rover Sports can develop cracks. A subframe is the steel structure that ties the suspension to the body of the car, so a cracked one means the load paths engineers designed for a crash are no longer the load paths you actually have. NHTSA’s summary puts it plainly: reduced structural integrity, higher risk of injury in a crash, diminished handling and control.[1] Dealers will inspect and replace subframes as necessary, free of charge, but the catch is the word interim: owner letters go out November 20, and JLR says a second letter will follow once the final remedy is available, which means the fix does not fully exist yet.

5
NHTSA campaigns touching the 2026 Range Rover Sport so far in 2026, from wrong labels to a cracking subframe

Count them. In January, the certification label carried the wrong weight information, which matters because overloading a vehicle changes how it crashes. In February, the panoramic sunroof trim could detach and become a road hazard. In May, the seatbelt warning chime might stay silent, a straight failure of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 208. Last week brought two more: one recall covering a single vehicle whose powertrain control module circuit board was misaligned during production, and the subframe cracks. Five campaigns, one model year, nine months.[1][2]

This is not the first time a Range Rover has cracked where it should not. In August 2025, NHTSA campaign 25V514 recalled 121,509 of the 2014 through 2017 Range Rover and Range Rover Sport because the front suspension upper knuckle joint could crack, detaching the suspension arm.[3][4] We covered that one in July, and Land Rover’s answer was a retaining bracket or a new knuckle.[6] Thirteen months later the new generation is cracking in a different place. Different component, different chassis, same sentence structure in the federal filing: a structural part may crack.

Somewhere in the middle of this sits the strangest recall I have read all year. Campaign 26V607, filed one day before the subframe recall, covers exactly one 2026 Range Rover Sport. One. A single circuit board inside its powertrain control module was misaligned during production and may be deformed, which can kill drive power without warning.[2] The one-car recall made the September 28 weekly recall roundup; the subframe recall had not yet appeared in it, which tells you how fresh this is.[5] JLR’s quality net is fine enough to catch one bad board in one car yet too coarse to stop subframes from cracking across three model years.

Now I will argue the other side, because the pattern I just drew might be a mirage. A knuckle on a 2015 chassis and a subframe on a 2025 chassis share almost nothing: different suppliers, different alloys, different load cases, different engineers. JLR finding both could mean the monitoring works, not that the metal does not. Nobody has reported a crash or an injury from the subframe cracks. The one-car PCM recall is arguably the system functioning at its best, a defect caught before it ever met a highway. You could read this whole year as a company that reports aggressively rather than one that builds badly.

What I cannot tell you is how many vehicles 26V613 covers, because NHTSA’s public record does not say and I will not invent the number. I also cannot tell you why the subframes crack; the root cause is not in the public summary. What I can tell you is that a subframe is not a trim piece. It carries suspension loads, it anchors crash structure, and when it cracks, the car’s behavior in the crash you are about to have is no longer the behavior it was tested for. That is the part worth sitting with.

Here is what you can do this week: if you own a 2024 through 2026 Range Rover Sport, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and look for campaign 26V613. Expect an interim letter around November 20, and do not file it with the tire ads; call the dealer and ask specifically about the subframe inspection. If the car develops new clunks over bumps, a wandering feel at highway speed, or uneven tire wear that was not there last month, say the words rear subframe when you book the appointment. The inspection is free, and the letter is coming. The crack does not wait for either.
