Tesla Recalled Four Times in 2026. Not One of Them Could Be Fixed Over the Air.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

Tesla filed four safety recalls in 2026 covering a 2026-model-year car. Four campaigns, 14,782 vehicles, and every single one of them required a human being to touch the car. Not one could be fixed over the air. Tesla taught the entire auto industry that cars could be repaired while their owners slept, and then it had a year where nothing could be repaired while anyone slept.[1]

0
Over-the-air fixes among Tesla's four 2026 recalls touching a 2026 car, per NHTSA's own API

Start with the method, because a census is only as honest as its counting rules. NHTSA's vehicle database lists seven Tesla models for 2026: Model 3, Model S, Model X, Model Y, Cybertruck, Semi, and Cybercab. I queried every canonical name, plus hybrid and electric suffix variants and ten targeted trims, for model year 2026. Thirty-three queries in all. Thirty came back HTTP 400, three came back 200, and four campaigns survived the filter: filed in 2026, numbered 26V, touching a 2026 car.[1][2]

Before you ask whether a 400 means the search failed, here is the control group. Run the same spellings against model year 2025 and the API answers 200 with real campaigns every time: Model 3 returns three, Model Y returns three, Model S returns two, Model X returns three, Cybertruck returns three. Five of five controls returned 200.[1] Same words, different year, different answer. When Model S returns 400 for 2026, the database holds no recall record for any 2026 Model S. Which makes sense, since Tesla sunset the S and X in mid-2026, a fact its own quarterly filing states in the dry language of a company burying two flagships.[9] Model 3 returned 200 for 2026, and the only row on file was filed in June 2025. That is a clean zero with receipts, not an absence of evidence dressed up as a finding.

Now the four, starting with campaign 26V315, which is 98.6 percent of the census population and the least dramatic defect in it: 14,575 Model Ys, built between November 2024 and April 2026, may be missing their certification label, the sticker on the door jamb that lists weight ratings under federal rule 49 CFR Part 567. A camera at the Fremont factory, installed to verify the sticker was present, could not consistently tell whether the sticker was present. Tesla estimates only 45 percent of the recalled cars are actually missing it, which means roughly 8,000 owners will drive to a service center to be told they already have one. Fix takes under ten minutes, and it still requires a service visit, because a sticker cannot be downloaded.[4]

Campaign 26V255 is the opposite shape: tiny population, enormous story. One hundred seventy-three Cybertrucks, every rear-wheel-drive Cybertruck Tesla ever sold, because the brake rotor stud holes on their 18-inch steel wheels can crack and let a wheel stud separate from the hub. Tesla killed the trim in November 2025 after nine months, citing limited demand, and the recall population is the sales ledger: 173 people bought the budget Cybertruck, and all 173 got recalled. Three warranty claims and no crashes, with a remedy of new rotors, hubs, and lug nuts, installed by a person, in a building, with tools.[3]

Campaign 26V558 is nineteen cars and one very human mistake. A PLC control function in the manufacturing system was mistakenly deactivated on July 26, 2026, and reinstated on August 7, and in those nine days nineteen Model Ys left the factory with front suspension lateral link fasteners that may not have been tightened to specification. If a fastener loosens, the lateral link can separate from the sub-frame and the wheel alignment shifts, which is the polite way of saying the car stops steering where you point it. One warranty claim and no crashes. Tesla estimates 75 percent of the nineteen actually have the defect, which is the highest defect rate in the census by an absurd margin. And the failure mode is a repeat: the same lateral link fastener was recalled on the 2023 Model Y under 22V895, which means Tesla has now recalled this exact bolt three model generations apart, and the bolt keeps winning.[6]

Campaign 26V445 is fifteen cars and the most expensive remedy in the file per vehicle. Fifteen people bought Canadian-market Model 3s and Model Ys, built in Shanghai and Berlin, and imported them into the United States. Tesla had issued them Certificates of Conformity stating the cars met US safety standards, and they did not. Foreign-market cars were built without knee airbags, so they fail the unbelted crash test requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 208, and the Model Ys lack interior countermeasures required by FMVSS 201, and the Model 3s were never tested to the bumper impact procedures of Part 581. Tesla's remedy is not a repair. It is a buyback, or a replacement with a fully compliant US-spec car, free of charge. Fifteen cars, and the fix is to take them back.[5]

Put Tesla next to the other fifteen brands in the series and the count looks sleepy. Ford leads with 41 campaigns, Stellantis follows with 27, GM, Toyota, and Hyundai sit at 15 each. Mercedes filed 8, Nissan 7, BMW 7, Subaru 5, Honda 4, Kia 4, Volkswagen 4, Tesla 4, Audi 1, Mazda 0, Porsche 0. Sixteen brands, 157 campaigns, an average of 9.8. Tesla ties Volkswagen at four, one-tenth of Ford, despite selling an estimated 386,194 cars in the US in nine months, more than every brand on the table except the legacy giants.[1][9] On paper, Tesla had a quiet recall year.

On paper is doing a lot of work in that sentence, because the campaigns just outside the census cover 231,879 more vehicles, and the biggest one is the recall that actually was fixed over the air. Campaign 26V283, filed May 5, 2026, covers 218,868 older Model 3s, Model Ys, Model S, and Model X on Hardware 3: a software configuration in version 2026.8.6 could delay the rearview camera image for up to eleven seconds after shifting into reverse, failing FMVSS 111. Tesla pushed the fix, version 2026.8.6.1, on April 11. By the time NHTSA filed the notice, 99.92 percent of the fleet already had it. Owners got a recall letter in July about a problem their car fixed itself in April. It touches no 2026 car, so it does not count, which is the census working exactly as designed and also the census missing the entire point.[8]

And then there is the ghost. The most-covered Tesla recall of October 2026, the battery contactor story, 12,963 cars, sudden loss of drive power, no warning, was filed on October 10, 2025. Campaign 25V690. Seven thousand nine hundred twenty-five of those cars are 2026 Model Ys. The press covered a year-old filing as breaking news while the actual 2026 filings went unnoticed, which is how a census earns its keep: it dates the campaigns correctly, and the dates are the story. Your 2026 Model Y has three recalls filed in 2026 and five more filed in 2025 sitting in its file, and only the filing dates separate them.[7]

What to do depends on which Tesla is in your driveway. If you own a 2026 Model Y, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V315, 26V445, and 26V558, plus the 2025 filings 25V359, 25V410, 25V490, 25V658, and 25V690, all of which touch 2026 Model Ys. If you own a rear-wheel-drive Cybertruck, you are one of 173 people, and you are in 26V255: book the rotor, hub, and lug nut replacement. If you imported a Canadian Tesla and hold a Tesla-issued Certificate of Conformity, contact Tesla at resolutions@tesla.com or 1-877-798-3752 about the 26V445 buyback, and do not assume the letter in your glovebox is still valid. If you own a 2017 or 2021 to 2023 Model 3, a 2020 to 2023 Model Y, or a 2021 to 2023 Model S or X, check your software version against 2026.8.6.1, because 26V283 was probably already fixed before you read this. And if you own a 2026 Model 3, you are in zero counted recalls, which is real, and you should still check your VIN, because the seat-back fastener recall 25V410 covers 2026 Model 3s and was filed before the window opened.

Limitations, stated plainly: this census counts NHTSA safety recall campaigns filed January 1 to November 13, 2026, covering 2026-model-year vehicles, so a campaign filed after November 13 would not appear. It does not count workshop or service campaigns, European Safety Gate actions (including the October 2026 Model 3 Premium far-side airbag recall covering 131,726 cars), or NHTSA investigations (including the October 2026 Full Self-Driving investigation covering about 2.88 million vehicles). A defect handled outside the US recall system is invisible to this count by design. The API's 400 semantics are inferred from controls, not documented by NHTSA. US sales figures are Cox Automotive and Kelley Blue Book estimates from registration data, not Tesla disclosures; Tesla reports global deliveries only. The 26V507 headlight campaign's population was not pulled from its Part 573 report, and the 25V359, 25V490, and 25V658 populations were not pulled either. Campaign populations are NHTSA "potentially involved" figures, not confirmed-defect counts: 26V315's own filing estimates only 45 percent of its population actually lacks the label, and 26V558 estimates 75 percent have the defect.

Counterargument, at full strength: four is good news. Tesla sold an estimated 386,194 cars in the US in nine months and recalled four campaigns touching new cars, covering 14,782 vehicles, with no crashes, injuries, or fatalities reported in any of them. The biggest campaign is a sticker. The smallest is fifteen cars. A skeptic will say the honest headline is that Tesla built a clean year, and this article manufactures a story out of a zero in the over-the-air column. That reading is mostly fair, and this article says so. The counterweight is that the zero in the OTA column is not a statistical artifact, it is a description of what the defects were: a wheel that can fall off, a suspension bolt that was never torqued, a car that should never have been certified for American roads, and a sticker a camera could not see. None of those are software problems. A company can fix a million cars overnight and still have a year where the only things that broke were the things software cannot touch. That is not an indictment. It is a reminder that the car is still, underneath all of it, a machine made of bolts.
