# Research: Four Engines, One Failed Bearing (Article #1328)

**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor)
**Kicker:** Trend Watch
**News peg:** NHTSA's six-request information demand to GM under EA26005 is due TODAY, October 30, 2026 (TorquNews). Request 6 asks GM to enumerate what warning drivers get before the 6.2L fails.
**Kill test verdict:** PROCEED. The Crash Report has covered all four programs individually (Honda #781, Nissan #984, GM #864/#1025, Toyota V35A published story), but never framed them as one pattern. The census is the original contribution: ~3.4M vehicles across four engine programs, one common terminal failure point.

## The four programs

### 1. GM 6.2L L87 V8 (bearings)
- NHTSA Engineering Analysis EA26005, opened Aug 20, 2026. Population: 997,743 vehicles: 2021-2026 Silverado 1500, Suburban, Tahoe, Sierra 1500, Yukon/Yukon XL, Escalade/Escalade ESV.
- Original recall 25V-274: ~597,600 vehicles, build window Mar 1 2021-May 31 2024. Supplier manufacturing/quality problem.
- Post-remedy failures: 499 NHTSA complaints (473 passed inspection + oil change only; 26 got replacement engines). GM itself reported 6,953 post-remedy complaints. 191 failures in engines built after May 2024 (outside recall) -> scope widened to 2021-2026.
- GM's response to NHTSA's Sept 15 letter due Oct 30, 2026 (six requests; req 6 = what warning does the driver get before failure).
- 5.3L V8, 2.7L turbo, 3.0L diesel NOT included.
- Sources: torquenews.com (EA26005/6 requests/deadline), topspeed.com, squeezed.app.

### 2. Toyota 3.4L V35A twin-turbo V6 (bearings)
- 2022-2024 Tundra, 2022-2024 Lexus LX, 2024 Lexus GX. Root cause: microscopic machining debris -> #1 main bearing failure; cam housing clearance change -> stack-up of bearing pressure from timing chain tension + engine loading.
- Three recalls: 24V-381 (May 2024, ~102,000, engine replacement); 25V-767 (Nov 2025, ~126,691, resonant-frequency inspection of crankshaft front); 26V-320 (May 2026, 43,566 2024 Tundras built Feb 7-Aug 5 2024 at Alabama TMMAL; remedy under development at filing).
- Total: ~272,257 (~270K+). Engines built after July 2024 carry redesigned #1 main bearing.
- Sources: nationalsynthetics.com (three-recall table), thehealthyattitude.com.

### 3. Nissan VC-Turbo (1.5L KR15DDT + 2.0L KR20DDET) (bearings)
- 25V-437 (June 2025): 443,899 = 348,554 Rogues + 84,536 QX50 + 5,685 Altima + 5,124 QX55. Manufacturing defect in main, A-, C-, L-link bearings; 1.2% defect estimate. PE23023 (Dec 2023) -> closed into 25V-437.
- 26V-080 (Feb 2026): 323,917 Rogues 2023-2025; high oil temperature degrades lubrication -> bearing seizure; 0.6% estimate. Populations OVERLAP (Nissan filing says so) - cannot sum.
- March 2026: long-block warranty extended to 120 months/120,000 miles.
- Failure progresses over time; warnings: abnormal engine noise, rough running, MIL illuminated, cluster messages. Remedy: oil-pan debris inspection; none -> gasket + oil + ECM reprogram; debris -> new engine (up to 15 hr labor).
- Federal records tied to generation: 45 crashes, 6 fires, 29 injuries, no deaths.
- Sources: theweeklydriver.com, furilia.com, thetopselected.com, squeezed.app.

### 4. Honda J35 3.5L V6 (bearings)
- 23V-751 (Nov 2023): 248,999; crank pins ground convex -> connecting rod bearing oil starvation. Honda knew since 2020.
- RQ24013 (Nov 2024): 173 complaints from non-recalled vehicles.
- PE25008 (Aug 20, 2025): 414 owner + 2,598 manufacturer = 3,012 reports; population 1,410,806; several crashes/fires, no injuries; NHTSA: not the same crankshaft defect as 23V-751. NO wider recall after 14+ months.
- Sources: our story #781, autoevolution.com (ODI resume PE25008), manufacturing.net.

## The census (original contribution)
- GM: 997,743 (EA26005 probe pop; 597,571 recalled)
- Toyota: ~272,257 (three non-overlapping waves)
- Nissan: 443,899 + 323,917 (OVERLAPPING - state explicitly; do not sum as fact)
- Honda: 248,999 (recall) + 1,410,806 (PE pop, outside recall scope)
- Combined: roughly 3.4M vehicles inside the four programs' stated populations (with Nissan overlap caveat).

## The regulatory ladder (novel framing)
- Toyota: 3 recalls -> engines replaced, then inspected.
- Nissan: 2 bearing recalls + warranty doubled to 10yr/120K.
- GM: 1 recall -> fix under federal Engineering Analysis (deadline today).
- Honda: 1 recall -> 14 months of Preliminary Evaluation, no action.
Same component. Four different federal postures.

## Failure physics (Mia's beat)
- The bearing is the terminal failure point in all four: main bearings (Toyota #1 main, Nissan mains, GM mains/rods), connecting rod bearings (Honda).
- What a seized bearing does at highway speed: engine stops -> loss of motive power, power steering, power brake boost. That is the safety case for NHTSA involvement.
- Root causes differ: GM supplier quality; Toyota machining debris; Nissan bearing manufacturing defect + oil-temp lubrication; Honda crank-pin geometry. Distinct root causes is the honest counterargument material.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
Four engine programs, four different root causes. A bearing is also the most mechanically stressed component in any engine, and bearing seizure is the default terminal event of catastrophic engine failure - of course the terminal component is the same. This may be coincidence, not a systemic pattern. Also: all four engines are from the turbo/downsizing era of higher cylinder pressures and tighter tolerances; if anything the pattern is "modern engines are less forgiving," not "bearings are uniquely cursed." The article must state this.

## Limitations
- Population counts are NHTSA/manufacturer estimates, not exact census; Nissan waves overlap.
- Defect rates (1.2%, 0.6% Nissan) are manufacturer estimates.
- GM post-remedy complaint counts (499 NHTSA, 6,953 GM) are not a failure rate (torquenews notes 6,953 ~= 1.2% of 597,600 - context, not rate).
- FARS data was not used for this piece (fatal-crash data can't isolate bearing failures); methodology = census of NHTSA campaign/investigation records + press. State that.
