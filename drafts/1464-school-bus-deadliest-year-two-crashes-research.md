# Research: #1464 — Two Crashes Made 2024 the Deadliest Year Inside a School Bus

**Slug:** `1464-school-bus-deadliest-year-two-crashes`

**Kicker:** Sobriety Report

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
2024 total deaths fell 14% to 110, near the decade average of 107, while deaths inside the bus hit a decade high of 17. Both facts are true, and both are misleading: 12 of those 17 deaths came from two crashes, and 8 of the 12 weren't schoolchildren at all — they were farmworkers riding a retired school bus. Strip out the two crashes and 2024 drops to 5 inside-bus deaths, second only to the pandemic year 2020 (3), when schools were largely closed. The "deadliest year" is a story about concentration, classification, and impairment — not about school buses getting dangerous.

## Verified facts

### The 10-year picture (NHTSA DOT HS 813 819, July 2026; FARS 2015-2023 Final File, 2024 ARF)
- 962 fatal school-transportation-related crashes, 2015-2024; **1,069 people killed**, average 107/year; 0.3% of all fatal crashes.
- By person type, decade: **748 (70%) occupants of other vehicles; 162 (15%) pedestrians; 119 (11%) occupants of school transportation vehicles (49 drivers, 70 passengers); 40 pedalcyclists/other.**
- 204 school-age children (18 and younger) died in school-transportation crashes over the decade: 39 were school-vehicle occupants, 78 were occupants of other vehicles, **77 were pedestrians**, 7 pedalcyclists, 3 other.
- More school-age pedestrians were killed 7:00-7:59 a.m. than any other hour; nearly one-fifth (18%) of school-age pedestrians killed were struck by school transportation vehicles going straight.
- 483,041 yellow school buses provided daily transportation in the 2024-2025 school year (School Bus Fleet, via NSC).

### The 2024 twist (NHTSA DOT HS 813 819 Table 1; Millar Law Firm analysis, Oct 19, 2026; NSC Injury Facts)
- **2024 total: 110 deaths, down 14% from 128 in 2023** (NSC tabulations of FARS agree: 110 in 2024 vs 128 in 2023). Not the decade low: 2020 was 54 (COVID, schools closed); the decade average is 107. Annual totals 2015-2024: 115, 125, 97, 117, 111, 54, 108, 104, 128, 110.
- **Inside school vehicles: 17 deaths in 2024, up from 13 in 2023 (+31%), the highest annual total of the 10-year window** (5 drivers, 12 passengers).
- Occupants of other vehicles: 70 in 2024, down from 85 in 2023. Pedestrians: 19, down from 24.
- **Two crashes accounted for 12 of the 17 school-vehicle occupant deaths (71%):** Rushville, IL (4 aboard the bus) and Ocala, FL (8 aboard the bus). Millar counts 13 of 17 including the Rushville truck driver, who was not a bus occupant; the correct bus-occupant count from the two crashes is 12.
- **Original calculation 1 (concentration):** 12 / 17 = 70.6% of the year's inside-bus deaths came from two crashes on two days.
- **Original calculation 2 (counterfactual):** without Rushville and Ocala, 2024 inside-bus deaths = 5 — second only to the COVID year 2020 (3), when schools were largely closed and the total was 54. The "deadliest year" headline reverses entirely on two crashes.
- **Original calculation 3 (classification):** 8 of the 17 (47%) were adult farmworkers riding a retired school bus in Ocala, not schoolchildren. The child toll inside school vehicles in 2024 was 4 (3 Rushville children + 1 elsewhere implied by the 12-passenger total: 12 passengers = 3 Rushville children + 8 Ocala farmworkers + 1 other).

### Rushville, IL — March 11, 2024 (NTSB HIR-26-01, March 6, 2026; IL State Police)
- 11:29 a.m. CDT, US-24 near Rushville, Schuyler County. A 2020 Micro Bird MB II 25-passenger school bus (Schuyler-Industry Schools), eastbound at ~55 mph, negotiated a right-hand curve, crossed the centerline, and collided head-on with a westbound 2001 Mack CH613 truck-tractor hauling a 39-foot dump semi-trailer loaded with sand. Immediate fire consumed both vehicles.
- **5 killed:** bus driver Angela Spiker, 57; three child passengers Maria Miller, 5, Andrew Miller, 3, Noah Driscoll, 3; truck driver David Coufal, 72. Four of the five were aboard the bus.
- **NTSB probable cause (final report, March 6, 2026): the school bus driver's impairment and fatigue from multiple prescription medications**, which resulted in the bus crossing the centerline. The report also found Schuyler-Industry Schools had no safety policies or procedures in place as outlined by the Illinois State Board of Education, did not follow state maintenance requirements, and had no policy on cell-phone use by drivers. The district adopted a transportation handbook in December 2025.
- The truck driver was the only person involved not wearing a seat belt.

### Ocala, FL — May 14, 2024 (Florida Highway Patrol; AP)
- ~6:35-6:40 a.m., State Road 40, Marion County. A 2010 International **retired school bus** carrying 53 farmworkers to Cannon Farms in Dunnellon collided with a 2001 Ford Ranger driven by Bryan Maclean Howard, 41. The Ranger drifted toward the centerline; the vehicles sideswiped; the bus swerved off the road, crashed through a fence, struck a tree, and rolled over in a field.
- **8 killed, all aboard the bus; ~40 injured.** All eight were Mexican farmworkers. Howard was arrested on **eight counts of DUI-manslaughter**; he told investigators he had smoked marijuana oil and taken prescription medication hours before the crash. He pleaded not guilty.
- The bus had no seat belts. Marion County Public Schools clarified none of its school buses was involved — no children were aboard.
- Classification note: NHTSA counts the crash as school-transportation-related because the vehicle is a school-bus body type, even though it was a retired bus in farmworker service. That is why 8 adult deaths sit inside the "school vehicle occupant" column.

### The danger around the bus (NHTSA DOT HS 813 819; NASDPTS 2026 survey)
- Between 2000 and 2024, crashes involving drivers illegally passing stopped school buses killed 63 people, 27 of them pedestrians age 18 or younger.
- NASDPTS estimates **38.4 million illegal school-bus passings per 180-day school year** (2026 one-day count: 56,024 passes observed). Down from 45.2M (2023-24) and 39.3M (2024-25) — the count is falling, slowly.
- Over the decade, 77 school-age children died as pedestrians in school-transportation crashes — nearly double the 39 killed while riding in school vehicles.

## Original contribution
Three calculations nobody has published on this site:
1. **The concentration:** 12 of 17 inside-bus deaths (71%) came from two crashes on two days in 2024.
2. **The reversal:** without those two crashes, 2024's inside-bus toll is 5 — a decade low, not a decade high. The scariest number in the report is an artifact of two outliers.
3. **The classification correction:** 8 of the 17 (47%) were adult farmworkers in a retired bus. The child toll inside school vehicles in 2024 was 4, not 17 — the headline number overstates the risk to riding children by more than 4x.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The concentration argument cuts both ways. A system whose annual death toll can be more than tripled by two crashes is a system with no margin: the same rarity that makes the "decade high" misleading also means a single bad morning can produce a Rushville. And the two crashes were not freak weather or mechanical failure — both were impairment: a bus driver on prescription meds, a pickup driver high on marijuana oil. The NTSB found a school district with no safety policies at all. If the lesson of 2024 is "nothing is systemically wrong," that is the wrong lesson; the lesson is that the system's safety rests on individual drivers and districts doing things right, and when they don't, the numbers spike. Also, the decade-long decline in illegal passing (45.2M to 38.4M) is real but still means a stopped school bus is illegally passed more than 200,000 times every school day.

## Limitations
- 2024 figures are from the FARS Annual Report File (ARF), not the final file; late-reported deaths can revise totals slightly upward.
- The "1 other" child passenger death in 2024 (12 passengers minus 3 Rushville children minus 8 Ocala farmworkers) is arithmetic from the published table totals, not a separately sourced case; stated as implied, not verified.
- The Millar Law Firm analysis (Oct 19, 2026) counts 13 of 17 from the two crashes by including the Rushville truck driver; I use 12, the bus-occupant count, and flag the difference.
- NHTSA's "school-transportation-related" definition includes crashes where a school-bus-body vehicle is involved in any capacity, including retired buses in non-school service; the Ocala classification follows that definition.
- Illegal-passing counts are survey extrapolations (one-day count scaled to a 180-day year), not observed totals.

## Actionable takeaways
- If your district's buses don't have seat belts: ask why. The Ocala bus had none; 53 unrestrained adults rolled in a field. Compartmentalization protects children in frontal crashes, not rollovers.
- The deadliest moment for a child near a school bus is not the ride — it is the curb, 7:00-7:59 a.m. Teach kids to wait for the driver's boarding signal and stay 10+ feet from the curb (NHTSA guidance).
- Never pass a stopped school bus with its stop arm out. 38.4 million illegal passes a year; 63 people killed by illegal passing since 2000, 27 of them children.
- If you drive a school bus or hire drivers: Rushville is what happens when a district has no safety policies and a driver is impaired on prescription meds. Districts should have written fitness-for-duty and medication-disclosure policies — Schuyler-Industry adopted its handbook only after the crash.
- The two crashes that defined 2024 were both impairment crashes. If someone in your life drives for work while impaired, that is the intervention point.
