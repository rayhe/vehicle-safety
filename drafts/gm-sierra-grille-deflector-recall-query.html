GM Recalled 70,768 Sierras for a Detaching Grille, and Now NHTSA Wants the Other 464,871

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 16, 2026

On October 9, NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation opened recall query RQ26003 against General Motors, asking a question the auto industry dreads: not whether a defect exists, but whether a recall actually fixed it. Twenty-one of the 118 owner complaints allege the Sierra’s upper grille deflector detached after the dealer had already performed the recall repair.[1]

535,639
Trucks now under federal review — 7.6 times the original recall’s population

A recall query is NHTSA’s audit function. Most investigations ask whether a defect exists at all; this one skips that step, because GM conceded the defect in February 2025, recalled 70,768 trucks, and called the remedy free at the dealer.[2]

The question is narrower, and harder to wave away: did the fix hold, and did the recall cover enough trucks? ODI’s complaint file says no on both counts. Twenty-one owners report the deflector came off after the repair. The query population spans 2022 through 2024, chrome and non-chrome, while the original recall covered only 2022 trucks with chrome deflectors built in a ten-month window.[1]

The backstory reads like a closed case that reopened itself. A GM employee flagged a deflector coming off a driven 2022 Sierra through the company’s Speak Up for Safety program in January 2024. GM investigated, then closed the file that May after finding no defect trend, while warranty claims kept climbing. By December the case was open again, and two months later came recall 25V-060. GM’s justification for the narrow scope: later trucks used parts outside the supplier’s suspect production window.[3]

ODI describes the detachment as occurring “without detectability,” and lists the exposed parties as the truck, nearby vehicles, motorcyclists and pedestrians. Some owners reported cracked windshields, while others described traffic swerving around airborne trim. GM’s own filing counted 1,225 complaints and four accident allegations by the end of 2024, with no known injuries. None of that is disputed, but what changed is the numerator: owners keep filing, and some of them already visited the service bay.[1]

What this means for owners is simple. If you drive a 2022 Sierra 1500, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for recall 25V-060; the repair is free, and GM’s own warning sign is a rattle from the front of the truck. About 64 percent of recalled trucks had been repaired as of January, which leaves tens of thousands still carrying the original hardware.[4] If you drive a 2023 or 2024, the situation is stranger: no recall, no remedy, no determination. Your deflector may be fine, or it may be one of the reasons the complaint file exists, and that file is exactly what opened this query; adding your name to it takes five minutes.

Two caveats before the file closes: 21 post-remedy detachments out of 118 complaints is not a remedy failure rate; the denominator that matters is roughly 45,000 completed repairs, and ODI never claimed otherwise, because complaints are a tripwire, not a census. Second, GM has a real defense: it scoped the recall to the supplier’s suspect window, which is standard practice, and ODI has not found a defect in the 2023-2024 trucks. A recall query is a question, not a verdict. NHTSA closed a similar probe of Honda’s Ridgeline cameras earlier this month after its own lab tested the parts and found nothing.[5] This one could end the same way.
