## News peg
- Tesla began commercial Cybercab deployment in Austin, TX on **Sept 3, 2026** (two-seat, no steering wheel, no pedals, no mirrors; rides via Robotaxi app).
- NHTSA opened **Audit Query AQ26002** on **Sept 3, 2026** — the same day passengers started riding — covering an estimated **1,000 Cybercabs**. Opened per the filing "prompted by public information," i.e., press coverage, not a defect complaint.
- AQ26002 examines "the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab" and "the extent to which Tesla's certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable to the Cybercab."
- Tesla VP of Vehicle Engineering Lars Moravy: Cybercab certified as fully compliant with all applicable FMVSS; some standards simply "inapplicable" to a vehicle without the controls they regulate. This is NOT an exemption request — it is a legal determination the standards don't apply at all.
- NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison: "NHTSA fully supports the safe development and deployment of automated vehicles. But as the federal regulator, we need to ensure that all of our laws are followed."
- Tesla plans to "gradually expand commercial deployment to additional vehicles and locations" (roughly a dozen states per USA Today).
- Fleet as of Sept 4: **420 autonomous vehicles registered in Texas, including 45 Cybercabs** (state records per Reuters).
- The probe does NOT ground the fleet; an audit query is an investigative step, not an enforcement action. Robotaxi app continued taking bookings.

## The Zoox precedent (only directly comparable case)
- In **2022**, Amazon's Zoox self-certified its steering-wheel-free VH6 robotaxi under standard federal rules, arguing FMVSS for human controls didn't apply — the same argument Tesla makes now.
- NHTSA opened **audit query AQ23001** and challenged the determination.
- Zoox abandoned self-certification, pivoted to the formal **Part 555 exemption process** (49 CFR Part 555), filed for exemptions from **eight separate FMVSS**.
- NHTSA closed the self-certification inquiry **August 2025** with a requirements list; preliminary tech-demonstration approval followed.
- Weeks before clearance, Zoox **recalled its entire 105-vehicle fleet** after a software issue caused one robotaxi to struggle navigating heavy smoke (Aug 2026).
- **Commercial clearance arrived July 30, 2026**: charge fares, subject to **2,500-vehicle annual cap**, enhanced crash reporting, ongoing federal oversight.
- **Timeline: ~4 years** from audit query to commercial clearance.

## The exemption-cap math (novel contribution #1)
- 49 CFR Part 555 caps a manufacturer at **2,500 exempt vehicles per year**.
- Zoox's full national operation lives under that cap.
- Tesla's stated ambition: tens of thousands of Cybercabs annually, deployment to "roughly a dozen states."
- If NHTSA rejects self-certification and forces Tesla into Part 555: even a modest rollout breaks the cap fast — **12 markets x 250 cars = 3,000 vehicles, over the 2,500 cap in year one**. Tesla's scale-over-cap motive was a deliberate bet to avoid the cap, not a technical legal conclusion.
- Getting the cap raised requires Congress or a full NHTSA rulemaking — years, not months.

## The rulemaking race (novel contribution #2)
- In **June 2026**, DOT/NHTSA proposed **removing the manual brake pedal requirement** for autonomously driven vehicles.
- NHTSA told press it is "actively updating FMVSS provisions related to manual controls" and "looks forward to finishing these critical updates... in the coming months."
- If those rule changes finalize BEFORE AQ26002 concludes, the standards Tesla is accused of misapplying may no longer exist — **the probe could dissolve by losing its legal basis**. Two clocks are running: the audit and the rulemaking.

## Parallel federal exposure
- March 2026: NHTSA **escalated the FSD probe to Engineering Analysis**, covering ~**3.2 million** vehicles (S, X, 3, Y, Cybertruck) over low-visibility crashes — nine suspected incidents, one fatality.
- Cybercab runs a separate software branch (**FSD v15**), not the consumer FSD covered by EA26002. Legally distinct probes: one on the right to be on the road, one on software behavior. Both share the camera-only perception vulnerability.
- Internal cross-link: #772 "Tesla's Robotaxi Remote Operators Have a 100% Crash Rate in NHTSA Filings" (published 2026-09-04) — Cybercab has no manual controls, so remote operators are the only human in the loop. Worth a link.

## Standards at issue (from TechTimes reporting)
- FMVSS No. 111 (rearview/side-view mirrors), No. 124 (accelerator control systems), No. 135 (light vehicle brake systems), Nos. 203/204 (steering control impact protection / rearward displacement). The Cybercab has none of the components these standards govern.

## Consumer guidance (Actionable Insights — Clara's beat)
1. **Riding in Austin now:** the probe is investigative, not punitive — rides continue. But know: there is no steering wheel, no pedals, no mirrors, so there is no human override inside the car. Your safety driver is a remote operator somewhere else. (#772 context: NHTSA filings show those operators crashed every time.)
2. **Ask before you ride:** does Tesla have a Part 555 exemption for this vehicle? As of Sept 4, the answer is no — it is operating on Tesla's own inapplicability determination, now under federal audit.
3. **Watch the docket:** AQ26002 is public at NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation. Outcomes range from "closed, no action" (Tesla's inapplicability argument becomes an industry template) to mandatory Part 555 pivot, recalls, or required hardware modifications.
4. **Scale promises:** Tesla's "dozen states" expansion math collides with the 2,500/year exemption cap if self-certification fails. Treat expansion timelines as contingent on the audit outcome.
5. **What won't change the car:** the Cybercab is not sold to consumers; Tesla plans fleet-owner sales (operators run their own robotaxi services). If you were hoping to buy one, don't hold your breath.

## Limitations
- The AQ26002 filing text is summarized from Reuters/Electrek/TechTimes reporting; the full ODI document was not read directly (linked via press).
- The exact 2022 month of AQ23001 is not pinned in sources — "approximately four years" is the honest frame.
- FMVSS-at-issue list comes from TechTimes, not from the filing itself.
- NHTSA has made no finding; the probe could close with no action. The Zoox analogy is precedent, not prediction — different administration, different vehicle, different political posture on AVs.

## Strongest counterargument
Tesla's inapplicability argument may be legally correct: standards written for human driver controls genuinely do not map onto a vehicle designed without them, and NHTSA's own June 2026 proposal to remove the brake pedal requirement signals the agency agrees those standards are outdated. Under a pro-AV administration, NHTSA may uphold self-certification and set an industry template that renders Part 555 optional. Zoox chose the exemption path partly under a more cautious regulatory era; Tesla's bet is that the rules catch up to the hardware before the audit concludes. The rulemaking race could resolve AQ26002 by erasing its legal basis.
