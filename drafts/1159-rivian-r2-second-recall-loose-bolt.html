Rivian's Make-or-Break Car Has a Loose Bolt

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 13, 2027

Four months into deliveries of the most important vehicle in its history, Rivian has issued the R2's second recall. This one isn't software. It's a single bolt: fourteen 2027 R2s left Normal, Illinois with a high-voltage battery fastener that may not have been tightened to spec, and the NHTSA filing admits the failure gives no warning before the car loses drive power.

14
R2s recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V625, built May 18–August 27, 2026

What should actually keep you up at night isn't the 14 cars. It's how the defect was found. On September 17, a manufacturing operator spotted an under-torqued fastener that had already passed through Rivian's automated torque station. Built to catch exactly this failure, the machine looked right at it and waved it through. A human being, with eyes and boredom and all the glorious unreliability that implies, said no.

One bolt, part number SC00036202-D, an M6x1.0x24.5, sits inside the high-voltage battery system. If the connection fails, the car loses motive power without warning. No dashboard theater, no polite request to pull over, just an EV coasting into silence while traffic keeps moving around it. Rivian reprogrammed the station to reject improperly torqued fasteners. Nowhere in the filing does the company explain how the station was letting them through in the first place.

Recall one covered 2,481 R2s and fixed a backup camera glitch with an over-the-air update while you slept. This one needs a wrench and a service bay. Two recalls, two completely different failure modes, one vehicle that has to carry Rivian to profitability. And last month, Rivian reportedly grounded some R2s for high-voltage circuit fastener inspections without ever publicly commenting on it. Two high-voltage fastener incidents in two months: one disclosed in an NHTSA filing, one surfaced on Reddit.

Fair is fair. Fourteen cars, zero complaints, zero crashes, zero injuries. Every affected vehicle is already sitting at a service center. Rivian found the problem within a week and filed voluntarily, which is precisely how the system is supposed to work. Ford's recall machine churned through 146 campaigns in a year and nobody wrote the company's eulogy. But machines missed the bolt, and a person didn't, which is the cosmic joke buried in this filing. The entire industry is automating quality control to eliminate human error, and the one human left on the line is the reason this recall exists at all.

What to do: if you own a 2027 R2, your VIN becomes searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls on November 28, 2026. And if any EV dies under you at speed, don't wait for the machine to help. Hazards on, coast to the shoulder, assume you are on your own until you're stopped.
