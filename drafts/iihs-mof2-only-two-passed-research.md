# Research Notes: IIHS MOF 2.0 — Only Two of Sixteen Passed

## Angle
IIHS launched its toughest-ever moderate overlap frontal crash test in 2024-2025, adding a rear-seat dummy for the first time in U.S. crash testing. When they tested 16 new 2026 model year vehicles, only 2 earned Top Safety Pick+: the Hyundai Palisade and Toyota Prius. The Palisade is ironic context — recalled earlier in 2026 after a 2-year-old was killed by its power seats.

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing which vehicles passed/failed the new test against their FARS fatality profiles reveals a pattern: vehicles that the test exposes as dangerous for rear-seat occupants are the same models whose FARS lethality ratios are elevated. The test is finally catching what the data already showed.

## Key Data Points

### IIHS MOF 2.0 Test Results (May 2026)
- 16 vehicles tested under new protocol
- Only 2 earned TSP+: Hyundai Palisade, Toyota Prius
- New test adds rear-seat dummy (first time in US)
- Many vehicles struggled specifically with back-seat safety
- Audi A3, Lexus IS, Nissan Kicks, Toyota Tacoma crew cab: marginal ratings
- Cadillac CT5: poor ratings in TWO key crash tests

### FARS Data Context
- Rear-seat deaths: our data shows high lethality ratios for sedans (0.643 class avg) vs SUVs (0.526)
- Palisade FARS: 38 deaths, 0.06 rate, 0.458 lethality — very safe per-mile, but when it crashes, 46% of the time the occupant dies
- Prius FARS: 495 deaths, 0.55 rate — modest
- CT5 (Cadillac sedans): Cadillac sedans average 2.60 rate
- Lexus IS: Lexus sedans average 1.00 rate
- Nissan Kicks: categorized as SUV, low death counts (newer model)
- Toyota Tacoma: 2,274 deaths, 0.80 rate, one of the highest-volume pickups

### IIHS Test Protocol Changes
- Original MOF: single front-seat dummy, no rear assessment
- MOF 2.0: adds Hybrid III dummy in second row behind driver
- New metrics: head, neck, chest, abdomen, thigh injury for rear occupant
- Tests for submarining (body sliding under lap belt)
- Belt position sensors used for first time
- Less than half of new vehicles have advanced rear restraints
