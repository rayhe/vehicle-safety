We Recounted Ford's Recalls. It's 41, Not 40. And Lincoln Was Never Counted at All.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again, because the first count was wrong. Yesterday this site printed Ford at 40 recall campaigns for its 2026 models and GM at 15, both computed with the base-name method that Toyota and Honda already proved undercounts. I re-ran both with the corrected vocabulary, 404 queries against NHTSA's recalls database, and I ran Lincoln for the first time, a brand no census on this site has ever queried because it is a separate make in the API. Ford is 41. GM is 15, confirmed, and Lincoln is 10. Dedupe the campaigns the two Ford Motor Company makes share, and the company as a whole carries 44.[1]

41
Recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching 2026-model-year Fords, corrected count (was 40). Lincoln adds 10, three of them Lincoln-only

One campaign moved Ford's number, and it moved on a suffix. Campaign 26V415 covers 66,383 hybrid SUVs, 18,242 Explorer Hybrids and 48,141 Nautilus Hybrids, whose pedestrian warning sound may not emit below 30 kph while running in electric mode. An audio processing software error in a Harman digital signal processing module can silence the sound that tells a pedestrian a quiet car is coming. Ford logged 72 warranty claims before filing, 65 on Nautilus Hybrids and 7 on Explorers, and the campaign expands an October 2025 recall of the same defect. Query "explorer" and you get 12 campaigns, none of them 26V415. Query "explorer hybrid" and there it is. A base-name census prints 40 while the corrected census prints 41, and the one campaign it hides is a silent car in a crosswalk.[7][11]

Lincoln is the bigger find, because the miss was structural. Lincoln is a separate make in NHTSA's API, so a census that queries make=ford never sees a Lincoln campaign, even one shared with a Ford model, unless the Ford sibling's query returns it. Seven of Lincoln's 10 are shared and were visible anyway; three were not. In March, 26V120 recalled 1,189 Navigators whose driver seat could reverse unexpectedly at low speed, moving the driver away from the pedals. In October, 26V688 recalled 11,623 Nautilus and Corsair Hybrids that can shift themselves into neutral while driving, because a supplier changed the anti-rust oil inside the park module without telling Ford or validating that the new oil was compatible with the circuit board's coating, and the oil ate the coating until the board corroded. Also in October, 26V691 recalled 36,994 Navigators whose front passenger seat moves on its own because a stereo module commands a seat feature it has no business commanding. All three were covered as standalone stories on this site. None of them had ever been counted.[8][9][10][12]

And the trap reproduces inside the census built to catch it. Campaign 26V688 covers 63 Corsair Hybrids from the 2027 model year, but query "corsair hybrid" and the API returns zero rows. That campaign is visible only through the Nautilus Hybrid query. A census that queried only Corsair models would miss a Corsair recall. GM, meanwhile, did not move. Its corrected count is the same 15 campaigns #1474 published yesterday, same set, same order, which is a useful result in itself: the vocabulary trap is brand-specific, not universal, and a method that finds nothing new still earns its keep by confirming the old number. Across the six brands this site has now censused on the corrected method, the table reads Ford 41, GM 15, Toyota 15, Hyundai 15, Honda 4, Kia 4. Ford's lead over the three-way tie at 15 is 2.7 to one. And in every brand, in every census in the series, exactly zero software fixes were flagged over-the-air: 0 of 41 for Ford, 0 of 10 for Lincoln, 0 of 15 for GM. Every fix in this census is a dealer visit.[1][2][3][4][5][6]

Now the honest accounting, because 41 is a count, not a verdict. One campaign is a small correction, and it is an expansion of a 2025 recall rather than a new defect, so strip it and Ford is at 40 with a different composition. Lincoln's 10 is mostly shared with Ford, so the company-wide 44 is only 3 more than the Ford-only count, and the "never counted" framing oversells a 3-campaign delta. Counts are not severity scores: a 23-vehicle torque check and a 565,691-vehicle fire-risk recall each count once, and Ford files aggressively under its November 2024 consent order while other makers may set a higher bar. Worst of all, the method has a hole where the trucks are. NHTSA's API returns zero rows for F-250, F-350, and every Super Duty spelling tried, with or without a model-year filter, yet four of the 41 campaign summaries explicitly cover F-250 and F-350 trucks. Yesterday's census credited the F-250 with 6 campaigns and the F-350 with 5; those counts are not reproducible from the API, and the discrepancy is unresolved. Treat 41 as a floor with an unknown ceiling. It is the best count this method can produce, and the method cannot see heavy-duty pickups at all.[1]

What to do with this: if you own a 2025-2027 Explorer Hybrid or a 2024-2027 Nautilus Hybrid, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V415. Its symptom is silence, no warning sound below 30 kph in electric mode, and the 28-speaker Nautilus gets a new signal processing module while other vehicles wait on a remedy still under development. If you own a 2026-2027 Nautilus Hybrid or a 2027 Corsair Hybrid, check for 26V688: a wrench light with "Service Vehicle Soon" or "Shifting System Error" is the warning, and a shift into neutral while driving means pull over and call the dealer, because the replacement module has no firm date yet. If you own a 2025-2027 Navigator, check for 26V691, and if the front passenger seat moves on its own, the fix is a free software update that disables the chauffeur mode. If you are shopping a 2026 Ford or Lincoln, the Explorer carries 12 campaigns, the Bronco 9, the F-150 8, the Aviator 4, the Navigator 3. A first-year redesign with a dozen federal campaigns is a reason to buy the second year, or to negotiate like you know. And if you run a recall census of your own, never trust a bare model name, and never trust one make to cover a company. A 400 is not always a zero, and a make is not always a company.
