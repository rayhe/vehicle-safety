## 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Lede does its job in three sentences: six dead, nine-month-old story, sheriff rewrites it. The three-layer convergence paragraph is the article's spine and it holds. Weakest stretch is the bus-driver section, which spends a paragraph on a distraction that the article itself then concedes was probably irrelevant to the outcome; the concession is honest but the section still reads like a detour. Headline is three clauses long and works because each clause is a separate news fact. Structure (crash → lab → notebooks → other driver → fire → vehicle → caveats → action) is the right order. Docked for the bus-driver detour and for the headline's third clause repeating the fire point the body makes better.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.7/10
Rex's deadpan noir is present throughout: "The lab just had better evidence" (cut in rhythm fix, replaced by a cleaner merge), "A warning system cannot stop a decision," "the crossing was either a decision or a drug." No banned phrases, no AI tells in the final text. Rhythm now passes with real variance (3039) and a genuine mix of fragments and long builds. Two sentences still open with "And" ("And the Tacoma itself is not the story," "And if you drive a 2021 Tacoma"), which is a Rex tic the panel will tolerate once but not twice in future drafts. "The honest version" is the kind of phrase that would be a banned-pattern cousin if it recurred; it does not. Docked for the doubled "And" openers.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.6/10
The deceased pickup driver is named with her meth level, her notebooks, and her suicidal thoughts. All of it is public record (sheriff's announcement; NTSB public docket), and the article attributes the intent finding to the sheriff rather than asserting it. Bus passengers are unnamed in the article; only the two drivers are named, which is the right line for a piece whose analysis needs the drivers, not the passengers. The suicide framing is handled without speculation about motive beyond what the notebooks and the sheriff establish. One genuine concern: "the crossing was either a decision or a drug" is reductive about a woman whose toxicology and mental-health evidence point in more than two directions, and the article's own counterargument paragraph says so; the line survives because the caveat sits two sentences later. Docked for that compression and for naming the driver in the same breath as the meth number in the lede, which is accurate but brutal.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Attribution discipline is the article's best feature: sheriff's intent finding vs. NTSB pending probable cause is stated twice and never blurred; the postmortem-redistribution caveat on the meth number is in the same paragraph as the number; the bus driver's phone records are explicitly flagged as never obtained rather than silently omitted. All six reference URLs were opened and verified in the research run (NTSB prelim page, both docket PDFs, USA Today, FHWA PDF, FARS). "Five death certificates are being changed to homicide" matches the sheriff's "will be changed" phrasing. Docked for one soft spot: "about ninety seconds" for the escape window is derived from the fire timeline (window 1:15, survivor out 1:24, flames 2:43) and is presented as an approximation, which it is, but the derivation is not shown in the article text.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Limitations are stated in the article (pending probable cause, cavity-blood comparability, unknown precrash speed/braking, unseparated causes of death, missing bus-driver phone records) and expanded in the research file. Docked because the EDR precrash speed/braking data exists in the docket's Technical Reconstruction Attachment and was not pulled; the article discloses the gap, but the gap is real.
