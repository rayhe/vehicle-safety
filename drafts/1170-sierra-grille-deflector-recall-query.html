GM's Sierra Grille Recall Is Under Investigation. The Fix May Not Fix Anything.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 24, 2027

If you had the Sierra grille recall done, go check your truck. NHTSA's defect investigators opened a federal Recall Query on October 9 because the upper grille deflector keeps detaching at highway speed, and 21 of the 118 complaints in the record say it happened after the recall repair was already performed.[1] A recall is a promise that the problem is solved. This one is now being investigated for breaking the promise.

13%
Of the 535,639 trucks NHTSA now has under review, about 13% were covered by GM's original February 2025 recall

On February 6, 2025, the original recall went out under NHTSA campaign 25V-060: 70,768 2022 Sierra 1500s with chrome grille deflectors, after GM found the attachments holding the deflector can fracture over time until all eight break and the piece separates at speed.[2] Dealers would reinforce or repair the attachment points free of charge, plus a warning owners could actually use: a rattle from the front of the truck can precede full separation.[3] Narrow, specific, fixable: that is what a recall is supposed to look like, right up until the scope turned out to be wrong.

GM limited the recall to 2022s built with chrome deflectors after the mid-cycle refresh, excluding every 2022 without the chrome piece and every 2023 and 2024 on the grounds that non-chrome grilles were designed differently and had not shown the problem.[4] NHTSA's query names all three model years anyway. Population under review: 535,639 trucks.[1] Do the division. That is about 13 percent of the trucks regulators now say may be affected. When a recall reaches one truck in eight, "we recalled it" is a technicality.

Then the remedy question: twenty-one complaints allege the deflector detached after 25V-060 was performed. That is 17.8 percent of the complaints in the record, which reads as alarming until you do the denominator work: it is not a failure rate, because nobody knows how many repaired trucks the 21 came from.[5] But it is the number that matters, because a repair that fails is worse than no recall at all: it teaches owners the problem is handled. Complaints describe the loose piece cracking windshields and forcing nearby drivers into evasive maneuvers, while GM has logged four accidents tied to the issue with no reported injuries and the ODI opening resume lists no crashes, injuries, or fatalities.[1][4] Two records, slightly different tallies, one shared fact: nobody has been seriously hurt yet.

Credit where due, and it is due: a Recall Query is not a finding. NHTSA has not declared a defect, and these queries sometimes close with no action at all. Some of the 21 could be botched dealer repairs, owners who never actually got the work done, or a different failure mode wearing the same costume, and complaints are allegations, not verdicts. GM self-filed 25V-060 in the first place, which is exactly the behavior the system rewards. Auditing your own recall is good; needing the audit is not. It worked the way it was designed to work, and it worked because drivers did the paperwork.

What to do: drive a 2022 through 2024 Sierra 1500? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for campaign 25V-060, and listen for the rattle, which is GM's own early warning. If you had the repair done, inspect the front end anyway, because the complaint record says the fix can fail. ODI complaint volume is what opened this query, and 118 reports is what bought a federal investigation, so if your deflector is loose or gone, file at nhtsa.gov. Keep writing them in.
