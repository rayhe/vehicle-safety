# Research: Ford's Award-Winning 1.0L EcoBoost — From Engine of the Year to "Unreasonable Safety Risk"

## Angle
Ford's 1.0-liter EcoBoost three-cylinder engine won International Engine of the Year three years running (2012–2014), plus 10+ additional awards. NHTSA has now upgraded its defect investigation into the engine and declared it poses an "unreasonable risk to motor vehicle safety." The timing belt degrades, clogs the oil pump screen, and causes sudden power loss or engine seizure — at an average of 70,000 miles, well below Ford's 150,000-mile belt replacement interval. All three vehicles it powered in the US (Fiesta, Focus, EcoSport) are discontinued. Ford had "no immediate comment."

## Novel Analysis
Cross-tabulation: Award count vs. safety defect severity. The 1.0L EcoBoost may be the most decorated production engine in history to subsequently be declared an "unreasonable safety risk" by a federal regulator. The engineering failure mode is particularly ironic: the timing belt — a maintenance item Ford told owners to replace at 150,000 miles — is degrading at 70,000. Not a design defect in the traditional sense, but a fundamentally wrong maintenance interval that Ford published and owners followed.

The orphan vehicle problem: Ford has zero revenue incentive to recall these vehicles. All three models are discontinued in the US. Ford's response so far — a voluntary "customer satisfaction program" that reduces the maintenance interval and offers reimbursement — isn't a recall. NHTSA's upgrade to engineering analysis is the regulatory step that can compel one.
