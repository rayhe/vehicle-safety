## Angle
On November 16, 2026, Electrek's running investigation into fatal Tesla Autopilot/FSD crashes hidden in Tesla's own federal filings added three new cases and a fourth that had been missing from the data entirely. The count this site covered in #905 on September 10 was 8 crashes, 8 lives. It is now 12 crashes, 16 lives. The deaths doubled in nine weeks, and every one of the new cases was reported to the public as an ordinary accident with no mention of a driver-assist system. The newest batch includes a Model 3 driving into an overturned box truck at 71 mph, killing a former Marine who had stopped to help, in the same under-truck geometry that killed Joshua Brown in 2016 and Jeremy Banner in 2019. Tesla's report on that crash arrived more than three months late despite being filed in the urgent 5-day category. A second Naples crash in the same release was filed under October because Tesla logs timestamps in UTC, so anyone checking September's numbers would never find it.

## Verified facts

### The count
- September 10, 2026 (#905): 8 confirmed fatal crashes, 8 lives.
- November 16, 2026: **12 confirmed fatal crashes, 16 lives** (Electrek hub; The Driven).
- Four new cases added 8 lives: Vineland 1, Naples I-75 3, El Dorado Hills 3, Naples Golden Gate 1.
- September 2026 set a record: **264 Tesla driver-assist crashes reported in one month**, beating July's 236 (Electrek Naples article).

### The four new cases

**1. Naples, FL — Golden Gate Parkway, September 30, 2026, 11:43 p.m.**
- 2025 Tesla Model X heading east, approaching Estuary Boulevard. Left the road, hit a traffic signal pole, rolled over. Passenger, a 50-year-old woman from Fort Myers, pronounced dead at the scene. Driver, 48, also Fort Myers, hospitalized with non-life-threatening injuries. FHP: crash under investigation; no cause, speed, impairment, or driver-assist mention released. Public account: one sentence, the car "veered off the road."
- Tesla's SGO report: "Verified Engaged" (Autopilot or FSD confirmed on via telematics), pre-crash speed **39 mph**, pole/tree collision, airbag deployment, front and top damage. Tesla checked EDR, telematics, video, and "other" as available, and says it learned of the crash through its own telematics, not the media.
- Redacted as confidential business information: the crash narrative, the software version, whether the road was inside the system's operating domain. Same treatment Tesla gives 99.9% of its reports.
- Filing quirk: Tesla logged the incident at 03:41 UTC on October 1 (11:41 p.m. Florida, Sept 30). The record lists the crash in **October**, so anyone checking Tesla's data against September crashes misses it.
- The driver survived, so FHP can ask him whether Autopilot or FSD was on. Electrek reports asking FHP whether investigators knew the system was engaged and whether they got Tesla's video and raw data or only Tesla's interpretation. No answer reported yet.

**2. Naples, FL — I-75 near Pine Ridge Road, May 31, 2026, ~3:54 a.m.**
- A 31-year-old driver lost control of a 2014 Freightliner box truck near the Pine Ridge Road exit, steered back onto southbound I-75, and flipped it, blocking the center and outside lanes. Jonathan Garcia Arias, 25, a former Marine driving a BMW, pulled over and got out to help the truck driver.
- A southbound 2023 Tesla Model 3 carrying five young men from Naples came up on the scene. FHP: the front of the Tesla hit the underside of the overturned truck and Garcia Arias. Two passengers, aged 18 and 19, died at the scene. Garcia Arias died later in the hospital. The 20-year-old driver and two other passengers were seriously injured.
- Police and local media covered it as a truck wreck in the dark. None mentioned a driver-assist system. FHP trooper Kenn Watson on the Tesla driver: "No lights, no reflections, nothing to warn them." The BMW driver, a human, saw the obstacle and stopped.
- Tesla's SGO report: 2023 Model 3, highway, traffic incident on roadway, "Verified Engaged," pre-crash speed **71 mph**, FHP investigating, learned of the crash from police and media. Narrative, software version, operating domain redacted as CBI.
- Filing defect: Tesla filed it as a 5-day report, the urgent category for fatal crashes, but it did not land until Tesla's **September filings, more than three months after the crash**.
- The geometry is the same under-truck scenario that killed Joshua Brown in 2016 and Jeremy Banner in 2019 on Autopilot. Electrek notes it explicitly. Ten years, same crash.
- This is the second fatal Tesla driver-assist crash in Naples in the same NHTSA release. FHP investigated both. Neither was ever publicly tied to Autopilot or FSD.

**3. El Dorado Hills, CA — Serrano Parkway, September 12, 2026, ~10:38 p.m.**
- A 2024 Tesla Model Y with four people inside, heading west on Serrano Parkway between Terracina Drive and Silva Valley Parkway, a residential part of the Serrano community east of Sacramento. CHP Placerville: the car left the road "for unknown reasons," hit a tree on the north side, caught fire. Call came ~10:38 p.m.; on arrival the car was fully engulfed.
- Charles Anthony Cameron, 30, of Sacramento, thrown from the car, died at the scene. Alejandra Mendoza, 28, of Sacramento, and Kailauna Viola Mills, 28, of Roseville, a bride-to-be visiting from Arizona, found dead inside after firefighters put out the fire. A fourth person, a 28-year-old Sacramento woman, survived with major injuries. CHP: cause still under investigation; speed, impairment, and which woman was driving not released. Nobody said a word about Autopilot.
- Tesla's SGO report: 2024 Model Y, consumer vehicle, "Verified Engaged," pre-crash speed **74 mph**, fatality, CHP investigating. Narrative, software version, operating domain redacted as CBI.
- Two more filing defects: Tesla lists the roadway type as **"Unknown"** and the object struck as **"Unknown"**, even though police said it was a tree. And Tesla says it learned of the crash **from the media**, not its own telematics, despite the car phoning home constantly.
- Match: Tesla logged 05:36 UTC (10:36 p.m. Pacific, Sept 12), two minutes before the CHP call. Only fatal Tesla driver-assist report from El Dorado Hills in the data.

**4. Vineland, NJ — Route 55 (added between Sept 10 and Nov 16)**
- A 2021 Model Y crossed the Route 55 median, hit a tree, slid into oncoming traffic, killing 68-year-old Phillip Eastman, a school board member and longtime youth hockey coach, in his SUV. Police reported only that the Tesla's driver "lost control." No mention of a driver-assist system.
- Tesla's data: driver-assist verified engaged. Electrek flagged this case from the news as missing from the federal data, then watched it surface in the next NHTSA release, confirmed fatal.
- This is the case that proves the database itself has holes: a fatal crash with the system engaged was absent from the federal record until a journalist noticed it was missing.

### The transparency pattern (hub)
- Tesla reports its Autopilot/FSD crashes as required, then blacks out the narrative, software version, and operating-domain field on 99.9% of reports every year since 2019. GM, Ford, Honda, Toyota redact essentially none of their narratives. Tesla is the only high-volume reporter that hides everything.
- Selective disclosure: when the data helps Tesla, it comes out. In the fatal Katy, Texas crash, Tesla shared data pointing to the driver overriding the system. When it does not help, the report is sealed.
- NHTSA opened an Audit Query in August 2025 into Tesla's late crash reporting ("evaluate the cause of the potential delays in reporting, the scope of any such delays, and the mitigations"). Tesla blamed an "error" in data collection.
- Tesla's own AI trainers told Reuters they don't trust its safety stats (per hub).
- The hub's framing: Tesla sells "Full Self-Driving" and promises unsupervised autonomy while the system remains Level 2, human responsible at all times. The marketing invites trust; the redactions withhold the evidence.

## Original calculations (for the article)
- **Growth:** 8 crashes / 8 lives (Sept 10) to 12 crashes / 16 lives (Nov 16). Cases +50%, deaths +100%, in 9 weeks. The four new cases hold 8 of the 16 lives: half the death toll arrived in the update.
- **Deaths per crash:** 16 lives / 12 crashes = 1.33 deaths per crash. The three new multi-fatal crashes (I-75: 3, El Dorado Hills: 3) drive the average; the original eight were all single-fatality.
- **Filing lag:** I-75 crash May 31, report surfaced in September filings: 3+ months for a 5-day urgent filing. The rule says five days. Tesla took roughly twenty times that.
- **The month-shift:** Naples Golden Gate, Sept 30, 11:43 p.m. local, logged 03:41 UTC Oct 1. Filed under October. A September tally of Tesla's fatal driver-assist crashes misses a September death.
- **The record month:** 264 Tesla driver-assist crashes in September 2026, up from 236 in July. That is the entire industry's SGO volume dominated by one reporter: Tesla is ~85% of all reports, nearly 4,000 crashes total.
- **The redaction rate:** 99.9% of Tesla's reports black out the narrative. GM, Ford, Honda, Toyota: essentially 0%. The gap is not a rounding error. It is a policy.
- **Scale check (do not overclaim):** 16 lives is a rounding error against ~36,640 annual US road deaths (NHTSA 2025 early estimate). The story is not that these 16 deaths move the national total. It is that the public record of how a driver-assist system behaves in fatal crashes is being written by the company that sells the system, and the company keeps the sentences that matter.

## What this does NOT prove (limitations)
- "Verified Engaged" means Autopilot or FSD was on within 30 seconds of impact. It does not prove the system caused any of these crashes. The I-75 truck was overturned and unlit in the dark; the El Dorado Hills cause is still under investigation; the Naples pole crash has a surviving driver who has not been quoted. The article must say "engaged" every time, never "caused," and must carry the counterargument at full strength.
- Electrek's matching is journalism, not a federal finding. The matches rest on time, city, vehicle, and outcome lining up, and Electrek says so. One match (Naples Golden Gate) is two minutes off FHP's time; the method is sound but it is still a match, not a subpoena.
- The 12-case list is a floor, not a census. Vineland proves cases can be absent from the data entirely. There are more Tesla SGO fatal reports Electrek has not matched yet, and the count will keep moving.
- The 264-crash September figure is a report count, not a crash rate. Tesla has more ADAS miles than anyone, and more reports do not by themselves prove a higher per-mile crash rate. The hub says this itself.
- Tesla's 71 mph (I-75) and 74 mph (El Dorado Hills) figures are telematics snapshots, likely pre-crash speed rather than speed at impact. Electrek flags this. Do not write "hit the truck at 71 mph" as impact speed.
- The Joshua Brown / Jeremy Banner parallel is a geometry parallel, not a finding that the same software failure occurred. The systems, sensors, and software are ten years apart. The point is that the scenario keeps recurring, not that the cause is identical.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
Tesla will say, correctly, that none of this proves Autopilot or FSD caused a single one of these crashes. Every case has a human story that could explain it on its own: a truck overturned and invisible in the dark, a car leaving a residential parkway for unknown reasons, a driver who may have been distracted or impaired. The systems are Level 2 by law and by Tesla's own terms: the human is driving, the human is responsible, and a driver who trusts a Level 2 system as if it were autonomous is misusing it. Tesla reports more crashes than anyone because it has more driver-assist miles than anyone and because its cars phone home; GM's SuperCruise has 55 reports because almost nobody drives a SuperCruise. The redactions are legal: NHTSA's own rules let manufacturers claim confidential business information, and Tesla's competitors could do the same. And the company has a point about selective reading: when its data exonerated the system in Katy, Texas, it shared it, which is what a company confident in its product does. The counter to the counter: a 5-day urgent filing that takes three months is not a paperwork quirk, it is a violation of the reporting rule's spirit and possibly its letter, and NHTSA already opened an Audit Query over exactly this. A crash filed under the wrong month because of a UTC timestamp is a database that cannot be trusted to count. A report that lists the struck object as "Unknown" when the police report says "tree" is a company that did not read its own file. And "the human is responsible" is a strange defense from a company whose product is named Full Self-Driving, whose CEO promises unsupervised autonomy is months away, and which withholds the video that would settle whether the human or the machine made the fatal move. If the system did nothing wrong in Naples, Tesla has the footage to prove it. It sealed the footage instead.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you drive a Tesla with Autopilot or FSD: the system is Level 2. It is not autonomous, whatever the name on the screen says. Hands on the wheel, eyes on the road, and never trust it around an overturned truck, a stopped vehicle in a live lane, or a road departure at night. The three new cases are all scenarios where the system was on and the car still left the road or drove into something stopped.
- If you are investigating a Tesla crash, or you are a family member of someone killed in one: the police report is not the whole record. Tesla files a separate report with NHTSA under the Standing General Order, and the engagement status in that report is the fact the public record usually omits. Ask the investigating agency whether it received Tesla's raw telemetry or only Tesla's interpretation.
- The count is a floor. Electrek updates its hub as it confirms more cases. Check electrek.co/2026/09/01/tesla-autopilot-fsd-crashes-hidden-data/ for the running list.
- The September 2026 NHTSA SGO release is the dataset. It is public at nhtsa.gov. Tesla's redactions are the reason a journalist has to match crashes by hand.
