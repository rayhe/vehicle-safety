Hyundai Shipped 41 Cars With Loose Nuts. Then It Did It Again.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2026

Before you sign that lease on a 2026 Elantra, read this first. Hyundai filed two separate safety recalls in late October covering 41 brand-new vehicles, and both say the same thing: the nuts were never tightened.[1]

41
2026-model Hyundais recalled for insufficiently torqued fasteners, across two NHTSA campaigns

Campaign 26V692 covers 24 cars, twenty Elantras and four Tucson Hybrids, whose strut fasteners may separate from the suspension. Campaign 26V693 covers 17 cars, eight Venues and nine Elantras across the sedan lineup, whose wheel lug nuts may loosen until a wheel detaches.[2] A detached wheel at highway speed is not a maintenance issue but a projectile.

Both recalls came from Hyundai's Ulsan plant in South Korea, the same factory that builds U.S.-bound Genesis GV80s. Both trace to the same fastener supplier, Samjin Nut. Both blame manufacturing lapses, not defective parts.[1] Hyundai wants you to hear that as reassuring, and it is not. If the parts were fine and the process still shipped 41 cars with loose safety-critical fasteners, the quality system is what failed, and a quality system cannot be replaced at a dealer service bay.

The timelines are damning in different ways: for 26V693, a temporary manual torquing process ran at Ulsan while wheel-tightening tooling was modified, and the manual process ended September 16, 2026. For 26V692, the affected Elantras span production from December 30, 2025 through July 6, 2026, a seven-month window in which strut fasteners were inadequately torqued and the torque data was recorded incorrectly, meaning nobody even had a paper trail to check.[3][4] Hyundai implemented new torque monitoring and sign-off procedures on September 9. That is the date the plant started verifying something it had apparently not been verifying all year.

Now the part that should anger every owner: the notification letters go out December 19, 2026, roughly two months after NHTSA received the filings.[5] No incidents have been reported, and no do-not-drive advisory was issued, so Hyundai considers this a routine cadence. But if you bought one of these cars in September, your wheel lug nuts have been under-torqued for weeks while you waited for a letter, so do not wait; run your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, and call Hyundai at 1-855-371-9460 if it matches. The fix is free: a dealer inspects and retorques the fasteners, and replaces the hub bearing assembly where needed.

To be fair, this is the recall system working as designed. Forty-one cars is a tiny population, every one of them individually identifiable by VIN, zero injuries, and Hyundai self-reported both campaigns while tightening its own monitoring, which is prevention working as designed, and that is the whole point, but prevention that takes seven months to notice is not much of a system, and the next owner in line deserves to know exactly which plant, which supplier, and which two months of 2026 get the asterisk on their carfax.

What this story does not prove: NHTSA's records give population counts only, so we cannot say how many of the 41 are already remedied or still sitting in dealer inventory, and Hyundai's method for drawing the population boundaries is not public, so we cannot rule out that other vehicles touched by the same manual torquing process were excluded. The manual process ended September 16, which Hyundai cites as the containment date, but the recall documents do not publish the audit trail.
