- Novel angle: the 2024 "fix" was not a fix. It was an amputation. With no
  improved part ready, BMW's interim remedy disconnected the reverse gear
  control unit from the bike's electrical system, leaving owners with the
  hardware but without the feature for two years. The 2026 recall is the
  real repair, and BMW's own Part 573 report explains why the interim
  measure failed: some relay installs were done incorrectly, and in other
  bikes the corrosion already inside the unit at relay-install time was too
  advanced for the relay to prevent a thermal event. Original contribution:
  the timeline arithmetic (below) — a safety feature deleted for two years,
  a population that shrank 418 bikes between campaigns, and a defect whose
  geography (tropical, hot, humid markets first) told the story before the
  US filing did.

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- **NHTSA campaign 26V658 (report received Oct 7, 2026; acknowledgement
  letter Oct 15, 2026).** BMW of North America recalling certain 2021-2024
  BMW R 18 motorcycles equipped with the optional reverse gear. "The seal
  for the reverse gear control unit may deteriorate and allow moisture
  inside the control unit, which can result in the unit overheating."
  Consequence: "An overheated reverse gear control unit increases the risk
  of a fire." Population: **4,630 units**. Remedy: dealers replace the
  reverse gear control unit, free of charge. Owner letters expected
  **November 27, 2026**. BMW customer service 1-800-525-7417. "Motorcycles
  in this recall were previously recalled under 24V716. Motorcycles
  previously repaired under the prior recall will need the new repair
  completed." Component: POWER TRAIN:MANUAL TRANSMISSION:SEALS/GASKETS.
  (NHTSA RCAK-26V658-9668.pdf)
- **NHTSA campaign 24V716 (report Sep 27, 2024; 5,048 units).** Same seal
  defect, same model years. Interim remedy: "the reverse gear control unit
  will be disconnected from the electrical system of the motorcycle, free
  of charge. Once the final remedy is available, dealers will replace the
  reverse gear control unit, free of charge." Owner letters Nov 18, 2024.
  At filing BMW knew of no US accidents, injuries, or thermal events, and
  counted about 21 US warranty claims tied to the problem. Build window
  for the 2024 population: June 19, 2020 to July 11, 2024. (NHTSA
  RCAK-24V716-7101.pdf; FastLaneOnly summary of the 2024 defect report)
- **Part 573 report for 26V658 — why the interim fix failed.** April 15,
  2025: BMW informed of a thermal event in a tropical non-US market,
  despite the protective relay having been installed. June 2025 to May
  2026: additional field incidents in tropical or otherwise hot and humid
  markets, plus one incident in the US market. Engineering evaluation:
  several cases traced to incorrect repair work installing the relay; for
  all others, overheating occurred only with the ignition on, and the
  internal corrosion level (predamage) of the control unit at relay
  installation was at a level where the protective relay may not have been
  sufficient to prevent a thermal event. BMW decided Sep 30, 2026 to recall
  to replace the unit. BMW reports no accidents or injuries related to
  this issue. (NHTSA RCLRPT-26V658-0458.pdf)
- **The 2024 chronology (geography told the story first).** First thermal
  event outside the US: June 2023. Higher failure rates in hot, humid
  Southeast Asian markets led to a voluntary recall there in February
  2024; worldwide extension September 19, 2024. (FastLaneOnly summary of
  BMW's 2024 defect report)
- **Transport Canada recall 2026494 (published Oct 7, 2026).** Same defect
  and remedy: "the recall repairs completed by a BMW Motorrad dealer
  during Transport Canada recall no. 2024-561 may not be effective... the
  reverser control unit may not have been sealed properly. As a result,
  water could enter the control unit and cause it to corrode... A short
  circuit can create the risk of a fire." Corrective action: replace the
  reverser control unit. (Transport Canada recalls database)
- **Original arithmetic (computed for this article, inputs cited above):**
  5,048 (2024) minus 4,630 (2026) = **418 fewer bikes** in the second
  campaign, an 8.3% shrinkage. The filings do not explain the difference;
  do not assert a cause (scrappage, exports, and the "equipped with
  optional reverse gear" scoping are all possible, none confirmed).
  Time between campaigns: Sep 27, 2024 to Oct 7, 2026 = **just over two
  years** during which the official remedy was a disconnected feature.

## What this article must NOT claim
- That any R 18 caught fire in the US. BMW reports no US accidents or
  injuries; the US incident in the Part 573 report is described only as an
  incident that "may have been related."
- That the 418-bike population drop has a known cause. It is arithmetic,
  not an explanation.
- That the 2024 disconnect caused any crash or injury. It removed a
  convenience feature; no harm from the interim remedy itself is documented.
- That every 2021-2024 R 18 is affected. Only bikes equipped with the
  optional reverse gear are in 26V658.

## Actionable takeaways for readers
- If you own a 2021-2024 R 18 with reverse gear: do not assume the 2024
  dealer visit closed this out. The 2026 recall explicitly requires a new
  repair even for bikes repaired under 24V716. Check your VIN at
  nhtsa.gov/recalls; letters go out Nov 27, 2026, but the dealer can check
  now.
- Warning signs from BMW's own 2024 owner letter: reverse gear stops
  working, smell of overheating electrical components, or smoke. If any
  appear, stop riding, move away from traffic, call BMW Motorrad Roadside
  Assistance at 1-877-680-2176 (do not keep riding to the dealer).
- The remedy is a full control-unit replacement, free of charge, at any
  authorized BMW Motorrad dealer. BMW customer service: 1-800-525-7417.

## Limitations (honest)
- The Part 573 report's incident counts are BMW's own field reports, not
  independently verified failure counts; "thermal event" is BMW's term and
  the filings do not say how many of the tropical-market events involved
  open flame versus overheating/smoke.
- The two-year "feature deleted" framing rests on the interim remedy
  description (disconnect) plus the two-year gap to the replacement part;
  individual owners' experience may vary if dealers replaced units earlier
  under warranty.
- No US fire, crash, or injury is tied to this defect in any filing; the
  risk is prospective, stated by BMW and NHTSA as fire risk, not realized
  US harm.

## Strongest counterargument
A two-year disconnect followed by a free replacement part is the recall
system working at the speed of parts availability: BMW caught a
moisture-driven failure mode, neutralized it immediately by cutting
power to the unit, and shipped a real fix once one existed, with zero US
injuries in the meantime. Against that: the neutralization only worked if
the relay was installed correctly and the unit was not already corroded,
and BMW's own report concedes both failure modes happened. A safety
intervention that depends on perfect installation and pre-existing
condition is not a fix. It is a hope with a torque spec.
