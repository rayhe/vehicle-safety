Three New Cars Got the Park-Outside Order This Year. The 720,796-Car One Went to Used BMWs.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: NHTSA has two orders it almost never gives. Park outside. Do not drive. One means your car might catch fire where it sits. One means your car might come apart while you drive it. I counted both across every 2026 model-year vehicle sold in America, 39 makes, 2,782 API queries, and the answer is 3 park-outside orders and 4 do-not-drive orders, covering 96,389 and 4,882 vehicles. Out of 188 recall campaigns touching a 2026 car this year, seven carry an extreme advisory. That is 3.7 percent.[1]

720,796
Used BMWs in the park-outside order the census excludes, 7.5 times the entire counted park-outside population

Method first, because a count is only as honest as its rules. I pulled the canonical 2026 model list from NHTSA's VPIC database for every make, then queried the recalls API for every nameplate plus the Hybrid, Plug-In Hybrid, Electric, and Recharge variants, and kept campaigns with a 26V number and a report date in calendar 2026. Where the API's vocabulary failed, I closed the gap by hand: Mercedes needed trim-level spellings, not class names; the Blazer EV is missing from VPIC's Chevrolet list entirely; the Kicks hides under a bare name; Mack returns a 400 for every spelling tried. Every brand count in this census now matches its standalone census in this series, which is how I know the gaps are closed.[1]

The 3 park-outside campaigns touching a 2026 model-year vehicle
CampaignDateVehiclesWhat it is
26V106Feb 2469,153Subaru fuel cap gasket; near-full tank plus heat spills fuel from the filler neck. Park outside, half a tank. 33 leak reports, 0 fires.
26V188Mar 2551Nissan LEAF battery; cathode damage in manufacturing, internal short. Park outside, do not charge. 2 thermal incidents.
26V540Aug 2027,185Lucid Air eFuse overcurrent on exterior lighting circuits. Park outside until software 2.10.0. 3 fires, 4 smoke incidents.

All three carry a 100 percent defect estimate. Read that again. Not one percent, not ten. Every car in each population carries the defect. Park-outside is not issued for "some cars might." It is issued when the manufacturer can trace the defect to every single car, and the only honest instruction left is to move them all away from structures. A recall that covers 100 cars to catch 1 is a blunt instrument. A park-outside order is what happens when the instrument is sharp and the answer is still all of them.[2][3][4]

Nissan's is the smallest and the strangest. Fifty-one LEAFs. Every one defective. Two of them already burned, and both burned in the exact conditions the advisory prescribes: parked outside, turned off, not charging. One in Osaka on February 16, one in the United States on March 2. Nissan traced the bad batteries by ID to the VIN, all 51 of them, which is why the recall is 51 cars and not 51,000. Precision is supposed to be reassuring. Here it means the fire found the cars before the paperwork did.[3]

Lucid's is the largest and the most absurd. Twenty-seven thousand one hundred eighty-five Air sedans, essentially every Air ever built, and the fix is software the owner installs in the driveway. Version 2.10.0 went out over the air in July 2026, a month before the filing. At filing, 20,719 cars had it and 6,466 did not. So the order is: park outside, then fix the car from the driveway it is parked outside in. NHTSA's own API insists this fix is not over-the-air, flagging it false, while the remedy text says Lucid "has released an over-the-air (OTA) software update." Both cannot be true. The remedy text is right, and the flag is wrong, same as it was for the last three censuses in this series.[2][9]

The 4 do-not-drive campaigns touching a 2026 model-year vehicle

26V340May 274,653Ford ball joint, front lower control arm; separation means loss of control. 1% defect estimate.

Two of the four do-not-drives are the same component. Ford's 26V340 covers 4,653 Bronco Sports and Mavericks whose front lower control arm ball joints may have been assembled or repaired wrong, so the ball stud sits only partly engaged in the knuckle. Subaru's 26V685 covers eight Crosstreks and Foresters, four of each, built in August 2026, whose left-front ball joint castle nut may never have been tightened to spec. Same part, same year, 581 times the scale. One field report, dated September 15, 2026, describes a Subaru ball joint that had already separated. Subaru says there may be no warning before it happens.[5][6][8]

Somewhere in that gap sits the whole story of modern recalls. Ford cannot tell which of its 4,653 ball joints is bad, so all 4,653 owners get told not to drive. Subaru traced its eight bad nuts through supplier records to the exact cars, so eight owners get told not to drive. Both orders are correct. Only one of them is precise, and precision is a luxury bought with traceability, not with care. A manufacturer that can name its eight bad cars looks competent. A manufacturer that must ground 4,653 to catch 46 looks honest. Both are selling you a ball joint that can come apart.[5][8]

Now the filing the count leaves out, which is where the story actually lives for anyone who already owns a car. BMW's 26V733, filed November 4, covers 720,796 cars from model years 2012 through 2018, a water pump connector that can short and catch fire, with no remedy available and an instruction to park outside. It touches zero 2026 models, so the census excludes it, same as the L87 engine in the GMC census. Add the Mack twin of Volvo's wheel recall, 26V351, 644 trucks, same defect, same week, invisible to the API because every Mack model spelling returns a 400. For every new car NHTSA told to park outside this year, 7.5 used cars got the same order.[7][10][11]

Fairness requires the other side, stated at full strength, because the 3-and-4 count flatters the new-car market four ways. First, the count is small because the method is narrow: most extreme advisories hit older cars by design, and the 720,796 BMWs are the rule, not the exception. Second, the vehicle totals are dominated by one campaign: Subaru's fuel cap gasket is 69,153 of the 96,389 park-outside vehicles, and it is a fifty-cent part with zero fires. Third, the 100-percent pattern is partly definitional: manufacturers issue the extreme advisories only when they can trace the defect to every car, so the pattern says as much about when lawyers allow the order as about how dangerous new cars are. Fourth, the do-not-drive list is dominated by precision recalls, eight Subarus and 66 GMs, which are traceability exercises more than safety crises. Strip the fuel cap, and the park-outside total falls to 27,236. Strip the used BMWs back in, and the new-car story nearly vanishes.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 96,389 park-outside total counts every vehicle in each filing across all model years, not 2026-only cars. The 69,153 in 26V106 includes 51,707 2025 Forester Hybrids and a Canadian share (66,332 U.S. vehicles per Subaru's dealer bulletin), so the 2026-only park-outside total is smaller and cannot be computed exactly from the filings. Populations are the manufacturers' filed estimates, not counts of broken vehicles; the 1 percent defect estimate on Ford's 4,653 means roughly 46 cars actually carry the bad ball joint, and nobody knows which 46. NHTSA's over-the-air flag undercounts software remedies, as it has in every census in this series: zero of seven flagged campaigns carry a true flag, even though the Lucid fix is explicitly over-the-air. Late filings will move every number in this article, and the 2026 model year is still in progress.

What to do with all of this. If you own a 2022 through 2026 Lucid Air, check your software version now and install 2.10.0 or later, then park outside until you do; 6,466 cars had not installed it at filing, and those are the actual risk pool. If you own a 2026 Nissan LEAF, check your VIN for 26V188, park outside, and do not charge until a dealer inspects the battery; all 51 cars carry the defect, and the two that burned were already parked outside. If you own a 2026 Crosstrek Hybrid or a 2025 Forester Hybrid, keep the tank at half or below and park outside until the dealer replaces the fuel filler cap gasket; a half tank is a real mitigation, because the defect needs a near-full tank plus a temperature rise. If you own a 2021 through 2026 Bronco Sport or a 2022 through 2026 Maverick, do not drive it until a dealer inspects the front lower control arm ball joints. If you own a 2026 Crosstrek or Forester built in August 2026, check for 26V685; eight cars, four of each, and letters go out December 16, 2026. If you own a 2012 through 2018 BMW from the 26V733 list, park outside and away from structures, and do not let a 2024 repair receipt reassure you; VINs become searchable December 25, 2026. Check any VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you sign, and check a used BMW twice.
