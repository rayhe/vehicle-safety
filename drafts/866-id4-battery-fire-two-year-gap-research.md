# Research: #866 — The ID.4 Battery Fire Two-Year Gap
Journalist: Clara Rollover | Kicker: Investigation | Date: 2026-09-10

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Volkswagen first learned of an ID.4 high-voltage battery fire in January 2024 and got three more fire reports by December 2024, but did not start recalling cars until December 2025, nearly two years later. On September 4, 2026, a federal class action landed alleging the defect, and VW has admitted it still has not found a clear root cause.

## Original computation (the novel contribution)
Knowledge-to-recall gap: Jan 18, 2024 (first known fire) -> Dec 1, 2025 (first recall 25V836000) = 683 days, or 22.5 months, during which owners were routinely charging ID.4s in home garages overnight.
Campaign genealogy (US):
| Campaign | Date | Units | Scope | Defect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25V836000 | Dec 2025 | 629 | 2023-2024 | Misaligned electrodes; park outside, no overnight charging |
| 26V028000 | Jan 21, 2026 | 670 | 2023-2024 | Misaligned electrodes; elevated fire risk |
| 26V030000 | Jan 21, 2026 | 43,881 | 2023-2025 | HV battery overheating; software + module replacement |
Total US: 45,180 vehicles across three campaigns. March 2026 global expansion: ~94,000-100,000 VW EVs worldwide.
The interim guidance (cap charging at 80%, park outside after charging, no overnight indoor charging, avoid DC fast charging) effectively asked owners not to use the car as an EV, while VW admitted it still had no clear root cause for thermal incidents outside the supplier's identified ranges.

## Thesis
For nearly two years, VW knew its ID.4 could catch fire in your garage and kept selling and servicing them as normal cars; when the recalls finally came, the interim advice was "don't charge it like an electric car," and the root cause is still unknown. The September 4 class action is the bill coming due.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
Four alleged fires over tens of thousands of vehicles is a low absolute incident rate, and investigating a battery defect across a supplier's production range before recalling is arguably responsible engineering, not delay. Recalls did happen, and VW's "no clear root cause" disclosure is honest transparency about an ongoing investigation, which is what the system is supposed to produce. The class action complaint is a plaintiff-side document whose allegations are unproven; VW has not admitted the defect theory or the timeline framing. The 22.5-month figure also elides the investigation work VW says it was doing in between.

## Limitations
- Class action allegations are unproven; framed as allegations throughout, not findings.
- Fire count and circumstances come from the complaint's summary; the underlying NHTSA documents and complaint exhibits were not independently pulled.
- FARS has no ID.4 entries (2023+ nameplate), so no real-world fatal-crash comparison is possible.
- US vehicles use SK Battery America modules (Georgia); EU vehicles use different suppliers, so the US recall population and the global KBA campaign are not the same defect population.
- No injury or property-damage figures beyond the complaint's framing were verified.

## Actionable insights
- Own a 2023-2025 ID.4? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 25V836, 26V028, 26V030. Recalls are free and follow the car, not the owner, so used buyers must check too.
- Until the remedy is complete: follow the interim guidance (charge to 80% max, park outside after charging, no overnight indoor charging).
- Battery warning light or charging alerts on an ID.4? Call your dealer before your next charge, not after.
