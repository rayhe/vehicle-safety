# Research: #1236 — NHTSA Retires the Rollover Fishhook Test

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes)
**Story slug:** 1236-nhtsa-retires-rollover-fishhook-test
**Kicker candidates:** Investigation / The Gap

## The news peg

NHTSA published a request for comments in the Federal Register on August 17, 2026 (FR Doc 2026-16735, pp. 53339-53349), signed by Administrator Jonathan Morrison, proposing to remove two legacy test procedures from the New Car Assessment Program (NCAP, the federal 5-Star Safety Ratings):

1. The **Dynamic Rollover Resistance Test** — the "Fishhook" maneuver, which simulates a panicked driver jerking the wheel to dodge something. NHTSA's stated reason: electronic stability control (now mandatory on light vehicles since MY2012 under FMVSS 126) has made the test pointless. No vehicle has tipped up in NCAP Fishhook tests in the past 15 years.
2. **Side airbag out-of-position (SAB-OOP) testing** — checks side airbag behavior when an occupant (e.g., a child) sits in the wrong spot. NHTSA says the program has met its original goals.

The comment period (docket NHTSA-2026-1651) closed October 16, 2026.

**What replaces them:** Rollover ratings will still appear on the Monroney window sticker and NHTSA's website, but starting with model year 2027 they will be derived from the static stability factor — a pure measurement ratio (track width vs. center of gravity height) — plus reliance on the ESC standard's own test. No more dynamic maneuver. The rollover star becomes geometry homework.

## The twist (the actual story)

The removals are being sold as housekeeping to free up resources. But the same notice rewrites NCAP's 10-year roadmap and **pushes the replacement tests — the modernized crashworthiness and crash-avoidance evaluations safety advocates have waited years for — back by roughly two years** (cuts land MY2027; replacements start arriving late 2029, per The Auto Wire).

Consumer Reports filed comments on October 16, 2026, calling out the contradiction directly: NHTSA justifies the test removals as freeing agency staff and funding for higher-priority safety initiatives, "yet the agency is simultaneously proposing postponements across its 10-year roadmap citing resource constraints." CR's demand: if you reclaim resources, accelerate the modernized tests — don't delay them. CR also notes U.S. NCAP has "languished for years" with nearly every vehicle earning four or five stars, giving buyers no meaningful differentiation.

So: the test died of success (ESC), the funeral is fine, but the inheritance is being spent on nothing. The budget savings exist only in the press release; the roadmap moved the other direction.

## The numbers that make this uncomfortable

- Rollover crashes account for **less than 3% of all crashes but ~28-30% of passenger vehicle occupant fatalities** (2023: 28% of all U.S. passenger vehicle occupant deaths; by body type: pickups 38%, SUVs 33%, vans 25%, cars 20%).
- ESC cut the risk of fatal single-vehicle rollover crashes by **more than 70%** (IIHS) — which is exactly why no car tips in the Fishhook test anymore. The test's own success story is its obituary.
- The static stability factor replacing it measures one thing: geometry. It says nothing about what the electronics do mid-slide, which is where ESC actually lives.

## Kill test

Is this genuinely newsworthy after 1,235 articles? Yes — the comment period closed five days ago (Oct 16), it's a structural change to how rollover safety is rated on every window sticker, and the angle is a genuine paradox: the test succeeded so completely that NHTSA killed it, then used the savings to delay the tests that were supposed to replace it. Nobody has covered the CR comments' contradiction. The SSF-geometry angle (your rollover star is now math homework, not a maneuver) is visceral and shareable. Proceed.

## Risks / what this does not prove

- This is a proposal/RFC, not a final rule — NHTSA can modify it. Say so.
- The Fishhook genuinely may be redundant given FMVSS 126's own stability test; the story is not "NHTSA is wrong to retire it" but "the savings story doesn't add up."
- SAB-OOP testing removal has real child-safety implications worth one line, but the headline case is the rollover test + roadmap delay.
- Rollover still kills ~1 in 3 vehicle occupants even with ESC; the static rating will not change that geometry-driven reality, which is exactly why the delay of active avoidance tests matters.

## Actionable takeaways

- When shopping: a 5-star rollover rating from MY2027 onward is a tape-measure result, not a maneuver result — prioritize vehicles whose ESC has a proven track record and whose geometry (wide track, low center of gravity) you can check yourself; roof strength (IIHS ratings) still matters for the rollover itself.
- Pickups carry the worst rollover share (38% of occupant deaths); SUVs 33%. That geometry follows you off the lot.
- Public comment closed Oct 16, 2026 — the final rule is the next checkpoint.
