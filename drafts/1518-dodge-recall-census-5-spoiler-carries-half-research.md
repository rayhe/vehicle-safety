## Story in one line
Dodge filed five NHTSA recall campaigns in 2026 covering 153,660 campaign-vehicles, and a single August recall over a Durango rear spoiler that does not fit accounts for 74,578 of them, nearly half the year's total.

## Key verified facts
- **The five 2026 campaigns (Dodge-only counts):**
  - **26V262 (FCA 39D), Apr 23, 2026 — 8,528.** 2024-2025 Dodge Charger Daytona EV. Instrument panel display may fail from a software error; hides gear selection, brake, ESC, TPMS and other warnings; five FMVSS noncompliances (108, 126, 135, 138, 208). Supplier: Valeo. 100% defect estimate. Remedy: software update.
  - **26V510 (FCA 84D), Aug 6, 2026 — 40,386.** 2023-2025 Dodge Hornet. Rear outboard seat belts may twist in the retractor and fail to retract. Supplier: Autoliv ASP. FCA estimates 1.6% of the population carries the defect. 484 warranty claims logged by Jul 30, 2026. Remedy: replace retractors.
  - **26V528 (FCA 61D), Aug 13, 2026 — 74,578.** 2021-2023 Dodge Durango. Rear spoiler over-flush to the roof, contact damages it, may detach at speed and become road debris. Supplier: Nascote Industries. Third recall for the same defect (21V842 Oct 2021, 4,837; 23V115 Feb 2023, 139,019). FCA estimates 0.8% carry the defect (about 597 vehicles). Remedy was under development at filing; interim letters Sep 10, 2026; stop sale on dealer inventory.
  - **26V531 (FCA 78D), Aug 25, 2026 — 16,838.** 2026-2027 Dodge Charger. Radio software (Harman Becker Uconnect5) may prevent the rearview camera image from displaying; FMVSS 111 noncompliance. FCA estimates 100% of the population carries the defect. Remedy: radio software update.
  - **26V701 (FCA A2D), Oct 22, 2026 — 13,330.** 2026 Dodge Durango. Terminals in the tail light wiring harness may be damaged; tail lights may fail; FMVSS 108 noncompliance. Suspect production Jan 5 - Feb 20, 2026. Supplier: Ventra Greenwich. Remedy: inspect and replace tail lamp assemblies. Owner letters Dec 3, 2026.
- **Tally:** 8,528 + 40,386 + 74,578 + 16,838 + 13,330 = **153,660 campaign-vehicles**. This counts a vehicle twice if it is in two campaigns; it is not 153,660 separate Dodges. (In practice the five campaigns cover different model years, so overlap is minimal: no single Dodge sits in two 2026 campaigns.)
- **Shares:** spoiler 74,578 / 153,660 = 48.5%; Hornet belts 40,386 = 26.3%; Charger camera 16,838 = 11.0%; Durango tail lights 13,330 = 8.7%; Charger Daytona cluster 8,528 = 5.5%.
- **Cross-brand splits (the Dodge share of multi-brand campaigns):** 26V262: Dodge 8,528 of 20,271 (42.1%, the rest Wagoneer S). 26V510: Dodge 40,386 of 48,777 (82.8%, the rest Tonale). 26V531: Dodge 16,838 of 844,027 (2.0%, the rest Jeep/Ram/Chrysler). The headline totals quoted in press are multi-brand; the Dodge-only numbers are this article's tally.
- **The defect-rate contrast:** two campaigns estimate 100% of vehicles carry the defect (26V262 cluster software, 26V531 radio software — every car has the bad code). Two estimate under 2% (26V528 spoiler 0.8%, about 597 of 74,578; 26V510 belts 1.6%, about 646 of 40,386 Hornets). Same brand, same year, two opposite recall shapes: recall everyone because the code is in every car, or recall 74,578 to find 597.
- **The Charger went blind twice.** The Charger nameplate appears in two 2026 campaigns, both screens: the instrument cluster goes dark (26V262, April, Valeo software) and the rearview camera goes dark (26V531, August, Harman Becker software). Different suppliers, different screens, same failure mode: the display that is supposed to tell the driver what is happening stops telling them.
- **The Durango appears twice too, across model years.** 2021-2023 Durangos for the spoiler, 2026 Durangos for the tail lights. And the spoiler is a third recall: 21V842 + 23V115 + 26V528 = 218,434 recall notices for one body panel across five years (per #1401's arithmetic).
- **Dodge's whole lineup is three nameplates.** Durango, Hornet, Charger. All three appear in the 2026 census. There is no Dodge model year 2026 that escaped: the Hornet is in 26V510, the Charger in 26V531 (and the Daytona in 26V262), the Durango in 26V528 and 26V701.

## Original contribution (Mia's math and synthesis)
1. **The census tally with Dodge-only splits.** 153,660 campaign-vehicles, with each multi-brand campaign split to its Dodge share from Part 573 filings. No prior article has summed Dodge's 2026 campaigns or published the Dodge-only numbers for 26V262/26V510/26V531.
2. **The share breakdown.** One spoiler recall is nearly half the year at 48.5%, the largest single campaign, and bigger than the Hornet and Charger camera campaigns combined (57,224).
3. **The 100%-vs-0.8% contrast.** Software recalls are total: every car has the bad code, so the population is the defect. Hardware recalls are needles: 0.8% and 1.6% defect rates mean roughly 1,243 actually-defective vehicles hide inside 114,964 recalled ones. The recall system treats both shapes identically (same letters, same free fix), which is why the raw tally misleads in both directions.
4. **The Charger going blind twice.** Two screens, two suppliers, two campaigns, four months apart. The instrument cluster and the rearview camera are the two displays a driver relies on most, and Dodge recalled the Charger for both of them failing in the same calendar year.
5. **The third-recall correction, again.** One of the five "2026 recalls" is not a new defect at all: 26V528 is the third recall for a spoiler defect first recalled in 2021. The truly-new-2026 count is four campaigns, not five, and the year's biggest number is a five-year-old problem.

## Limitations
- Campaign populations overlap in principle but barely in practice: the five campaigns cover different model years (2021-2023 Durango vs 2026 Durango; 2024-2025 Charger Daytona vs 2026-2027 Charger), so double-counting is minimal. The article must still say the tally counts campaign-populations, not distinct vehicles.
- The 100% defect estimates for 26V262 and 26V531 are FCA's filed estimates (26V531's is explicit in the Part 573; 26V262's 100% comes from Carscoops' reading of the filing). They mean every vehicle has the suspect software, not that every screen has already failed.
- The 0.8% (26V528) and 1.6% (26V510) estimates are FCA's own numbers in its filings, unverified by NHTSA. The 484 warranty claims for 26V510 are claims, not confirmed defect occurrences.
- 26V701's defect-rate estimate was not published in the sources consulted; do not invent one.
- The NHTSA recalls API returns HTTP 400 for 2024-2025 Charger and 2024-2025 Durango queries; 26V262 was verified from the NHTSA acknowledgement letter PDF instead. If the API later returns those years, re-check for any additional 26V campaign (none appeared in press or in the 2026 Charger/Durango queries that did return).
- No FARS model-level data exists for 2026 Dodges; this is a recall census, not a fatality-rate story. Do not import Durango/Charger fatality rates as if they applied to these vehicles.
- The July 21, 2026 iSeeCars entry listing a rearview-camera recall for the Dodge Journey could not be verified against the NHTSA API (Journey 2019/2020 returned zero 26V campaigns) or any Part 573 filing, and is excluded from the tally. If a Journey camera campaign exists under another number, the tally is short by that population.

## Strongest counterargument
Five recalls is a normal year for a major manufacturer, and the actual danger per vehicle is small: two of the five are software updates with no parts to replace, the spoiler defect affects about 597 of 74,578 trucks, the seat-belt defect about 646 of 40,386 Hornets, and no deaths or injuries are tied to any of the five in the filings. Recalls are the safety system working, not failing: FCA found these, filed them, and pays for the repairs. The article must state this at full strength: the tally is big, the per-vehicle danger is small, and every fix is free at the dealer.

## Actionable guidance (required)
- If you own a 2021-2023 Durango: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V528 (FCA 61D). If your spoiler was already "fixed" under the 2021 or 2023 recall, you are in this one too. Listen for the spoiler scraping the roof when the liftgate cycles; that is the warning sign.
- If you own a 2026 Durango: check for 26V701 (FCA A2D) now, do not wait for the December 3 letter. You cannot see your own tail lights fail from the driver's seat; have someone stand behind the car while you hit the brakes, or check the VIN.
- If you own a 2024-2025 Charger Daytona: the cluster software update (26V262, FCA 39D) is free and quick. If your dash has ever gone blank, even once, get it done.
- If you own a 2026-2027 Charger: the rearview camera fix (26V531, FCA 78D) is a radio software update. Until it is done, treat the camera as advisory and turn your head.
- If you own a 2023-2025 Hornet: check for 26V510 (FCA 84D). Pull each rear belt fully out and let it retract; if it twists or sticks, the retractor is the defect.
- Buying a used Dodge: confirm all open recalls were completed. Ask for the service record, do not take the seller's word. The Durango spoiler recall has already been "fixed" twice before.
