Toyota Recalled 4,118 Replacement Airbags Built by the Company That Swallowed Takata: Finding the Trucks Is Somebody Else's Problem.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 3, 2026

Somebody already crashed these trucks once, and that is the whole point. Toyota just recalled 4,118 curtain airbags that were never installed at the factory: service replacement parts, fitted during post-crash repairs to 2016–2023 Tacomas and 2014–2021 Tundras.[1][2] Your airbag did its job in crash number one. The replacement might not do its job in crash number two.

4,118
Replacement curtain airbags recalled; Toyota estimates 1% are defective, about 41 airbags

Two recalls, two defects, one supplier. Campaign 26E050 covers 2,381 Tacoma assemblies whose curtain airbag may never have been properly secured to its mounting bracket. Campaign 26E051 covers 1,737 Tundra assemblies that may have been rolled wrong at the factory, so the curtain snags instead of unfurling along the roofline in the milliseconds after the charge fires.[1][2] Both came from Joyson Safety Systems' plant in Torreón, Mexico, and both were caught by lot acceptance tests in late 2025, followed by months of inspections in which the supplier found, in Toyota's delicate phrasing, "a high percent" of bad ones.[1][2]

Now the lineage. Joyson Safety Systems is the company created in April 2018 when Key Safety Systems, a subsidiary of China's Ningbo Joyson Electronic, bought substantially all of Takata's global assets out of bankruptcy.[3] Takata, for the record: 50 million inflators, 37 million vehicles, at least 15 Americans dead.[6] Fairness requires the distinction: Takata killed people with ammonium nitrate chemistry that turned inflators into grenades, while this is a folding error and a bracket error, the kind of thing that happens when humans assemble fabric origami at industrial speed. Different mistake, different decade, and still the optics are terrible.

Here is the part that kept me up. Both filings say Toyota will notify "all owners who are known to have had a curtain shield airbag replacement part installed."[2] Known to whom? Dealers must audit their records and trace parts they sold to independent body shops, then figure out which vehicles got them.[4] Picture it: a 2019 Tacoma gets T-boned in 2024, the curtain deploys, a body shop orders the replacement from a dealer parts counter, the truck is fixed and sold to a second owner in 2025. Somewhere in that chain is a paper record connecting a part number to a VIN. Somewhere. These are equipment recalls, not vehicle recalls, so they may never appear in a VIN lookup. The letters go out September 13 through 27.[4] If the paper chain breaks between the parts counter and your driveway, nobody is coming to find you.

So find yourself: if you own a 2016–2023 Tacoma or a 2014–2021 Tundra Double Cab or CrewMax whose curtain airbags were replaced after a crash (after May 2024 for Tacoma, after November 2021 for Tundra), get it inspected whether or not a letter arrives.[4] Skip the VIN lookup as your only check and call Toyota at 1-800-331-4331 with your repair history. Buying used? Demand the invoice and check it against the suspect part numbers in the federal filings.[1][2] The only acceptable number of bad airbags above your head is zero, and the only person guaranteed to check is you.

Credit where due, because the system worked this time. Testing caught both defects before a single field report or injury anywhere in the country, and Toyota voluntarily recalled after months of analysis.[1][2] Factory airbags are untouched. The "high percent" of bad recovered parts sounds worse than it is, since those parts were pulled from the suspect production range, an enriched sample. What the file cannot tell you is how many of the 4,118 units sit on warehouse shelves (harmless) versus already sewn into headliners (the actual risk). Nobody has that number, which is exactly why the notification list says "known to have had," and exactly why you should not wait to become known.
