# Research — #1382: Three RV Makers, One Power-Seat Fuse, One Fire Risk

**Slug:** `1382-rv-power-seat-fuse-triple-recall`
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — car-buying guidance, "what this means for you")
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date researched:** November 3, 2026

## Story in one line
Three unrelated RV manufacturers recalled trailers in the same October/November window for the identical defect, word for word: a power-seat cord fuse that may not provide adequate circuit protection, so a short circuit raises the fire risk. Combined: 4,463 trailers.

## Kill test: PASS
Proposed angle: nobody connected the three filings. Each recall reads like a small, boring electrical fix. Added together they describe a component class, power seating in towable RVs, whose circuit protection was specified wrong (or not specified at all) across three separate supply chains at the same time. The site has never covered Heartland, Ember, or Alliance power-seat cords: filename checks across drafts/ and stories/ return zero matches for heartland and alliance, and the ember matches are all "september" false positives. Prior coverage of nearby topics (RV September ESC/propane recalls #1039, Lucid eFuse #802, Kalmar fuse #1114) covers different components and different failure modes, not this one.

Challenge: is this surprising after 1,381 articles? Yes, three ways. (1) The identical defect title across three independent manufacturers is itself the finding; recalls usually fail in company-specific ways, and here the language is copy-paste identical. (2) The scale inversion: the biggest of the three (Alliance, 3,686) dwarfs the other two combined, yet all three carry the same remedy and the same one-sentence consequence. (3) The calendar: owner letters stagger Nov 23, Nov 30, and Dec 7, so owners of the same defect get warned weeks apart depending on which brand's trailer they sleep in.

## Facts (verified, primary/credible sources)

### Campaign 1: Alliance RV — 26V680
- 3,686 units: certain 2025-2027 Valor and 2026-2027 Delta recreational trailers equipped with certain power seating assemblies.
- Defect: "The power cord fuse may not provide adequate circuit protection."
- Consequence (NHTSA wording via RV trade press): in the event of a short circuit, inadequate protection increases the risk of a fire.
- Remedy: dealers replace the power cord, free of charge.
- Owner letters expected Dec 7, 2026. Alliance customer service: 1-574-218-7165.
- Source: NHTSA recall record as republished by RVBusiness and Camper Report October 2026 RV recall roundups.

### Campaign 2: Heartland — 26V710
- 518 units: certain 2024 Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, 2022-2023 Gravity, and Road Warrior fifth wheels equipped with certain power seating assemblies.
- Defect: identical wording, "The power cord fuse may not provide adequate circuit protection."
- Consequence: in the event of a short circuit, inadequate protection increases the fire risk.
- Remedy: dealers replace the harness and power cord, free of charge.
- Owner letters expected Nov 30, 2026. Heartland customer service: 1-877-262-8032. Heartland number 990182.
- Source: NHTSA recall record as republished by RV News, Nov 2, 2026.

### Campaign 3: Ember — 26V714
- 259 units: certain 2024 E-Series travel trailers equipped with certain power seating assemblies.
- Defect: identical wording.
- Remedy: dealers replace the power cord, free of charge.
- Owner letters expected Nov 23, 2026. Ember customer service: 1-844-732-4204.
- Manufacturer report date Oct 28, 2026.
- Sources: NHTSA acknowledgment letter RCAK-26V714-5979 (Oct 30, 2026); RV News, Nov 2, 2026.

### Arithmetic (original contribution)
- Total: 3,686 + 518 + 259 = 4,463 trailers.
- Alliance share: 3,686 / 4,463 = 82.6%. Heartland: 11.6%. Ember: 5.8%.
- Letter stagger: Ember Nov 23, Heartland Nov 30 (7 days later), Alliance Dec 7 (14 days after Ember). The largest population gets warned last.
- All three remedies are cord/harness replacement, not a fuse-rating change alone, which means the fix is a parts swap at the dealer, not an owner-serviceable fuse pull.

## What this analysis does NOT prove (limitations)
- The public summaries do not name a shared seat or cord supplier across the three manufacturers, so this article does NOT claim one common supplier. The shared fact is the identical component description and defect wording, not a proven shared vendor. Heartland (Thor Industries) and Alliance and Ember are independent companies as far as the filings show; any supplier link would need the full Part 573 supplier sections, which the summaries reviewed do not publish.
- No crash, fire, injury, or complaint counts are published in the summaries reviewed. "No fires cited" is not "no fires happened."
- "Certain power seating assemblies" is the filings' own qualifier: not every Valor, Delta, Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, Road Warrior, or E-Series trailer is affected. Only a VIN check settles an individual trailer.
- Model-year spans differ (Alliance 2025-2027, Heartland 2022-2024, Ember 2024), so this is not one production run gone wrong; it is the same component description failing the same way across different years.

## Strongest counterargument
4,463 trailers is a rounding error against the millions of RVs on the road, the remedy is a free cord swap, and three small recalls with similar wording may be nothing more than three companies buying similar commodity seat hardware and writing similar defect descriptions. It is also possible the fuse rating was marginal rather than absent, meaning most cords would never see a short circuit in the first place. All fair. It still matters because the failure mode is fire inside a sleeping space, the protection device is the only thing standing between a short and that fire, and the largest group of owners waits until December 7 to be told.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- If you own a 2025-2027 Alliance Valor or 2026-2027 Delta, a 2024 Heartland Torque/Bighorn/Cyclone, a 2022-2023 Heartland Gravity or Road Warrior, or a 2024 Ember E-Series: check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, do not wait for the letter, and call the brand's customer service number above.
- Until the cord is replaced, treat the power seat like a suspect appliance: do not run it unattended, and if the seat stops moving, smells hot, or trips anything, leave it unplugged and call the dealer.
- When shopping for a used trailer in these lines, ask whether the power-seat cord recall was performed; a free remedy that was never claimed is still an open fire risk.
