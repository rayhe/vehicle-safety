Your Dashboard Forgot How to Warn You. 202,000 Jeeps Recalled, Letters Out Today.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 29, 2026

Before you toss that envelope from FCA in the recycling, open it. Starting today, 201,976 Jeep owners are getting letters about a recall with a special insult built in: the part that broke is the part whose entire job is telling you when other parts break.[1] A software bug in the radio-frequency hub can make the tire-pressure monitoring system lose its reference data, so it may never warn you about a low tire, and it may never light the warning lamp either. The dashboard goes quiet, which looks exactly like healthy tires.

530,357
Jeeps recalled in September 2026 across two unrelated defects (328,381 + 201,976)

The recall is FCA 54D, NHTSA campaign 26V559. It covers the 2024 through 2026 Grand Cherokee, Grand Cherokee 4xe, and Grand Cherokee L, the 2024 through 2026 Grand Wagoneer line, the Wagoneer, and the brand-new 2026 Cherokee.[1] Because a warning system that does not warn violates the federal standard requiring the warning to exist, this is a straight FMVSS 138 noncompliance. The fix is a dealer software reflash, free of charge, and VINs have been searchable since September 9.[5] Letters run September 29 through October 21. You do not need to wait for yours.

Tire-pressure monitoring did not become mandatory because regulators love dashboard icons. In the late 1990s, the Firestone and Ford tire controversy was linked to more than 100 deaths from rollovers after tread separations, and Congress answered with the 2000 TREAD Act, which ordered TPMS into every light vehicle sold after September 1, 2007.[3] The US Department of Transportation estimates under-inflated tires cause 40,000 crashes, 33,000 injuries, and more than 650 deaths a year, and a healthy tire bleeds 3 to 9 psi a year with no puncture at all.[3] So the system that exists because 100 people died, built to warn about the thing that kills 650 people a year, is now being recalled for failing to warn, and reading that sentence again does not improve it.

September has been a busy month for the Jeep service bay, which brings me to the math. Earlier this month FCA recalled 328,381 Grand Cherokees because the rear coil springs can detach while driving, a defect serious enough to supersede two earlier recalls.[2] Add the 201,976 from this TPMS recall: 328,381 + 201,976 = 530,357 Jeeps recalled in a single month for two completely unrelated defects, across different model years with no overlap, landing in the same mailbox. One is mechanical, a spring physically falling off the car. The other is digital, a warning forgetting to warn. Jeep will fix both for free, which is the least a company can do after recalling half a million of its SUVs in thirty days.

Now the part where I argue with myself, because the honest version matters. This is the least frightening recall on the board. Nobody has reported a crash or an injury. It is a software reflash, not a tow truck. TPMS was always a backstop, not a primary control: it only complains at 25 percent under-inflation, which is already well into the danger zone, and a five-dollar gauge plus a monthly habit makes the whole system redundant. You could fairly say the system is working exactly as designed. NHTSA found a noncompliance, FCA is fixing it free, letters go out on schedule. A warning light with a software bug is not a wheel falling off. I am not pretending otherwise.

What I cannot tell you is whether any of these 201,976 Jeeps will ever actually go silent, because the recall covers vehicles that may carry the bad software, not vehicles proven to. FARS does not record TPMS status, so nobody can measure the real-world harm of this specific defect from public data. And our own reporting found that about half of recalled cars never get fixed at all, which means a meaningful share of these Jeeps will drive around with a mute dashboard for years.[4]

What you can do today, in order. One: if you own a 2024 through 2026 Grand Cherokee, a Wagoneer or Grand Wagoneer, or a 2026 Cherokee, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now and look for 54D. Two: call 1-800-853-1403 and book the free reflash. Three: buy the five-dollar gauge anyway, because the dashboard only yells at 25 percent under and your door-placard number checked monthly beats any warning lamp. Four: if your cluster shows dashes where tire pressures should be, that is the defect introducing itself. Do not wait for the letter.
