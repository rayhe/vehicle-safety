Rivian Recalled Its Turn Signals Last Year. Now It's Recalling the Recall.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 10, 2026

Somewhere in a supplier warehouse, there was a bin labeled "do not use." The bin got used. Now 3,829 more Rivian owners are getting recall letters about blinkers that may not blink, fourteen months after Rivian already recalled the same blinkers once. Campaign 26V-508, filed August 5, is an expansion of last June's campaign 25V-387, and its official cause of death is this: the supplier failed to quarantine the bad parts.[1]

31,711
R1S and R1T vehicles now covered across both turn-signal recalls

Quarantine is one of those words that got very popular a few years back, and apparently Myotek, Rivian's turn-signal lamp supplier, treated it as aspirational. Manufacturing defects can trigger internal failures in the lamps, so the supplier was supposed to pull the suspect units out of circulation. Instead, incorrectly manufactured lamps kept rolling into vehicles built between June 2024 and May 2026. Rivian kept monitoring after the original recall, logged 17 turn-signal failures between June 2025 and July 2026, and concluded the containment had not contained.[2]

This makes the 2025 R1S the proud holder of ten recalls. Ten. For context, your blinker is the simplest promise a car makes. It says "I am about to turn." When the promise breaks, other drivers can't read your intent, NHTSA calls it a crash risk, and the car itself rats you out with a cluster message: Turn signal lights not working. Service it soon. Whatever signals survive start blinking at double speed, like a metronome having a panic attack.[3]

0.6%
Rivian's estimate of expansion-recall vehicles actually carrying the defect

In the company's defense, Rivian estimates only about 0.6% of the 3,829 newly recalled vehicles actually have the defective lamps. No crashes, injuries, or fatalities have been reported. The fix is free, takes under an hour, and can be done by mobile service without a service-center visit. All of this is admirably civilized. It is also the story of a safety system whose backup system was another safety system, and the backup needed a backup. Quarantines, it turns out, require quarantine for the quarantine.[4]

The notification letters went out by September 29, so if you drive a 2025 or 2026 R1S or R1T and never got one, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or Rivian's recall lookup. Watch for the cluster warning and the double-speed blink; those are your car confessing. And remember the deeper lesson, which costs nothing: when somebody tells you a defective part has been "contained," ask to see the bin.

What this article did not prove. The 0.6% defect estimate is Rivian's own calculation, not an independent measurement; the actual failure count in the expansion population is unknown until inspections happen. The 17 field failures occurred across the entire monitored fleet after 25V-387, not exclusively within the 3,829 newly recalled vehicles. Consumer Reports predicted-reliability rankings reflect predicted, not measured, reliability. No crashes or injuries have been tied to this defect, so this is a compliance and process failure story, not a body-count story. Stated plainly.
