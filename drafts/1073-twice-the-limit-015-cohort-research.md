## Angle (1-2 sentences)

NHTSA's fresh June 2026 Traffic Safety Facts (FARS 2024 ARF) shows 68% of alcohol-impaired crash deaths — 8,097 of 11,904 — involved a driver at BAC 0.15 or higher, nearly double the legal limit. The "drunk driving problem" is not a population of casual drinkers making one bad call; it is an extreme-BAC tail: repeat offenders, unbelted, driving at night, overwhelmingly young men — a cohort that every PSA and checkpoint aimed at "responsible drinkers" misses completely.

## Facts (all from NHTSA DOT HS 813 815, "Alcohol-Impaired Driving: 2024 Data," June 2026, FARS 2023 Final / 2024 ARF, unless noted)

1. 11,904 alcohol-impaired-driving fatalities in 2024 = 30% of all US traffic fatalities. Down 3.9% (12,382 → 11,904) from 2023. Overall traffic fatalities: 41,025 (2023) → 39,254 (2024), down 4.3%. National alcohol fatality rate: 0.36 per 100M VMT (down from 0.38).
2. **68% of the 11,904 — 8,097 deaths — involved a driver at BAC 0.15+ g/dL.** (Fact sheet Key Findings, p.2.)
3. 5,387 passenger-vehicle drivers killed who were alcohol-impaired. Of those with known restraint use: **64% unrestrained overall; 65% at BAC 0.15+; 57% at BAC 0.01–0.07; 41% sober (0.00).**
4. Age: 21–24 highest share of alcohol-impaired drivers in fatal crashes (28%); 25–34 at 27%.
5. Sex: male 22% vs female 16% of drivers impaired in fatal crashes; 4:1 male-to-female ratio among impaired drivers (8,889 vs 2,209).
6. Time: impairment rate **3x higher at night** than during the day.
7. Driving record: alcohol-impaired drivers in fatal crashes **3x more likely to have prior DWI convictions** (6% vs 2% for sober drivers).
8. Role split: 61% of alcohol deaths are the impaired drivers themselves (7,247); 12% their passengers (1,483); 16% occupants of other vehicles (1,858); 11% nonoccupants (1,316).
9. Vehicle type: 25% of motorcycle riders and 25% of passenger-car drivers in fatal crashes were impaired; light trucks 19%; large trucks 4%.
10. NHTSA disclaimer (p.1, verbatim): "use of the term does not indicate that a crash or a fatality was caused by alcohol impairment, only that an alcohol-impaired driver was involved in the crash."
11. BAC values are **imputed when unknown** (Rubin/Schafer/Subramanian multiple imputation, DOT HS 808 816). Per the 2023 state estimates fact sheet: only 36% of drivers in fatal crashes had known BAC results (59% of killed drivers, 19% of surviving drivers).

## Original calculations (show math in article)

- 8,097 ÷ 39,254 = **20.6%** — more than one in five US traffic deaths involves a driver at nearly twice the legal limit.
- 11,904 − 8,097 = **3,807 (32%)** — the 0.08–0.14 band, the population the entire .05-limit debate and most PSAs address, is the minority of alcohol deaths.
- Restraint gradient: 65% (0.15+) − 41% (sober) = 24-point gap; relative 1.59x. The same drivers who ignore the .08 line ignore the belt line.
- Counterfactual ceiling: 8,097 lives = the max annual saving from a perfect 0.15+ intervention (interlock-for-all-offenders class), vs. #1000's 837/year convicted-only capture → current approach captures at most ~10% of the ceiling.
- Repeat-offender concentration: 6% vs 2% prior DWI = 3x. On 11,367 impaired drivers in fatal crashes (2024), ~682 had a prior DWI on record — and FARS only captures *convictions*, not arrests or uncaught episodes.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)

The 68% is partly a measurement construct. NHTSA imputes BACs when test results are unknown — and for surviving drivers, only 19% are tested. Heavy drinkers are more likely to be tested (suspicion, severity), so imputation and testing bias both skew the observed distribution upward. Chronic heavy drinkers also develop tolerance: a 0.15 alcoholic can be less functionally impaired than a novice at 0.08, so the tail may overstate *impairment* even where it correctly states *concentration*. And the 32% in the 0.08–0.14 band is still 3,807 dead people — "the tail is the problem" is not "the rest is fine." The .05-limit debate and general deterrence may well shift the whole distribution downward, tail included.

## Limitations (honest accounting)

- **2024 data is ARF (Annual Report File), not final** — FARS final revisions can move numbers; 2023 figures used are final-file.
- **BAC imputation**: unknown BACs are statistically estimated, not measured; the 8,097 is a modeled estimate with confidence intervals the fact sheet doesn't publish.
- **"Involved" ≠ "caused"**: NHTSA's own disclaimer — a 0.15+ driver rear-ended at a stoplight counts in the 68%.
- FARS excludes crashes on private property (parking lots, driveways) — some impaired fatalities are invisible here.
- FARS captures *convictions* for prior DWI, not arrests; the 6% repeat-offender rate is a floor.
- Can't separate chronic alcoholics from episodic binge drinkers in FARS — the policy prescription differs (treatment vs. deterrence) but the data doesn't split them.

## Actionable takeaways

- **The belt is the intervention that survives intoxication.** The 0.15+ cohort is 65% unbelted. Buckle up sober and leave the reminders on — auto-belt chimes exist because the highest-risk population disables judgment first. If you're riding with someone who's been drinking heavily, your seatbelt is the one countermeasure that works while theirs doesn't.
- **Night is the multiplier.** Impairment rates triple at night. After midnight, assume the other driver profile skews 0.15+ and drive accordingly: bigger following distances, no late-night left turns across traffic if you can avoid the route.
- **For the policy-minded:** the .05-limit debate targets the 32% band; the 68% tail doesn't respond to marginal deterrence — it responds to interlocks, license restriction, and detection tech. All-offender interlock laws (now in most states in some form) are the only deployed tool aimed at the actual cohort. Check whether your state requires interlocks for first offenders — many still don't.
- **Repeat offenders are the addressable population.** 3x prior-DWI concentration means the registry exists; enforcement is the gap.
