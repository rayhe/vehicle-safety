# Research — #1185: In crashes involving teen drivers, the teen driver usually isn't the one who dies

**Journalist:** Clara Rollover | **Kicker:** Body Count
**Slug:** `1185-teen-drivers-passengers-die-more`
**Headline:** "Your Teen Isn't the One Who Dies in the Crash. Their Friends Are."

## What happened / the data

**National Teen Driver Safety Week runs Oct 18-24, 2026** (20th annual observance; announcements Oct 15 from National Road Safety Foundation and National Safety Council — AutoConnectedCar, Oct 15, 2026).

**NHTSA Crash•Stats: Young Drivers 2023 Data** (DOT HS 813 736, July 2025; "young drivers" = 15-20 operating motor vehicles):
- 2,148 young drivers died in traffic crashes in 2023, +5% from 2,037 in 2022.
- 5,588 traffic fatalities in crashes *involving* young drivers in 2023, +4.2% from 5,361 in 2022.
- Young drivers = **5.1% of all licensed drivers, but 8.9% of all drivers in fatal crashes** in 2023. Overrepresented by 1.74x.
- 5,133 young drivers involved in fatal traffic crashes in 2023, +5% from 4,876 in 2022.
- Young female fatal-crash involvement rate per 100,000 licensed drivers: 22.47 in 2023 (male rate higher; cutoff in scrape).

**NRSF + NSC announcements, Oct 15, 2026** (AutoConnectedCar):
- NRSF (citing NHTSA): **2,162 teen drivers died in 2024**; ~184,129 teens injured in traffic crashes in 2024.
- NSC (ages 15-20, all parties): **5,247 deaths in crashes involving young drivers in 2024**, -6.5% from the year before. Includes passengers and other road users, not just the young drivers.
- NHTSA 2023 teen-driver (15-18) breakdown: **2,611 people died in crashes involving a teen driver. Only 822 were the teen drivers.** Another 604 were passengers in the teen's vehicle; **377 of those passengers (62%) were teens themselves**. 87,074 teen drivers injured.

**IIHS fatality facts (teenagers):** drivers ages 16-19 are **nearly 3 times more likely to be involved in a fatal crash than drivers 20+**, per mile driven (cited via NHTSA/Mercury Insurance release, Feb 2026). Crash risk is highest in the first year of independent driving.

**GDL effectiveness (Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, NHTSA-supported; via ScienceDaily):** graduated driver licensing reduces fatal crashes of 16-year-old drivers by an average of 11%; the most comprehensive programs (5+ of 7 components: min permit age 15.5, 3+ month waiting period, 30+ supervised hours, night/passenger restrictions, etc.) cut them ~20%.

## Kill test (novelty vs 1,180+ prior articles)

PASS. The pipeline has never covered teen drivers (only tangentially via impairment). The standard "teens are risky" framing is well-trodden nationally — but **the inversion is the story**: in crashes with a teen behind the wheel, the dead person is usually *not the driver*. 822 of 2,611 (2023) were the teen driver — that's 31%. The other 69% are passengers and strangers. The actionable lever isn't scare tactics aimed at the teen; it's the **passenger restriction** (the GDL component that directly targets who dies in the back seat: 377 teen passengers dead in 2023) and **parental supervised hours**, the two interventions the federal data actually supports. The article's actionable section will frame the three evidence-backed rules: 50+ supervised hours before solo driving, no teen passengers for the first year, no night driving — plus the parent-as-model finding (teens mimic parental speeding/phone use; the adults' habits are the risk input).

## Limitations

- NSC (15-20, all parties, 2024) and NHTSA (15-18 breakdown, 2023) use different age bands and years; figures are not directly interchangeable — article states each with its own band/year.
- NHTSA 2023 breakdown numbers (2,611/822/604/377) come via AutoConnectedCar's summary of NHTSA data, not the primary DOT HS report retrieved directly; cite the chain explicitly.
- The 2,162 teen-driver deaths (2024, NRSF via NHTSA) vs 2,148 (2023, Crash•Stats) are different age bands ("teen drivers" vs "young drivers 15-20"); do not present as a time series.
- No FARS model-level fatality-rate tie-in for teen-driven vehicles; the story is demographic/behavioral, not vehicle-design.

## Actionable insights (hard gate)

- **No teen passengers for the first year of solo driving.** 377 teen passengers died in 2023 in crashes where a teen was driving. Passenger restrictions are the GDL component with the clearest death toll attached.
- **50+ hours of supervised driving before solo licensure** — the Hopkins data shows supervised practice is one of the proven reducers; parents are the intervention.
- **No night driving (9pm-5am window) during the first year** — GDL night restrictions target the highest-risk hours.
- **Enroll in your state's GDL program components even if not required** — comprehensive GDL cuts 16-year-old fatal crashes ~20%.
- Parents: teens drive like you drive. Your speeding and phone use are the training data.
