## 1. General Critic — 9.0/10
Structure is classic Rex: hook (the reputation-inverting stat), data dump, the turn ("a second table ruins everything"), the mechanism (fleet age), the spicy secondary finding (Nissan), the steelmanned counterweight (Mercedes), limitations, actionable close. The "So Kia wins... Except there is a second table" pivot is the article's engine and it fires. The kicker ("Youth is no immunity. It is just better odds.") lands. Deduction: the middle ranking paragraph is a dense comma-list data dump; it works as a recitation but asks the reader to hold nine numbers at once. The pull-stat relieves some of this.

## 2. Voice Critic — 9.0/10
Unmistakably Rex: "the federal morgue data," "A 2006 car was born with a CD player," "Honda has been selling Accords since bell-bottoms," "The badge is a birth certificate." Forensic, wry, allergic to press-release language. The "birth certificate" motif recurs three times, which is intentional refrain rather than accident, but a fourth occurrence would tip into tic. Deduction: one phrase ("stated at full strength") is slightly stilted against the surrounding plainspokenness.

## 3. Ethics Critic — 9.0/10
The article actively resists its own most clickable misreading (that Kia is "safe"), which is the honest move. The Nissan claim ("worst mainstream sedan brand in America by a distance") is explicitly framed as a crude age-adjustment inference, not a measured fact. Limitations are unusually thorough: fatal-only data, no demographic controls, death-weighted model year conflating fleet age with per-year risk, survivorship bias framed as an affordability effect. No individual is harmed; the used-car advice (buy newest affordable, check recalls) is sound public guidance. Deduction: the headline's first clause will travel without the second on social, and the piece cannot fully control that; the og:description ("the trophy starts to tarnish") mitigates it.

## 4. Social Critic — 9.0/10
Headline is a self-debunking curiosity gap, ideal for sharing. The 4.6× pull-stat is the shareable number. The Nissan callout is the spicy take that travels in comments. Quotables: "The badge is a birth certificate," "Youth is no immunity. It is just better odds." The old-vs-new sedan hero image reinforces the thesis at a glance. Deduction: no embedded table or chart for the model-year reveal, the single most visualizable moment; the inline list carries it but a visual would travel further.

## 5. Legal Critic — 9.5/10
Nissan "worst... by a distance" is opinion grounded in disclosed data with an explicit qualifier ("adjust even crudely"), the classic protected-opinion structure. All brand fatality figures trace to cited FARS data. Kia claims are favorable. No individuals named. Sources hyperlinked, methodology disclosed, disclaimer present. The nhtsa.gov/recalls link is a neutral government resource. No legal exposure identified.

## 6. Rigor Critic — 9.5/10
The aggregation formula was verified against a published per-model rate (Silverado 9,591 deaths → 1.25 reproduced exactly) before use. Death-weighted mean model year is restricted to 1995–2023 to prevent 1980s survivors from dragging the average. 500+ death inclusion threshold guards against small-sample wagging. The Ram 0.12 exclusion is documented as a denominator artifact, not silently dropped. The limitations section names the exact confound the article cannot separate (fleet age vs. per-year risk) and the survivorship-bias mechanism (cheap old cars, high-risk owners). Deduction: "roughly 2015, when small-overlap structures went mainstream" is approximate (IIHS small overlap began 2012, phased in); "roughly" carries it, but a stickler could ask for the phase-in curve.

## 7. Data Presentation Critic — 8.5/10
All numbers needed to check the argument are present inline: full sedan ranking, SUV ranking, pickup note, model-year means for five brands. The inline comma-list format is scannable and matches house style. The model-year reveal ("Honda's: 2006.4... Kia's: 2015.1, so read that again") is paced for impact. Deduction: the piece's central comparison (rate vs. mean model year by brand) begs for a two-column presentation, and inline prose is the weakest available vehicle for it; acceptable, not ideal. This is the floor score and it passes.
