The Clip Holding Your Toddler In Comes Apart in Small Pieces

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 24, 2026

On November 13, NHTSA opened a federal probe into the magnetic chest clip on 2023 and 2024 Chicco Fit360 car seats. Five reports describe the clip breaking into small pieces, and in at least one, a child put a piece in their mouth while the car was moving.[1] No recall exists, and no defect has been found. But the clip is the thing standing between your toddler and a loose harness, and it is coming apart in parents' hands.

3 years
Between the first documented clip disassembly and the day NHTSA opened its probe

That gap is the story. A parent in Bartlesville, Oklahoma reported the exact same failure on October 2, 2023, when a daughter disassembled the magnetic chest clip while seated in the car and placed a small part in her mouth.[2] Two years later, a November 2025 complaint described a clip that clicked together and came undone by the time the car reached its destination, fifteen months after purchase.[3] By June 2026, two more parents had filed nearly identical reports. Five matching reports is the threshold NHTSA says can open a case, and it took three years to collect them.

And the clip fails in two ways, not one. In the breaking version, it disassembles into small pieces, which is a choking hazard in a moving car. In the other version, from both June 2026 complaints, the magnet pulls the halves together so the clip looks closed while the mechanical latch never engages, then pops open under light pressure, without anyone pressing the release buttons.[4] A broken clip you can see, and a false-latched clip you cannot. Its job is keeping the harness straps at armpit level in a crash, and a clip that opens by itself is a restraint that quietly stops restraining.

Meanwhile Chicco has been handling this one customer at a time. The October 2023 complainant says the company sent a plastic replacement clip. A June 2026 complainant says Chicco sent a replacement and indicated it should fix the issue, without inspecting the seat or the original clip.[4] Chicco also sells the clip on its own parts store for $20, twice the price of the clip on its KeyFit infant seat, and neither the company nor NHTSA says whether the replacement differs from the clip under review.[5] A per-customer fix with no public notice is the cheapest way to handle a defect and the worst way for every other owner to learn about it.

Fairness demands the other side in full: five reports is a small number, and the probe's record states no injury and gives no age for the child in the mouth incident. A Preliminary Evaluation is the earliest stage of a federal investigation, and most never become recalls. A chest clip is a positioning device, not the primary restraint; the buckle and the harness carry the load in a crash. None of that changes the timeline: the October 2023 complaint describes the same disassembly the probe names, three years earlier, and a positioning device that opens on its own has stopped positioning.

So check the clip before the next ride. Squeeze the halves together lightly, then pull them apart without pressing the release buttons. If they separate, or if the clip looks closed but never clicked, stop using the seat and call Chicco at 1-877-424-4226 with the model number and date of manufacture in hand. A damaged harness part means the seat is out of service by Chicco's own guidance.[5] If a clip has broken, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov, because five reports opened this case and yours is how the count grows. Register the seat: a recall notice goes to registered owners within 60 days of a recall decision, and an unregistered seat is off the list.[6]
