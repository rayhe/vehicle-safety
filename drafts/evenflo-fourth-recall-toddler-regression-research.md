# Research: Evenflo Fourth Recall + Toddler Restraint Regression

## News Hook
- **Evenflo Reo by Revolve360 recalled July 2026** — 59,661 units. Headrest may shift in positions 10-11 during crash testing. Fourth recall of the Revolve360 line.
- Existing article covered 3 recalls: `evenflo-revolve360-three-recalls.html`
- This is now the FOURTH recall — new product line "Reo by Revolve360" with the same shifting-headrest issue.

## Key Data Sources

### Source 1: IIHS 2024 Child Fatality Statistics
- **550 children under 13 died in traffic crashes in 2024**
- 167 (30%) were unrestrained
- Restraint rate among fatally injured children: 56% restrained (up from 15% in 1985)

### KEY FINDING: Toddler Regression
- **Age 1-3 unrestrained deaths: 36 → 44 (+22% increase)**
- **Age 1-3 unrestrained rate: 26% → 38% (+12 percentage points)**
- This is DESPITE total toddler deaths dropping (141 → 116)
- Every other age group improved their restraint rates
- Infants (<1) also worsened slightly: 24% → 25%

### Source 2: NSC Injury Facts (2024 data)
- Car seats reduce fatal injury risk by 71% for infants, 54% for toddlers in passenger cars
- 91.2% overall seat belt use rate nationally
- Unrestrained occupants account for 48.4% of ALL deaths

### Source 3: Evenflo Recall Details
- Model: Reo by Revolve360 child seats
- 59,661 units recalled
- Defect: headrest shifts position during crash testing in positions 10-11 (forward-facing) and 11 (rear-facing)
- Can continue using in positions 1-9 (forward) / 1-10 (rear)
- No injuries reported
- 6 model numbers affected (US), 2 (Canada)

## Novel Analysis
The original contribution: **The car seat industry's attention economy is inverted.** Four recalls for a headrest that shifts in the highest adjustment positions — positions most toddlers never reach — while the actual toddler death problem is getting WORSE because of non-use.

44 toddlers died unrestrained in 2024. Zero died because an Evenflo headrest shifted.

The recall targets a theoretical risk at the edges of the product's adjustment range. The actual deaths are happening at the other end: no seat at all.

## Counterargument
The recall process is automated and regulation-driven — manufacturers can't choose to ignore test failures just because misuse kills more kids. NHTSA's recall system catches manufacturing defects; restraint education is a different problem requiring a different solution. Both can and should happen simultaneously. The recall isn't "instead of" education — it's a separate regulatory function.

## Limitations
- 2024 FARS data is the first release; some numbers may be revised
- Year-to-year fluctuations in small samples can be noisy (toddler deaths N=116)
- The Evenflo recall is for a newer product (Reo) not the original Revolve360
- We cannot know from FARS alone whether recalled seats contributed to any restraint failures
- "Unrestrained" in FARS includes incorrectly installed seats — not just empty seats
