# Research: #1391 — GM Recalled the Vans, Then Recalled the Recall

**Slug:** 1391-gm-express-savana-recall-of-recall
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — paradox/investigation beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** November 4, 2026

## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** General Motors just recalled 12,715 Chevrolet Express and GMC Savana vans for a loose steering gear nut. That is the same defect, on the same model years, that GM already recalled 26,541 of these vans for in June 2026. The second recall exists because the first recall's inspections were not done properly: NHTSA's own summary for campaign 26V718 states that vehicles "not properly inspected under recall 26V399 will need to be re-inspected under 26V718." The fix needed a fix. A van owner who dutifully took their van in during the summer, got the inspection, and drove away cleared is now recalled again.

**Challenge:** is this genuinely new after 1,390 articles? Yes, four ways. (1) Zero prior drafts, stories, or queue entries mention 26V718 or GM number N262579350 (full-content grep, no truncation, November 4, 2026). (2) The site covered the June recall (26V399) in two unnumbered research files, but neither covers a second campaign, because 26V718 did not exist until October 29, 2026. (3) The novel contribution is the recall-of-recall mechanism itself plus the arithmetic: 47.9 percent of the June population is being called back for re-inspection, and this is the vans' third steering-related recall of 2026. (4) The news peg is six days old: NHTSA received the report October 29, 2026, and USA Today reported it November 4, 2026.

**Killed in research (duplicates, do not revive):** GM EV brake recall 26V717 (#1387); GM L87 Nov 13 deadline (#1367); IIHS Oct 31 moderate-overlap wave, same six vehicles and ratings as #1244 (RAV4 Acceptable, RAV4 PHEV Marginal, Audi Q3 Poor, Trailblazer Poor, Kicks Good, Jetta Good); Thomas Built 26V702 (#1369); Ford F-150 tailgate cable (#1297); Audi Q4 e-tron 26V698 (#1268, and the #1351 duplicate of it); Genesis G80 seat-switch 26V664 (#1290, #1330); GHSA pedestrian 2025 data (#1374); 2025 traffic deaths early estimate (already published on site); DST fall-back pedestrian piece (#1157).

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)

1. **The second recall (26V718):** NHTSA campaign 26V718000, report received October 29, 2026. GM number N262579350. Population: certain 2025-2026 Chevrolet Express and GMC Savana vehicles. Defect: the steering gear assembly may have a nut that was not properly tightened. Consequence: a loose nut that separates from the steering gear assembly while driving can cause a loss of steering control. Remedy: dealers will inspect the serial number and replace the steering gear assembly if necessary, free of charge. Owner letters expected December 14, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since October 29, 2026. Decisive sentence in the NHTSA summary: "Vehicles that were not properly inspected under recall 26V399 will need to be re-inspected under 26V718." Source: NHTSA recalls API, Chevrolet Express 2026 and GMC Savana 2026 records (both return identical 26V718 text).
2. **The size and split:** 12,715 vehicles total, per USA Today (November 4, 2026), which lists: 2025 Chevrolet Express 5,434; 2026 Chevrolet Express 3,259; 2025 GMC Savana 3,568; 2026 GMC Savana 454. GM estimates only 1 percent of the population carries the defect. As of October 8, 2026, GM was not aware of any injuries tied to this campaign. Contacts: GMC 1-888-988-7267, Chevrolet 1-800-222-1020.
3. **The first recall (26V399):** NHTSA campaign 26V399000, report received June 23, 2026. GM number N262563570. Population: 26,541 model-year 2025-2026 Express and Savana vans. Same defect wording, same consequence wording. Root cause per GM via CarBuzz (June 29, 2026): a malfunctioning sensor at supplier Nexteer Automotive let 51 steering gear assemblies leave the factory with nuts only hand-tight instead of properly torqued. GM knew the 51 serial numbers but not which vans received them, so every van in the population needed a serial-number inspection. GM reported around ten field reports potentially related, none involving a crash or injury. Owner letters mailed July 20, 2026 (per NHTSA API; June coverage said August 10, the API's July 20 mailing date is the later record and is used here).
4. **The third steering recall of 2026 (26V345):** NHTSA campaign 26V345000, report received May 28, 2026. GM number N262558671. Population: 232 model-year 2026 Express and Savana vans. Defect: an incorrectly manufactured steering column assembly (also Nexteer) could give false tactile gear-selection feedback, so a driver could think the van is in Park when it is not. Remedy: replace the transmission control on the steering column. Same vans, same year, same supplier family, third steering defect.
5. **The platform:** Express/Savana have been in continuous production since model year 1996, the same generation for 30 years, making them the oldest single-generation vehicle still sold new in America (per June 2026 coverage). The steering gear in current vans dates to a 2017-or-later design, not the 1996 original.
6. **FARS context (2014-2023, from this site's prior research files):** Chevrolet Express: 475 deaths, rate 0.92 per 100M VMT. GMC Savana: 166 deaths, rate 0.54. These are nameplate-wide figures across all model years, not figures for the recalled 2025-2026 vans, and the article must say so.

## Computations (original to this piece)

- Re-inspection share: 12,715 / 26,541 = **47.9 percent** of the June recall population is called back under 26V718.
- Defect estimate applied: 1 percent of 12,715 = about **127 vans** actually carrying a loose nut, by GM's own estimate. The other ~12,588 are inspection paperwork.
- 2026 steering-recall tally on these vans: 232 (26V345) + 26,541 (26V399) + 12,715 (26V718) = **39,488 recall mentions** across three campaigns in one calendar year, on a platform GM has built for 30 years.
- Timeline: June 23 (26V399 filed) to October 29 (26V718 filed) = **128 days** between the recall and the recall of the recall. June inspections ran roughly July through October; owners who complied early have now been recalled twice for one nut.
- Owner-letter gap for round two: October 29 filing to December 14 letters = **46 days** in which VIN search is the only way to know.

## What this article must NOT claim

- That 26V718 is a new defect. It is the same loose-nut defect as 26V399; what is new is that the first round's inspections were improper.
- That all 12,715 vans have loose nuts. GM's estimate is 1 percent.
- That any crash, injury, or death is tied to either campaign. None is reported in either NHTSA summary.
- How many inspections were improper, or why they were improper (dealer error vs. procedure vs. paperwork). The NHTSA summary does not say, and the article must state that gap rather than filling it.
- That a June "all clear" still stands. That is exactly what 26V718 overturns: a van inspected under 26V399 needs its status re-checked.

## Strongest counterargument

The system arguably worked twice. GM caught a supplier sensor failure, recalled 26,541 vans, then caught that some of those inspections were not done properly and recalled the affected subset again rather than quietly re-inspecting them at the next service visit. No crashes or injuries appear in either file. A serial-number inspection is cheap, the replacement is free, and 99 percent of the round-two population likely has a properly tightened nut. The scandal framing (recall of a recall) is fair, but the safety outcome so far is zero reported harm, and a manufacturer that re-recalls its own recall is disclosing a failure it could have buried.

## Limitations

- The NHTSA API summary does not state the number of improperly inspected vehicles, the cause of the improper inspections, or whether the 12,715 population is exactly the improperly inspected set or a wider precautionary net. The 47.9 percent figure is a population ratio, not a confirmed improper-inspection rate.
- The 1 percent defect estimate is GM's estimate, not a measured rate.
- FARS figures span all model years 2014-2023 and cannot be attributed to the recalled vans.
- Owner-letter dates are estimates ("expected to be mailed December 14, 2026").

## Actionable takeaways for readers

- If you own a 2025-2026 Express or Savana: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, even if your van was already inspected under the June recall. A June clearance does not clear you under 26V718.
- If your van is in 26V718: the dealer inspects the steering gear serial number and replaces the assembly if necessary, free. Letters arrive around December 14, but VINs have been searchable since October 29, so do not wait for the letter.
- If you bought one of these vans used since the summer: the seller may honestly tell you "the recall was done." Ask whether it was done under 26V399 or re-checked under 26V718. Only the second answer is current.
- If your steering ever feels loose or you hear clunking from the front end on one of these vans: stop driving it further than safety requires and call the dealer. A separating steering gear nut means loss of steering, not degraded steering.
