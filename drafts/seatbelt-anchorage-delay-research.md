# Research: Seatbelt Anchorage Delay — Three More Years of Weaker Testing for Heavy Trucks

## Core Angle
NHTSA published an updated seatbelt anchorage standard (FMVSS No. 210) in September 2024. The Truck and Engine Manufacturers Association (EMA) petitioned for reconsideration. NHTSA is now proposing to delay the compliance date from September 1, 2027 to September 1, 2030 for vehicles over 10,000 lbs (4,536 kg) GVWR. That's a 3-year delay for the heaviest trucks on the road.

The delay was published in the Federal Register on June 26, 2026 (NHTSA-2025-0039). Comments due July 27, 2026.

## Key Facts

### What the updated standard does
- Updated test procedures for seatbelt anchorages
- Added an alternative test device as a compliance option
- More realistic testing with updated dummy sizes and force patterns
- Original compliance: September 1, 2027
- Delayed compliance for heavy trucks: September 1, 2030 (proposed)
- Light vehicles (≤10,000 lbs) still Sept 1, 2027

### Who petitioned
- Truck and Engine Manufacturers Association (EMA)
- Represents the major truck/engine OEMs

### FARS data (2014-2023) — pickup truck fatalities
- **Total pickup deaths: 41,593**
- Chevrolet Silverado: 9,591 deaths
- Ford F-150: 9,194 deaths
- Dodge RAM: 4,407 deaths
- GMC Sierra: 3,337 deaths
- Average ~4,159 pickup deaths/year

### Heavy-duty trucks specifically
- Ford F-250: 909 deaths
- Ford F-350: 164 deaths
- Ram 2500: 153 deaths
- Ram 3500: 0 deaths (too few to track)
- Total tracked HD: 1,226 deaths over decade

### IIHS rear-seat data (context)
- Full-size trucks from Ram, Ford, Chevy ALL rated "Poor" in IIHS rear-seat crash test
- IIHS launched updated moderate overlap test after research showed rear seat occupants face HIGHER fatal injury risk than front occupants in newer vehicles
- Seatbelt anchors are especially critical for rear-seat protection — front seats have advanced airbags and pretensioners rarely available in back

### Original Contribution
Calculation: If the updated seatbelt anchorage standard prevents even a 1-2% reduction in pickup fatalities (conservative), a 3-year delay costs lives:
- 4,159 pickup deaths/year × 3 years = 12,477 pickup deaths during delay window
- Even 1% reduction = ~125 preventable deaths delayed
- Heavy-duty trucks over 10K GVWR specifically: smaller numbers, but these are the most dangerous to OTHER road users due to mass

The broader pattern: This is not the first time industry has delayed a safety standard. NHTSA often takes YEARS to issue new regulations after granting rulemaking petitions (per today's Tesla door egress news — NHTSA says "often takes years").
