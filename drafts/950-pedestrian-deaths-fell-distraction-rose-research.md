## Original calculations (novel contribution)
- **The proportional-decline test:** pedestrian share of all traffic deaths — 2023: 7,472 / 41,025 = **18.2%**; 2024: 7,237 / 39,254 = **18.4%**; 2025: 6,732 / 36,640 = **18.4%**. The pedestrian decline is exactly proportional to the overall decline. Pedestrians captured ZERO extra safety benefit; they are riding the same falling tide as everyone else.
- **The headwind arithmetic:** 2024 distracted-driver-involved pedestrian deaths ≈ 7,237 × 21.7% ≈ **1,570**. At the 2019 rate (15.6% of ~6,411 implied 2019 deaths ≈ 1,000). The distraction rate rose 39% (15.6 → 21.7) while total deaths fell — meaning the forces saving lives had to overcome ~570 extra distraction-driven deaths just to break even.
- **Pedestrian distraction nearly doubled:** 15.3% → 29.2% (2019 → 2024). Both drivers AND walkers are looking at screens more.
- **Slower recovery than the total:** overall deaths are back to ~2019 levels (36,640 vs 36,096); pedestrians are still **5% above** 2019. The pedestrian share of deaths is higher now than pre-pandemic.
- **New Mexico natural experiment:** 53 → 27 in H1 (49% drop) — the state that had the worst rate in 2023 — attributed to flashing crosswalk warning lights. Five states drove >2/3 of the 371-death H1 decline. Concentration = cause for optimism (interventions, not luck).

## The paradox / angle
Everyone wants to celebrate the third straight decline. But (a) it's perfectly proportional to the overall decline — pedestrians aren't disproportionately safer, they're just passengers on the same trend; (b) distraction got dramatically WORSE over the same period (driver 15.6→21.7%, pedestrian 15.3→29.2%); (c) 50.4% of deaths are from light trucks and 75%+ after dark — the two design problems (tall front ends, bad headlights) haven't changed. The decline happened DESPITE the inputs getting worse, not because of improvement. That means: enforcement + tech countermeasures (NM flashing lights, pedestrian AEB, stop-and-remain-stopped laws) are fighting a headwind, and winning so far. The good news is real; the complacency would be fatal.

## Limitations (honest)
- GHSA 2025 numbers are preliminary (state highway safety office projections, not FARS final). Will revise when FARS Final 2024 + ARF 2025 publish in early 2027.
- Distraction is underreported in crash data (phones aren't always checked; officers code what's visible). 21.7% is a floor, not a ceiling.
- Light truck share (50.4%) is of deaths, not exposure-adjusted; light trucks are also a large share of the fleet. No fleet-adjusted lethality claim without VMT data.
- State-level concentration (NM etc.) is correlation with interventions, not proven causation — but it's the strongest quasi-experimental signal in the report.

## Strongest counterargument
"Proportional decline is still decline — 740 fewer dead pedestrians is 740 fewer funerals, and the absolute trend is what matters, not the share." Fair. The article should grant this fully: the streak is real and good. The argument is about *why*, and therefore *what to do next*: if the decline were pedestrian-specific (better crosswalks, pedestrian AEB working), we'd invest differently than if it's a general tide (enforcement, speed). Misattributing cause risks defunding the actual drivers of the improvement.

## Actionable insights (gate)
- Shoppers: prioritize IIHS Good pedestrian front crash prevention ratings + Good headlights (75%+ of pedestrian deaths after dark). Headlight ratings apply to every trim on some models — check the rating, not the badge.
- Everyone: check VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls (pedestrian AEB software updates ship via recalls).
- Pedestrians: the 29.2% distraction figure — put the phone down at crossings. Crosswalk tech (NM-style flashing beacons) works; ask your city for it.
- Drivers: "stop and remain stopped" laws (OR/WA model) have the evidence; Hawaii is considering one — support it.
