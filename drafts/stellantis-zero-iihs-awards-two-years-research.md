# Research: Stellantis Zero IIHS Awards — Two Consecutive Years

## Angle
Stellantis is the only major automaker that earned zero IIHS safety awards in 2026 — not a single Top Safety Pick, not a single Top Safety Pick+. For the second consecutive year. Sixty-three vehicles won awards. Zero were Jeep, Dodge, Ram, Chrysler, Alfa Romeo, or Fiat.

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing the 2026 IIHS award data with FARS fatality data reveals Stellantis isn't just failing lab tests — its vehicles also dominate the real-world death rate rankings. Three Stellantis models (Dodge Charger HEMI, Chrysler 300, Dodge Challenger) appear in the IIHS's own 20 deadliest vehicles list. The company that builds the deadliest cars also can't pass the safety tests.

## Counterargument (at full strength)
IIHS awards are a snapshot of lab performance under specific test conditions. A vehicle can fail to win because of headlights or seat belt reminders rather than structural crashworthiness. The 2026 criteria raised the bar — new moderate overlap front test, backseat occupant protection, vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention at 31/37/43 mph — and many non-Stellantis vehicles also failed (Cadillac CT5, Lexus IS, Nissan Kicks, Toyota Tacoma). Stellantis may be investing in next-gen platforms rather than iterating existing ones to meet evolving test criteria. Also, testing continues through September 2026, so awards could still come.

## Limitations
- IIHS tests a subset of all models; some Stellantis vehicles may not have been submitted or tested yet
- The "two consecutive years" claim is based on freep.com reporting, not independently verified against 2025 IIHS data
- IIHS lab performance doesn't perfectly predict real-world outcomes (though in this case, real-world FARS data aligns directionally)
- Several Stellantis models are being discontinued (Charger, Challenger), which reduces the incentive to engineer test improvements
