One Driver Wrote a Letter to the Government. Now the Feds Are Investigating 18,150 BMWs.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 22, 2026

Someone you have never met did more for your safety this month than your dealership ever will. On September 1, one person filed a defect petition with NHTSA about the 2022 BMW 540i, and the federal government opened an investigation covering 18,150 vehicles: one letter, eighteen thousand cars, and that is how this is supposed to work even though almost nobody knows it.

18,150
Vehicles under federal investigation after a single defect petition filed September 1, 2026

Some background, because BMW already knew about this joint. In August, the company recalled 27,720 cars under campaign 26V525: the 2021-2023 5 Series, the 2022-2026 8 Series, and the 2024-2026 750e plug-in hybrid. The problem sits where the driveshaft meets the rear differential, and BMW described it as a connection that "may, over time, be subject to excessive wear." The prescribed remedy is an inspection, followed by a smear of adhesive on the joint or, if the wear has gone too far, a full replacement of the driveshaft and differential. BMW estimates that 0.2 percent of the recalled cars actually have the defect, which works out to roughly 55 cars out of 27,720: an enormous dragnet thrown wide around a defect BMW's own statistics say almost nobody has, and the petition is betting that characterization is wrong.

Fine. Except the petitioner says BMW's framing is wrong, and the distinction is not academic. "Excessive wear" is a phrase with a service appointment attached to it. "Total failure" is what happens when your driveshaft stops being connected to your differential at highway speed: the engine runs, the transmission shifts, and the rear wheels get nothing. Park the same car on a slope and it can roll away. The parking pawl holds the transmission, not the wheels. Snap the link between the driveshaft and the differential and that pawl is holding a transmission that is no longer attached to anything that matters. The petitioner alleges the joint can fail entirely; BMW says it wears, and those are not the same claim. Read that again.

Now the part that should make you furious you did not know this sooner: anyone can do what this petitioner did, because the defect-petition law exists for exactly the moment when one owner sees a pattern the manufacturer minimized and refuses to let it die quietly. Federal law allows any person to file a defect petition with NHTSA, and the agency has to answer it. Not a class action, not a lawyer on a billboard, just a letter. The petitioner did not sue BMW; they asked the government to look, and the government opened the file. Your dealership will never volunteer this information, because it is leverage you hold over every manufacturer, and it costs nothing to use, which is precisely why the people who profit from your ignorance have no incentive to mention it.

There is a number in this story that deserves a second look, because it sits at the exact intersection of the recall BMW filed and the investigation the petitioner forced. The petition singles out the 2022 540i and cites a population of 18,150 vehicles, which matches BMW's own 5-Series recall headcount exactly, a numerical coincidence that either means the petitioner lifted the figure straight from BMW's filing or noticed something the rest of us missed. Coincidence, maybe. But it raises the question this whole investigation is really about: is the petition describing a defect inside the recall population that BMW's "wear" framing underestimates? A nastier failure mode, hiding inside a group the manufacturer already characterized at 0.2 percent, now described by an outsider as total failure. If the petitioner is right, the recall's remedy, adhesive on a joint, is calibrated to the wrong defect.

Counterpoint, stated at full strength, because a filed petition is an allegation with a docket number rather than a verdict, and the distinction between those two things matters more than the outrage. BMW has received zero accident or injury reports tied to this joint. The petitioner could be describing the endpoint of the same progressive wear, which is the recall's own worst case, not a second defect at all. BMW's 0.2 percent figure is the company's own math. The petitioner's evidence is not public. Neither number can be checked independently yet. NHTSA closed a Honda probe this month with no action after a two-year review. This investigation could end the same way, quietly, with the recall unchanged. The file is open, and nothing else is decided.

What you should do does not depend on the verdict. If you own a 2021-2023 540i, 540i xDrive, or M550i xDrive, a 2022-2026 840i or 840i xDrive, or a 2024-2026 750e xDrive: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today. Check it now, not when the letter arrives. Owner letters are scheduled to mail on October 2, which leaves an eight-week gap between BMW's recall decision and the mail truck, so do not wait for the envelope. Until the repair is done, engage the parking brake every single time you park: not as a precaution, but as the only thing between your car and the bottom of the hill.
