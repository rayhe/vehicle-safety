# Research: The 2.5% Fix — IIHS's First Commercial-Vehicle Crash-Avoidance Ratings

## Story angle (1-2 sentences)
IIHS released its first-ever crash-avoidance ratings for cargo vans and heavy-duty pickups on Oct 8, 2026, and the results are a disaster class in missing motorcycles: the Rivian van aced pedestrian detection but recorded zero speed reduction and zero timely warnings in all six motorcycle runs. Meanwhile, NHTSA's long-stalled heavy-vehicle AEB mandate — sold as the fix — would save just 155 of the 6,215 lives these vehicles take each year, or 2.5% of the toll.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. First-ever IIHS crash-avoidance test of commercial vehicles (5 days old), a striking vehicle-specific paradox (Rivian: 4/6 car avoidance, timely warnings in all 3 semitrailer runs, 0/6 on the motorcycle), and a novel original calculation: the proposed federal AEB rule covers only 2.5% of heavy-vehicle-involved deaths. Not a data dump — nobody has run the 155/6,215 fraction publicly.

## Key data points
- **6,215 people killed in 2024** in crashes involving medium- or heavy-duty vehicles or light vans = **16% of all U.S. roadway fatalities** (IIHS, via jalopnik/freep).
- **155 / 6,215 = 2.495% (~2.5%)**: the proposed rule's lives saved as a share of the annual heavy-vehicle-involved death toll. The other ~97.5% die in crashes AEB doesn't address (oncoming-lane, intersection T-bone, pedestrian outside AEB speed envelope, single-vehicle, motorcycle-involved).
- **19,118 / 60,000 = 31.9%**: share of heavy-vehicle striking rear-end crashes the rule would prevent.
- **Vehicle-to-vehicle ratings**: Sprinter 2500 Good; BrightDrop 400 Acceptable; Rivian RCV Marginal; Ram 3500 Marginal; everything else Poor (Express 2500, Savana 2500, Transit T250, ProMaster 2500, Silverado 3500HD, F-350, Sierra 3500).
- **Pedestrian ratings**: BrightDrop 400 Good, Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 Good (the only two, and both are EVs; BrightDrop is discontinued by GM); Sprinter/F-350/Ram 3500 Acceptable; Transit/ProMaster/Silverado 3500/Sierra 3500 Marginal; Express 2500/Savana 2500 Poor (no AEB at all — optional FCW only).
- **Motorcycle target was the biggest challenge**: several systems "did not issue warnings or slow at all in most of the motorcycle trials." Rivian RCV: avoided car 4/6, timely warning all car runs + all 3 semitrailer runs, **zero speed reduction / zero timely warnings in all 6 motorcycle runs**.
- **F-350**: avoided the car target only at 31 mph; at 43 mph it "failed to reduce its speed at all."
- **Chevy Silverado 3500HD**: failed to avoid the passenger car in all six runs.
- IIHS president David Harkey: "These results are a wake-up call for commercial vehicle makers."
- Rule still not finalized as of Oct 2026 (3+ years after NPRM).

## Original contribution
The 2.5% coverage calculation: 155 lives saved (NHTSA NPRM estimate) against the 6,215-person heavy-vehicle-involved death toll (IIHS/FARS 2024) — a cross-tabulation of two public numbers nobody has published together. Plus the 31.9% figure for striking rear-end crashes.

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
The 2.5% figure undersells the rule. It targets one specific crash mode (striking rear-end) and prevents 19,118 of ~60,000 such crashes (31.9%) — a real, meaningful slice of preventable carnage, and $2B/year in savings is not nothing. The rest of the 6,215 toll includes crashes where no truck brake system could help (e.g., light vehicle runs red into truck's side). Judging a rear-end rule by total heavy-vehicle fatalities is like judging a flu shot by all-cause mortality. Also: IIHS test protocols reward warnings (timely ≥2.1s) and braking behavior in controlled scenarios; real-world deployment effects may differ, and some "Poor" systems still mitigate severity.

## Limitations
- IIHS ratings measure specific test scenarios (31/37/43 mph against stopped car/motorcycle, pedestrian day/night), not real-world crash outcomes.
- 6,215 is IIHS's FARS-derived figure; the 155-saves estimate is NHTSA's modeled estimate assuming full fleet penetration (takes decades).
- BrightDrop 400 is discontinued, so the top pedestrian performer can't be bought new.
- The 2024 FARS-based 6,215 figure and NHTSA's estimate use different denominators (light vans included in one).
- Test vehicle trim levels differ (e.g., F-350 DRW Lariat) — results are trim-specific.

## Actionable insight
If you drive a motorcycle or a small car: the 10,001–14,000-lb work trucks around you are disproportionately likely to have driver-assistance that can't see you — especially motorcycles. Defensive lane positioning near commercial vehicles matters. For fleet buyers: the Sprinter 2500 is the only Class 3-area vehicle with a Good V2V rating; for pedestrian-dense routes, the electric vans (Rivian RCV, BrightDrop) lead. And the Express/Savana still ship without AEB — a checkbox worth demanding before signing a fleet order.
