# Research notes — #818: School bus danger zone

Slug: `school-bus-safest-ride-deadliest-curb`
Journalist: Rex Driverton (fatality-rate investigations, paradox beat; kicker: Investigation)
Date: 2026-09-04

## Thesis
The school bus is the safest vehicle on American roads, and the federal data proves it with almost comical margins. But NHTSA's new 10-year review (2015-2024, released ~July 2026) contains an inversion: of 1,069 deaths in school-transportation crashes over the decade, only 119 (11%) were bus occupants. 70% were occupants of other vehicles. 15% were pedestrians. The bus doesn't kill its riders; the traffic around the bus kills everyone else, and the victims skew very young: 55% of school-age pedestrians killed were ages 5-10, two-thirds died away from intersections, and the deadliest hour is 7-7:59 a.m. Meanwhile NASDPTS's 2026 one-day count found 56,024 illegal stop-arm passes, projecting 38.4 million per school year. Back-to-school peg: kids returned to classrooms this week.

## Original contribution (Rex's math)
- **Per-rider risk:** 70 passenger deaths over 10 years = 7/year across ~21.4M daily riders = ~0.33 deaths per million riders per year. A school-age child is roughly twice as likely to die as a pedestrian around the bus (77 over the decade) as riding inside it (39).
- **The inversion, stated plainly:** the vehicle's mass protects its occupants and kills everyone else. 70% of the decade's 1,069 deaths were people in other vehicles, the people who hit the bus.
- **The violation arithmetic:** 213,800 illegal passes per school day ÷ ~484,000 buses = roughly one illegal pass per bus every 2-3 days. The violation is routine; the killing is rare; that gap is exactly why drivers keep doing it.
- **The morning spike:** 7:00-7:59 a.m., mid-block (not intersections), ages 5-10. The danger zone is the walk to the stop in the dark, not the ride.

## Strongest counterargument
The 38.4M figure is a projection, not a count: a voluntary one-day spring snapshot from 26% of drivers in 39 states + DC, extrapolated to a full school year. Self-selection, weather, and day-of-week effects all multiply in the extrapolation. Most passes are low-speed rolling violations on multi-lane roads, not full-speed blasts through a loading zone; the headline number overstates the lethal subset. And the bus's safety record is partly operational, not vehicular: low-speed residential routes, professional CDL drivers, high-visibility paint, flashing lights. Put the same crash structure on highway duty and the numbers would look different. The NSC 0.01-vs-0.96 comparison mixes accident rates with very different exposure profiles.

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only. Non-fatal injuries (12,500 in school-transportation crashes in 2022, incl. 6,000 bus passengers) are outside this dataset.
- 107 deaths/year is a small number; single multi-fatality crashes swing annual figures.
- The 2015-2024 review includes COVID years when ridership and traffic patterns were abnormal.
- Near-misses, the largest category of danger-zone events, are uncounted by every dataset here.
- Ridership (21.4M) is a 2022-23 figure applied to a 10-year window; approximate.

## Actionable insights
- Drivers: on undivided roads, stop for flashing reds and the extended stop arm, both directions. Fines reach $1,000 for a first offense in several states (AR, CA, TN, HI), plus license points and suspension; Washington mandates a $500 fine.
- Parents: the ride is the safest part of the trip. Teach the 10-foot danger zone, wait for the driver's signal before crossing, never walk behind the bus. The 5-to-10 crowd, mid-block, at 7 a.m. is the exact profile NHTSA's data flags.
- The single highest-leverage fix per the data isn't on the bus at all; it's the passing motorist.
