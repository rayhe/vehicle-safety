Lucid Recalled Nearly Every Air It Ever Built Because the Fuse Was Software, and the Software Had a Bug

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 3, 2026

Lucid is recalling 27,185 Air sedans, spanning every model year from 2022 through 2026, because the software standing guard over the exterior lighting circuits could let the wiring cook itself into a fire. That is roughly 172 percent of every car the company delivered last year.[1]

27,185
Air sedans recalled. Lucid delivered 15,841 cars in all of 2025.

A fuse has exactly one job: it sits there, dumb and patient, and melts before the wires do. Lucid replaced that dumb little wire with an eFuse, a software-defined sentinel that watches current draw and cuts power when things get hot. According to the company's own filing with NHTSA, the software logic "may provide insufficient overcurrent protection by allowing continued circuit energization at excess amperage."[2] Read that twice, because the smart fuse kept feeding power to a faulted circuit, which is the single thing a fuse is never supposed to do.

The tally so far: three fires, four smoke events, and 33 warranty claims for fried wiring in the center high-mounted stop lamp circuit.[3] The first fire happened in June 2023, in a company-owned car, and nobody could pin down the cause. Then came nearly three years of quiet, until a customer's car burned in May 2026 and kicked off the real investigation.[4] The numbers smirk, thirty-eight months after the first flame.

The obituary needs a correction, though, because Lucid already shipped the fix. Software version 2.10.0 went out over the air in July, a full month before the recall, tightening the eFuse thresholds and refusing to re-energize a faulted circuit within the same key cycle.[2] Telemetry showed 20,719 cars already carried it when the paperwork was filed, which meant no dealer visits and, so far, no injuries reported. As large-scale recalls go, this one is weirdly competent, which carries its own unease: a car company that can see every vehicle's software version in real time still needed 38 months to diagnose a fire.

If you drive a 2022-2026 Air, the instructions are blessedly simple. Check your software version tonight, install 2.10.0 or anything newer, and park outside away from structures until you do. Formal notices go to the stragglers by October 16.[2] The broader lesson is stranger. For a software-defined car, tapping "install update" is now a fire-safety procedure, filed somewhere between checking tire pressure and not storing gasoline next to the water heater. What this story does not prove: nobody has been reported hurt, the fire counts come from Lucid's own filing rather than independent investigation, and "nearly every Air ever built" is approximate, since the production window and the recall population do not line up perfectly.
