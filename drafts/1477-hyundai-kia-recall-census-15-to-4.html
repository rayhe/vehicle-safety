The Gap

Same Company, Same Parts Bin. Hyundai Recalled Its New Cars 15 Times. Kia Recalled 4.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

Before you sign that lease, look at the badge twice. Hyundai and Kia are the same company. They share platforms, suppliers, and a parts bin so common that this January both brands filed a recall for the same blank dashboard, the same week, for the same faulty chip. I ran the corrected census this site built for Toyota and Honda across both brands, 100 queries against NHTSA's recalls database, and the result is Hyundai at 15 federal recall campaigns for its 2026 models against Kia at 4, same parent, same parts, and a gap of nearly four to one.[1]

15 to 4
Recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching 2026-model-year vehicles: Hyundai vs Kia, two brands of one company

Start with the defect both brands share, because it proves the parts bin is real. In late January, Hyundai recalled 41,651 vehicles and Kia recalled 42,677, a combined 84,328 cars, because electrical noise in a power management chip could reboot the instrument cluster mid-drive and blank the speedometer. Hyundai's filing calls it a reboot while Kia's calls it an overheat protection trip. Same chip, same supplier, same blank screen, two filings, two wordings. Genesis filed a third parallel recall for the identical failure, 83,877 more cars, excluded from this census only because Genesis is a separate make in the database. One defect, three badges, more than 168,000 dashboards that can go dark while you are doing seventy.[5][6][7]

Count carefully, because the database will lie to you politely. NHTSA's recalls API answers a make, model, and model-year query with rows or an HTTP 400, and every census on this site has treated 400 as zero campaigns. That rule works for a gas model with no recalls. It also fires when the model name is wrong. Query "elantra" and you get three campaigns, but query "elantra hybrid" and you get a fourth, 26V308, a fire risk in the hybrid power control unit covering 54,337 cars, four field incidents including one fire, and a remedy that is a free software update. A base-name census prints Hyundai 14 while the corrected census prints 15. One campaign, 54,337 cars, a fire risk, hidden behind a suffix.[10]

One campaign in Hyundai's 15 is the only one that should make you angry, and it is the biggest. In March, Hyundai recalled 61,093 Palisades after a two-year-old girl in Ohio was killed when a third-row power seat pinned her. Second- and third-row power seats in Limited and Calligraphy trims could keep moving after contacting a person, because the seat never registered the contact. Hyundai logged one fatality, four minor injuries, and seventeen vehicles with claims, halted sales, and finalized a software fix on April 8 that requires the tailgate to be open before the seats will fold. Five months later the same defect showed up on the brand-new Ioniq 9 as 26V588, same feature, same failure, new flagship. A fix that took a child's death to ship, then needed shipping twice.[8][9]

October brought the pair that best shows how the sausage gets made. Two recalls, one supplier, one plant, one cause. Campaign 26V692 covers 24 cars whose front strut fasteners were not tightened properly. Campaign 26V693 covers 17 cars whose wheel lug nuts may loosen until the wheel detaches. Both trace to Samjin Nut hardware at the Ulsan plant in South Korea, where a temporary manual torquing process ran after tooling changes. Seventeen cars is a rounding error, but a loose wheel is not. It is the one failure in this census that can kill someone in the other lane, and Hyundai caught it in production sampling before any of the seventeen hurt anybody, which is exactly what a recall system is supposed to do.[11]

Now the honest accounting, because a 15-to-4 headline is a count, not a verdict. Kia's 4 is not proof Kia builds better cars. Kia sells fewer 2026 nameplates, ten against Hyundai's fifteen, and its two biggest sellers, the Telluride and Seltos, had zero campaigns touching 2026 models. Hyundai's 15 is inflated by the Palisade, which alone carries three campaigns and the year's only fatality, and by the Tucson family, which carries four or five campaigns per powertrain because every Tucson variant inherits every Tucson defect. Strip the shared defects and the gap narrows to thirteen distinct Hyundai defects against three distinct Kia defects, still four to one and still real, but the mechanism is visible: Hyundai's new hardware, a redesigned Palisade, a new Ioniq 9, Tucson Hybrid volume, is where the defects live, and Kia's 2026 lineup is older metal, so shop by model, not by badge. Across the six brands this site has now censused, the table reads Ford 40, GM 15, Toyota 15, Hyundai 15, Honda 4, Kia 4, and the spread inside one company is as wide as the spread across the whole industry.[2][3][4]

What to do with this: if you own a 2026 Palisade or Palisade Hybrid in Limited or Calligraphy trim, get the free power-seat software update and do not use the one-touch tilt-and-slide or power-fold functions with a child near the seat until it is done. If you own a 2024–2026 Elantra Hybrid, the fire-risk fix is a free dealer software update, and a no-start condition, limp mode, or the malfunction indicator lamp lighting up are your warning signs. If you own a 2026 Tucson, Ioniq 5, Santa Cruz, Kona, Santa Fe, Sonata, Palisade, K4, Sorento, Carnival, EV9, K5, or Sportage, your instrument cluster may go blank while driving, and the fix is free and can arrive over the air. If you own a 2026 Venue or Elantra built in August 2026, check your VIN for the lug-nut recall, because seventeen cars is a small recall and a loose wheel is a large problem. Every fix in this census is free, and every one of them starts at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
