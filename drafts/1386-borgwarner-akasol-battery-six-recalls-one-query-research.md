## Kill test: PASS

Proposed angle: On October 30, 2026, NHTSA opened recall query RQ26005
into BorgWarner Akasol Gen 3 battery packs — not a new defect, but a
query into whether SIX existing recalls of the same battery actually
worked. NHTSA's own summary is two phrases: "Scope and remedy
effectiveness" of recalls 25E018, 25E035, 25V055, 25V184, 25V185, and
25V203. One supplier component, five vehicle brands, six campaign
numbers, and the federal question is whether the first round of fixes
scoped the problem or closed it.

Challenge: is this surprising after 1,385 articles? Yes, three ways.
(1) The fragmentation is the story: BorgWarner filed an information
report under 49 CFR 573.3(f), not a recall, so each vehicle maker owns
its own remedy — a fleet running mixed Volvo, Mack, Nova Bus, and
Gillig electrics must track six campaign numbers for one loose piece
of hardware. (2) The repeat: Akasol Gen 3 was already recalled in 2023
for a torque failure (over-torqued rivnut inserts becoming debris);
the 2025 wave is a second torque failure (under-torqued / reworked
terminal-connector hardware). Same battery generation, same failure
family, twice. (3) The timeline: first thermal event January 6, 2025
(Egypt body builder, battery not yet installed) to federal query
October 30, 2026 is 662 days from spark to "did the fix work."

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)

- **RQ26005, opened October 30, 2026, manufacturer BORG WARNER
  CORPORATION, component ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:
  TRACTION BATTERY.** NHTSA summary: "Scope and remedy effectiveness
  of recalls 25E018, 25E035, 25V055, 25V184, 25V185, and 25V203."
  (OEMDTC investigations list, mirroring NHTSA ODI.)
- **The six recalls, one component.** BorgWarner equipment report
  25E035 (received May 16, 2025) covers Gen 3 Akasol HV TCC
  (terminal connector counterpart) packs built July 16, 2023 to
  August 8, 2024, based on recall determinations by Volvo (25V055),
  Mack Trucks (25V185), Nova Bus (25V184), Cummins (25E018), and
  Gillig (25V203). Defect: "Loose hardware inside the battery may
  detach, causing the battery pack to short circuit and fail."
  Consequence: loss of drive power (crash risk) plus short-circuit
  fire risk. (OEMDTC 25E035 summary; NHTSA Part 573 report 25E035.)
- **Pack counts in BorgWarner's own filing:** approximately 138
  suspect packs for Volvo/Mack, 21 for Nova Bus, 26 for Gillig, 19
  for Cummins — about 204 suspect packs total. OEMDTC lists 543
  potential units affected for the equipment report; the two counts
  measure different things (packs named by brand vs. equipment-report
  units) and must not be added together. (NHTSA Part 573 report
  25E035.)
- **Volvo's vehicle recall 25V055:** certain 2022–2025 Volvo VNRE
  (electric) vehicles, built May 13, 2021 through November 13, 2024;
  153 vehicles affected (142 U.S., 11 Canada). Cause: manufacturing
  process parameters "relative to torque and rework" at the supplier
  factory; improperly torqued hardware could break and fall into
  the pack. (Volvo Trucks safety recall notice, rev. 5, June 10, 2025.)
- **BorgWarner filed an information report, not a recall.** Under
  49 CFR Part 573.3(f), BorgWarner "is not conducting a recall of
  its products" — each vehicle/equipment manufacturer determines its
  own remedy. (NHTSA Part 573 report 25E035.)
- **Timeline, 2025 wave:**
  - January 6, 2025: Volvo Bus Europe reports a thermal runaway on a
    BorgWarner Akasol Gen 3 battery at a body builder in Egypt. The
    battery was not yet installed on a vehicle.
  - January 7, 2025 (per 25E035 chronology): Volvo reports smoke from
    a high-voltage battery waiting for installation; non-U.S.,
    Europe-built unit.
  - January 28, 2025: BorgWarner Akasol advises Volvo that the
    problematic manufacturing parameters seen at Darmstadt, Germany
    were also seen at its Hazel Park, USA facility. Initial analysis
    had believed only Darmstadt packs were of concern.
  - January 30 / February 3, 2025: Volvo Bus and Nova Bus / Volvo
    Truck product safety committees determine a defect exists.
  - February 5, 2025: Nova Bus files with Transport Canada and
    submits a foreign recall report to NHTSA (25F-009).
  - March 20, 2025: Volvo receives a new suspect-battery list from
    BorgWarner expanding the impact to Mack (Canada), Volvo
    (Canada), Mack (USA), and Nova Bus (USA).
  - March 25, 2025: Nova Bus files its Part 573 report.
  - August 8, 2024 (correction date): BorgWarner revised
    manufacturing parameters and processes to prevent overstress of
    the terminal connector counterpart.
  - No accidents or injuries related to this defect, and no warranty
    claims, per the 25V184 defect-determination basis.
  (NHTSA 25V184 basis document; NHTSA Part 573 report 25E035.)
- **The 2023 precedent, same battery, same failure family:** Akasol
  Gen 3 batteries were recalled in 2023 because insufficient
  manufacturing controls led to potential over-torquing of rivnut
  inserts on the bus bars connecting battery cells; failed inserts
  could become debris inside the pack and short it. That recall
  covered 173 vehicles built April 1, 2019 to February 10, 2023, and
  replaced batteries regardless of whether the vehicle had completed
  the previous Akasol recall. No field reports at the time.
  (Volvo Trucks "Note to Field," Akasol battery recall v3,
  July 25, 2023.)
- **Secondary characterization (attributed, not NHTSA's words):**
  The EV Report (November 2, 2026) describes RQ26005 as citing
  "repeated recall expansions and failures after the remedy." NHTSA's
  own published summary says only "scope and remedy effectiveness."
  The article must keep those two claims separate.

   campaign numbers to their brands for the first time on this site —
   25E035 (BorgWarner equipment), 25V055 (Volvo), 25V185 (Mack),
   25V184 (Nova Bus), 25E018 (Cummins), 25V203 (Gillig) — so a mixed-
   fleet operator can see the whole patchwork in one place instead
   of chasing six recall notices for one defect.
2. **The 662-day arc (new calculation):** January 6, 2025 (Egypt
   thermal runaway) to October 30, 2026 (RQ26005) = 662 days from
   first thermal event to a federal query about whether the fix
   worked. Within that arc, the scope expanded twice: Darmstadt-only
   to Hazel Park (January 28, 2025), then a new suspect list
   (March 20, 2025).
3. **The second-torque-wave framing (new):** 2023 over-torque
   (rivnuts) → 2025 under-torque/rework (terminal connector) →
   2026 query on remedy effectiveness. Three chapters, one battery
   generation, one failure family: torque applied wrong inside a
   high-voltage pack, leaving loose metal where a short can start.

## Limitations (what this article does NOT prove)

- A recall query is not a finding of defect and not a recall. RQ26005
  asks about scope and remedy effectiveness; it does not allege a
  specific number of post-remedy failures. The "failures after the
  remedy" phrasing comes from The EV Report's summary, not from
  NHTSA's published resume, and the article says so.
- The article does not have RQ26005 complaint, crash, fire, or injury
  counts: the published resume excerpt lists no counts. Do not invent
  them. The 2025 defect determinations report no accidents, injuries,
  or warranty claims for this defect.
- The 543-unit equipment-report figure and the ~204 named suspect
  packs count different things; do not sum them or present either as
  the total vehicles affected. Vehicle-level counts (e.g., Volvo's
  153 VNRE) are per-brand and also overlap with the pack counts.
- The 2023 rivnut recall and the 2025 terminal-connector recall are
  distinct defect mechanisms inside the same battery generation.
  Do not claim the 2023 fix "failed" — claim only that the same
  generation returned with a second torque-related failure mode.
- RQ26005 covers electric trucks and buses (commercial fleets), not
  passenger cars. The risk profile (passenger-carrying buses, depot
  charging) differs from a passenger-car recall and the article
  should not generalize to consumer EVs.

## Strongest counterargument (stated at full strength)

This is a tiny, professionally managed problem being handled the way
the system is supposed to work. About two hundred suspect packs, zero
reported accidents or injuries, zero warranty claims in the 2025
determinations, and every affected vehicle belongs to a commercial
fleet with a maintenance department and a supplier that inspects and
replaces packs. A recall query is routine oversight, not an alarm:
NHTSA opens dozens, and roughly half of all investigations end without
a recall at all. Nobody's family sedan is involved, nobody has been
hurt, and the 2023 precedent shows the same supplier replacing whole
batteries proactively. Treating a paperwork query about bus batteries
as a scandal overstates it. That is all true — and it is also why the
query matters more than its size suggests: the vehicles are buses and
trucks, a shorted high-voltage pack is a fire risk with passengers
aboard or a depot full of chargers nearby, and the query exists
because the first fix, applied across five brands through six
separate campaigns, may not have scoped or held. Small populations
with catastrophic failure modes are exactly where "did the remedy
work" is the right question.

## Actionable takeaways

- Fleet operators with Volvo VNRE, Mack LR Electric, Nova Bus LFSe,
  Gillig Low Floor Electric, or Cummins-powered equipment using
  Akasol Gen 3 packs built July 2023–August 2024: check VINs against
  25V055, 25V185, 25V184, 25V203, and 25E018 — do not assume a 2025
  remedy closed the file while RQ26005 is open.
- If your fleet completed a 2025 Akasol remedy, ask the vehicle maker
  (not BorgWarner — it filed an information report, not a recall)
  whether your packs are inside RQ26005's scope review.
- Check any vehicle at nhtsa.gov/recalls by VIN; recall repairs are
  free.
