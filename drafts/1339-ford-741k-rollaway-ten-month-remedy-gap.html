By The Numbers

741,195 Fords Can Roll Away After You Park Them. The Fix Arrives in April 2027.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again. They didn't get better, but they did get more specific. In July, Ford recalled 741,195 vehicles because the transmission can slam the parking pawl in while you are driving, chewing up the park system until the car rolls away the next time you shift to Park.[1] Interim letters went out in August. The actual fix arrives in April 2027.[2] Between now and then, those 741,195 cars will drive roughly 8.34 billion miles with a known, admitted rollaway defect and no remedy. I checked that math three ways. It holds up.

Start with the defect itself, because it is a two-act tragedy. Inside the 10-speed automatic, the valve body separator plate can restrict fluid flow to the park valve, which causes the parking pawl to engage while the vehicle is still moving.[3] That is Act One: your transmission brake-stabs itself at speed. Act Two comes later, when the damaged pawl fails to engage at all, and the 5,800-pound Expedition you just left on the driveway decides to go sightseeing without you. Ford has logged 285 reports of potential movement, 24 property-damage allegations, and 9 alleged injuries, two of which Ford classifies as emotional.[1] The whole recall covers the 2018-2021 Expedition (246,202 units), 2020-2021 Explorer (313,147), 2021 F-150, 2020-2021 Lincoln Aviator, and 2018-2021 Navigator.[4]

None of this was a mystery. NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation knocked on Ford's door in early April 2026 after eleven owner questionnaires described the same problem.[3] Ford opened its Critical Concern Review Group investigation on April 14. And Ford had issued a technical service bulletin back in May 2023 instructing technicians to fit an improved separator plate that is visually distinct from the original.[4] Read that timeline again: the root-cause fix existed on paper three years before the recall. The remedy owners get in April 2027 is mostly that software update plus an inspection of the damage the delay allowed.

Now the part that will ruin your morning. Federal rules require automakers to notify owners as soon as a defect is confirmed, even when the fix is not ready. So every owner got a letter that essentially says: your car can roll away, we cannot fix it yet, use the parking brake every time.[2] That is a fine instruction for a parked car. It does nothing for the pawl slamming in while you are driving, which has no driver workaround at all. And when the fix finally arrives, NHTSA's own data says a big chunk of these cars will never see it: vehicles 5 to 10 years old complete recalls at a 60 percent rate, and every one of these is 5 to 8 years old.[6] Run that out: roughly 296,000 vehicles statistically will never be repaired. Ever.

What you do with all this depends on which side of the recall you are on. If you own one of the 741,195: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, set the parking brake every single time you park, and treat a wrench light on the dash like a smoke alarm.[5] If you are shopping used 2018-2021 Expeditions, Explorers, F-150s, Navigators, or Aviators: demand the VIN's recall status in writing before you sign, because a hefty share of these will still be open on the fix next spring. And if you are wondering why the interim letter feels useless, consider the footnote nobody headlines: Ford's "roll away detection" is supposed to auto-apply the parking brake if the car moves in Park, but the powertrain control module may be asleep after the vehicle has been powered down, unable to detect movement.[5] The backup system has a hole in it shaped exactly like your driveway.
