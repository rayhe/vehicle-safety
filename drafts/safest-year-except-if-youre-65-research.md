# Research: Safest Year Except If You're 65

## Angle
In H1 2024, every subcategory of traffic death declined except two: motorcyclists (+1%) and people over 65 (+1%). The 65+ divergence isn't new — it's a decade-long trend that's accelerating. While America celebrates near-record-low fatality rates, older Americans are dying more, not less. And 65+ pedestrian deaths are up 56% since 2014.

## Self-Critique Gate
**Is this genuinely surprising after 784 articles?** Yes. The site has covered motorcyclist divergence (below-one-not-if-you-ride), sedan fragility (fragility-tax-retiree-sedans), and state-level divergence (nine-states-wrong-direction). But nobody has specifically named the 65+ group as the ONLY other category going the wrong direction, or connected it to the decade trend.

**Kill test:** The fact that every subcategory improved except motorcyclists and over-65 is genuinely newsworthy. The 56% increase in older pedestrian deaths over a decade is alarming and under-reported.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: NHTSA H1 2024 Early Estimates (subcategory breakdown)
- Via RevZilla/Common Tread analysis of NHTSA data (Aug 2026)
- Overall fatalities down 3.2% H1 2024 vs H1 2023
- Subcategory changes (all H1 2024 vs H1 2023):
  - Pedestrians: down 3%
  - Bicyclists: down 1%
  - Passenger vehicle drivers: down 3%
  - Passenger vehicle passengers: down 7%
  - Men: down 3%
  - Women: down 2%
  - At night: down 4%
  - On weekends: down 4%
  - During out-of-state travel: down 12%
  - Speeding-related: down 6%
  - Rollover crashes: down 6%
  - Single-vehicle crashes: down 1%
  - Crashes involving large truck: down 1%
  - **Motorcyclists: UP 1%** (only vehicle category increasing)
  - **Over age 65: UP 1%** (only demographic category increasing)
- Source URL: NHTSA press release via https://www.revzilla.com/common-tread/survival-odds-are-improving-for-nearly-everyone-on-us-roads-except-motorcyclists

### Source 2: NHTSA Traffic Safety Fact — Older Population: 2023 Data
- NHTSA NCSA publication (crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov)
- 2014 to 2023 changes for 65+ population:
  - Total traffic fatalities among 65+: increased 38% (males +46%, females +24%)
  - 65+ pedestrian fatalities: increased 56% (males +68%, females +34%)
  - 65+ pedalcyclist fatalities: doubled for both men and women
- Per 100,000 population rate: ~9.90 (2014) → ~12.51 (2023) for 65+ group
- Under-65 rate also rose (~12 to ~14/100K), but that's mostly the pandemic spike; 65+ increase preceded the pandemic

### Source 3: NHTSA 2025 Traffic Death Estimates & 2024 FARS
- 2024 final FARS: 39,254 deaths, rate 1.19 per 100M VMT
- 2025 estimate: 36,640 deaths, rate 1.10 per 100M VMT (6.7% decrease, second-lowest rate in recorded history)
- Fatalities decreased in 39 states + DC + PR in 2025
- VMT increased 29.8B miles (0.9%) in 2025
- Source URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/traffic-deaths-2025-early-estimates-2024-annual

### Source 4: NHTSA Older Driver Data/Surveillance Page
- Crash rates per VMT overstate risk for older drivers (they drive more local roads)
- Crash rates per licensed driver understate risk (many hold licenses but reduce driving)
- Key insight: older adults' increased fragility means same crash is more likely fatal
- 65+ population proportion continues growing (15% to ~18% of US population)
- Source URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/book/countermeasures-that-work/older-drivers/data-surveillance

### Source 5: NSC Injury Facts — Older Drivers Data
- State-level variation in 65+ driver involvement in fatal crashes:
  - Lowest: Vermont (4.97/100K licensed drivers), Rhode Island (5.28)
  - Highest: Mississippi (32.36/100K), Missouri (26.36), Kentucky (25.58)
  - 6.5x variation from safest to most dangerous state
- Source URL: https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/road-users/older-drivers/data-details/

### Source 6: US Census Bureau population data
- 65+ population grew from 46.2M (2014) to 59.4M (2023) = ~29% growth
- But 65+ fatalities grew 38% = outpacing population growth
- Implies genuine per-capita risk increase, not just population growth artifact

## Novel Contribution
Cross-tabulating:
1. The 2024 subcategory breakdown (65+ only non-motorcycle category with increase)
2. The decade-long 65+ trend (38% fatality increase, 56% pedestrian death increase)
3. The population-adjusted analysis (29% population growth vs 38% fatality growth)
4. The state-level 6.5x variation (Mississippi vs Vermont)

This reveals a genuine public health divergence that's been masked by the overall good news of declining fatality rates.

## Strongest Counterargument
The 65+ population grew ~29% over the decade. A 38% fatality increase means the per-capita increase is more like 7%, which is less dramatic than it sounds. And "up 1%" in H1 2024 is within the margin of error for NHTSA early estimates. The real story might be fragility (older bodies less likely to survive any given crash) rather than anything specific to driving safety.

## Limitations
- H1 2024 subcategory data is preliminary (NHTSA early estimates, not final FARS)
- "Up 1%" for 65+ in H1 2024 could be noise — small enough to not be statistically significant
- FARS data doesn't capture non-fatal crashes; 65+ might be getting into fewer crashes but dying more when they do
- Some of the 65+ fatalities are as passengers or pedestrians, not drivers
