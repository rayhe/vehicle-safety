# RESEARCH: Jeep Grand Cherokee Rear Coil Spring — Third Recall in 3 Years (#891)

## Topic
FCA/Stellantis recall 89D (NHTSA 26V562000, Sept 2026): 328,381 Jeep Grand Cherokees recalled for rear coil springs that may detach while driving. **It supersedes both previous recalls (26V051 and 23V413). Vehicles already repaired under the previous recalls must be repaired AGAIN.** Third campaign for the same component since May 2023.

## Recall timeline (primary sources)
- **23V413 (May 2023, FCA 64A):** Original recall, ~345,000+ Grand Cherokee + Grand Cherokee L. Rear coil springs improperly installed; dealers told to inspect spring position/orientation with isolators.
- **26V051 (Feb 2026):** Second recall — the *repair from 23V413 was defective*. 80,620 vehicles (29,139 Grand Cherokee + 51,481 Grand Cherokee L, air suspension excluded) needed a "repair of a prior repair." Stellantis: during 23V413 repairs, springs were installed incorrectly or left incomplete. Filing cited 20 customer assistance records + 284 warranty claims, no known accidents/injuries.
- **89D / 26V562000 (Sept 2026):** Third recall, 328,381 vehicles (2022-23 Grand Cherokee, 2022-23 Grand Cherokee 4XE, 2021-23 Grand Cherokee L). Supersedes BOTH prior campaigns. Remedy ESCALATED: this time dealers replace the rear coil spring lower isolators (hardware, not just inspection). Interim owner letters Sep 29, additional letters "at a later date" once final remedy available (per Delaware Online); VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov starting Sep 9.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Same component, third recall in 40 months; the *repair itself* failed twice; remedy escalated from visual inspection to hardware replacement. Repair-of-a-repair-of-a-repair.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. Not just "recall happened" — the ladder of failed remedies: inspect (2023) → re-inspect (2026) → replace parts (2026). A coil spring is a bent-steel coil; the failure is in the process, not the metallurgy. Nobody else framed this as a recall-repair quality-system story. Distinguishes from #771 (Stellantis camera, same-company repeat but different defect).
- **Original contribution:** cross-campaign arithmetic (345k → 80.6k → 328.4k), the remedy-escalation ladder, and the point that each prior "fix" expanded the pool of cars needing the next fix.

## Draft angle
Mia Crumplezone (engineering beat). Frame: the spring isn't the defect; the *repair procedure* was the defect, twice. 80,620 cars came back the second time specifically because the first fix was done wrong. Engineering insight: an isolator is a molded rubber seat that costs pennies and takes minutes — the fact that seating one correctly required three federal campaigns is a process-control story, not a parts story.

## Actionable takeaway
If you own a 2021-23 Grand Cherokee L, 2022-23 Grand Cherokee, or 2022-23 Grand Cherokee 4XE: you are in this recall whether or not you already had the springs "fixed" in 2023 or 2026. Check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable since Sep 9). Letters arrive Sep 29; the final remedy is still pending a second letter — ask your dealer specifically whether the *isolator replacement* (89D) has been applied, not just the earlier inspection.

## Limitations
- No accidents or injuries have been reported across all three campaigns; severity is hypothetical (spring detaching at speed is scary, but zero events in ~3.5 years).
- Population overlap across campaigns is unknown; cannot claim any specific vehicle was recalled three times.
- FARS data not used; this is a recall-procedure story, not a fatality-rate story. Stated explicitly.
- 20 assistance records + 284 warranty claims are Stellantis's self-reported counts at 26V051 filing.

## Strongest counterargument
Three recalls for the same component could be read as the system working: NHTSA caught the bad repair, Stellantis kept expanding the net, nobody got hurt, and the remedy kept escalating until the root fix (hardware replacement) landed. A generous read: this is recalls functioning as designed, just slowly and at consumer inconvenience.
