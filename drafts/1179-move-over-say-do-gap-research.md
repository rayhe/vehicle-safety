# Research: #1179 — Move-Over Say-Do Gap
**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk Chief; beat = driver behavior + impairment stats)
**Kicker:** By The Numbers
**Slug:** 1179-move-over-say-do-gap
**News peg:** National Slow Down, Move Over Day = Oct 17, 2026; three same-day data drops (Oct 15, 2026)

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? YES. Three independent same-day data releases (Ohio DOT crash counts, AAA Foundation national survey, AAA national newsroom) converge on one finding: drivers massively over-report move-over compliance (90% claim vs 64% observed). Plus a trend reversal: Ohio move-over crashes declined 2022-2024, then spiked to 903 in 2025 and are already at 730+ through mid-Oct 2026. Novel angle: the 26-point honesty gap + the compliance collapse. Not previously covered in ~1,178 articles (checked: no move-over articles in archive/queue).

## Primary sources

### 1. Columbus Dispatch, Oct 15, 2026 — Ohio DOT data
- 730 "Move Over Crashes" recorded in Ohio through mid-October 2026
- 93 people died or were seriously injured in those crashes
- 75 involved ODOT crews, vehicles, equipment; 11 involved Ohio State Highway Patrol officers (per AAA news release cited in article)
- Trend: spiked in 2025 to 903 total crashes, after numbers declined 2022-2024 (ODOT spokesperson Matt Bruning)
- "We would like to see it continue to go down, and then last year kind of reversed that trend" — Bruning
- Ohio law: move over a lane OR slow down when approaching any stationary vehicle with flashing lights; applies to emergency, road service, waste collection, PUCO, highway maintenance, public safety vehicles. Does NOT apply to a normal driver with hazards on.
- All 50 states have a version of the law.
- URL: https://www.dispatch.com/story/news/local/2026/10/15/ohio-crash-data-2026-odot-oshp-police/92267334007/

### 2. AAA newsroom (national), Oct 15, 2026 — AAA Foundation survey
- Survey ahead of National Slow Down, Move Over Day (Oct 17): 90%+ drivers reported slowing down/moving over last time they passed a disabled vehicle, tow truck, or police car on the shoulder
- Previous observational research: only 64% of vehicles subject to Slow Down, Move Over laws actually changed lanes and/or reduced speed. 36% did neither.
- 2,100+ people struck and killed while stranded or working at roadside, 2019-2023
- 2025: AAA responded to 30M+ roadside events near active traffic; 15 service providers killed in Slow Down, Move Over-related incidents; 6 involved hit-and-run drivers, 5 involved impaired drivers
- 90% of drivers support the laws when explained; 2% oppose
- Quote: "Every roadside stop is a danger zone" — Gene Boehm, AAA President/CEO
- URL: https://newsroom.aaa.com/2026/10/new-aaa-foundation-survey-highlights-deadly-roadside-safety-risks/

### 3. AAA Auto Club Group newsroom, Oct 15, 2026 — corroborating release
- Same 36% figure; "More than one-third (36%) of drivers fail to move over or slow down when passing roadside vehicles, despite most saying they would"
- Tiffany Wright (AAA - The Auto Club Group): being struck is the top roadside concern; 52% of NC drivers named it their biggest fear if stranded
- URL: https://newsroom.acg.aaa.com/aaa-more-than-one-third-of-drivers-fail-to-slow-down-or-move-over/

### 4. AAA (older release) — BLS occupational fatality context
- ~2 emergency responders (incl. tow workers) struck and killed per month by drivers failing to move over (Bureau of Labor Statistics)
- Tow operators killed at ~43 deaths per 100,000 workers vs 3 per 100,000 for all industries
- URL: https://newsroom.acg.aaa.com/aaa-applauds-gov-evers-for-signing-move-over-law-expansion/

## Key derived numbers (show the math)
- Say-do gap: 90% claim - 64% observed = 26-point honesty gap
- Ohio 2026 pace: 730 crashes / ~9.5 months ≈ 77 per month; annualized ≈ 920, on pace to exceed the 2025 spike of 903
- Ohio lethality: 93 dead-or-seriously-injured / 730 crashes ≈ 12.7% of move-over crashes produce death or serious injury
- National: 2,100 roadside struck-and-killed / 5 years (2019-2023) = ~420 per year
- Impairment link: 5 of 15 provider deaths in 2025 involved impaired drivers (1 in 3); 6 of 15 were hit-and-run
- Tow-worker occupational risk: 43 per 100k vs 3 per 100k all-industry = ~14x the average worker fatality rate

## Limitations (must state in article)
- Ohio "Move Over Crash" counts are ODOT-classified; classification criteria not published; may include crashes where the roadside vehicle was present but not causal
- 64% observed compliance is from "previous observational research" cited by AAA — methodology/sample not in the release; treat as directional
- 30M roadside events vs 15 provider deaths: provider deaths are AAA's own workforce + contractors, not all roadside workers nationally
- 2,100 national struck-and-killed (2019-2023) spans stranded motorists AND workers; not separable from the cited figure
- No FARS cross-tab run for this article; figures are from the cited releases, not independently recomputed

## Strongest counterargument
The 64% observed figure comes from unspecified prior observational research, and the 90% self-report is from a different survey at a different time. Comparing them as a clean "honesty gap" assumes the two samples are comparable, which the releases don't establish. Also possible: compliance genuinely improved since the observational study (drivers may have learned since), making the gap smaller than stated. And Ohio's 2025 spike could partly reflect better crash classification/reporting rather than more crashes. The article must present the gap as the releases frame it, with the comparability caveat stated.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- For drivers: move over a FULL lane when you see flashing lights on the shoulder; if you can't change lanes, slow down well below the limit (many states specify 20 mph under); kill the phone for the 10 seconds it takes to pass
- For stranded motorists: pull as far off the shoulder as possible, hazards on, and if you can safely reach the next exit, do it; stay in the vehicle with seatbelt on if you must wait
- For the policy-minded: 90% support for move-over laws exists; the failure is compliance, not consensus — automated enforcement and targeted patrols at known roadside-work zones are the lever
