# Research: New Hampshire — Live Free or Die Unbuckled

## Angle
New Hampshire is the only state in America where an adult can legally ride unbuckled. A peer-reviewed study published July 2026 in the Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery ran seven years of FARS data (2017-2023) and found the price: crashes inside New Hampshire are 2.8x more likely to kill someone unrestrained, and New Hampshire-licensed drivers carry the risk with them — 2.06x elevated odds of dying unrestrained even when they crash in *other* states. The law doesn't just shape behavior inside the border. It exports it.

## Self-Critique Gate
- **Propose:** Cross-tabulate NHTSA's fresh October 2026 occupant-protection state tables (2024 data) with the July 2026 Rice et al. FARS study to price the only no-belt-law state in America.
- **Challenge:** Is this genuinely surprising after 1,565 articles? Seat belt stories exist (belt paradox, belt reminders, belt recalls). But no article on this site has ever covered New Hampshire's no-law status, the enforcement-regime natural experiment (68% vs 7%), or the "exported risk" finding that NH-licensed drivers die unrestrained out of state. The Rice study is four months old. The NHTSA state tables are six weeks old. Novel data + novel cross-tab = genuine contribution.
- **Verdict:** Proceed.

## Primary Sources (3+)

### Source 1: Rice et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2026;101(1):80-88 (PMID 41941382)
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41941382/
- DOI: 10.1097/TA.0000000000004988
- Retrospective cross-sectional study, all fatal MVCs in the US 2017-2023, NHTSA FARS data. Multivariable logistic regression adjusted for demographic, environmental, vehicle, and driver-related factors. Level IV evidence.
- 146,655 fatal MVCs eligible; 474 occurred in New Hampshire; 485 involved NH license holders.
- Unrestrained share of fatal MVCs: 71.5% for crashes geographically in NH; 68.7% for occupants with NH licenses.
- Odds of unrestrained MVC fatality: NH crash location OR 2.80 (95% CI 2.15-3.65); NH license OR 2.74 (95% CI 2.13-3.52).
- NH-licensed drivers crashing OUTSIDE New Hampshire: OR 2.06 (95% CI 1.28-3.33). The habit travels.
- NH seat belt use 78% vs national 91% (study's framing).

### Source 2: NHTSA, Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2024 Data (DOT HS 813 840, October 2026)
- URL: https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/93868/dot_93868_DS1.pdf
- National 2024: 39,254 total traffic deaths; 22,713 passenger-vehicle occupants killed (58% of all deaths). Of those: 10,402 restrained (46%), 9,758 unrestrained (43%), 2,553 unknown (11%). Of known restraint use: 52% restrained, 48% unrestrained.
- Unrestrained deaths fell 7.4% vs 2023, but still 48% of known-restraint deaths.
- Pickup drivers killed: 59% unrestrained (worst vehicle type). Passenger car 47%, SUV 46%, van 42%.
- Ages 21-24 and 25-34: 60% of deaths unrestrained (highest of any age group). Ages 75+: 26% (lowest).
- Men 53% unrestrained vs women 40% (of known).
- Table 7 (state table): NH 2024 — 77 PV occupants killed; 27 restrained (35%), 39 unrestrained (51%), 11 unknown (14%); of known: 41% restrained / 59% unrestrained; observed belt use 78.2%.
- Same table: Wyoming — 67 killed, 61% unrestrained of known, observed use 73.4% (lowest in the nation). US total 48% unrestrained of known, 91.2% observed use.

### Source 3: NHTSA, Seat Belt Use in 2024 — Use Rates in the States and Territories (DOT HS 813 766, November 2025)
- URL: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813766
- National observed use 91.2% (NOPUS). State range: 73.4% (Wyoming) to 97.5% (California).
- 26 states + DC at 90%+ use.
- Enforcement split: among primary-enforcement jurisdictions, 68% hit 90%+ use; among secondary-enforcement or no-law jurisdictions, only 7% did.
- NH 2024: 78.2% (up 0.3 pts from 77.9% in 2023; 67.6% in 2017). Bottom five: Wyoming 73.4%, Arkansas 77.7%, NH 78.2%, Montana 79.3%, North Dakota 79.8%. Top: California 97.5%, Hawaii 97.2%, Oregon 95.5%, Connecticut 95.0%.

### Source 4: NHTSA, Restraint Use and Motorcycle Helmet Use Laws (June 2026)
- URL: https://cdan.dot.gov/tsftables/Laws.pdf
- 35 states + DC + Puerto Rico had primary seat belt laws in 2024. NH is the only state without a seat belt law for adults; it has a primary child passenger safety law covering under-18s.
- NH is also one of three states (with Illinois and Iowa) with no motorcycle helmet requirement for any age.

### Source 5: NH legislative history / NSC effectiveness figure
- NH House killed the 2023 adult belt bill 206-162 (SlashGear, citing NH legislative record).
- NHTSA: seat belts cut fatal-injury risk ~45% for front-seat occupants; NSC 2024 data: unrestrained deaths = 48.4% of occupant deaths while use is 91.2% (Injury Facts).
- NSC analysis: NH 59% of occupant deaths unrestrained (2024, known-restraint basis), consistent with NHTSA Table 7.

## Original Contribution
1. **The enforcement natural experiment, priced:** 68% of primary-law jurisdictions clear 90% belt use; 7% of secondary/no-law ones do. A 61-point gap that rides on one line of statute, not culture, weather, or vehicle mix.
2. **The exported-risk finding:** NH-licensed drivers are 2.06x more likely to die unrestrained even when the crash happens outside New Hampshire. The absence of a law is a habit-forming exposure, not a geographic one.
3. **The NH penalty in 2024 hard numbers:** 59% of NH's known-restraint deaths were unrestrained vs 48% nationally — 11 points above the country, in a state with 78.2% observed use vs 91.2% nationally. And the honest wrinkle: Wyoming (secondary law, 73.4% use, 61% unrestrained deaths) is worse on both numbers, so the story is "no law at all" vs "a law with no teeth," not "NH is uniquely worst."

## Limitations (for the article)
- The Rice study is observational (Level IV); it shows association, not proof the law itself caused the difference. Adjusted for demographics, environment, vehicle, and driver factors, but residual confounding (rural roads, speeds, vehicle age) remains.
- NH numbers are small: 474 fatal MVCs over seven years; 77 PV occupant deaths in 2024. Single-year state figures wobble.
- Restraint use was unknown in 11% of 2024 deaths (14% in NH); unknown cases are excluded from the "of known" percentages.
- NOPUS/state observational surveys measure daytime front-seat use only; they are not the same population as people in fatal crashes.
- Wyoming is worse than NH on both use rate and unrestrained death share despite having a (secondary) law — enforcement strength and culture matter, not just the law's existence.

## Strongest Counterargument (for the article)
New Hampshire voters have rejected belt bills repeatedly, most recently 206-162 in 2023, and the "Live Free or Die" position is a considered choice about bodily autonomy, not ignorance. The state's overall death rate per mile (0.96 in 2024) sits mid-pack nationally, not at the bottom — so the no-law experiment is not producing a statewide bloodbath. And the unrestrained share is a share of a small denominator: 39 unrestrained deaths in a state of 1.4 million people. The counter, stated fairly, is that freedom includes the freedom to make a bad bet with your own body. The reply: the bet is not only yours. Unrestrained occupants become projectiles that injure belted passengers, and the trauma care for the unbelted is socialized through hospitals and insurance pools. Also: the 2.06x exported-risk finding shows the habit does not stay in New Hampshire.

## Actionable Takeaways (for the article)
- Buckle up in every state, including the one that doesn't require it. The belt cuts your fatal-injury risk ~45%.
- If you drive in NH regularly, treat the missing law as a missing guardrail, not a permission slip.
- Buying a used car? Check the belt reminder system works in all seating positions; rear-seat occupants die unrestrained at 58% (second row, known-restraint basis, 2024).
- Parents: NH's child-passenger law covers under-18s with primary enforcement. The adult exemption does not extend to your kids.
