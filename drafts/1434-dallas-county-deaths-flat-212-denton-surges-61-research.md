# Research — #1434: Dallas County's Death Toll Didn't Move. Denton's Jumped 61%.

**Slug:** `1434-dallas-county-deaths-flat-212-denton-surges-61`
**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (Data Visualization Editor; county-level statistical comparison is his beat — direct sequel to #1433 Denton)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date:** November 8, 2026

## Story in one line
Dallas County recorded 212 traffic deaths in the first nine months of 2026, exactly one more than the 211 a year earlier, while injuries rose 4% on a crash count that barely moved. Same metro, same database, same nine months: next door in Denton County, deaths surged 61%. Dallas is the county that didn't blow up.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** YES. Fresh data (TxDOT CRIS exports processed October 15-16, 2026; analysis published late October 2026), the largest county in the DFW metro, and a clean four-county divergence measured the same way, in the same window, from the same database.
- **Novel angle?** YES. Full queue + stories grep: zero slugs contain "dallas-county". #1433 covered Denton/Tarrant/Collin only. No prior article has used the Dallas County CRIS series. The novel contribution is the four-county divergence table, the Dallas lethality arithmetic (flat at 5.9 deaths per 1,000 crashes while Denton's jumped 60%), and the split inside Dallas's flat total: commercial-vehicle deaths fell by a third while passenger-vehicle deaths rose.
- **3+ primary sources?** YES. (1) Wooley Law Firm's Dallas County analysis of TxDOT CRIS query exports; (2) Wooley's Denton/Tarrant/Collin analyses of the same CRIS series, same window (via #1433 research); (3) Wooley Texas statewide truck-wreck analysis for the CMV contrast; (4) National Safety Council H1 2026 preliminary estimate; (5) NHTSA Q1 2026 early estimate, DOT HS 813 833.
- **Actionable?** YES. Dallas County drivers: I-35E alone logged 2,656 crashes in nine months; Q3 was the most lethal quarter per crash (6.11 deaths per 1,000 crashes); commercial-vehicle deaths fell sharply, so the flat total is a passenger-vehicle story — seat belts, speed, and impairment are the levers. Grand Prairie crashes jumped 11.7% while Carrollton fell 11.6%: the county's risk is moving, not shrinking.

## Facts (verified November 8, 2026)

### Dallas County, Jan 1–Sep 30, 2026 vs same period 2025 (TxDOT CRIS)
| Measure | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported crashes | 35,905 | 35,975 | +0.2% (+70) |
| People injured | 17,202 | 17,887 | **+4.0% (+685)** |
| Suspected serious injuries | 1,246 | 1,204 | -3.4% (-42) |
| Traffic deaths | 211 | 212 | +0.5% (+1) |

- County averaged ~132 crashes and ~66 injured people per day over the 273 days.
- Records processed through October 15, 2026 (2026 file) and October 16, 2026 (2025 file).
- September 2026 alone: 3,892 crashes, 27 deaths in 26 fatal crashes (Wooley September piece, CRIS processed through Oct 19, 2026).

### Deaths by quarter (Dallas County, 2026)
| Quarter | Crashes | Injured | Serious | Deaths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (Jan–Mar) | 11,565 | 5,469 | 377 | 64 |
| Q2 (Apr–Jun) | 12,453 | 6,288 | 416 | 75 |
| Q3 (Jul–Sep) | 11,957 | 6,130 | 411 | 73 |

- Q2 had the highest totals on all four measures. Q3 fell vs Q2 on all four (-4.0% crashes, -2.5% injured, -1.2% serious, -2 deaths) but stayed above Q1 on all four (+3.4% crashes, +12.1% injured, +9% serious, +9 deaths).
- Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025: crashes 12,343 → 11,957 (-3.1%), injured 5,834 → 6,130 (+5.1%), serious 465 → 411 (-11.6%), deaths 76 → 73.

### Where in the county
- City of Dallas: 19,159 crashes (+3.0% vs 18,605), 53.3% of county crashes. The city's +554 crashes exceeded the countywide +70 because declines elsewhere offset it.
- Grand Prairie: 1,619 → 1,808 (+11.7%). Mesquite: 1,708 → 1,777 (+4.0%). Irving -4.1%, Garland -2.9%, Carrollton -11.6%.
- Busiest roadway labels: I-35E 2,656 (-5.4% vs 2,808); I-635 2,283 (+5.4% vs 2,167); I-30 2,087; I-20 1,397 (-5.7%); Loop 12 1,162; US-75 1,158; SH-183 575; SH-161 528; I-45 457; US-67 409.

- Four-county combined deaths: 445 → 453 (+1.8%). Dallas alone is 46.8% of the four-county 2026 total (212 of 453).
- Denton's +19 exceeds the other three counties' combined net change (-11). The metro total barely moved while Denton swung hard and Dallas stood still.

### National context

## Original contribution (Axle's math, show work)
1. **Lethality per 1,000 crashes, Dallas:** 2025: 211 / 35,905 x 1,000 = **5.88**. 2026: 212 / 35,975 x 1,000 = **5.89**. Unchanged to two decimal places. The county's crashes got no more lethal at all, while Denton's lethality went 3.09 → 4.96 (+60.5%). This is the article's core number: same metro, same nine months, one county's per-crash lethality flat, the other's up three-fifths.
2. **Injuries per crash rose while lethality didn't:** 2025: 17,202 / 35,905 = 0.479 injured per crash. 2026: 17,887 / 35,975 = 0.497 (+3.8%). And 685 extra injured people on 70 extra crashes = 9.8 injured per additional crash. Crashes got more injurious but no more deadly — a severity split, not a single trend.
3. **The flat total hides two opposite stories:** CMV deaths 27 → 18 (-9); non-CMV deaths 184 → 194 (+10, +5.4%). Trucks improved by a third; passenger-vehicle deaths rose. Net: +1. Same pattern as Denton (non-CMV +74%) but muted to a whisper.
4. **Quarterly lethality:** Q1 2026: 64 / 11,565 x 1,000 = 5.53. Q2: 75 / 12,453 x 1,000 = 6.02. Q3: 73 / 11,957 x 1,000 = 6.11. Q3 was the most lethal quarter per crash even though it had fewer crashes than Q2. In Denton the deadliest quarter was Q1; in Dallas it was Q3. The two counties don't even agree on when the danger peaks.
5. **Four-county lethality, 2026 (computed from published counts):** Dallas 5.89, Denton 4.96, Tarrant 142 / 24,868 x 1,000 = 5.71, Collin 49 / 12,148 x 1,000 = 4.03. Dallas has the highest per-crash lethality of the four — its flat death count is not a safe county, it is a big county whose crashes were already the most likely to kill.
6. **Deaths per day:** Dallas 2026: 212 / 273 days = one death every ~1.29 days. Denton: one every ~5.5 days. Dallas kills roughly four times as often as Denton, every day, and nobody notices because the number never changes.

## Limitations (required)
- **Small changes, big base:** a +1 death change on a 211 base is noise-level; the honest claim is "flat," not "improving." The lethality figures are stable because the base is large (35,975 crashes), but year-over-year county comparisons still carry reporting-lag noise.
- **No causation:** CRIS records as published in this analysis do not break out speed, impairment, belt use, road type, or time of day for the fatal crashes. The data establishes that deaths were flat while injuries rose; it does not establish why.
- **Not exposure-adjusted:** figures are raw counts, not rates per VMT or per capita. Dallas County is growing, so part of the crash count reflects more driving. Growth cannot explain the lethality pattern, but it is a confounder for the volume figures.
- **Preliminary:** records processed through October 15-16, 2026; late-filed reports can revise counts slightly upward. The 2026 file was revised between Wooley's H1 and through-September pieces (H1 crashes 23,970 → 24,018).
- **Source chain:** figures come from a law firm's published analysis of TxDOT CRIS query exports, not from a TxDOT publication. The methodology (two exports, matching date ranges, same county filter) is stated in the source and the arithmetic checks out against the published tables, but this is a secondary presentation of a public primary database.
- **Tarrant/Collin 2025 crash counts** used for any 2025 lethality comparison are derived from the published percentage changes and are approximate; the article's headline lethality numbers use only directly published 2026 counts.

## Strongest counterargument (required)
Two hundred twelve deaths is a terrible number, and "flat" is not "good" — Dallas County's per-crash lethality (5.89) is the highest of the four DFW counties measured, higher than Denton's even after Denton's surge. Framing Dallas as the success story next to Denton mistakes stability for safety: a county that kills one person every 1.3 days, every year, without variation, has a chronic problem, not a solved one. The honest reading: Dallas didn't get worse, which in a year when the national death rate kept falling is its own kind of failure. The flat line is the story, but it is not a compliment.
