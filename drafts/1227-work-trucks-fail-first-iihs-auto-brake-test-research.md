# #1227 Research: Work Trucks Fail First IIHS Auto-Brake Tests

## Angle
On October 8, 2026, IIHS published its FIRST-EVER front crash prevention ratings for
commercial vehicles (cargo vans + Class 3 heavy-duty pickups, GVWR 10,001-14,000 lb).
Eleven vehicles were driven at dummies on a track. Result: not a single Class 3 pickup
earned "Good" in either test. The F-350, Silverado 3500HD, and Sierra 3500 scored Poor
on vehicle-to-vehicle; the Ram 3500 only managed Marginal. On the pedestrian test,
F-350 and Ram 3500 got Acceptable, Silverado/Sierra 3500HD got Marginal. The Chevy
Express 2500 and GMC Savana 2500 (ancient platforms) scored Poor in BOTH, neither
offers AEB at all. Only three vehicles avoided Poor anywhere: Sprinter 2500 (Good
vehicle-to-vehicle), BrightDrop 400 and Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 (Good pedestrian).
IIHS's own line: the vehicles that pose the most danger to others get the least
regulation. Per IIHS citing NHTSA, 6,215 people died in 2024 in crashes involving
heavy/medium-duty vehicles or light vans = 16% of all U.S. traffic deaths. No
manufacturer had announced a fix as of Oct 12. This is the second chapter of IIHS's
commercial program (June 2026 driver-protection checklist covered by stories
iihs-heavy-truck-first-scorecard and iihs-first-commercial-test-regulatory-void);
this is the first time the trucks had to actually brake for the camera.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: first-ever test of this class (published 12 days ago), a real
regulatory gap (AEB mandates that apply to passenger cars do not apply here, and even
some airbag requirements don't), and a clean numbers story (11 vehicles, 2 tests,
nearly all poor/marginal). Novel angle: the biggest vehicles on the road are held to
the lowest standard, and now there's a scorecard proving it.

## Key data points
- 11 vehicles tested at IIHS Vehicle Research Center, Ruckersville, VA; ratings
  published Oct 8, 2026.
- Class 3 pickups (4): Ford F-350, Chevrolet Silverado 3500HD, GMC Sierra 3500HD, Ram 3500.
  Vehicle-to-vehicle: F-350/Silverado/Sierra = Poor; Ram = Marginal. Pedestrian:
  F-350/Ram = Acceptable; Silverado/Sierra = Marginal. NO Good ratings among pickups.
- Cargo vans: Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 2500 = Good (vehicle-to-vehicle, only one in group),
  Acceptable pedestrian. Chevy BrightDrop 400 + Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 = Good pedestrian.
  Chevrolet Express 2500 / GMC Savana 2500 = Poor both tests; neither offers AEB.
  Ford Transit T250: Poor vehicle-to-vehicle, Marginal pedestrian (warnings came 0.6-1.6s
  before impact; pre-collision assist failed on motorcycle tests).
- 6,215 deaths in 2024 in crashes involving heavy- or medium-duty vehicles or light vans
  = 16% of all U.S. roadway fatalities (IIHS, citing NHTSA).
- IIHS Pres. David Harkey: "a wake-up call for commercial vehicle makers. Front crash
  prevention is especially important for these larger, heavier vans and trucks because
  of the danger they pose to other road users."
- IIHS launched the commercial program in June 2026 (driver protection checklist:
  airbags, seat belts, reminders). This Oct release is the crash-avoidance chapter.
- Next: IIHS plans headlight evaluations, then Class 4-6 trucks.

## Actionable insight
Fleet buyers can now compare before they buy: Sprinter 2500 for vehicle-to-vehicle,
BrightDrop 400 / Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 for pedestrian protection. And if your
contractor's fleet runs Express/Savana vans or 3500-series pickups, know their AEB
is rated Poor to Marginal — treat following distance like the manufacturer did.

## Limitations / counterarguments
- These are track tests of IIHS's own protocol, not real-world crash outcomes; a
  Poor rating measures performance under the test, not confirmed on-road danger.
- Sample = 11 vehicles, selected by notoriety and insurance registrations; not the
  full commercial fleet.
- Class 3 pickups are a small share of registrations; most fatal heavy-vehicle
  crashes involve tractor-trailers, which IIHS hasn't scored yet.
- Good ratings exist in each category, so the technology is proven — the gap is
  deployment, not physics.
