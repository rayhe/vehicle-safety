# Research Notes — #992: VW Steering Screw Goes Global (4M Vehicles)

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — fatality rate investigations, vehicle-specific deep dives; deadpan dark humor)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**News peg:** Sept 25–26, 2026 — Volkswagen expands the steering rack mounting bolt recall globally: ~2.86M VW+Audi (AP) and ~4M across four brands incl. Skoda and Seat (Reuters via Handelsblatt/KBA). "Biggest such action since the Dieselgate scandal."

## The facts

- **US baseline:** NHTSA Campaign 26V590000, filed 09/11/2026 — 208,724 vehicles (2018 Tiguan 85,713; 2018–2019 Atlas 82,601; 2019–2021 Audi Q3 40,410). Component: STEERING. Failure chain: "One of the bolts that attaches the steering rack to the subframe may corrode and break... the steering rack housing can break... A broken steering rack housing can result in a loss of steering control." Remedy: replace right-side mounting bolt, free. VW recall nos. 48LG/48VT. Letters mailed Nov 10, 2026. Defect estimate ~1% (Dow Jones via Morningstar). VW: zero known crashes/injuries/deaths. (Captured from #953 research, primary: NHTSA recalls API fetched 2026-09-20; USA Today Sept 19, 2026)
- **Global expansion (NEW):** AP (Sept 25/26): 2.86M VW and Audi branded vehicles recalled as "a precautionary measure over a screw whose failure could cause a loss of steering." Models: VW Tiguan, Touran, Golf, Golf Variant, Caddy (built Sept 2013 – July 2024); Audi Q3 (Oct 2017 – May 2024). KBA: no known incidents with damage to persons or property. Cause: "certain environmental conditions and yearslong operation under the influence of road salt." Repair ≤1 hour. German recall code 48VS / KBA ref 17016R; ~895,832 VW + ~58,555 Audi Q3 in Germany alone (NDR via KBA).
- **4M figure:** Reuters/Handelsblatt (Sept 25): ~4M across four brands — 2.16M VW, 700,000 Audi, 1M+ Skoda, 200,000 Seat (KBA figures; Seat confirmed). "Biggest such action since the Dieselgate scandal." VW declined to comment on cost.
- **Timeline:** first field failure report to VW technicians Oct 15, 2024 → internal stress tests on corroded bolts completed Sept 10, 2026 → regulatory filings logged Sept 24, 2026 → public announcement Sept 25–26, 2026. ~23 months from first field report to recall.

## The novel angle (Rex's investigation framing)

**The US filing is 5% of the story.** NHTSA's 26V590000 covers 208,724 vehicles. The same screw now recalls ~4 million worldwide. The American recall is a regional footnote in a global corrosion epidemic. The regulatory asymmetry: Germany's KBA drove a four-brand, 4M-unit precautionary action while the US record lists a single 208k campaign — and VW says there have been zero known incidents with damage to persons or property (KBA). A maximum-severity failure mode (steering loss), nearly 2 years of internal study, and the company recalls 4M cars before a single documented casualty. That is either the precautionary principle at its finest or the most expensive corrosion experiment in automotive history.

Original contributions:
- **Scope ratio:** 4,000,000 ÷ 208,724 ≈ **19.2x** — the US filing is ~5.2% of the global action.
- **Brand split:** VW 2.16M (54%), Audi 700k (17.5%), Skoda ~1M (25%), Seat 200k (5%). Skoda — the value brand — carries a quarter of a steering-failure recall.
- **Age span:** affected production Sept 2013 – July 2024 = nearly 11 model years; oldest bolts corroding under road salt for ~13 years before the recall.
- **Latency:** ~23 months from first field report (Oct 2024) to recall (Sept 2026).
- **Defect extrapolation (labeled as such):** if the US ~1% defect estimate holds globally, ~40,000 bolts worldwide could fracture — 40,000 steering racks held by one remaining bolt. The estimate is Dow Jones's US figure; the global application is mine, stated as arithmetic, not fact.
- **Repair logistics:** 4M vehicles × ≤1 hour = 4M service hours minimum — the workshop throughput story.

## What the article must NOT claim

- Do NOT claim any crashes, injuries, or deaths — KBA and VW both state zero known incidents with damage to persons or property.
- Do NOT present the 4M figure as VW-confirmed — attribute to Handelsblatt/Reuters ("reported on Friday"); VW declined to comment on cost and did not confirm the total. AP's 2.86M is the VW-attributed number (VW Group announcement, VW+Audi only).
- Do NOT present the 1% defect rate as global — it is a US-campaign estimate; any global extrapolation must be labeled arithmetic.
- Do NOT re-cover #953's US-only angles (invisible bolt, 100:1 ratio, second-owner gap) beyond a passing acknowledgment — this article is the global followup.
