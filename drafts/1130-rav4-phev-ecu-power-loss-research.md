# Research: #1130 — Toyota Recalls 31,000 RAV4 Plug-in Hybrids: The ECU That Can Shut Down Your Car at Speed (and the Letter That Won't Arrive Until December)

## The facts
- **Recall filed:** October 7, 2026, by Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing (Plano, Texas).
- **Vehicles:** certain model year 2026 RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid vehicles. Approximately **31,000** in the U.S.; ~**7,846** in Canada (Toyota Canada notice, same day); **5,925** in Australia (official recall REC-006742, campaign 6GG85, published Oct 9, 2026; variants AXAP62R 2WD and AXAP64R 4WD).
- **Root cause (Australia notice, most precise):** a manufacturing issue with the **motor generator electronic control unit (MG-ECU)** — the motor initialisation process may not complete correctly. At higher speeds, the electric motor may not operate as intended.
- **Failure mode:** warning lights illuminate; the vehicle may enter fail-safe mode or shut down → **sudden loss of motive power**, specifically flagged by Toyota as occurring "while driving at higher speeds." Increased risk of a crash. No injuries reported in the filing; Toyota disclosed no count of actual power-loss incidents.
- **Remedy:** dealer software update to the affected ECU, free of charge. Not a hardware replacement.
- **Notification timeline:** U.S. owner letters expected by **early December 2026** — roughly eight weeks after the filing. VINs are searchable NOW at toyota.com/recall or nhtsa.gov/recalls. Toyota Brand Engagement Center: 1-800-331-4331.
- **NHTSA campaign number:** not yet published in press materials at filing time; owners check by VIN.
- **Market context:** the 2026 RAV4 is the first all-new generation in years, and the U.S.-spec lineup is now exclusively hybrid — Toyota hit an all-time-best 61% electrification mix in September 2026. Toyota shares fell 1.4% on the news.
- **Nameplate precedent:** 2021 RAV4 Prime recall — hard accelerator input during cold-weather EV-mode driving could shut down the hybrid system; 2025 MY recalled twice, including an instrument panel display issue. Same failure class (propulsion system shuts down), third distinct software/logic cause in five model years.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? America's best-selling crossover's newest electrified variant, 31,000 brand-new vehicles that can lose propulsion at highway speed. Yes.
- Novel? Zero RAV4 plug-in / PHEV coverage in 335 queue entries. Duplicate check: no "rav4", "plug-in", "plugin", "phev" slugs or titles anywhere in the queue.
- Novel contribution: (a) global three-market tally — 31,000 (US) + 7,846 (Canada) + 5,925 (Australia) = **44,771** vehicles across known markets; (b) the ~8-week notification gap as the actionable core — the fix exists and the VIN is searchable today, but Toyota's letters arrive in December; (c) the failure-class lineage: third distinct cause of RAV4 propulsion shutdown in five model years (2021 cold-weather Prime, this MG-ECU manufacturing issue) — Toyota keeps fixing the symptom in software while the failure class recurs.
- Counterargument at full strength: a fail-safe shutdown at speed typically leaves power steering and brakes functional (12V system), so the vehicle remains controllable and can coast to the shoulder — this is an inconvenience-grade failure for most scenarios, not a stuck-throttle; 31,000 is a modest population; software remedy is cheap and complete; Toyota's recall completion rates are among the industry's best.

## Limitations
- Toyota disclosed no incident count — we don't know whether zero or dozens of RAV4s have actually shut down. The 0.1%-style defect-rate estimates in other recalls are absent here.
- The NHTSA campaign number was not in press materials at filing; exact U.S. build-date window not published yet.
- We don't know whether the "manufacturing issue" is confined to one supplier lot or one plant; the software update suggests the ECU logic can't be made to tolerate the hardware variance.

## Actionable takeaway
- Own a 2026 RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or toyota.com/recall THIS WEEK — do not wait for the December letter. If the campaign is open on your VIN, book the dealer software update. If you're shopping one, check the VIN before signing.
- The regular 2026 RAV4 Hybrid is NOT in this recall — but note it shares the all-new platform; the defect is MG-ECU-specific to the plug-in's powertrain.
