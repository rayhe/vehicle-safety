Their Brakes Couldn’t Do Math. The Fix Was to Make Them Dumber.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 5, 2026

Inside the computers of 1,242 nearly new tanker trucks, a piece of software is doing arithmetic wrong. Mack is recalling 850 Anthem and Pioneer tractors (NHTSA 26V620) and Volvo is recalling 392 VNL and VNR tractors (26V619) because the tanker application package calculates axle loads inaccurately.[1] Wrong axle-load math means degraded braking performance while the trucks pull smoothbore tanker trailers: longer stops, more crash risk, exactly the failure mode you never want on a rig hauling thousands of gallons of sloshing liquid.[2]

6,379
Volvo Group trucks recalled for braking defects in 2026, across the same four model lines, twice in five months

Pause on the remedy, because it is the part that makes engineers wince. Dealers will install new software that disables the dynamic trailer braking calculations.[1] The official repair is not a repair at all: it permanently takes away the computer’s math homework. Somewhere a software team spent months building dynamic axle-load modeling into the braking stack, and the field fix was a kill switch. Smoothbore tankers are the trailers where the liquid cargo surges the most, which is why they got the smart braking in the first place, which means the feature was wrong precisely in the case it was built for.

This is not Volvo Group’s first braking recall of the year. In May 2026, Volvo recalled 2,800 VNL/VNR tractors and Mack recalled 2,337 Anthem/Pioneer tractors (26V260 and 26V261) because rear brake modulator electrical connectors could short-circuit and knock out ABS and electronic stability control entirely.[5][6] Same four model lines, and the same two brands are one company. Run the totals: 5,137 trucks in May plus 1,242 this week equals 6,379 braking-defect recalls in 2026, and some VINs very plausibly appear in both campaigns. Two brake recalls in five months is a pattern, not a coincidence.

And braking was not even the biggest recall of the week. Mack also recalled 3,096 Anthem and Pioneer tractors (26V631) because the intake and grille bezels may detach and become road hazards.[3] Add it up: 850 + 392 + 3,096 = 4,338 trucks recalled across the two brands this week. The defect where the truck sheds pieces of itself onto the highway outnumbered the braking defect 2.5 to 1. It was quite a week for the Volvo Group.

The counterargument, stated at full strength: disabling the dynamic calculation is not a lobotomy but the correct engineering response. If the dynamic trailer braking calculation was producing braking worse than the standard baseline, then every affected truck is safer with the feature off than it was with the feature on. Mocking the fix as “dumber” ignores that the alternative, leaving known-bad math in the loop on loaded tankers, is strictly worse. Nobody lost a capability that worked. They lost a capability that was actively hurting them, which is the one case where deleting software is the responsible fix.

What the public record does not tell us: how wrong the axle-load calculations were. No stopping-distance delta, no deceleration figures, no test data appear in the published summaries, so “reduced braking performance” is the entire technical description. No crashes, injuries, or field incidents are cited for this campaign, which means the defect was caught before the body count or before anyone counted publicly. And we cannot confirm VIN overlap between the May and October campaigns; same model lines and same brands make it plausible, but plausible is not proven.

What you do with this. If you run a fleet or drive a 2026–2027 Mack Anthem/Pioneer or a 2025–2027 Volvo VNL/VNR with the tanker application package, check your VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls; they became searchable October 5.[7] Owner letters mail November 27, the software update is free, and the reference numbers are Mack SC0500 (1-800-866-1177) and Volvo RVXX2610 (800-528-6586).[1] Tractors without the tanker package are not in this campaign, but run the May recalls (26V260/26V261) through your VIN list anyway, since they covered the same model lines. And the next time a recall remedy reads “we turned the feature off,” ask what the feature was doing before it was turned off, and for how long.
