# Research: JD Power Cup Holders vs. 37,810 Deaths

## Angle
The auto industry's most celebrated quality metric — JD Power Initial Quality Study — just had its best improvement since 1997. The single biggest contributor? Cup holders. Meanwhile, 37,810 people died on American roads in 2025. JD Power doesn't measure whether your car kills you. It measures whether your cup holder fits a Yeti.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? **Yes.** Nobody has cross-referenced JD Power's 10 quality categories against actual FARS safety data to show they measure completely different things. The cup holder factoid has been reported but not analyzed. The fact that automakers use JD Power rankings to MARKET "quality" — while the metric ignores mortality — is a novel consumer deception angle.

## Primary Sources

### 1. JD Power 2026 U.S. Initial Quality Study (June 25, 2026)
- Overall: 175 PP100 (down from 192 PP100 in 2025)
- Best year-over-year improvement since 1997
- **Cup holders were the "biggest single contributor to the year-over-year improvement in initial quality based on more accessible location and capacity to hold a variety of sizes of cups/water bottles"** (per Carscoops)
- Ford #1 among mass market brands at 152 PP100
- Porsche #1 overall at 138 PP100
- Methodology: 78,514 respondents, 227 questions, 10 categories, first 90 days of ownership
- 10 categories: infotainment, features/controls/displays, exterior, driving assistance, interior, seats, powertrain, driving experience, climate, unspecified (repair)
- Source: https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2026-us-initial-quality-study-pr/

### 2. NSC 2025 Fatality Estimate (Feb 24, 2026)
- 37,810 estimated motor vehicle deaths in 2025
- Down 12% from revised 42,789 in 2024
- Mileage death rate: 1.14 per 100M VMT (down 12.3%)
- Despite increase of 0.9% in miles driven
- Source: https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/overview/preliminary-estimates/

### 3. NHTSA H1 2025 Fatality Estimate
- 17,140 deaths Jan-Jun 2025
- Down 8.2% from 18,680 in same period 2024
- Fatality rate dropped to 1.06 per 100M VMT (lowest mid-year since 2014)
- Source: NHTSA press release

### 4. FARS Data (2014-2023)
- Death rates range from 0.12 (Mazda CX-5) to 8.54 (Hyundai Veloster)
- JD Power measures zero of these outcomes
- A car can score perfectly on JD Power IQS and be a deathtrap on FARS

### 5. Ford's Ad Campaign
- July 4, 2026: Ford launches "We put quality first. Now Ford is first in quality."
- Based on JD Power IQS #1 among mass market brands
- Same month: Ford recalled ~2 million vehicles
- 60 recalls in 2026, leading all manufacturers

## Original Contribution
JD Power's "driving assistance" category — the only one that could plausibly relate to safety — measures whether ADAS features ANNOY the driver (false alerts, too-sensitive lane departure), not whether they PREVENT DEATHS. The metric optimizes for "fewer complaints in 90 days," which means the safest ADAS system (one that aggressively warns and intervenes) would SCORE WORSE on JD Power because drivers complain about it.

This creates a perverse incentive: automakers tune ADAS to be LESS intrusive to score better on JD Power, which makes it LESS effective at preventing crashes.

## Strongest Counterargument
JD Power never claimed to measure safety. IQS measures customer satisfaction with initial ownership experience. It's a purchase-decision metric, not a mortality metric. Fair point. But:
1. Automakers use "quality" branding to imply safety ("We put quality first")
2. Consumers conflate "quality" with "safe"
3. The media reports JD Power rankings alongside safety ratings without distinguishing them
4. The single "driving assistance" category actively penalizes aggressive safety systems

## Limitations
- JD Power and FARS measure different things by design
- IQS has never claimed to predict crash outcomes
- The cup holder finding is a legitimate measure of user experience
- The perverse incentive argument (ADAS tuned for less intrusiveness) is an inference, not a proven causal claim
