## Core facts (from NHTSA ODI resume DP26010, via reporting)

- **What:** NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation opened defect petition file **DP26010** on **October 9, 2026**.
- **Subject line (NHTSA's own):** "Exceeds posted speed limits in active school zones."
- **Petition received:** September 23, 2026 (ODI petition number 11766676).
- **Vehicles:** Model-year 2026 Tesla Robotaxi vehicles — Model Y in robotaxi configuration and the Cybercab — with the Automated Driving System (ADS) engaged.
- **Population:** ~1,000 vehicles (NHTSA estimate).
- **Evidence:** Petitioner supplied 3 video links. NHTSA tallies 4 incidents total: 1 formal ODI complaint + 3 social-media reports included in the petition.
- **Harms recorded:** zero crashes or fires, zero injury incidents, zero fatalities.
- **Decision clock:** Under 49 CFR Part 552, NHTSA has 120 days from receipt to grant or deny the petition → deadline around **January 21, 2027**. A grant means a formal defect investigation; that can end in a recall.
- **NHTSA has not found a defect.** Tesla has not publicly addressed DP26010.
- **Context:** This is the third federal action aimed at Tesla's driverless fleet in roughly five weeks, after an audit query (AQ26002) and a Special Order over how the steering-wheel-free Cybercab was self-certified for FMVSS compliance (Tesla ordered to prove under oath; sworn responses were due; NHTSA has not made them public).
- **Tesla's wider NHTSA file:** PE opened Oct 2024 (~2.4M FSD vehicles, reduced-visibility response) → escalated to Engineering Analysis March 2026 (~3.2M vehicles); separate broader FSD investigation opened Oct 2025 (alleged traffic-law violations incl. red-light running). DP26010 is narrower: 1,000 robotaxis, one allegation type.
- **Fleet facts:** Tesla doesn't disclose robotaxi fleet size. Cybercab began commercial service in Austin Sept 3, 2026; Austin fleet past 300. Service cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, limited Bay Area. NHTSA's 1,000 figure may reflect Texas DMV certification filings rather than active-service vehicles.

## School-zone safety data (context)

- NHTSA consumer alert: 104 people died in school-transportation-related crashes in 2022. From 2013-2022, more than 1,000 people died in school-transportation-related crashes; 198 were children age 18 and younger. Of those children: 40% occupants of other vehicles, 38% pedestrians, 18% occupants of school transportation vehicles, 3% bicyclists, 1% other (skateboards/scooters/wheelchairs).
- Safe Kids Worldwide: at 15 mph, risk of pedestrian death ~5% and severe injury ~10%; at 35 mph, risk of death ~30% and significant injury ~64%.
- Police1/Jenoptik (Feb 2026): study of 25,000+ vehicles found over 70% of drivers speeding more than 20 mph over the posted limit during morning drop-offs and afternoon pickups in school zones. A pedestrian struck at 30 mph is twice as likely to be killed as one struck at 25 mph.
- Canadian Underwriter (citing NIH study): 50% to 90% of motorists drive above the speed limit in school zones.

## Original contribution for this article

1. **The petition-ladder explainer:** DP26010 is the bottom rung. Defect petition (private citizen asks NHTSA to look) → grant/deny (120 days) → Preliminary Evaluation → Engineering Analysis → recall request. NHTSA opened the file "to evaluate the allegations and determine whether to grant or deny the petition." This is procedural, not a finding — and that distinction is the story's spine.
2. **The exposure-window math:** Texas school zones are typically 15-20 mph, active roughly 2.5 hours per school day (morning arrival + afternoon dismissal) across ~180 school days → roughly 450 hours per year when the limit is live and flashing. A robotaxi fleet doing school-adjacent corridor service drives through active zones constantly. The petition alleges the failure mode (speeding while the zone is active), not a general speeding problem.
3. **Fleet-size reveal:** NHTSA's ~1,000-vehicle estimate is the first federal number for Tesla's robotaxi fleet. Community trackers counted a few dozen active cars in June 2026 — the fleet has scaled roughly an order of magnitude in ~4 months.
4. **Strongest counterargument (stated at full strength):** Four incidents, zero crashes, zero injuries, zero deaths. School-zone speeding is endemic among human drivers (70%+ speed 20+ over in one study; 50-90% exceed limits per NIH). The safety question is never "is the robotaxi perfect" — it's "is it better than the human baseline in the same zones." A denied petition is a plausible outcome. The petition deserves tracking, not a verdict.
5. **Limitations:** The videos aren't public; NHTSA's resume doesn't name cities, speeds, or dates. We don't know whether Tesla has responded. We don't know the grant rate of defect petitions. The 1,000 figure is an estimate.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- If you live in Austin, Dallas, Houston, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, or the Bay Area where Tesla robotaxis run: you can check NHTSA's ODI investigations page for updates and file your own complaint at nhtsa.gov — defect petitions can be filed by anyone, and this file exists because one person did.
- The decision deadline is ~January 21, 2027. Watch whether NHTSA grants or denies; a grant would escalate to a Preliminary Evaluation.
- School zones are time-gated: the limit is only live during arrival/dismissal windows. If your child walks to school, the most dangerous minutes are the same ones the petition is about — regardless of who's driving.
