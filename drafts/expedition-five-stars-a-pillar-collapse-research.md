# Research: Expedition Five Stars, A-Pillar Collapse

## Angle
NHTSA gives the Ford Expedition its only 5-star overall safety rating in the large SUV segment. IIHS says its structure collapsed in the small overlap test — "the sort of result we haven't seen in years." Two agencies, opposite conclusions. Which one should families trust?

## Key Findings

### IIHS Test Results (June 2024)
- **Expedition small overlap: Marginal** — steering column partially detached from instrument panel; A-pillar separated from rocker panel on BOTH sides; excessive footwell intrusion; high right-leg injury risk
- **Expedition moderate overlap (front-seat only): Good** — but driver-side curtain airbag FAILED to deploy
- **Expedition updated moderate overlap (with rear dummy): Marginal** — rear curtain airbag also failed to deploy; high chest injury risk from belt forces
- **Tahoe updated moderate overlap: Poor** — high head/neck injury risk; lap belt slid onto abdomen
- **Wagoneer: Only one to earn 2024 Top Safety Pick** (but not TSP+, due to rear-seat protection gaps)
- IIHS quote: "The structure collapsed significantly — the sort of result we haven't seen in years"
- Raul Arbelaez (IIHS VP Vehicle Research): "These discouraging results show that some popular vehicles still lag behind"

### NHTSA Rating
- Ford Expedition: 5-star overall — the ONLY vehicle in the large SUV segment to get it

### FARS Data (2014-2023)
- Expedition: 1,515 deaths, 2,641 fatal crashes, rate 2.31/100M VMT
- Tahoe: 2,592 deaths, 5,078 fatal crashes, rate 2.49/100M VMT
- Midsize SUVs with IIHS TSP+: Palisade 0.06, Telluride 0.04, CX-5 0.12 deaths/100M VMT
- The large SUVs have fatality rates 20-60x higher than the midsize SUVs that IIHS rates highest

### Original Contribution
Cross-referencing NHTSA 5-star ratings with IIHS structural failure results AND FARS fatality rates reveals that the vehicle NHTSA says is the safest in its class has a fatality rate 2.31 per 100M VMT — roughly 40x the Telluride's 0.04, a midsize SUV that IIHS rates TSP+. Rating methodology, not vehicle physics, drives the discrepancy.
