# Research: The Chevrolet S-10 — A Discontinued Truck Still Killing at 17x Its Replacement's Rate

## Angle
The Chevrolet S-10 was discontinued in 2004. Twenty years later, it still accumulated 1,427 deaths in the FARS database (2014-2023) at a rate of 4.83 per 100M VMT — that's 4.4x the pickup class average. Its replacement, the Colorado, kills at 0.28 per 100M VMT. That's a 17:1 ratio. The S-10 kills at SEVENTEEN TIMES the rate of the truck Chevy built to replace it.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? YES.
- 17x lethality gap between a truck and its direct replacement is extraordinary
- 75.5% per-crash lethality (1,427 deaths / 1,890 crashes) — 3 out of 4 S-10 crashes produce a corpse
- Impairment rate is 20.5% — BELOW the pickup class average. The drivers aren't the problem. The truck is.
- Nobody writes about the S-10 because it hasn't been sold in 22 years. It's invisible in the safety discourse.

## Original Finding
Cross-tabulating discontinued compact pickups reveals a clear safety technology cliff: pre-ESC-mandate compact pickups (S-10, Ranger, Sonoma) have 2x-5x the death rate of their replacements. The S-10-to-Colorado transition is the single most dramatic improvement: 17:1.

But here's what makes this genuinely novel: the S-10's impairment rate (20.5%) is nearly identical to the Colorado's (19.0%). Same type of driver. Same type of use case. The difference is entirely structural — ESC, side airbags, crumple zone engineering, rollover resistance.

## Limitations
- FARS fleet estimates use rough industry sales data, not actual registration counts. The S-10's estimated fleet of 218,750 may be overstated (many are off-road, junked, or unregistered), which would make the true rate even HIGHER.
- VMT estimates assume average annual miles; actual S-10 miles may differ (work trucks vs recreational).
- The S-10 fleet is aging out — the youngest are now 22 years old. Survivor bias: the ones still on the road may be disproportionately in rural areas with higher speed roads and longer response times.

## Strongest Counterargument
Fleet estimates for a 22-year-old truck are inherently unreliable. If the actual surviving S-10 fleet is 50% of the estimate, the per-VMT rate would be roughly halved — to about 2.4, still well above the class average but not the 4.83 headline. But even at 2.4, the S-10-to-Colorado improvement ratio would still be ~9:1, which is still extraordinary.
