## Key verified facts (from DOT HS 813 824)

- 6,228 motorcyclists killed in 2024, down 2.2% from 6,366 in 2023. 16% of all traffic fatalities; 20% of all motor vehicle occupant fatalities.
- An estimated 86,887 motorcyclists injured in 2024, UP 5.2% from 82,567 in 2023.
- Per 100M VMT, motorcyclist fatality rate 28.00 vs 1.05 for passenger car occupants (almost 27x) and 0.63 for light-truck occupants (over 44x).
- Motorcycles: 3% of registered vehicles, 0.7% of all VMT, 16% of all deaths.
- **The left turn:** In 2024 there were 3,396 two-vehicle fatal crashes involving a motorcycle and another vehicle. In 45% (1,532) of them, the other vehicle was turning left while the motorcycle was going straight, passing, or overtaking. Both vehicles going straight: 689 crashes (20%). Left-turn crashes outnumbered straight-through crashes 2.2 to 1.
- 78% of motorcycles in fatal two-vehicle crashes were impacted on the front. Only 6% on the rear.
- 60% of motorcycles in fatal crashes: most harmful event was collision with a motor vehicle in transport (3,790 of 6,366). 24% collided with fixed objects (vs 16% passenger cars, 12% light trucks, 5% large trucks).
- **Helmet-law natural experiment:** In states without universal helmet laws, 48% of motorcyclists killed (known helmet use) were not wearing helmets, vs 11% in states with universal helmet laws. Only 17 states + DC + PR require helmets for all riders. Reported helmet use among killed riders 67%, passengers 48%.
- **Licensing:** 35% of motorcycle riders in fatal crashes had no valid motorcycle license, vs 14% of passenger vehicle drivers in fatal crashes.
- **Speeding:** 37% of riders in fatal crashes were speeding (vs 22% passenger car drivers, 15% light trucks, 8% large trucks). Riders 15-20: 56% speeding, the highest of any age group.
- **Alcohol:** 26% of killed riders (1,529 of 5,950) were alcohol-impaired (BAC .08+). 40% of riders who died in single-vehicle crashes were impaired, vs 18% in multivehicle crashes. Riders killed at night were 2.6x more often impaired than those killed in daylight (37% vs 14%).
- **Young riders got deadlier while the total fell:** deaths under 15 rose 114% (14 to 30), ages 15-20 rose 20% (508 to 609), ages 21-24 rose 15% (613 to 705), even as total motorcyclist deaths fell 2.2%. Average age of a rider killed: 40.
- **Where:** 66% urban, 62% not at intersections, 56% daylight, 91% on non-interstate roads.
- **States:** Florida 642 deaths, Texas 583, California 522 (per secondary summary of the same NHTSA data; verify before use — cited via permit.bike summary of DOT HS 813 824).

## Original computations (not in the source)

- Left-turn share of ALL rider deaths: 1,532 fatal left-turn crashes against 6,228 total motorcyclist deaths. Treating one death per crash as the floor, left-turning cars account for at least 24.6% of every rider death in America — roughly 1 in 4.
- Left-turn vs straight-through: 1,532 / 689 = 2.22x. The car turning across the bike's path kills more than twice as often as both vehicles simply going straight.
- Helmet-law gap: 48% / 11% = 4.4x higher unhelmeted share among the dead in no-universal-law states.
- License gap: 35% / 14% = 2.5x more likely unlicensed than a car driver in a fatal crash.
- The Teoh 26% (2017-2021) vs NHTSA 2024 45% gap is a DEFINITION difference, not a trend: Teoh counted only "another vehicle turned left in front of an oncoming motorcycle," while NHTSA 2024 counts "other vehicle turning left while the motorcycle was going straight, passing, or overtaking." Both agree it is the single most frequent fatal two-vehicle configuration.

## Novel angle (kill test: PASS)

The motorcycle-safety conversation is always about the rider: helmets, speed, booze, licensing. The 2024 data says the single biggest killer in multi-vehicle crashes is the OTHER driver making a left turn across a bike going straight — 45% of fatal two-vehicle crashes, 2.2x the next configuration. The stereotype (reckless rider) is wrong in the plurality of cases where another vehicle is involved. Site coverage check: zero queue hits for left-turn / turning-left / helmet-law; #867 covered rider alcohol, #1213 covered the per-mile rate, #1426 covered FMVSS 127 motorcycle detection. Nobody on this site has ever written the left-turn story. The FMVSS 127 angle (AEB that cannot see motorcycles) is the natural policy hook: the car that turns left across a bike is exactly the car whose crash-prevention system was never tested against a motorcycle.

## Strongest counterargument (must state)

FARS records the maneuver, not legal fault. "Other vehicle turning left while the motorcycle went straight" does not prove the car driver was at fault in every one of the 1,532 crashes — a speeding bike, a stale yellow, or a rider running a red light can put the bike in the wrong even when the car is turning. Left turns are also simply the most common crossing maneuver, so part of the 45% is exposure, not unique lethality. And riders carry their own blame in the other slices: 37% speeding, 26% drunk, 35% unlicensed. The honest claim is narrower: in fatal two-vehicle crashes, the left-turning car is the most common configuration, and it is a car-driver perception failure more often than a rider error. Teoh's 26% (narrower definition) is the conservative floor for that claim.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- For car drivers: the left turn is the move that kills riders. Wait for the gap you can see, not the gap you hope for — a motorcycle's speed is systematically underestimated, and its headlight is a single point, not a car-width array.
- For riders: 78% of fatal two-vehicle impacts are frontal. The bike going straight through an intersection is the highest-risk geometry in the dataset; cover the brake and slow for every left-turning car, because the car driver is looking for a car-shaped gap.
- For buyers: no current FMVSS 127 test puts a motorcycle in front of a car's automatic emergency braking. Check IIHS front-crash-prevention ratings, but know the motorcycle test does not exist yet.
- Helmet law matters: if you ride in a state without a universal helmet law, the dead-rider unhelmeted rate is 4.4x higher. Wear one regardless of what the law says.

## Limitations (required)

- 2024 figures are from the FARS Annual Report File, subject to revision when the Final File lands.
- Injury figures (86,887) are CRSS estimates, not counts; sampling uncertainty of several percent.
- FARS records maneuver, not fault; the 45% is a configuration share, not a fault share.
- The Teoh 26% vs NHTSA 45% figures use different definitions and are not directly comparable as a trend.
- State death totals (FL 642, TX 583, CA 522) come from a secondary summary of the NHTSA fact sheet, not the fact sheet text itself; treat as directional unless verified against FARS state tables.
