# Research: Mass Shooting Ripple Effect on Traffic Fatalities

## Angle
A JAMA Psychiatry study (July 2026) found that fatal traffic crashes rise 14.3% nationwide the day after a major mass shooting — adding ~20 deaths per event. The extra road deaths from 10 shootings equaled about 75% of the shooting victims themselves. FARS data used as the primary source.

## Primary Sources

### 1. JAMA Psychiatry Study (July 2026)
- **Title:** "The Broader Impact of Mass Shootings on Traffic Fatalities"
- **Authors:** Vishal R. Patel et al
- **DOI:** 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.1912
- **Published:** JAMA Psychiatry, July 2026
- **Data:** FARS (Fatality Analysis Reporting System), 2008-2023
- **Method:** Quasi-experimental analysis, 10 deadliest mass shootings
- **Key findings:**
  - 14.3% increase in fatal traffic crashes the day after
  - ~20 additional deaths per event nationwide
  - 8 of 10 deadliest shootings showed the increase
  - Total extra traffic deaths = ~75% of shooting victims
  - Spike correlated with peak Google search interest
  - Effect is nationwide (not just near shooting site)
  - Consistent across all demographics: age, gender, urban/rural, weather
  - Comparison: day-after vs. average of 10 days before each event

### 2. FARS Database
- NHTSA FARS records every fatal motor vehicle crash on US public roads
- Data available at https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/fatality-analysis-reporting-system-fars

### 3. Context
- ~100+ mass shootings per year in the US
- ~2/3 of Americans say mass shootings are a major source of stress
- ~7% of Americans have been at the scene of a shooting
- Psychology research: exposure to shocking news → attention locked → harder to process other details → critical while driving
- Modern media amplifies exposure: live updates, phone alerts, social media

## Novel Contribution
Cross-tabulation of FARS fatality data with mass shooting event dates. Nobody in vehicle safety has systematically linked these datasets before. The finding that the road toll from distraction approaches the direct toll of the shooting itself is genuinely surprising.

## Limitations to Acknowledge
- Quasi-experimental, not randomized — could be confounded by other factors
- Only 10 events (the deadliest) — small sample size for statistical inference
- Correlation with Google Trends suggests distraction mechanism but doesn't prove it
- FARS only captures fatal crashes, not the larger universe of injury/property-damage crashes
- Can't distinguish whether drivers were actively reading news, or just emotionally disrupted
- Cultural events besides the shooting may affect a specific day

## Strongest Counterargument
Correlation ≠ causation. The 10 deadliest mass shootings occurred on specific dates that might coincide with other patterns (day of week, holiday weekends, seasonal effects). The study controls for this by comparing to the 10-day pre-event baseline, but 10 events is a small n. A skeptic would note that 2 of 10 events showed no increase, and the 14.3% average could be driven by a few outlier days.

## Actionable Insights
- After a major mass shooting, awareness campaigns could warn about distraction risk
- This quantifies a hidden cost of mass violence that policymakers can use
- For individual drivers: be conscious of your distraction state after absorbing traumatic news
- For NHTSA/safety advocates: this is another data point for phone-use-while-driving interventions
