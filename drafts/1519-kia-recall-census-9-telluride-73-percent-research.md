## Story in one line
Kia filed nine NHTSA recall campaigns in 2026 covering 775,093 campaign-vehicles, and the Telluride alone accounts for 568,715 of them, 73.4 percent, across five separate campaigns, four of which are about the seat or the belt that is supposed to hold you in it.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** YES. The census corrects the site's own prior count: #1477 (Nov 12) put Kia at 4 campaigns using the 2026-model-year method. The all-model-years census finds 9, more than double, because five campaigns touch older models the model-year filter excluded, including the year's biggest number (462,869 Tellurides, a re-recall of a 2024 fix that did not hold) and a 568-car campaign that shares a defect family with the Hyundai Palisade power-seat recall tied to a child's death. The newest campaign (26V545, EV9 airbag sensor, 21,290) was filed August 22, 2026, and owner letters went out October 20, less than a month ago.
- **Novel angle on data?** YES. The tally itself (775,093) with Kia-only counts; the Telluride share (73.4%, five campaigns, one nameplate); the restraint-system thesis (six of nine campaigns, 76.3% of the year's vehicles, are about the seat, the belt, or the airbag sensor); the 1%-estimate pattern (four campaigns carry Kia's 1% defect estimate, putting roughly 8,810 actually-defective vehicles inside 775,093 recalled ones); the re-recall correction (26V430 replaces 24V407, so eight campaigns are truly new); and the Samsong Mexico thread (one supplier, two belt failures, three months apart, same new Telluride).
- **3+ primary sources?** YES. (1) NHTSA recalls API, api.nhtsa.gov, queried live this run: 14 models x 9 model years + 15 suffix/variant names x 4 years + 18 models x 4 pre-2019 years, ~270 queries, raw results saved at drafts/1519-kia-recall-census-raw.json; (2) NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Reports 26V105 (RCLRPT-26V105-2893.pdf, 85,515), 26V173 (RCLRPT-26V173-8909.pdf, 568), 26V046 (RCMN-26V046-8034.pdf, 42,677, via #1477); (3) NHTSA acknowledgement letters RCAK-26V545-9713.pdf (21,290) and RCAK-26V105-0484.pdf (85,448 original, amended to 85,515); (4) MotorTrend, Jul 10, 2026 (26V430: 462,869, 18 incidents, 7 fires); (5) Autoblog, Mar 14 + Mar 26, 2026 (26V135: 14,870; 26V173: 568, Palisade link); (6) Autoblog, Apr 19, 2026 (26V232: 141,032 Carnival + 94,760 Genesis twin); (7) The Drive, Mar 24, 2026 (26V173 stop-sale); (8) Electrek, Aug 28, 2026 (26V545: 21,290, 6% estimate); (9) AutoEvolution + OEMDTC, Jul 2026 (26V431: 8 vehicles, SK On, twin Hyundai 26V432).

## Key verified facts
- **The nine 2026 campaigns (Kia counts, all model years):**
  - **26V046 (Kia SC361), Jan 27, 2026 — 42,677.** 2025 K4, 2025-2026 Sorento/Sorento Hybrid/Sorento PHEV, 2026 Sportage/Sportage Hybrid/Sportage PHEV, K5, EV9, Carnival/Carnival Hybrid. Instrument panel display may fail from a software error (same PMIC defect family as Hyundai's 26V047); FMVSS 101 and 138 noncompliance. Remedy: OTA or dealer software update. Owner letters Mar 26, 2026.
  - **26V105 (Kia SC362), Feb 24, 2026 — 85,515.** 2025 Telluride with power front seats. Front seatback recliner improperly manufactured (DAS North America); seatback may not restrain the occupant in certain rear impacts. 1% defect estimate (~855 vehicles). No accidents, injuries, or fatalities. Remedy: inspect and replace seatback frame assemblies. Owner letters Apr 24, 2026.
  - **26V135 (Kia SC364), Mar 7, 2026 — 14,870.** 2027 Telluride (13,499) + 2026 K4 Hatchback (1,371). Rear center / third-row center seat belt anchor buckle may not latch (supplier Samsong Mexico). No improper latching found in field data; no accidents, injuries, or fatalities. Remedy: inspect and replace anchor buckle assembly. Owner letters May 5, 2026.
  - **26V173 (Kia SC366), Mar 20, 2026 — 568.** 2027 Telluride Hybrid SX Prestige / X-Line SX Prestige with Executive Package. Second-row power seats may fail to detect a person and keep moving during one-touch tilt-and-slide or power fold-and-stow (supplier Nidec Mobility Korea). Same defect family as Hyundai Palisade 26V160, which is tied to the death of a two-year-old in Ohio on Mar 7, 2026. Stop-sale on the two trims. Remedy: control-unit software update, OTA or dealer. Owner letters Apr 20, 2026.
  - **26V232 (Kia SC368), Apr 11, 2026 — 141,032.** 2022-2026 Carnival. Crossover fuel pipe nuts not torqued to specification (supplier Usui International Korea); fuel may leak at the pipe-to-fuel-rail connection. 1% defect estimate (~1,410 vehicles). Twin Genesis campaign covers 94,760 vehicles (235,792 combined). No fires, crashes, or injuries reported. Remedy: inspect and tighten or replace the fuel pipe. Owner letters Jun 2, 2026.
  - **26V356 (Kia SC372), Jun 3, 2026 — 6,264.** 2027 Telluride and Telluride Hybrid (4,367 hybrid + 1,897 gas), built Mar 24 - May 12, 2026. Driver seat belt emergency locking retractor may lock from an incorrect vehicle sensor (supplier Samsong Mexico), so the strap will not extend; FMVSS 209 noncompliance. ~1% defect estimate (~63 vehicles). Remedy: replace the driver seat belt assembly (about 1 hour). Owner letters Jul 31, 2026.
  - **26V430 (Kia SC374), Jul 2, 2026 — 462,869.** 2020-2024 Telluride, built Jan 9, 2019 - May 29, 2024. Front power seat motor may overheat from a stuck slide knob or an improper 24V407 repair; fire risk while parked or driving. 1% defect estimate (~4,629 vehicles). 18 incidents (7 localized seat/under-seat fires, 11 melted motors), no injuries, crashes, or fatalities. Park outside. Remedy: install an electronic fuse assembly. Owner letters Aug 13, 2026. **Replaces 24V407 (June 2024, same 462,869 vehicles).**
  - **26V431 (Kia SC375), Jul 3, 2026 — 8.** 2022-2024 EV6 (7) + 2024 EV9 (1). High-voltage battery cells may contain misaligned electrodes (supplier SK On); internal short circuit, fire risk while parked or driving. Park outside and limit charging to 80% until repair. Dealers replace the battery system assembly. Zero fires recorded by Kia. Twin Hyundai campaign 26V432 covers 6 Ioniq 5s. Owner letters Aug 7, 2026.
  - **26V545 (Kia SC379), Aug 22, 2026 — 21,290.** 2025-2026 EV9, Georgia-built only (May 17, 2024 - May 13, 2026). Occupant Detection System mat bladder tube improperly routed under the front passenger seat; the airbag may not deactivate for a child or child seat; FMVSS 208 noncompliance. ~6% defect estimate (~1,277 vehicles). Korean-built EV9s excluded. Owner letters from Oct 20, 2026.

This counts recall actions, not unique vehicles; overlap is minimal (different model years and build windows: 26V135 covers 2027 Tellurides built Jan 2 - Feb 19, 2026; 26V356 covers 2027 Tellurides built Mar 24 - May 12, 2026; 26V173 covers Telluride Hybrids built Feb 2 - Mar 13, 2026; 26V046 covers 2025 K4 while 26V135 covers 2026 K4 Hatchback).
- **Shares:** 26V430 462,869 = 59.7%; 26V232 141,032 = 18.2%; 26V105 85,515 = 11.0%; 26V046 42,677 = 5.5%; 26V545 21,290 = 2.7%; 26V135 14,870 = 1.9%; 26V356 6,264 = 0.8%; 26V173 568 = 0.07%; 26V431 8 = 0.001%.
- **The Telluride is 73.4% of Kia's year.** Five campaigns touch the Telluride: 26V105 (85,515), 26V135 (13,499 of 14,870), 26V173 (568), 26V356 (6,264), 26V430 (462,869). Telluride total: **568,715 of 775,093**. The brand-new second-generation 2027 Telluride alone accounts for three campaigns and 20,331 vehicles (13,499 + 568 + 6,264) before most owners' first oil change.
- **Six of nine campaigns are about the restraint system.** 26V105 (seatback), 26V135 (belt anchor), 26V173 (power seats), 26V356 (belt retractor), 26V430 (seat motor), 26V545 (airbag sensor): 591,376 campaign-vehicles, **76.3% of the year's total**. The seat you sit in, the belt that holds you in it, and the sensor that decides whether the airbag fires: Kia recalled all three, repeatedly, in one year.
- **Three fire risks, two park-outside advisories.** 26V232 (fuel leak), 26V430 (seat motor), 26V431 (battery) are all fire risks. 26V430 and 26V431 carry park-outside advisories. The NHTSA API records parkOutSide=false for 26V232 and 26V545 despite two secondary sources claiming otherwise; this article follows the API.
- **The 1% pattern.** Four campaigns carry Kia's 1% defect estimate (26V105, 26V232, 26V356, 26V430). Estimated actually-defective vehicles across the year, excluding the cluster software (26V046, rate not published) and 26V135 (rate not published): ~855 + ~1,410 + ~63 + ~4,629 + ~1,277 (26V545 at 6%) + 568 (26V173, all units affected per Autoblog) + 8 (26V431, all 8 suspect) = **~8,810**. Roughly 8,810 defective vehicles hide inside 775,093 recalled ones.

## Original contribution (Dale's math and synthesis)
1. **The 9-campaign census with corrected vocabulary.** 775,093 campaign-vehicles, found by querying 14 models + 15 suffix/variant names + pre-2019 model years (~270 API queries). 26V173 hides under "Telluride Hybrid" and never appears in base-name queries; a base-name-only census prints 8. No prior article has summed Kia's 2026 campaigns.
2. **The 4-to-9 correction.** #1477's 2026-model-year Kia count of 4 missed five campaigns, including the year's biggest number. The model-year filter is a reasonable method, but it undercounts a brand whose recall year lives in older metal.
3. **The Telluride share.** One nameplate, five campaigns, 568,715 vehicles, 73.4% of the brand's year. The 2027 Telluride alone: three campaigns, 20,331 vehicles, all about the seat or the belt.
4. **The restraint-system thesis.** Six of nine campaigns and 76.3% of the year's vehicles are about the seat, the belt, or the airbag sensor. Kia's 2026 is not a random defect scatter; it is a restraint-system year.
5. **The 1%-estimate arithmetic.** Roughly 8,810 actually-defective vehicles inside 775,093 recalled ones, with the estimates stated as Kia's own filed figures.6. **The re-recall correction.** One of the nine "2026 recalls" is not a new defect: 26V430 is the second recall of the same 462,869 Tellurides for the same seat-motor fire risk, after the 2024 fix did not hold. The truly-new-2026 count is eight campaigns, and the year's biggest number is a two-year-old problem.
7. **The Samsong Mexico thread.** One supplier, two belt failures, three months apart, on the same new Telluride: the anchor buckle that will not latch (26V135, March) and the retractor that locks the strap (26V356, June).
8. **The Palisade link.** 26V173 is the same defect family as Hyundai's Palisade power-seat recall, which is tied to a child's death. Kia's version is 568 cars with no incidents known, but it is the only campaign in Kia's year that shares DNA with a fatality.

## Limitations
- Campaign populations are recall actions, not unique vehicles; the tally counts a vehicle twice if it sits in two campaigns. Overlap is minimal here (different model years and non-overlapping build windows), but the article must still say the tally counts campaign-populations.
- The 1% and 6% defect estimates are Kia's own filed figures, unverified by NHTSA. The 18 incidents for 26V430 (7 fires, 11 melted motors) are Kia's field-data count, not an estimate of per-vehicle probability.
- 26V046's defect-rate estimate and 26V135's were not published in the sources consulted; do not invent them. 26V046's defect is a software error present in every recalled vehicle's cluster code, but the failure is intermittent, so no per-vehicle rate is stated.
- The Palisade fatality belongs to Hyundai's campaign 26V160, not to any Kia campaign. 26V173 shares the defect family; it does not share the fatality. The article must keep that line bright.
- The NHTSA API returns HTTP 400 for make+model+year combinations with no matching rows; treated as zero after cross-checking against press and Part 573 filings. A campaign could still hide under an untested model spelling (the 26V173 catch proves the risk is real); the direction of any remaining bias is undercount.
- No FARS model-level data exists for 2026 Kias; this is a recall census, not a fatality-rate story. Do not import Telluride fatality rates as if they applied to these vehicles.
- The census counts campaigns filed Jan 1 - Nov 15, 2026. Late filings will move the count.

## Strongest counterargument
Strip the re-recall and the year collapses: 26V430's 462,869 is the same population as 24V407 from 2024, so Kia's genuinely-new 2026 total is 312,224 across eight campaigns, and the Telluride share drops to 105,846 of 312,224, about a third. Four campaigns estimate that roughly 1% of the recalled vehicles actually carry the defect, so the real defective population is around 8,810 cars, not 775,093. The 8-car battery recall is a rounding error with a park-outside advisory attached to it. No deaths or injuries are tied to any of the nine campaigns in the filings; the Palisade death is Hyundai's, and Kia's version of that defect has zero incidents known. Recalls are the safety system working: Kia found these, filed them, and pays for the repairs, and two of the nine are software updates with no parts to replace. A reader who walks away thinking Kia builds dangerous cars has misread the data; the tally is big, the danger per vehicle is small, and every fix is free at the dealer. That said, a seat-motor fix that fails and has to be recalled again is not the system working, and a brand-new Telluride recalled three times for its seats and belts before its first oil change is not a rounding error either. Both of those sentences are true at the same time.

## Actionable guidance (required)
- If you own a 2020-2024 Telluride: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V430 (Kia SC374) and park outside and away from structures until the electronic fuse assembly is installed. If you already had the 2024 fix (24V407), you are in this one too; the old fix is the reason for the new recall.
- If you own a 2025 Telluride with power front seats: check for 26V105 (Kia SC362). A loose seatback or a grinding noise when you recline is the warning sign.
- If you own a 2027 Telluride or Telluride Hybrid: check for all three — 26V135 (Kia SC364, third-row center belt anchor), 26V356 (Kia SC372, driver belt retractor), 26V173 (Kia SC366, second-row power seats, hybrid SX Prestige trims only). Pull the driver's belt all the way out before your next drive; if it locks and will not extend, do not drive with it unbuckled, call the dealer.
- If you own a 2022-2026 Carnival: check for 26V232 (Kia SC368). A fuel smell inside or outside the van, or a visible leak under the hood, is the warning sign; the fix is a free inspection and re-torque or pipe replacement.
- If you own a 2025-2026 EV9 built in Georgia: check for 26V545 (Kia SC379). Do not put a child or child seat in the front passenger seat until the ODS mat is inspected; the airbag may not deactivate.
- If you own a 2022-2024 EV6 or a 2024 EV9: check for 26V431 (Kia SC375). Only 8 vehicles are in it, but if yours is one, park outside and cap charging at 80% until the battery assembly is replaced.
- If you own a 2025 K4, 2025-2026 Sorento, 2026 Sportage, K5, EV9, or Carnival: check for 26V046 (Kia SC361). The cluster software update is free and can arrive over the air; if the screen blanks, the car still drives, so pull over rather than guessing your speed.
- Buying a used Kia: confirm every open recall was completed and ask for the service record. A Telluride that had the 2024 seat-motor fix still needs the 2026 one.
