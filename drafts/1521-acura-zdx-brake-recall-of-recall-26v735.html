Acura Fixed the ZDX’s Brakes in 2024. Now It’s Recalling 3.5 Times as Many Cars for the Same Failure.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 15, 2026

Acura recalled 4,174 ZDX electric SUVs in August 2024 because the brakes could quit below 25 mph. On November 5, it filed the same recall again, same defect, same software fix, same warning about brakes going inoperative, except this time the population is 14,648 cars.[1] That is 3.5 times the first recall, and roughly three-quarters of every ZDX Acura ever built.

14,648
2024 ZDX all-wheel-drive SUVs in recall 26V735, about 77 percent of every ZDX Acura ever sold

Let’s talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds. Below 25 mph, on dry pavement, the ZDX’s electronic brake control module gets jumpy. Its anti-lock logic fires when nothing is slipping, and once it fires, a specific sequence of wheel movements keeps it dumping pressure instead of modulating it. Acura’s own filing says the service brakes can become inoperative.[2] Not degraded, not spongy, but inoperative at parking-lot speed, turning into a driveway, exactly where a driver trusts the pedal most.

GM built this car; Acura only badged it. ZDXs roll out of GM’s Spring Hill, Tennessee plant on GM’s Ultium platform, and the brake family is GM’s too. So watch the calendar: GM told NHTSA about the Lyriq brake defect on August 8, 2024, and Acura filed its ZDX recall seven days later. GM filed its 67,380-car brake recall on October 29, 2026, and Acura filed this one seven days later, same lag, same defect family, two years apart.[3] When the company that built your car finds a brake problem, your recall arrives the following week.

Federal investigators are watching a much bigger population. NHTSA’s engineering analysis EA26006 covers an estimated 1,164,820 GM and Honda vehicles with the same eBoost brake system, including the ZDX and its Honda twin, the Prologue. As of August, the file held 745 reports, 21 crashes or fires, and six injuries.[5] GM’s October recall and Acura’s November recall together cover 82,028 of those vehicles. That leaves 1,082,792 still sitting in the investigation with no recall at all. Prologue owners should read that number twice: their car shares the ZDX’s plant, platform, and brake system, and it has no brake-pressure recall yet.

Acura killed the ZDX in September 2025 after eighteen months and 18,911 sales.[7] No 2025 model, no 2026 model, and no future. Its biggest recall is arriving after its funeral, with owner letters going out December 14 to people who bought a discontinued car with a brake defect that needed two recalls to scope. Every ZDX on the road is now a used car, and three-quarters of them are in this campaign.

Credit where it is due: nobody has reported a crash or injury in a ZDX over this defect, and the fix is a free software update that arrives over the air. A recall that expands is not automatically a recall that failed. But the defect description in the November filing is word-for-word the same as the August 2024 filing, and the remedy is word-for-word the same too. Acura scoped the first recall to the cars it had heard about. Two years of field data said the software lived in three and a half times as many cars. That is not a rounding error, but a manufacturer guessing at the size of its own problem and guessing low.

What to do: own a 2024 ZDX with all-wheel drive? Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and look for campaign 26V735, Acura number CQC. Already took the 2024 fix? Check again, since the new campaign expands the old one, and a car fixed under UJE can still be in CQC. Own a Honda Prologue? No brake recall covers it yet, so check the VIN monthly until EA26006 closes. Shopping a used ZDX? Six recalls touch the 2024 model, and this is the biggest. Verify all six are closed on the actual VIN before money changes hands.

What this does not prove: the new campaign’s complaint count is not public yet, since NHTSA’s Part 573 filing for 26V735 had not surfaced at publication time, so do not quote a crash or injury number for it. GM’s recall and Acura’s recall are cousins, not twins: GM’s is a brake system control module slip-calculation error, Acura’s is an electronic brake control module ABS-activation error. And the 77 percent figure uses sales through August 2025, just before production ended, so treat it as roughly three-quarters, not an exact census.
