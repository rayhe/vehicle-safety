# Research: Washington's Drunk-Driving Death Jump + Untested Blood Tests
**Article #1023 | Journalist: Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk) | Kicker: Sobriety Report**

## Angle
Washington posted the nation's 4th-steepest rise in alcohol-impaired road deaths over 2015-2024 (+46.28%, 2.06 to 3.01 per 100K residents) while simultaneously: (1) letting its DUI blood-test backlog hit 16,700 cases at end of 2025, (2) watching 59% of court-ordered ignition-interlock drivers never install the device, and (3) killing the year's main legislative fix (SB 5067, 0.05 BAC limit) in a House committee. The state has the data showing the crisis, the tools proven to reduce it, and a legislature that declined to use either.

## Kill test verdict: PROCEED
- Genuinely newsworthy: fresh Sep 23, 2026 Seattle Red synthesis of the Lantana Recovery "Alcohol Across America" study (2015-2024 FARS state rankings) + Jan/Feb 2026 legislative outcome + Jan 2026 JLARC audit.
- Novel: not just "deaths up" - the collision of rising deaths with unused enforcement tools (untested blood, uninstalled interlocks, stalled bill). No site article covers the Washington state-impaired-driving policy failure; #782 covered lab backlogs nationally without the WA rate jump.
- Original contribution: enforcement-gap arithmetic - 66,000 drivers x 59% noncompliance = ~38,940 unmonitored DUI offenders; WTSC evaluation shows interlocks cut repeat-offender recidivism 26-28%; devices catch active impairment (avg start-failure BAC 0.09, above legal limit) - the proof-of-drunkenness tool sits in a warehouse while the state argues about lowering the limit by 0.03.

## Limitations (for the article)
- Lantana Recovery is a for-profit addiction treatment company; its "Alcohol Across America" study is marketing-adjacent. Seattle Red reviewed methodology and it rests on NHTSA FARS data, but the framing serves a treatment company's interests.
- State rankings carry NHTSA's own caution: testing rates vary 10x; imputed values mean the 46.28% jump could partly reflect changes in testing/imputation, not just behavior.
- WTSC 2025 numbers are preliminary with dozens of lab tests pending - the "improvement" may revise upward.
- Lovick's "7x more likely" and "100 more Washingtonians" are sponsor claims, not peer-reviewed findings; attribute, don't assert.
- IID recidivism figures are from a 2012 WTSC evaluation; 41%/59% compliance from JLARC 2026 audit.

## Strongest counterargument
2025 preliminary data actually shows improvement: total deaths down to 659 (lowest in years), impaired-involved deaths down to 297. The state authorized private labs, funded the audit, and the House chair pledged to revisit 0.05 in 2027. One could argue Washington is already responding and the decade-trend ranking is a lagging indicator. Also: lowering the limit targets 0.05-0.08 drivers (light drinkers) while the untested-backlog/uninstalled-interlock problem involves the heaviest offenders - the bill and the enforcement gap address different populations.
