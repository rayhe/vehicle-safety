## Story in one line
Zero recall campaigns were filed November 21–27, 2026, so the month closes exactly where #1591 left it: 38 campaigns, 2,225,873 vehicles, 20 touching a 2026 model, 202,010 vehicles, 9.1 percent. The recount run a day later moved nothing, which is itself the news: the first census in the series where the late-publication correction is zero. Meanwhile every recall headline published in the final week was about a filing from an earlier week, including a Toyota RAV4 plug-in hybrid recall announced November 24 that was actually filed October 7.

### The recount that moved nothing
- #1591 (run Nov 26) counted Nov 1–20 as 38 campaigns, 2,225,873 vehicles. This run (Nov 27) re-ran the full-month enumeration from scratch and found the same 8 2026-touching campaigns the enumeration can see, and the sweep confirms the same 38-campaign universe with the same newest campaign (26V766). **No late publication added a campaign to Nov 1–20 overnight.**
- Contrast with the last recount: #1591's re-run of Nov 1–12 added 12 campaigns and 471,509 vehicles (+55 percent) to #1528's count. This time the correction is zero. The publication lag that bit #1528 did not bite #1591, at least not in the first 24 hours.
- The enumeration's known blind spots are unchanged and recorded, not hidden: it found 8 of the 20 2026-touching campaigns. The other 12 come from the sweep — Spencer (26V729), Lamar (26V734), Utilimaster (26V739), Altec (26V738, 26V752, 26V753), Rosenbauer (26V740), New Flyer (26V741), Indian (26V742), Winnebago (26V745, VPIC/query timeouts again this run), Gillig (26V755, VPIC timeout), and BMW i5 (26V731, model-key spelling gap). - Method audit, this run: 571 timeout/failure lines across 3,978 queries (VPIC lookups and recalls-API queries, concentrated in the RV/bus/fire-apparatus tail: winnebago, thor motor coach, tiffin, western star, thomas built, peterbilt, pierce, keystone, heartland). ### The announcement trap, quantified
- Vehicle Service Pros published its "NHTSA Recalls Nov. 23–27" roundup on Nov 27. Its five campaigns: 26V765 (filed Nov 19), 26V756 (Nov 19), 26V726 (Nov 2), 26V761 (Nov 19), 26V757 (Nov 19). **Every campaign in the week's recall news was filed in an earlier week.** The roundup's date range is the publication window, not the filing window, and the two are 8 to 25 days apart.
- MotorSafety announced the Toyota RAV4 PHEV recall (26V659, 30,978 vehicles) on Nov 24. The NHTSA API's ReportReceivedDate is Oct 7. **The week's most-covered "new" recall is 48 days old as a filing.** It is an October campaign by the census's definition, and it was already covered standalone three times (#1130, #1166, #1233) under earlier campaign numbers for the same defect family.
- Autoblog's Genesis GV80 story ran Nov 27, 7:00 AM EST, for 26V766 — filed Nov 19 (mirror) / Nov 20 (API). Three of the five VSP campaigns were also announced in prior weeks' coverage. The final week's recall news was a re-run of the third week's filings.

## Limitations / counterarguments (must state at full strength)
- The census window is filings as published, not filings as filed: a campaign filed Nov 24 and published Nov 28 is invisible to this census, exactly as Nov 1–12 filings were invisible to #1528. The zero for Nov 21–27 is a statement about the published record through Nov 27, not a claim that no manufacturer reported a defect that week. If a Nov 21–27 filing surfaces in December, this finale's zero is wrong in the same way #1528's 855,544 was wrong, and the correction belongs in a December piece.
- The Thanksgiving reading is circumstantial: Nov 21–27 is Thanksgiving week, but the census cannot prove the holiday caused the gap. NHTSA publishes a few days after manufacturers report, so a filing drought and a publication drought look identical in this data, and both could reflect the holiday, or neither could. The 7-day gap is a fact; the cause is an inference.
- Campaign populations are full-campaign totals; the 2026-only slice of mixed-year campaigns is not published, so 202,010 overstates the number of 2026 vehicles. The 9.1 percent is an upper bound, not the share itself.
- The recount-moved-nothing finding covers 24 hours. #1591's correction took 10 days to materialize (Nov 16 to Nov 26). A zero correction after one day is weaker evidence than a zero correction after ten days, and the article says so.
- Zero crashes/injuries is claimed only where the filings state it; most Part 573 reports do not state crash/injury counts, and the article does not invent them.
- The "announcement trap" framing counts filings, not announcements, by design. A reader who tracks announcements will see a different month, and for owners the announcement date is what matters: the letter arrives when it arrives, not when the manufacturer filed.
