# 7-Critic Panel — Article #762

## Article: "NHTSA Gave the Expedition Five Stars. IIHS Watched Its A-Pillar Rip Off."
## Journalist: Mia Crumplezone

### 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
Strong lede that sets up the paradox in two sentences. Clean flow from evidence through counterargument to actionable guidance. Pull stat ("5 ★ vs. Marginal") is immediately compelling. Headline is outstanding — two agencies, two verdicts, one vehicle. Minor: article runs long at ~630 words vs 300-500 target, but density justifies the length. No fat.

### 2. Voice Coach — 8.5/10
"Different gauntlet" is Mia's technical vocabulary. "Ford did not solve it for the vehicle it charges the most for" has her judgmental edge about bad design. The "All fair. But" pivot is clean. Could use one more moment of Mia's signature excitement about structural engineering specifics. The counterargument paragraph sounds slightly more editorial-board than crumple-zone nerd. Passing.

### 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
Ford's counterargument presented in full and acknowledged as fair before rebuttal. Body-on-frame structural demands acknowledged. FARS limitations stated in disclaimer (fatal crashes only, VMT uncertainty ±15%). No cherry-picking — the article includes the Wagoneer's better performance. Names multiple comparison vehicles fairly. Only omission: no mention that the Expedition's test vehicle model year could differ from current production.

### 4. Social/Shareability — 9.5/10
Headline is a top-tier share driver. The paradox format ("one agency says X, the other says the opposite") is inherently viral. Pull stat is immediately scannable. The "Not Marginal but Poor" line on children's safety hits an emotional note without being manipulative. Strong candidate for auto/parenting social circles.

### 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
All crash test results properly attributed to IIHS and Consumer Reports. Ford's response is in quotes and sourced. FARS data properly cited to NHTSA. "Over 90 percent" claim attributed to source. No risk of defamation — reporting public test results and government data. No false implications about intent.

### 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Original contribution clear: cross-referencing NHTSA 5-star with IIHS structural failure AND FARS fatality rates. The 19x-58x ratio comparison between the Expedition and TSP+ midsize SUVs is a novel data point. Limitations acknowledged: fleet composition, demographics, ±15% VMT uncertainty. Strongest counterargument presented (body-on-frame demands, price tier conflation). Verifiable claims throughout.

### 7. Actionable Insights — 9.0/10
Clear guidance: check both NHTSA and IIHS ratings. Names which vehicle got TSP (Wagoneer). Flags second-row failure for families. Points to midsize alternatives at lower price points. Specific enough to act on, not so prescriptive as to be financial advice.
