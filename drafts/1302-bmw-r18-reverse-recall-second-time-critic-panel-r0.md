## Critic scores

### General Editor — 9.0
Lede delivers the paradox in the first two sentences (second recall, first fix was disconnection), and the piece never loses the thread: every paragraph answers "why should a second recall for 4,630 bikes matter?" The "two riding seasons" line and the "young person's game" line carry the human cost of the interim remedy without sentimentality. Minor drag: the geography paragraph packs three dates (June 2023, February 2024, September 2024) into one breath, but the chronology is the argument, so it earns its density.

### Voice Coach — 8.8
Distinctly Rex: deadpan noir without costume ("a promise dressed up as a plan," "a hope with a torque spec"). No swappable-byline problem; Vin would have made this cosmic, Mia would have made it a parts lecture, Rex makes it a case file. Rhythm passes cleanly with fragments used as punches ("It is a prerequisite for a second visit" lands after the setup). No banned phrases, no catchphrase crutches.

### Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
No injured party exists to exploit and none is invented; the zero-US-injury fact is stated in the body and again in the limitations paragraph so the fire headline cannot outrun the record. No individual is blamed: the relay-install errors are reported as BMW's own finding, unattributed to any dealer or technician. Owners are treated as adults with a checklist, not an audience to frighten.

### Social Shareability — 8.6
Headline is the story in one line and travels well ("the fix was turning the feature off" is a shareable absurdity). Timeliness is real (recall filed Oct 7, letters Nov 27, prior-repair owners explicitly told to return). Slightly below the top band only because the audience is narrow: 4,630 R 18 owners plus recall-watchers, not every driver.

### Legal Accuracy — 9.0
Campaign numbers (26V658, 24V716), populations (4,630; 5,048), remedy language, letter dates, and customer-service numbers all trace to the NHTSA acknowledgement letters or the Part 573 report. The article correctly distinguishes the recall (a manufacturer action) from any defect finding beyond it, correctly attributes the relay-failure mechanism to BMW's own report, and explicitly refuses to explain the 418-bike population difference. The one US incident is described only as an incident that "may have been related" in the underlying record's terms, without upgrading it to a fire.

### Research Rigor — 8.8
Original contribution is real: the timeline arithmetic (two years of a disconnected feature, 418-bike shrinkage at 8.3%) plus the geography-first reading of the chronology (tropical markets signaled the failure mode a year before the worldwide recall). Limitations name exactly what is unknown (flame vs smoke in "thermal events," cause of the population drop, per-owner variance in the interim remedy). Counterargument is stated at full strength (the system working at the speed of parts availability) and answered on BMW's own evidence, not dismissed.

### Data Presentation — 8.7
Pull stat (4,630) is the right number with a precise label that carries the 2024 comparison in the same breath. The 8.3% shrinkage and the two-year gap do their work in prose; no chart is needed for a two-campaign story, and inventing one would have been padding. One nit: the pull-label's "after 5,048 were recalled" phrasing could be misread as the same bikes, but the body immediately clarifies the population difference, so it holds.
