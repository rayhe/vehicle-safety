# Research: Evenflo REO — Fifth Recall, Clean Slate Fails

## Angle
Evenflo's REO by Revolve360 was supposed to be the clean-slate redesign after the Revolve360 line accumulated four recalls. It failed NHTSA crash testing within 15 months of production. The headrest shifts position during crashes at its highest settings — the exact settings your growing child needs. Five recalls across one product family isn't bad luck. It's a pattern.

## Key Data Points

### The New Recall (July 2026)
- **Product:** REO by Revolve360, 59,661 units
- **Defect:** Headrest shifts position during crash at positions 10-11 (forward) and 11 (rear) — highest settings
- **Discovery:** NHTSA crash test June 15, 2026 using 3-year-old dummy
- **Manufactured:** March 2025 – June 2026 (15 months of production)
- **Evenflo decision to recall:** July 12, 2026
- **FMVSS violation:** Standard 213, Child Restraint Systems
- **Remedy:** Under development (no fix yet)
- **Injuries reported:** Zero (discovered in testing, not field)

### The Revolve360 Recall Timeline
1. **Revolve360 Original** — 3 recalls (covered in evenflo-revolve360-three-recalls)
2. **Revolve360 Slim** — choking hazard (foam dislodges from headrest), recall 25C010 (covered in evenflo-fourth-recall-toddler-regression)
3. **REO by Revolve360** — headrest shifts during crash, July 2026 (THIS story)
