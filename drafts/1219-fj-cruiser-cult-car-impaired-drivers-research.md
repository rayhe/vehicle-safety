# Research Notes — #1219: FJ Cruiser Impairment Paradox

**Slug:** 1219-fj-cruiser-cult-car-impaired-drivers
**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III (toxicology desk)
**Kicker:** Sobriety Report
**Headline (working):** The FJ Cruiser Is One of Toyota's Safest Vehicles. One in Four of Its Drivers Was Impaired.

## The finding

Cross-tabulating FARS toxicology against fatality rates surfaces a vehicle where the
engineering and the driver cohort tell opposite stories:

- Toyota FJ Cruiser: **25.3% of drivers in fatal crashes impaired** (n=265 tested drivers),
  ranking **16th of 307 models** with 100+ toxicology screens. Breakdown: 19.6% alcohol,
  10.9% drug.
- Dataset weighted mean: 20.0% impaired. The FJ's drivers run ~26% hotter than average.
- Yet the FJ's fatality rate is a thoroughly average **0.43 deaths per 100M VMT**
  (95 deaths, fleet ~175,000) — rank 165 of 286 models. Middle of the pack.
- IIHS rated it Good in frontal-offset and side-impact tests; NHTSA gave it five stars
  for driver safety in frontal collisions (Edmunds summaries of the ratings).

The paradox: a cult-beloved, Toyota-reliable, IIHS-Good off-roader whose fatal-crash
drivers are among the least sober in America — and the vehicle keeps the death rate
average anyway. The engineering compensates for the drivers.

## Context / comparison points (FARS 2014–2023)

- Sibling Toyotas: RAV4 18.4% impaired / rate 0.19; Highlander 16.4% / rate 0.42;
  Sequoia 20.2% / rate 0.83; Land Cruiser 8.9% / rate 6.27 (small fleet artifact).
- FJ's impairment rate is closer to the Corvette (26.2%) than to any Toyota sibling.
- Cult status: sold in the US 2007–2014 (~220,000 units); resale peaked at $80k for
  pristine examples in 2022; 2026 market now $15k–$35k — "first real buying opportunity
  since 2014" (carbuzz.com). Discontinued due to declining sales + emissions regs.
- NHTSA FARS only captures fatal crashes; 265 tested drivers is a moderate sample.

## Kill test

Genuinely newsworthy? YES. The FJ Cruiser is an automotive icon with a ferocious
reliability reputation and a passionate community — and its fatal-crash drivers are
impaired at rates you'd expect from a muscle car, not a Toyota. The "engineering
out-compensates drivers" mechanism is an original cross-tab nobody ran: impairment
ranking vs fatality-rate ranking on the same nameplate.

## Original contribution

Ranking FJ Cruiser's two FARS personas against each other: 16th-worst impairment
cohort but 165th-by-fatality-rate outcomes. The gap between driver behavior and
vehicle outcomes is the safety story — and a warning that a used FJ's clean safety
record is the vehicle's achievement, not its drivers'.

## Actionable takeaway

Shopping a used FJ in the $15k–$35k 2026 window? Its crash-test scores and reliability
are real, but buy the vehicle, not the myth: the model-year fleet skews toward
drivers who take risks. Check VIN recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and remember that
no ladder frame compensates for a drunk driver.
