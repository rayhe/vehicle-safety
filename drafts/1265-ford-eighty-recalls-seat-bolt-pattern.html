Ford Hit 80 Recalls This Year. Number 80 Was a Loose Seat Bolt.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
December 29, 2027

Ford hit 80 recalls in the United States in 2026, and then kept going. Runner-up Stellantis sat at 39, less than half. No other automaker was close, and no one at Ford seemed embarrassed, because Ford insists the count proves its quality control works. Read that sentence again if you need to.[1]

80 vs 39
Ford recalls in the U.S. in 2026 against runner-up Stellantis, as of October 19, 2026

Campaign number 80 was almost insultingly small. Certain 2025-2027 F-150 pickups, 2,276 of them, may have front seat-to-body bolts that were never properly secured. Ford found the problem in a warranty report, then found 56 warranty reports and 16 field reports behind it, and still knew of no crash or injury tied to the defect. A loose seat bolt will announce itself with movement or noise, which is more warning than most recall defects bother to give.[1]

2,276
F-150 pickups in the milestone 80th campaign, against 4,380,609 vehicles in Ford's largest single 2026 campaign

Scale is where the count starts lying. Ford filed 55 campaigns covering more than 12 million vehicles in the first half of the year alone, and its largest single campaign, a software glitch that could knock out trailer brake lights and brakes, covered 4,380,609 vehicles of which an estimated 1% actually had the defect. One campaign, 4.38 million vehicles, roughly 44,000 defective ones. Another campaign, 2,276 trucks. Both count as one recall. Any ranking built on campaign counts alone treats those two events as equals, which is why 80 is a headline and not a verdict.[2]

Powertrain led the breakdown with 11 campaigns, followed by electrical systems with 10, backover protection with 9, and structure and visibility with 7 each. Two of the biggest were software: nearly 1.4 million 2015-2017 F-150s recalled for unexpected downshifts, fixed with a module update, and 741,195 Ford and Lincoln vehicles recalled for a rollaway risk traced to the same module's software. Software recalls are cheap to fix and expensive to count, because each one still costs every owner a dealer visit or at least a notification letter, and Ford itself concedes many of its quality recalls have taken more than five months to complete.[1]

Ford's defense deserves a fair hearing. It says a high recall count shows problems getting caught, not quality collapsing, and J.D. Power named it the top-rated mainstream brand in its 2026 Initial Quality Study. Both facts can be true at once: a company can build good cars and still ship 80 separate things it later has to fix. Within days of the milestone, the count moved again, with 102,159 F-Series trucks recalled because analog speedometers and tachometers could stick or swing erratically.[3] So 80 is already history. If you own a Ford, the number that matters is not 80. It is one: your VIN, checked.

What to actually do with this

If you drive a 2025-2027 F-150, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and call Ford at 1-866-436-7332. Letters for the seat-bolt campaign (Ford 26S74) went out October 26-30, 2026, and dealers inspect the bolts and retorque or replace them free. A seat that shifts or rattles under you is not a quirk to live with until the next oil change. And if you are shopping used: a long recall history on a model is not proof it is unreliable, but an open recall on the specific VIN in front of you is a repair you should get done before you sign, not after.
