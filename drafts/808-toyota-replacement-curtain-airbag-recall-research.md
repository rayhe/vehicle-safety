# Article #808 — Research Notes

## Angle
Toyota recalled 4,118 replacement curtain airbags for Tacoma and Tundra pickups. These are not factory parts. They are the airbags installed AFTER a crash, during body-shop repairs. The replacement meant to protect you in your second crash may not deploy correctly. And Toyota cannot find all the trucks: owner letters go only to owners "known to have had" the part installed, with dealers asked to trace parts through body shops. The supplier is Joyson Safety Systems, the company created from Takata's bankruptcy assets in 2018.

## Core Data (Primary Sources)

### The two recalls (NHTSA Part 573 reports, filed Jul 29, 2026)
- **26E050 (Tacoma)**: 2,381 curtain shield airbag assemblies, production Jun 1 2024 – Dec 31 2025. Defect: CSA may not have been properly secured to its mounting bracket. Fits 2016–2023 Tacoma. Toyota mfr numbers 26TK02/26TH02.
- **26E051 (Tundra)**: 1,737 assemblies, production Dec 1 2021 – Sep 30 2025. Defect: CSA may not have been rolled properly. Fits 2014–2021 Tundra Double Cab and CrewMax. Toyota mfr numbers 26TK03/26TH03.
- **Total: 4,118 units.** Estimated 1% with defect = ~41 airbags.
- Safety risk (both): portions of the curtain airbag may not reach intended positions on deployment; reduced occupant restraint performance; increased injury risk in certain crashes.
- Supplier: **Joyson Safety Systems**, Torreón, Mexico plant (Carretera a Mieleras Km 6.3, Torreón, Coahuila).
- Only service replacement parts affected. Factory-installed airbags and other replacement stock not affected.

### Discovery chronology (from the 573 reports)
- Tundra: Sep 16, 2025, supplier lot acceptance test found a "trapped condition preventing the CSA from fully inflating"; investigation found improper rolling. Nov 2025–Mar 2026: recovered parts inspected, "a high percent" had the condition.
- Tacoma: Dec 22, 2025, lot acceptance test found CSA not fully attached to mounting bracket, plus 10 more in investigation. Jan–May 2026: "a high percent" of recovered parts had the condition.
- Apr–Jul 2026: Toyota engineering analysis. **Jul 24, 2026: voluntary recall decided.**
- As of Jul 21, 2026: zero field technical reports, zero warranty claims in the US. Caught by testing, not by bodies.

### The traceability gap (the investigation's core)
- Remedy text (both reports): "All owners ... who are **known to have had** a curtain shield airbag replacement part installed ... will be notified."
- "Dealers will also be notified and asked to identify any such parts sold to body shops/collision repair facilities and any vehicles that may have had a replacement part installed."
- Owner notification window: **Sep 13–27, 2026** (this article ships ~Sep 10, just ahead).
- Repairer Driven News (Jul 31, 2026): owners of 2014–2021 Tundra with replacement installed after Nov 2021, and 2016–2023 Tacoma with replacement after May 2024, will be notified; **owners who believe their airbags were replaced should go to a dealer even without notification**; "other owners will be notified as their contact information is received."
- Equipment recalls (E-numbers, not V-numbers) frequently do not appear in VIN-based recall lookups, since they attach to parts, not vehicles. Actionable consequence below.

### The supplier lineage (verified)
- Joyson Safety Systems press release, Apr 11, 2018: Key Safety Systems (subsidiary of Ningbo Joyson Electronic) completed acquisition of "substantially all of the global assets" of Takata Corporation out of US/Japan bankruptcies; combined company rebranded Joyson Safety Systems, HQ Auburn Hills, MI. Takata's PSAN inflator liabilities were NOT acquired.
- Scale of the original Takata disaster (Consumer Reports): 50M inflators, 37M vehicles, 19 automakers; 15+ US deaths, 250+ injuries.
- To be fair: this is a folding/bracket assembly defect, not an inflator chemistry defect. Different failure, different era. The irony is lineage and category, not recurrence of the same bug. State this plainly.

### The math (for article)
- 2,381 + 1,737 = **4,118** potentially involved units.
- 1% estimated defective = **~41 airbags** expected bad (0.01 × 4118 = 41.18).
- Tension to note honestly: supplier found "a high percent" of *recovered* parts defective, but recovered parts came from the suspect production range (enriched sample), so 1% of the full population can coexist with that. Do not present as contradiction; present as the reason the estimate deserves scrutiny.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: owner letters mail Sep 13–27, 2026. This ships days before.
- Novel? Yes: no existing site coverage of equipment-recall traceability; the "replaced after your crash" irony; the Takata-successor lineage verified from primary sources. Carscoops/Autoblog covered the recall as news; none did the traceability investigation or the supplier-lineage angle.
- Verdict: PROCEED.

## Counterargument (full strength)
The system worked. A lot acceptance test caught both defects before a single reported injury or warranty claim. Toyota investigated for months, then voluntarily recalled. The 1% estimate means roughly 41 bad airbags out of 4,118, and every one of them is a *replacement* part, meaning the affected population is people whose curtain airbags already deployed once and were repaired, a small subset of owners. Factory airbags are untouched. Tracing replacement parts through independent body shops is genuinely hard, and Toyota's remedy (dealer record audits plus body-shop outreach) is the standard playbook. No evidence anyone has been hurt.

## Limitations
- FARS cannot illuminate this: no field incidents, no deaths, no injuries reported. This is a recall investigation, not a data story; the numbers are NHTSA filing counts, not crash outcomes.
- The 1% defect estimate is Toyota's engineering judgment, not a measured rate; the true number is unknown.
- Cannot verify how many of the 4,118 units were actually installed vs. sitting on shelves (installed units are the risk; shelf stock gets quarantined).
- Equipment recalls may not surface in VIN lookups; readers should call Toyota (1-800-331-4331) or visit a dealer rather than relying on nhtsa.gov/recalls alone.

## Actionable insights
1. Own a 2016–2023 Tacoma or 2014–2021 Tundra (Double Cab/CrewMax) that had curtain/side airbags replaced after a crash or malfunction (Tundra: after Nov 2021; Tacoma: after May 2024)? Go to a Toyota dealer for inspection even if you get no letter.
2. Do not rely on a VIN check alone for equipment recalls; call Toyota customer service at 1-800-331-4331 with your repair history.
3. Keep body-shop invoices showing part numbers; part numbers 62170-0C131, 62180-0C131, 62170-0C121, 62180-0C121 (Tundra) and 62180-04040, 62170-04040, 62180-04050, 62170-04050 (Tacoma) are the suspect populations.
4. If buying a used Tacoma/Tundra in these year ranges, ask whether the curtain airbags were ever replaced and demand the invoice.
