Ford's Fusion Hybrid Lost ABS and Traction Control. NHTSA Called It Wear and Tear.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 28, 2026

Sixteen years after it left the factory, a 2010 Ford Fusion Hybrid can lose its anti-lock brakes and traction control, light up its dashboard, and still not qualify as defective. NHTSA said so on October 23, 2026, denying defect petition DP26004 after a ten-month review that began with nine incident reports and zero crashes, zero injuries, and zero deaths, and the denial landed 305 days after petitioners filed on December 22, 2025, with ODI opening its review on April 9, 2026.[1][2]

How much higher the complaint rate was for recalled non-hybrid Fusions than for the Fusion Hybrid

Petitioners wanted the hybrid folded into recall 19V904. That campaign covered 600,166 model-year 2006-2010 Ford Fusion, Mercury Milan, and Lincoln Zephyr/MKZ cars, and Ford already knew of 15 crashes when it launched, because ABS valves in those cars could stick open after reacting with aged DOT 3 brake fluid, lengthening pedal travel without ever lighting a warning lamp or setting a diagnostic code.[1][2]

ODI answered with engineering, not dismissal. Hybrid braking blends hydraulic friction brakes with regenerative braking, and an electrically activated master cylinder supplies force feedback through the pedal, so a stuck-open normally closed valve in the hybrid ABS unit does not lengthen pedal travel the way it did in the recalled cars. Complaint data pointed the same way, with recalled 2010 Fusions drawing complaints at six times the hybrid rate, hybrid complaints mostly citing an illuminated ABS lamp, and recalled cars mostly citing nothing at all, no lamp, no code. At 20,816 cars, the hybrid fleet amounts to about 3.5 percent of the 600,166 cars covered by recall 19V904, a scale gap that underscores why ODI treated expansion as a design question rather than a paperwork exercise.[1]

Age did the rest, as ODI called the low complaint volume consistent with expected wear and tear on a 16-year-old fleet, found no unreasonable risk to highway safety, and closed the petition while reserving the right to act again if circumstances change, a reservation owners should read carefully. Normal pedal feel proves nothing here, because force feedback can keep the pedal feeling right while ABS and traction control are already gone, and slippery roads are exactly where a driver discovers the difference, at speed, with no second chance to test the theory.[1][2]

Petitioners were not crazy to ask, given the same nameplate, same model year, same hydraulic control unit family, and same degraded braking, while a shrinking 16-year-old fleet could partly explain a lower complaint rate without proving a safer design. Still, a lamp that lights is a real difference from silence, and ODI's distinction rests on testable engineering, not on vibes. What you can do is concrete: if you own a 2010 Fusion Hybrid, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, treat an illuminated ABS lamp as a shop visit for a specific hydraulic control unit diagnosis rather than a generic brake check, keep brake fluid fresh because aged DOT 3 fluid was the chemical actor in the recalled cars, and file an NHTSA complaint if a dealer waves the lamp away, since ODI left the door open to future action.
