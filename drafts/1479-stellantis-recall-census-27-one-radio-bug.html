Stellantis Recalled Its New Cars 27 Times. One Radio Bug Blinded 848,511 of Them.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

I counted Stellantis the way I counted Ford, GM, Toyota, Hyundai, Honda, and Kia: every canonical model name in NHTSA's database, every hybrid and plug-in variant, every spelling the API might hide a campaign under. 469 queries. Six makes. Stellantis recalled its 2026 models 27 times, and one radio bug accounts for 844,027 of the vehicles. Jeep carries 12 of the 27, Ram 11, Chrysler 5, Dodge 2, Alfa Romeo 1, Fiat none. Add the brands up and you get 31, because two campaigns cross makes, and the company total after dedupe is 27.[1]

27
Recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching Stellantis's 2026-model-year vehicles, deduped across six makes. One campaign covers 844,027 vehicles; another covers five trucks

Campaign 26V531 is the one that matters, and it was already the biggest story on this site back in August: a software error in the Harman Becker radio can prevent the rearview camera image from displaying when the driver shifts into reverse, which means 15 model lines across four brands, Wrangler to ProMaster EV, all running the same Uconnect5 radio architecture, all failing the same federal rear-visibility standard at the same time. NHTSA's filing estimates that 100 percent of the recalled vehicles have the defect, which is the kind of sentence that usually comes with a smaller number attached. Ram 1500s account for 211,114 of them, Wranglers 170,033, Pacificas 131,681, and three ProMaster EVs round out the bottom of the list, because somebody has to be last. Stellantis announced 848,511; the Part 573 filing counts 844,027 built with the suspect radio. Either way, it is the largest single campaign in this entire census series, and it was filed on a Tuesday in August, five weeks before the same defect needed a second campaign, 26V560, for another 239,131 Ram 1500s the first scope missed.[2][4][10]

Second place is a quieter disaster. Campaign 26V059, filed February 2, covers 456,287 Rams and Jeeps whose trailer tow module can kill the trailer's lights and brakes without any dashboard warning at all, which is a special kind of failure for a vehicle whose entire job is pulling things: the truck keeps driving, the trailer behind it goes dark and unbraked, and nobody in either cab knows. Ram 1500s account for 312,365 of them. Wagoneer S contributes 321. And the 2026 Cherokee, a nameplate that did not exist last year, contributes 57, which brings me to the Cherokee.[3]

Jeep killed the Cherokee after 2023 and brought it back for 2026 as a hybrid-only SUV, and its first model year already carries four campaigns, including 26V223, a brake module that can take out the anti-lock brakes and the electronic stability control at the same time, on a vehicle with no prior model year to learn from. Elsewhere in the count, the Ram 2500 needed its stability-control recall expanded five months after it was filed, 26V651 adding 1,724 trucks the first scope missed, a 26 percent undercount. And at the far end of the table sits 26V622, a recall of exactly five Ram 1500s, five VINs, built between February 28 and May 18, whose side-airbag connector may not be secured. Five trucks. Same federal system, same campaign number format, same weight in the count as the 844,027-vehicle camera recall. Counts are not severity scores, and this census contains both extremes in the same table.[5][6][7]

Now the honest accounting, because 27 flatters Stellantis and undersells it at the same time. It flatters, because a company with six brands files more campaigns than a company with two, and the honest comparison is per brand: Jeep 12 and Ram 11 against Ford's 41 or GM's 15, not the company-wide 27. Dodge's 2 is not a safety triumph, since Dodge barely sells cars anymore, and Fiat's 0 is a rounding artifact of selling one electric hatchback. It undersells, because the method has a hole where the vans are: campaign 26V531 explicitly covers the Ram ProMaster, yet no ProMaster spelling in the API returns a single row, the same vocabulary trap that hid a Corsair recall inside a Nautilus query in the Ford census. And the over-the-air column reads zero for all 27 campaigns, which is wrong on its face, since the camera fix is delivered over the air. NHTSA's flag undercounts software remedies, so treat every zero in this series as a flag reading, not a fact.[1][8]

What to do with this: if you own a 2026 Jeep, Ram, Chrysler, or Dodge, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V531. If the camera image fails to appear in reverse, the fix is a free radio software update, over the air or at the dealer, and it is worth doing promptly, since backover crashes kill about 210 people a year. If you tow with a 2025-2026 Ram or a 2024-2026 Wagoneer S, check for 26V059 and do not trust the dashboard to warn you; the trailer tow module fails silently, and the fix is a free replacement. If you own a 2026 Cherokee, check for 26V223 and 26V559, and if you are shopping one, four campaigns in a first model year is a reason to buy the second year. If you own a 2026 Ram 2500 that was cleared under the April stability-control recall, check again, because the first scope missed 1,724 trucks. And if you run a recall census of your own, never trust a bare model name, and never trust one make to cover a company. A 400 is not always a zero, and a make is not always a company.
