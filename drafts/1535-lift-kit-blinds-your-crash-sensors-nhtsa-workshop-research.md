## Story in one line
NHTSA is holding a workshop on December 10, 2026 because the lift kits, aftermarket bumpers, and wheel swaps Americans bolt onto their trucks can knock the crash sensors out of calibration, and the automatic emergency braking that cuts rear-end crashes in half only works when those sensors point where the factory aimed them.

## Verified facts

1. **The workshop.** NHTSA will hold a one-day workshop on Thursday, December 10, 2026, 8:00 AM–3:30 PM Eastern, in the West Atrium of DOT headquarters, 1200 New Jersey Avenue SE, Washington, DC. Registration is free but required by December 3, 2026. It will not be live streamed; NHTSA will record it and post the recording on its website afterward. Federal Register Doc. 2026-22067, filed October 28, 2026, published October 29, 2026 (Vol. 91, No. 208), docket NHTSA-2026-1882. (Federal Register via govinfo.gov; NHTSA events page; Holland & Knight alert.)
2. **The four modifications NHTSA names.** Changes to ride height, bumper geometry, body panels, and wheel offset can alter sensor position or calibration and weaken crash detection. The notice's own language: systems that are not calibrated accurately are "likely to detect crash-imminent situations less well" and could produce false activations, which the notice calls potential safety hazards. A false activation is the system braking for a threat that is not there. (Federal Register 2026-22067; Motoring Chronicle; Dealership Tech Report; Truck Driver News.)
3. **The stated goal, and its limit.** The workshop aims to "explore practical, data-driven approaches to maintaining safety-critical driver-assistance functions while preserving vehicle owner autonomy in altering their vehicles." It proposes no rule, creates no standard, recall, repair mandate, or enforcement policy, and changes nothing for owners today. (Federal Register; Dealership Tech Report; Holland & Knight.)
4. **The deadline mess in the government's own paperwork.** The notice says the docket stays open 90 days after the meeting and asks for comments by April 19, 2027. Ninety days after December 10 is March 10, 2027. Regulations.gov lists January 27, 2027 as the comment due date for the same docket. Three different dates; the safe one is January 27. (Motoring Chronicle; Federal Register.)
5. **What the systems do, and what they are worth.** Forward collision warning + automatic braking cuts rear-end crashes by about 50% and rear-end crashes with injuries by 56% (IIHS, 2017 study). Warning alone cuts them 27%. Pedestrian-detecting automatic braking cuts pedestrian crashes 27%. Blind spot detection cuts lane-change crashes 14%. Those results come from systems working as designed. (IIHS advanced driver assistance topic page, via Motoring Chronicle.)
6. **The mandate and its wobble.** FMVSS 127 (May 2024) requires AEB, pedestrian AEB, and forward collision warning on all new light vehicles (10,000 lbs GVWR or less) by September 1, 2029 (September 1, 2030 for small-volume manufacturers). Systems must brake automatically for a lead vehicle at speeds up to 90 mph and for pedestrians up to 45 mph, in daylight and darkness. On January 17, 2025 the Alliance for Automotive Innovation petitioned the D.C. Circuit for review; on February 24, 2025 DOT moved to hold the case in abeyance pending review, granted February 28, 2025. On March 23, 2026 DOT told the court it is preparing a notice of proposed rulemaking that would extend the compliance lead time by two years. The September 2029 deadline remains in place unless the change is adopted. (Federal Register, Nov 26, 2024; Nelson Mullins, Apr 22, 2026.)
7. **The voluntary baseline already in place.** Twenty automakers representing 99% of U.S. light vehicle sales committed with IIHS and NHTSA to make front crash prevention standard; by the 2023 deadline each affirmed at least 95% of its vehicles built in the preceding year (up to 8,500 lbs) carried it. (Motoring Chronicle, citing NHTSA/IIHS.)
8. **The claim-side payoff, and its price.** HLDI's August 2026 comparison of otherwise identical 2017–2022 model-year vehicles with and without a driver-assistance bundle: collision claim frequency about 10% lower, collision claim severity about 10% higher, overall collision losses about 5% lower; property damage liability claim frequency almost 40% lower, severity 15% higher, overall losses almost 30% lower. IIHS's Matt Moore: "every crash avoided is a deductible you don't need to pay." (Motoring Chronicle; 4AutoInsurance, citing IIHS/HLDI.)
9. **Calibration is already a repair-bill problem.** The sensors behind AEB and blind spot detection are expensive and often require calibration after replacement (IIHS/HLDI, Matt Moore). The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the rise in average vehicle repair prices at more than 40% since 2020. A bumper repair that skips recalibration is a sensor left pointing wrong. (Motoring Chronicle.)
10. **Owners switch the systems off.** In an IIHS-cited study of more than 2,000 vehicles arriving at dealerships of six automakers in 2023, automatic emergency braking was active in 93% of equipped vehicles, but among vehicles with lane departure warning and no lane departure prevention, only 57% had the warning active. A system that is off prevents nothing. (Motoring Chronicle, citing IIHS.)
11. **Heavy trucks have no AEB rule at all.** The July 2023 joint NHTSA/FMCSA proposal for AEB on vehicles over 10,001 lbs was never finalized; the agencies are rewriting it, with a supplemental proposal on the 2026 regulatory agenda. No federal AEB requirement is in effect for heavy trucks as of September 2026. (Flick Truck Accident Law, Sept 12, 2026.)
12. **The stakes number.** NHTSA says 39,254 people died in motor vehicle crashes in 2024, many tied to human error. (Motoring Chronicle, citing NHTSA.)

## Original contribution: the calibration-gap arithmetic + the deadline mess

**The 21.7% fleet.** Pickups account for 41,593 of 191,193 deaths in this site's FARS 2014–2023 dataset (21.7%). The two best-selling pickups, Silverado (9,591 deaths, rate 1.25) and F-150 (9,194 deaths, rate 1.04), account for 18,785 deaths between them — nearly 10% of every death in the dataset, in two nameplates. These are the vehicles most likely to get a lift kit, a steel bumper, and wider wheels with different offset. The workshop is about a modification culture aimed squarely at the deadliest class of vehicle on the road.

**The qualifier nobody prints.** Every IIHS effectiveness number for front crash prevention carries an unprinted condition: the system must be working as designed. NHTSA's notice says a lifted or re-bumpered truck's system may not be. The mandate (FMVSS 127) assumes factory geometry at the moment of sale; nothing in the rule follows the car to the accessory shop. The gap between "AEB cuts rear-end crashes in half" and "your AEB cuts rear-end crashes in half" is a calibration appointment nobody is required to book.

**The three-date docket.** The government's own paperwork gives three different comment deadlines for the same docket: April 19, 2027 (the notice), March 10, 2027 (90 days after the meeting, by the notice's own arithmetic), and January 27, 2027 (Regulations.gov). The workshop is about precision engineering. Its paperwork cannot agree on a date.

**The mandate is slipping while the fleet is being modified.** DOT told a federal court in March 2026 it wants two more years on the AEB mandate, pushing universal AEB toward 2031. Meanwhile the 2023 voluntary commitment already put front crash prevention on 95%+ of new cars, and owners are modifying those cars now, with no recalibration requirement anywhere in the pipeline. The systems are arriving faster than the rules for keeping them aimed.

## FARS cross-check
Repo FARS dataset (2014–2023), 337 models, 191,193 deaths:
- Pickup class: 41,593 deaths (21.7% of dataset) on a fleet of 34,781,250.
- Chevrolet Silverado: 9,591 deaths, rate 1.25 per 100M VMT (est.); Ford F-150: 9,194 deaths, rate 1.04; Dodge Ram: 4,407 deaths, rate 0.78; GMC Sierra: 3,337 deaths, rate 1.01.
- For contrast, the lowest-rate high-volume models: Tesla Model Y 0.03 (57 deaths), Kia Telluride 0.04 (31), Tesla Model 3 0.05 (92) — all on young, small fleets through most of the window; report only as context.
- Caveat: FARS does not record whether a vehicle was modified, lifted, or recalibrated. The FARS numbers establish which vehicle class the workshop's subject (aftermarket modification) most plausibly touches; they do not prove modified trucks crash more. Say exactly that.

## Kill-test notes / what NOT to claim
- Do NOT say lift kits make AEB fail, or that any specific number of crashes were caused by modified sensors. NHTSA says modifications "could" affect performance by altering calibration; the notice's finding is about detection likelihood, not a crash count. No crash data tied to modifications exists in the sources.
- Do NOT say the AEB mandate has been delayed. The September 1, 2029 deadline remains in place; DOT is *preparing* a proposal to extend it two years, and the D.C. Circuit case is in abeyance. Say exactly that.
- Do NOT say the workshop will produce a rule. It proposes no rule and commits NHTSA to no outcome. It is a convening.
- Do NOT present the three comment deadlines as a settled fact about which one governs; report the discrepancy (notice: April 19, 2027; 90-day arithmetic: March 10, 2027; Regulations.gov: January 27, 2027) and advise filing by the earliest.
- Do NOT present the FARS pickup death counts as evidence that modified trucks are more dangerous. FARS does not record modification status. The FARS numbers show exposure (which class gets modified), not causation.
- Do NOT claim heavy trucks are covered by FMVSS 127. The rule covers light vehicles up to 10,000 lbs GVWR; heavy-truck AEB was never finalized and is back at the proposal stage.
- The IIHS 50%/56%/27%/14% figures are for systems working as designed, from a 2017 study (FCW+AEB) and a 2022 study (pedestrian AEB) as summarized on the IIHS topic page; attribute them, do not present them as 2026 measurements.
- Bendix's warning about added truck parts blocking radar signals (commercial-truck context) is secondary color; if used, attribute to Bendix via Truck Driver News and keep it out of the light-vehicle claims.
