# Research — #1315: Tesla Robotaxi School-Zone Speeding (NHTSA DP26010)

## Angle
A driverless car got caught speeding in a school zone. There is no driver to ticket, no owner to fine, no teenager to scold. The only "driver" in the vehicle is Tesla's Automated Driving System, and it allegedly decided the school zone speed limit didn't apply. NHTSA opened defect petition file DP26010 on Oct 9, 2026.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. First federal action alleging a robotaxi fleet commits traffic violations around children. Third federal action against Tesla's driverless fleet in ~5 weeks.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. Unlike FSD (Supervised), where a human picks the "Mad Max" profile, a Robotaxi has no human at fault. The liability question has no precedent: who gets the speeding ticket when nobody is driving? Plus: NHTSA's 1,000-vehicle estimate is the first federal number for Tesla's fleet size.

## Facts (all from cited sources)
- NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation opened DP26010 on Oct 9, 2026 (opening resume).
- Petition received Sept 23, 2026, targets MY2026 Tesla Robotaxis (Model Y + Cybercab) with ADS engaged.
- Affected fleet estimate: ~1,000 vehicles.
- Evidence: 3 social media videos + 4 incidents in NHTSA tally. Zero crashes, zero injuries, zero deaths.
- 49 CFR Part 552: NHTSA has 120 days from receipt to grant/deny → deadline ~Jan 21, 2027.
- Context stack: Oct 2024 Preliminary Evaluation (~2.4M vehicles, FSD low-visibility); Oct 2025 FSD traffic-law-violation investigation (red lights, wrong lane); Mar 2026 EA (~3.2M vehicles); Tesla shipped "Mad Max" FSD profile Oct 2025 that ignores speed limits; audit query + Special Order (Cybercab certification, answers due Sept 30, 2026).
- Waymo comparison: Austin ISD reported ~20 Waymo vehicles illegally passing stopped school buses late 2025; Waymo did a voluntary software recall Dec 2025.
- Tesla began commercial Cybercab service in Austin Sept 3, 2026.

## Original contribution
- Framing: first case where a traffic violation has literally no human perpetrator, stated at legal/logic strength.
- Calculation: 1,000-vehicle fleet vs June 2025 community-tracker counts of "a few dozen" — fleet grew ~20-30x in a year.
- Timeline table of Tesla's NHTSA file (Oct 2024 PE → Oct 2025 FSD probe → Mar 2026 EA → Sept 2026 audit/Special Order → Oct 9, 2026 DP26010).

## Limitations
- Petition is unevaluated; NHTSA has NOT found a defect. Evidence is 3 videos + 4 incidents; zero crashes.
- Fleet count is NHTSA's estimate, likely from Texas DMV certifications; not all 1,000 may be active.
- No public Tesla response found.

## Counterargument
School-zone speed limits often confuse humans too; GPS lag or construction zones may explain videos. A driverless system briefly exceeding the limit may be no worse than the human baseline. Also, with zero crashes, this may be regulatory theater.

## Actionable
- No VIN to check. But: parents in Austin/Dallas/Houston can report robotaxi violations to NHTSA directly; the DP26010 grant/deny decision lands ~Jan 21, 2027 — that is the date to watch for a real investigation or recall.
