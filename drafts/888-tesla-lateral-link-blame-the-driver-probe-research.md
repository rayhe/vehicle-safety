# Research — #888: The Part Tesla Blamed Drivers For Is Under Federal Investigation in 1.2 Million Cars

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
NHTSA opened Preliminary Evaluation PE26006 on July 29, 2026, covering an estimated 1,198,300 Tesla Model 3 (2018-2020) and Model Y (2021-2023) vehicles after 156 complaints that the front lower lateral link detached, most with no advance warning. The same component family sits at the center of a 2023 Reuters investigation showing Tesla internally tracked chronic suspension failures for years while telling customers and regulators the damage was "driver abuse" — and in 2020 Tesla recalled these links in China under regulatory pressure while declining to recall them in the US.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: yes. This is not commodity "regulator probes Tesla" news. The novelty is the documented arc: 2020 China recall under pressure (no US recall), 2023 Reuters exposes internal knowledge + blame-the-driver pattern + Senate letter to Musk, 2023 US saw only two tiny batch-limited recalls (2,791 and 422 cars), and now 2026 brings a 1.2-million-vehicle preliminary evaluation that NHTSA explicitly says "exceeds the scope" of those earlier recalls and is "not related to the production issue" behind them. The data tells a story of a structural defect repeatedly re-framed as a production-batch problem. The 156-complaint count over ~1.2M vehicles (~13 per 100,000) matters because NHTSA's own resume notes most failures had no warning at all — the component that holds your wheel onto the car at speed fails silently.

## Key numbers (all cited, none fabricated)
- PE26006 opened July 29, 2026; estimated population 1,198,300 vehicles (MY2018-2020 Model 3, MY2021-2023 Model Y)
- 156 ODI complaints; 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injuries, 0 fatalities in the resume
- ~13 complaints per 100,000 vehicles in the population
- Prior US recalls: 21V-835 = 2,791 vehicles (MY2019-2021 Model 3, production issue); 23V-235 = 422 vehicles (MY2018-2019 Model 3). Combined: 3,213 vehicles, ~0.27% of the PE26006 population
- 2023 Reuters: at least 11 drivers told Tesla a crash was caused by suspension/steering/wheel-assembly failure (2018-2021 records); China recalled front suspension aft link + rear suspension upper link in 2020 under regulator pressure; no US recall of those parts
- Dec 2023: two US senators demanded Tesla recall the components and "correct the record with NHTSA"

## Novel contribution
The population-to-recall math is the story. Tesla's two prior US recalls covered 3,213 vehicles for this component family — 0.27% of the 1.2M vehicles now under PE26006 — and NHTSA's resume says the current failures are NOT the production issue those recalls addressed. Every prior action framed this as a batch problem; the current investigation treats it as a scope problem. Plus the China divergence: the same suspension components were recalled in China in 2020 because regulators there applied pressure; in the US, the response was a 2023 Senate letter. Six years later, the part is detaching in American cars with no warning, and the federal count just started.

## Counterargument (full strength)
A preliminary evaluation is the first and mildest stage of NHTSA's defect process — it is not a finding of a defect, not a recall, and the resume shows zero crashes, injuries, or fatalities across all 156 complaints. Tesla's prior recalls addressed what it identified as production issues, and NHTSA itself notes most (not all) failures had no warning, so some owners did hear noises. The 2023 Reuters reporting covered fore and aft links, a different (though adjacent) part of the suspension from the lateral link at issue in PE26006 — linking them is an editorial inference, not an established engineering connection. Tesla has not commented on the new probe, and the company may argue the failure rate (13 per 100K) is low for a wear component over 6-8 model years.

## Limitations
- Do not claim the Reuters-documented blame-the-driver pattern applies to these specific lateral link failures; the pattern covered the suspension component family broadly (fore/aft links, control arms). Frame as the documented company history on the same component class.
- PE26006 is an investigation, not a defect finding; say so explicitly. No crashes, injuries, or fatalities are attributed.
- The driveteslacanada PDF is a hosted copy of the NHTSA resume; cite it as such.
- Do not assert that Tesla will be forced to recall, or predict the investigation's outcome.

## Actionable takeaways
1. If you own a 2018-2020 Model 3 or 2021-2023 Model Y: listen for clunking or knocking from the front suspension and watch for uneven front tire wear — the few owners who got any warning heard noises. Most got nothing.
2. File a complaint at nhtsa.gov if you have had a front suspension link replaced or failed on one of these vehicles; the entire PE26006 population estimate runs on owner complaints.
3. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for the two prior lateral-link recalls (21V-835, 23V-235) — they covered only 3,213 vehicles, but if your car is in them, get the remedy.
4. Ask the service center to inspect front suspension links at your next visit regardless of warning lights; the resume states the vehicle provides no visual warning before this failure.
