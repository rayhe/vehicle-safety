# Research: #828 — The Phone Is the Only Honest Witness
Journalist: Vin Wreckage | Kicker: Existential Dread | Date: 2026-09-06

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
IIHS's new telematics study (Aug 27, 2026) linked ~17,000 real crashes to phone-sensor data and found police reports miss pre-crash phone use by at least 7x, with cops agreeing with the phone on the exact activity in only 3 of 17,000 crashes. Vin's cosmic-absurdity take: the national distraction statistics are fiction written by people with every incentive to lie, and the only reliable witness to your crash is the glowing rectangle that caused it.

## Original computations (the novel contribution)
- Activity-specific undercount ratios: handheld calls 22/7 = 3.1x; hands-free 90/1 = 90x; manipulation 639/20 = 32x; passive 586/20 = 29x. The behavior cops catch best (calls, 3.1x) is the rarest; the behavior that dominates crashes (tap/swipe) is missed 32-to-1. Laws written around "talking on the phone" aim at the 22 while the 639 walk free.
- Agreement rate: 3 / ~17,000 = 0.018%. When both sources flagged phone use, they described the SAME activity three times in seventeen thousand crashes.
- Selection-bias floor: sample = voluntary safe-driving-app enrollees (chose monitoring for insurance discounts, self-selected for caution). Even they were manipulating phones before 639 crashes. 7-8% is a floor for the general population, not a ceiling.
- The maps-app paradox: passive screen-unlocked ~as common as active use; IIHS concludes navigation apps likely account for much of it. The most "distracting" screen may be the one giving directions. ~25% of drivers admitted watching video while driving in past 30 days (separate IIHS survey).
- Back-of-envelope illustration (labeled as such): apply the 7x to NHTSA's 3,208 distraction deaths -> ~22,500. Caveat: study measured phone PRESENCE in crashes, not causation, and mostly non-fatal sample. Present as "the instrument misses 7 of 8" not "the real death toll is 22,500."

## Limitations (stated explicitly)
- 4 states only, 2021-2024; voluntary enrollees, not representative.
- 15-minute / 1-mile match window is fuzzy by design.
- Telematics proves activity, not causation, and not that the driver looked at the screen.
- Cannot track CarPlay/Android Auto (screen stays locked); cannot see which apps.
- Passive use (unlocked, untouched) includes navigation that may not be distraction at all.
- CMT is a commercial telematics vendor with a business interest in the monitoring economy.

## Strongest counterargument
The study's 7-8% could be read as GOOD news: maybe the distracted-driving epidemic is overstated, since even the all-seeing phone says 92% of crashes had no phone activity in the 30 seconds before impact. Police reports are enforcement documents, not epidemiological instruments; they are designed to record illegal behavior, and much of the phone use IIHS counts is perfectly legal, so undercounting it is by design, not failure. And 2 seconds of eyes-off-road doubling crash risk is real, but the study cannot say the phone caused any particular crash.

## Actionable insights
- For drivers: the risk is not the call, it is the tap. Set navigation before moving; phone face-down or out of reach. 639 manipulation crashes vs 22 handheld calls in the study.
- For shoppers/insured: safe-driver telematics programs record all of this; the discount has a surveillance price, and CMT's own policy allows disclosure when required by law.
- For lawmakers: 21 states ban holding phones; IIHS research says broader bans outperform narrow texting bans. Enforcement can't see the behavior that matters, so the law has to target the hand, not the act.
