# Research: Distracted Driving's Share of Pedestrian Deaths More Than Doubled in Five Years

## Core Finding
GHSA data shows distracted driving accounted for 39% of pedestrian fatalities in 2024, up from 15% in 2019. That's a 160% increase in share during a period when overall pedestrian deaths were declining.

## Primary Sources

1. **GHSA Pedestrian Traffic Fatalities report (2024 data, published Jul 2026)**
   - Distracted driving: 39% of pedestrian fatalities in 2024 vs 15% in 2019
   - SUVs/pickups/vans: 50.4% of pedestrian fatalities
   - Urban areas: 84% of pedestrian crashes, but lower fatal rates (9.2%)
   - Rural areas: 15.1% fatal rate
   - Pedestrian fatalities now 10.1% of ALL crash fatalities (first time double digits since 2015)
   - Source: Streetsblog USA coverage of GHSA report (Jul 14, 2026)

2. **NHTSA Q1 2026 Estimates**
   - 7,770 deaths in Q1 2026, down 4.3% from Q1 2025
   - Fatality rate: 0.99/100M VMT (lowest Q1 since 2014)
   - Overall trend: declining deaths

3. **NHTSA 2025 Annual Data**
   - 36,640 deaths, down 6.7% from 2024 (39,254)
   - Rate: 1.10/100M VMT (second-lowest ever)

4. **FARS 2014-2023 (vehicle-specific)**
   - F-150: 10,872 "other person" deaths across 20,066 fatal crashes
   - Silverado: 10,141 "other person" deaths across 19,732 fatal crashes
   - 50.4% of pedestrian deaths involve large vehicles (GHSA data aligns with FARS "other person" patterns)

## Novel Angle
The story isn't "distracted driving is bad." It's: the composition of what kills pedestrians has FUNDAMENTALLY shifted. DUI enforcement and infrastructure improvements are reducing impaired and speed-related pedestrian deaths. But distraction is rising fast enough to fill the gap. The net improvement in pedestrian deaths is SMALLER than the improvement in occupant deaths because distraction is harder to enforce than impairment.

The math: if distraction stayed at 15% instead of rising to 39%, and total pedestrian deaths declined at the same rate, approximately 3,000 fewer pedestrians would have died over 2019-2024 from distraction alone.
