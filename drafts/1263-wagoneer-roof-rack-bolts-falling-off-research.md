# Research — #1263: Wagoneer Roof Rack Bolts Falling Off

**Slug:** 1263-wagoneer-roof-rack-bolts-falling-off
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate / car-buying guidance beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Proposed headline:** The Roof Rack Bolts on 97,349 Wagoneers Were Never Tightened Right. The Driver Behind You Pays for It.

## Kill test: PASS
- Genuinely newsworthy: a federal recall of 97,349 flagship SUVs, filed September 10, 2026, for roof rack crossbars that can detach at speed and become a road hazard. Interim owner letters went out October 8, 2026, and the final remedy was still pending at that point.
- Novel angle vs site history: no prior Crash Report story covers a roof rack or roof rail detachment, and none covers a recall where the filing's estimated defect rate is 100 percent. Distinct from the Sierra grille recall-query stories (a trim part falling off after a failed fix): here the fix is redesigned bolts, not a re-torque, and the risk statement names other drivers as the victims.
- Data anchor: one NHTSA campaign with exact counts per model, production windows, supplier, warranty-claim count, and a verbatim risk statement, corroborated across five independent outlets.

## Primary sources

1. **NHTSA recall campaign 26V584 (26V584000), FCA US recall 81D** — filed September 10, 2026. Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) recalling certain 2022-2025 Jeep Wagoneer, 2022-2026 Jeep Grand Wagoneer, 2023-2025 Jeep Wagoneer L, and 2023-2026 Jeep Grand Wagoneer L vehicles. "The pivot bolts for the roof rack crossbars may not have been tightened properly, allowing them to detach." Potential number of units affected: 97,349. Estimated percentage with defect: 100%. Remedy: dealers will replace the roof rack crossbar pivot bolts free of charge. Interim notification letters expected to be mailed October 8, 2026; a second notice will be sent once the final remedy is available. Owners may contact Chrysler customer service at 1-800-853-1403. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov September 17, 2026. Consumer advisories: no do-not-drive, no park-outside.
   - Reported via: OEMDTC summary of the NHTSA record (26V584000, 81D, 97,349 units, 100% defect estimate, Oct 8 interim letters, 1-800-853-1403, VINs searchable Sept 17, 2026); PickupTruckTalk (same campaign facts, STRUCTURE component); MoparInsiders (per-model counts and production dates, 26V-584 / 81D).
2. **FCA's Part 573 safety-risk statement (verbatim, as quoted by multiple outlets):** "Crossbars which detach from a vehicle may create a road hazard to operators and occupants of other vehicles, and can cause such vehicles to crash without prior warning." Warning signs per FCA: "customers may hear a rattling noise, notice that the crossbar is missing a bolt or came apart at the pivot joint."
   - Reported via: Backfire News (verbatim risk statement, filed Sept 10, remedy bolts "are a different design"); The Cooldown (verbatim risk statement and warning signs, 15 warranty claims worldwide as of Aug 31, no crashes or injuries reported, remedy uses redesigned pivot bolts, owner/dealer notification starting Oct 8).
3. **Per-model population (adds to 97,349 exactly):**
   - 2022-2025 Jeep Wagoneer: 54,249 (production March 8, 2021 through October 10, 2025)
   - 2023-2025 Jeep Wagoneer L: 11,606 (production May 11, 2022 through October 9, 2025)
   - 2022-2026 Jeep Grand Wagoneer: 22,144 (production February 24, 2021 through October 30, 2025)
   - 2023-2026 Jeep Grand Wagoneer L: 9,350 (production May 4, 2022 through October 30, 2025)
   - Reported via: MoparInsiders (counts and production dates); Backfire News (same counts, production span Feb 24, 2021 to Oct 30, 2025).
4. **Supplier and claim count (secondary, attributed):** GCN reports the suspect assemblies' supplier as Minth, Plastic Trim International, and that both filings identify it. The Cooldown reports 15 warranty claims worldwide that may be related as of August 31, 2026, with no crashes or injuries reported in relation to this issue.
5. **Contrast case (site history, for the original-contribution framing):** NHTSA recall query RQ26003 (opened October 9, 2026) into GMC Sierra 1500 grille deflectors, where 21 of 118 complaints involved trucks that had already received the recall repair. The Sierra story is a fix that failed. The Wagoneer story is a fix that changes the part: redesigned bolts rather than a re-torque of the same bolts.

## Original calculations (done for this article, inputs shown)
- Model shares of the 97,349 population: Wagoneer 54,249 = 55.7%; Wagoneer L 11,606 = 11.9%; Grand Wagoneer 22,144 = 22.7%; Grand Wagoneer L 9,350 = 9.6%. Check: 54,249 + 11,606 + 22,144 + 9,350 = 97,349 exactly.
- L variants combined: 11,606 + 9,350 = 20,956 = 21.5% of the population.
- Grand Wagoneer family combined: 22,144 + 9,350 = 31,494 = 32.3%.
- Warranty-claim rate: 15 claims against 97,349 vehicles = 0.015%, against a 100% filing defect estimate. The gap between "every truck has the defect" and "15 claims" is the article's central tension: under-torque is latent until a bolt backs out far enough to matter.

## What is NOT claimed
- No crashes, injuries, or deaths are attributed to this defect in the sources found; FCA's risk statement describes a hazard to other vehicles, not a crash count. Do not invent one.
- The 100% figure is the filing's estimated defect rate (every recalled vehicle carries under-torqued pivot bolts), not a prediction that every crossbar will detach. Under-torque is a spectrum; most bolts may hold for the vehicle's life. The counterargument section states this at full strength.
- Torque specification values (foot-pounds) are not in the sources found; the article does not state a spec number.
- The supplier identification (Minth, Plastic Trim International) rests on GCN's reporting of the filings, not on a directly opened Part 573 PDF; attributed accordingly.
- No claim that any specific crossbar has detached on a public road; the 15 warranty claims are described as "may be related," per The Cooldown.
- Roof rack load or cargo is not blamed; the defect is the pivot bolt torque, per the filing.
