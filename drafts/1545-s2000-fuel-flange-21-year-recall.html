Honda Is Recalling the S2000. The Cars Are 21 Years Old.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

Honda is recalling 16,387 S2000 roadsters, and the youngest car on the list rolled off the line in September 2005. A fuel flange joint assembly can loosen, leak fuel, and stall the engine while you are driving. No one has been hurt, and no warranty claims exist. One customer in Japan, who had just had the part replaced, complained in December 2025. That complaint is the reason this recall exists at all.[1][2]

16,387
2004-2005 S2000 roadsters recalled for a fuel flange joint that may have been built without brazing material.

Start with the part, because the whole story is a factory that moved and a checklist that did not follow. Sanoh Industrial, the supplier that makes the joint, moved its brazing operation and never properly verified the process afterward. Sixty-seven replacement parts came off the line without brazing material at the joint, the bond that holds a press-fit connection together. Without it, the joint works loose, fuel leaks, pressure drops, and the engine can stall.[2][3]

Here is the number that makes this recall strange. Honda sold 7,320 S2000s in the United States in 2004 and 7,780 in 2005, a combined 15,100 cars. This recall covers 16,387, which is 1,287 more cars than Honda sold here in those two model years put together, and the gap is the point. Honda's population review also swept in cars that may have had the bad part fitted later as a replacement, so some recalled cars left the factory fine and picked up the defect at a service counter. The assembly line did not build this problem. The parts bin did.[3][5]

The discovery reads like a slow-motion detective story. December 2025: a Japanese customer complains about a fuel flange joint he had just had replaced. January 2026: Honda starts hunting for a used car to test. May 2026: tests finally run on that car, five months after Honda went looking for one. Five or six more months of investigation, and the recall lands November 5, 2026. Eleven months from one complaint to a federal filing, for a defect with zero injuries. That is either admirable thoroughness, or a system that only moves when a customer does the finding.[2]

Fairness demands the other side. Sixteen thousand cars, no crashes, no injuries, no warranty claims, and a free fix at any Honda dealer. Honda traced a supplier error and recalled every car that could carry the part, including cars never built with it. But working is not the same as fast. A fuel leak on a car with no fuel-pressure warning announces itself as a smell, then a stall, then a car stopped in a lane. The only reason nobody has been hurt yet is that 67 bad parts is a very small number with a very large blast radius.[1][3]

What to do: if you own a 2004 or 2005 S2000, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today. VINs became searchable November 20, and the owner letters do not go out until January 4, 2027, so waiting for the mail costs you six weeks. If your car ever had fuel-system work, especially a fuel flange joint replaced under part 16623-PCX-003, bring the receipt to the dealer, since the inspection and replacement are free. And if you are shopping for one, ask for the recall clearance first. A 2004 car in good condition is a $32,200 asset by Hagerty's October 2026 guide, and that is collector money for a car with an open fuel recall.[1][7]
