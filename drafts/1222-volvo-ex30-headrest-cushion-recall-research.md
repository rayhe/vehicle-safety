# Research: Volvo EX30 Headrest Cushion Recall (Article #1222)

**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — restraint systems / whiplash beat)
**Kicker:** Trend Watch
**Working headline:** "Your Headrest Could Pop Off in a Crash. Volvo's Fix: More Glue."
**Slug:** 1222-volvo-ex30-headrest-cushion-recall

## The news

- **NHTSA campaign 26V671000**, Volvo internal ref **R10396**. Filed with NHTSA **October 9, 2026**; VINs searchable the same day.
- **U.S. scope: ~9,900 vehicles** — the NHTSA report cites 9,872 (8,770 EX30 + 1,102 EX30 Cross Country, MY 2025–2026 and 2026 CC per USA Today); KBB reports 9,930. Discrepancy is publication rounding; use "about 9,900."
- **Global scope: ~234,000 EX30s** (MY 2024–2027) per Volvo press statement (Road Ethos). Sweden: 15,000+. Denmark: 5,542. Australia: 4,141 (MY24–26, recall REC-006745).
- **Defect:** front-seat head restraint cushion insufficiently fastened. In a severe crash (high speed, impacts from multiple directions), the cushion may detach from the head restraint, reducing head/neck support and raising injury risk.
- **No injuries or fatalities reported.** Volvo says it's acting preventively.
- **Remedy:** dealers apply **two-component epoxy adhesive at three attachment points** with specified tools. Physical testing confirmed "a clear improvement in attachment strength." Free of charge. Owner notification letters begin **November 30, 2026** (phased).
- **Chronology (per NHTSA report, via Autoblog):** September 2025 — Volvo received information about a potential attachment issue → joint internal investigation with the supplier, **Magna Seating Baoji** → about a year later, Volvo classified it as critical and launched the recall.
- **Defect rate:** NHTSA report says **100% of the affected vehicles are defective** (per Autoblog).

## The angle (kill-test verdict: PASS)

Volvo is the company whose brand IS safety (three-point seatbelt, WHIPS whiplash system). The head restraint exists for one job: catch your skull in the first milliseconds of a crash. Whiplash is the most common crash injury. On 234,000 of its newest EVs, that cushion was attached so poorly that a severe crash can pop it off, and the engineering remedy is **dealer-applied epoxy**. Plus: Volvo sat on the information for a full year before classifying it critical — from September 2025 to October 2026. Fresh (11 days old), global, ironic, and a 100% defect rate gives the story teeth.

## Actionable takeaway

EX30/EX30 Cross Country owners: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now — don't wait for the November 30 letter. The repair is free. Volvo customer service: 1-800-458-1552 (ref R10396).
