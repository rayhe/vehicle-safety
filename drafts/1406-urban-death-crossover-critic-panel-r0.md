## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10

Sentence rhythm passes all three gates with margin (variance 830.5 vs 200 floor; short 0%; long 46.7%). The merged short sentences from the rhythm fix read naturally, not stitched. ## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victim-blaming: the article explicitly refuses to blame city drivers and shows the behavioral factors are identical (speeding 28/29%, alcohol 30/30%). It does not overclaim urban danger per mile; it corrects that misreading in its own text. Actionable advice is genuine and specific (night arterials, unlit two-lanes, intersections, belts). No sensationalized victim detail, no self-congratulation.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5/10
Headline is built to travel ("The Dying Moved to Town"). The pull stat and the 4,536 vs 3,694 excess/saved contrast are the shareable nuggets. Deduction: the counterfactual arithmetic, the article's most original finding, sits in paragraph 3 behind a "run the counterfactual" throat-clear; a social reader who stops after the lede misses the best number. Acceptable, not fatal.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Every figure traces to NHTSA DOT HS 813 828 (July 2026) or DOT HS 813 791, both linked in references with publication numbers. No legal claims, no case law, no statutory interpretation to get wrong. Disclaimer states the FARS ARF revision caveat, the boundary-reclassification caveat, and that the excess figures are the article's own arithmetic. One looseness: "8,518 people died in urban crashes last year" is urban *intersection* deaths; the sentence's antecedent is intersections, so the reading is fair, but a hostile reader could call it imprecise.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Original contribution is real and shown, not asserted: the excess-deaths arithmetic (urban +4,536, rural -3,694, net ~840) with inputs stated in-article, plus the crossover year (2016) named for the first time in the queue. Strongest counterargument (Census boundary expansion) is stated at full strength and conceded as real. Limitations are in the disclaimer, not buried. Deduction: the implied-VMT derivation (deaths divided by rate) is explained in the research file and summarized in the disclaimer, but the article itself does not show the VMT derivation step; a reader cannot reproduce the 2,268B figure from the article alone. Minor.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Counts and rates are kept properly distinct throughout; the article never confuses "more deaths" with "more dangerous per mile" and says so explicitly. All percentages independently re-verified against the fact sheet this round: urban +30.3%, rural -9.6%, rates +24.7%/-18.7%, shares 58.4%/40.8%, roadway departure 62.7%, intersection 72.4%, night 59.2%, dark-unlit 87.9%, speeding 28.1%/29.3%, alcohol 30.3%/30.4%, 2015 gap 142. One rounding looseness: "two-thirds of rural deaths are roadway departures" for 62.7%; defensible, noted.
