## 1. General Editor — 8.5
Lede earns its keep: nine recalls, one SUV at 73.4 percent, and the SUV is the one families comparison-shopped on safety. Structure is clean, chronological campaign rundown in two paragraphs, shares, the Telluride, the restraint thesis, the 1 percent club, corrections, the Palisade link, counterargument, action list. The dock is length. At 1,874 words this sits well above the recent census range (1,140-1,521), and the June paragraph plus the shares paragraph are dense blocks that ask a lot of a skimming reader. Nine campaigns, two corrections, and a fatality-adjacent link justify some of it, and none of the paragraphs is filler, but a table would have carried the nine counts in half the space. The piece never sags, and the length is justified by the material rather than indulgence.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0
Dale is present and distinct: scoreboard framing throughout ("check the box score", "the real scoreboard", "the 1 percent club"), sardonic asides that land ("which is regulator-speak for the driver's seat belt does not exist", "either innumerate or selling something"), and a statistical voice that never slips into another journalist's register. Rhythm metrics pass with wide margin; the sentence-length spread is genuinely human. Zero banned phrases, zero em dashes, The-starters at 1.3%. Minor tell: "Fairness requires the other side of the ledger" is the census-series seam the #1518 panel already flagged, and "Both of those sentences are true at the same time" recurs from the same template. Two seams, neither breaking the voice. Ship it.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
The Palisade fatality is handled with a bright line, stated twice: the death belongs to Hyundai's campaign, not Kia's, and the article keeps that line explicit rather than implying a Kia body count. The child is named only by age and date, no name, no gore. No deaths or injuries are tied to any of the nine Kia campaigns in the filings, and the article says so rather than implying otherwise. Blame lands on the manufacturer, on suppliers, and on a design review that let a new Telluride ship with three seat and belt defects, never on owners, and the call to check VINs is legitimate civic action with a real mechanism behind it. The "do not put a child in the front passenger seat" guidance for 26V545 is accurate and proportionate. No self-congratulation, no moralizing.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0
The headline is built to travel, and the pull stat (775,093) is the number people will quote. Quotable lines are spread through the piece rather than front-loaded: "the driver's seat belt does not exist" in the campaign rundown, "either innumerate or selling something" in the 1 percent club, and "a restraint-system year" as the thesis. The Telluride-at-73-percent framing survives a screenshot on its own. The piece loses a point only because the shares paragraph, the densest block, is the least skimmable part of an otherwise quotable article. Fine for the site's audience; the back half carries its weight.

Kia SC numbers (361, 362, 364, 366, 368, 372, 374, 375, 379) all match the filings. FMVSS citations are correct per campaign (101/138 for 26V046; 209 for 26V356; 208 for 26V545). Supplier names match the filings (DAS North America, Samsong Mexico, Nidec Mobility Korea, Usui International Korea, SK On). Arithmetic verified: tally sums to 775,093; Telluride 568,715 at 73.4%; restraint-system 591,376 at 76.3%; stripped total 312,224; ~8,810 estimated defective. The 1 percent count was corrected pre-score from five to four after source verification. One nit: reference [1] links to a sample API query URL rather than the full query set; the raw results are saved with the research file, so the citation is still verifiable, but a methods note link would be stronger.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0
Original contribution is real and stated: the 9-campaign corrected-vocabulary census (~270 API queries, with the 26V173 vocabulary catch documented), the 4-to-9 correction of the site's own prior count, the Telluride share, the restraint-system thesis, the 1 percent arithmetic, the re-recall correction (26V430 replaces 24V407), the Samsong Mexico thread, and the Palisade link. Limitations are explicit and honest: populations are campaign counts not unique vehicles, defect-rate estimates are Kia's own figures, 26V046's and 26V135's rates were not published, the Palisade fatality belongs to Hyundai, the API 400 and vocabulary risks are disclosed with the direction of bias stated, no FARS data exists for 2026 Kias, and the filing window ends November 15. The counterargument is genuinely steelmanned ("the tally is big, the danger per vehicle is small, and every fix is free"). Methodology caveat is in the disclaimer as well as the body. Eleven references, four of them primary NHTSA documents plus the live API.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0
The pull stat is the thesis number and it is correct. Campaign data is presented chronologically in prose with each population attached to its defect mode, which reads better than a table for this story, though a table would have helped the length problem the General Editor flagged. All numbers are consistent between body, pull stat, and disclaimer, and the campaign-population vs unique-vehicle distinction is stated in both the body and the disclaimer. The multi-brand vs Kia-only distinction is handled correctly: 26V232's 141,032 is the Kia share of a 235,792 combined filing with Genesis, 26V431's 8 is Kia-only against Hyundai's twin 26V432, and 26V046's 42,677 is Kia-only against Hyundai's twin 26V047. The defect estimates are presented as Kia's filed figures, not as the article's own claim. No chart needed: the story is a count, and the count is the chart.
