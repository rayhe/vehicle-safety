Honda’s Cars Keep Turning Themselves Off at Green Lights. NHTSA Just Opened a Second Probe

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 16, 2026

On Monday, November 16, 2026, the federal government opened its second investigation into Honda cars that turn themselves off and refuse to turn back on. This one covers 1,033,632 Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline, Pilot and Acura MDX vehicles from model years 2024 through 2026, and it exists because the first investigation never really ended.[1]

2
Simultaneous federal engineering analyses into the same symptom, engine fails to restart after auto stop-start, on overlapping model years of the same cars.

Four years. Two probes. One symptom. In June 2022, NHTSA’s Office of Defects Investigation opened a preliminary evaluation after owners of 2016–2019 Honda Pilots reported the same story: Auto Idle Stop shut the engine off at a traffic light, and the engine would not restart on its own.[2] Honda answered in January 2023 with two service bulletins, a software update first and then a hardware warranty extension, a two-stage fix that was supposed to close the book. Complaints kept arriving anyway, many from owners who said the fix had already been applied to their cars, so in March 2025 the agency upgraded the case to Engineering Analysis EA25004 and widened it to roughly 2.2 million vehicles across six nameplates.[4]

Run the density math, because the raw counts hide the comparison. EA25004 collected 1,861 reports across about 2.2 million vehicles, roughly 85 complaints per 100,000 cars, over a population that spans a decade of model years. Today’s probe has 701 complaints across 1,033,632 vehicles, 67.8 per 100,000, on cars that are one to three years old. A defect that is complaining at four-fifths the rate of the one that already earned an engineering analysis, and doing it on nearly new cars, is not a footnote. Both numbers are floors, not rates, since most stranded drivers never file with NHTSA at all, and some of the EA25004 reports allege a jump start was needed just to get moving again.[3]

Now the part that makes this a story instead of a sequel. Model years 2024 and 2025 sit under both engineering analyses at the same time. EA25004 covers them as newer, substantially similar vehicles. Today’s probe covers them as its own population, with NHTSA stating the new group shows different root causes and responds differently to repairs. Same cars, same symptom, two federal files, two different ways to not restart. Honda’s own dealer messages from October 2025 show the company was still hunting specimens a full year into the first engineering analysis, asking dealers to flag 2023–2025 Pilots with no-restart complaints and inspect them before any repair, while admitting the push button ignition may or may not restart the engine.[5] When the manufacturer tells its dealers not to fix the car yet because it wants to see the failure first, the failure is not understood.

Context matters, because this was a bad fortnight for Honda either way. One week earlier, the company recalled 635,808 Pilots, MDXs and TLXs after steering software was found to divide by zero and switch off power steering assist, a recall this site covered as #1457. Different system, different defect, same fourteen days. A car company can survive one software embarrassment. Two in a week, one of them a four-year-old failure that just spawned a second federal probe, starts to look like a pattern instead of bad luck.

Fair hearing, because the other side has one. No crashes or injuries appear in the Reuters account of the new probe, and none are claimed in the EA25004 documents I reviewed. Seven hundred and one complaints across 1,033,632 vehicles is 0.07 percent of the population, and complaint counts are self-selected. Auto Idle Stop saves real fuel across a million cars, which is a real emissions benefit, and most owners will never see the failure. An engineering analysis is an investigation, not a defect finding and not a recall. NHTSA may close it without action. All true. Also true: a dead engine at an intersection is a hazard even when nobody crashes, because the driver behind you cannot tell your engine is off, and because a driver who cannot restart with the push button either, which Honda’s own dealer message admits happens, is stranded in live traffic with no workaround except a jump start.

Honest limits, because this file has them. No investigation number for the new probe appeared in the public reporting I could verify, so this article names the date and the population instead of guessing a number. Complaint counts are allegations, not confirmed defects, and the 1,861 figure for EA25004 and the 701 figure for the new probe come from different dates and different scopes, so they are not directly additive. Roughly 2.2 million is the contemporary reporting figure for the EA25004 population, not an NHTSA population count, so the density comparison is an approximation and says so. FARS covers only one of the five affected nameplates, the Acura MDX at 163 deaths and a 0.30 rate, so no fatality-rate claim is made for the other four. And no recall exists for the new probe’s population. There is nothing to get fixed yet, which is itself part of the story.

What to do: if you own a 2024–2026 Odyssey, Passport, Ridgeline, Pilot or Acura MDX, every one of these has an Auto Idle Stop off button on the console. Press it every drive until this resolves, since the feature resets to on at each start. If the engine ever fails to restart after idle stop, file a Vehicle Owner Questionnaire at nhtsa.gov. Those 701 complaints are what opened this probe; the count is the mechanism. If the push button will not restart it either, do not keep cranking. That is the scenario in Honda’s own dealer messages. Call for a jump or a tow, keep the paperwork, and note the date and the intersection. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, too. Nothing will show for the new probe yet, because it is not a recall, but the 2024–2025 models also sit in EA25004, and any future recall will appear there first.
