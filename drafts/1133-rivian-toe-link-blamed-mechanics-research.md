# Research: Rivian Blamed Its Mechanics for a Falling-Apart Rear Suspension. NHTSA Isn't Buying It.

Slug: `1133-rivian-toe-link-blamed-mechanics`
Journalist: Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — component-level failure analysis)
Number: 1133

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. NHTSA opened a live federal investigation into whether a rear-suspension joint on ~115,000 Rivian trucks and SUVs can separate at speed, and the dispute at the center is philosophical: Rivian says it was a wrench-turning problem confined to trucks that were serviced wrong; NHTSA suspects the bolt joint itself is fragile under foreseeable conditions. A recall that blames technicians while the bolts keep fracturing is exactly The Crash Report's beat. Novel angle: the recall fixed 19,641 trucks; the probe covers 114,922.

## Primary sources
1. NHTSA ODI Preliminary Evaluation PE26004 (opened May 26, 2026) — rear suspension toe link bolt fracture, estimated population 114,922 R1T/R1S, MY 2022-2025. Two Vehicle Owner Questionnaires (2023 and 2024 R1S) reporting left rear toe link separation while driving; one crash (swerved across lanes, struck another vehicle and a roadside barrier); onboard video, photographs, repair records, and a police crash report collected. Two incidents, one crash, no injuries or fatalities per the failure summary. Probe examines the joint's sensitivity to expected road and service conditions and Rivian's current repair procedures. Source: NHTSA investigations database.
2. NHTSA Safety Recall 26V003000 (filed Jan 5, 2026, Rivian FSAM-1794) — 19,641 vehicles (12,610 R1T, 7,031 R1S, built Aug 23, 2021 - Mar 6, 2025). Root cause stated: rear toe link joint may not have been reassembled to design intent during service between Apr 1, 2022 and Mar 10, 2025 using the older service procedure; Rivian switched to updated procedure March 10, 2025. Remedy: replace rear toe link bolts (part SC00002057-A, M14x2.0x80 class 10.9 flange bolt) free at dealer. Rivian estimated ~1% of recalled population had the defect. Source: NHTSA recall database.
3. Failure mechanics (engineering analysis): the rear toe link is the rod that holds rear-wheel toe alignment; bolt fracture means the rear wheel is free to steer itself, typically toeing out under load — the vehicle yaws without driver input. One complaint vehicle had prior service; the other had only a prior collision, months and thousands of miles separating the event from the failure. Source: NHTSA PE summary + WardsAuto/TorqueNews coverage of the questionnaires.
4. Rivian's position: internal investigation concluded "R1 toe link joints are operating as intended"; the company attributes failures to incorrect service assembly, not design. NHTSA is proceeding with the PE anyway — the dispute is whether the joint is overly sensitive to foreseeable assembly and road conditions. Source: Rivian statement via Road & Track; NHTSA PE26004.
5. Context: toe link separation has no graceful degradation mode — unlike a soft tire, a rear wheel going to full toe-out at highway speed produces sudden yaw that a driver's steering correction amplifies. Relevant precedent: rear-suspension separation defects historically triggered large recalls (e.g., GM truck control arms). The 6,215 annual U.S. deaths in medium/heavy-vehicle crashes (IIHS) is a different population but underscores how suspension/control-loss failures scale. Source: IIHS fatality statistics.

## Numbers (all sourced)

- 19,641: vehicles under recall 26V003000
- 1 crash / 2 incidents / 0 injuries (per NHTSA failure summary)
- M14x2.0x80 class 10.9 flange bolt, part SC00002057-A
- Suspect service window: Apr 1, 2022 - Mar 10, 2025
- Recall filed Jan 5, 2026; PE opened May 26, 2026 (5 months after Rivian said it was fixed)

## Actionable insight
Own a 2022-2025 R1T or R1S: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V003000; if you had ANY rear-suspension service before March 2025, get the bolt replacement done. Even trucks outside the recall should ask the service center to check toe-link torque at the next visit while PE26004 is open — the bolt is the load path, and there is no warning light for a fractured fastener.
