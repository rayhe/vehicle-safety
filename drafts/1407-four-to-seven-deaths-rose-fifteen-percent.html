Deaths Fell 4.3% in 2024. For 4-to-7-Year-Olds, They Rose 15%.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 6, 2026

2024 was the year American roads finally started healing, with deaths down 4.3%. Pedestrian deaths fell, speeding deaths fell, drunk-driving deaths fell. Then comes the line in NHTSA's own report that ruins the press release: children aged 4 to 7 died 15% more often than the year before, 244 dead against 212 in 2023.[1] Babies under 1 did worse still, up 17%. Every other child age group fell or held flat.

244
4-to-7-year-olds killed in traffic crashes in 2024, up from 212 in 2023 — NHTSA Children 2024 fact sheet

Put the two youngest brackets together. Under-1s plus 4-to-7s went from 277 dead in 2023 to 320 in 2024, a 15.5% jump, while the national toll dropped by 1,771. That is a 19.4-point divergence between the kids in booster seats and everyone else. Three children a day died on American roads in 2024, and an estimated 439 more were injured daily.[1]

Most of these kids died as passengers: 783 of the 1,032 killed were occupants, 249 nonoccupants. Here is where the anger belongs: of the 677 children killed as passenger-vehicle occupants, restraint use was known for 585, and 229 of them, 39%, were unrestrained. For the 4-to-7 group, 50 of 148 killed occupants with known restraint status were unrestrained. A booster seat cuts fatal injury risk roughly in half for kids this age, yet a third of the dead 4-to-7-year-olds were riding with nothing holding them in place.[1]

Adults set the example. When the driver was belted, 71% of the children who died were belted too. When the driver was unbelted, 65% of the dead children were unbelted as well. Kids do not buckle themselves; their parents decide, every single trip, and in 2024 the parents who skipped their own belts mostly skipped their kids' too. Alcohol compounded it: 221 of the 1,032 child deaths, 21%, happened in alcohol-impaired crashes.[1]

Now the honest counterweight. Thirty-two additional deaths is a small count, and one multi-fatality crash can swing a bracket this size by several percent. The 2024 file is still an Annual Report File, subject to revision when finalized. Over the full decade, 4-to-7 deaths are down 13% from 282 in 2015, and total child deaths are down 10%. This might be noise, but the pattern has a shape noise does not usually make: the two groups that rose are the two that cannot protect themselves, while every group old enough to buckle its own belt improved. Nonmotorist deaths among 5-to-9-year-olds jumped 32% in the same year, and the school-transportation review found 55% of school-age pedestrians killed over the last decade were 5 to 10, two-thirds away from intersections, deadliest hour 7 to 8 a.m.[3][5]

What to do with this, concretely. A child belongs in a booster seat until the adult belt fits properly, usually 4 feet 9 inches tall and between 8 and 12, not when they turn 4 and start complaining about the baby seat. If the belt rides up on the belly instead of lying flat on the hips, it is a belt that will injure, not protect. Buckle yourself first, because the data says your belt is your kid's belt. Slow down on residential streets before school, not just inside marked school zones. Two-thirds of the school-age kids killed on foot died away from intersections, in the morning, where nobody was expecting them.[1]
