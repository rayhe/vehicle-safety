# Research: Nissan Maxima — "Four-Door Sports Car" Kills at 2.5× the Camry's Rate

## Core Data (FARS 2014-2023)
- **Deaths:** 1,544
- **Crashes:** 2,331 (fatal crash involvements)
- **Rate:** 5.11 deaths per 100M VMT
- **Lethality:** 66.2% (occupant dies in 66.2% of fatal crash involvements)
- **Fleet:** ~262,500
- **Annual deaths:** 154.4/year
- **Impairment:** 20.9% any (536 of 2,570 drivers), 15.8% alcohol, 9.4% drug — moderate, not extreme

## Model Year Deaths
- MY 2000: 134, MY 2001: 102, MY 2002: 67, MY 2003: 44
- MY 2004: 137, MY 2005: 75, MY 2006: 74, MY 2007: 66, MY 2008: 45
- MY 2009: 56, MY 2010: 79, MY 2011: 48, MY 2012: 74, MY 2013: 57
- MY 2014: 68, MY 2016: 69, MY 2017: 71, MY 2018: 24, MY 2019: 10, MY 2020: 12, MY 2022: 5
- 4th gen (2000-2003): ~347 deaths
- 5th gen (2004-2008): ~397 deaths
- 6th gen (2009-2014): ~382 deaths
- 8th gen (2016-2023): ~191 deaths (newer, smaller fleet contributing less)
- Discontinued after 2023 model year

## Key Angles
1. **"Four-Door Sports Car" branding:** Nissan marketed the Maxima as a "4DSC" — performance sedan. Rate of 5.11 is 2.5× the Camry, 1.8× its own Altima sibling.
2. **Lethality problem:** 66.2% is highest among major midsize sedans. When a Maxima is in a fatal crash, 2 in 3 times it's the Maxima occupant who dies. Camry lethality is 59.3%.
3. **Not purely a behavior story:** 20.9% impairment is moderate — below the Accord (needs check), roughly average for sedans. The car structure + driver demographics are the issue.
4. **Discontinued 2023:** Another ghost vehicle in the making. Fleet will age with no new models replacing crashed units. Similar trajectory to Xterra.
5. **Within-brand gap:** Maxima (5.11) vs Altima (2.88) vs Sentra (2.13) — Nissan's own lineup shows a clear hierarchy: the sportier the marketing, the higher the death rate.
6. **MY 2004 spike:** 137 deaths — peak year, coincides with 5th-gen launch (VQ35DE, 265hp V6). Performance attracted aggressive driving without commensurate safety.
