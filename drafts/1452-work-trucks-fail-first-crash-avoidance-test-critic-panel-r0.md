## Scores

### 1. General Editor — 8.9/10
Lede states the finding before any number lands, then the van table, the pickup failure, the regulatory cliff, action, and counterargument in a clean arc. The headline carries the whole thesis. The action paragraph serves four audiences at once (fleet buyers, van shoppers, pickup owners, everyone else) and gets slightly crowded, but each beat earns its sentence. Length matches recent longform pieces (#1451: 990 prose words).

### 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10
Clara is unmistakable: direct, practical, angry on behalf of the buyer. "Drive like your truck's crash prevention is decorative, because IIHS just showed that it mostly is." "The good van is already dead. The vans that can't stop are still on sale." No banned phrases, zero em dashes, 1.3% "The" starters, rhythm gate passes clean. One stock transition: "So what do you do with it?" does no work a sharper hinge could not do better. Not worth a revision round.

### 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
All figures are aggregate; no individual victim is mined for color. The child-dummy passage is factual and mechanistic, never exploitative. Blame lands on manufacturers and the weight cutoff in the rule, not on drivers. The counterargument is stated at full strength rather than strawmanned, and the limitations are explicit, including that the death figure counts crashes involving these vehicles, not caused by them. No self-congratulation, no moralizing about other drivers.

### 4. Social / Shareability — 8.8/10
Headline is a strong share trigger: a 14,000-pound truck that cannot stop itself. "One pound of rated capacity" is the hook a reader repeats at dinner. "A beep where a brake pedal should be" is quotable. Pull stat 9 of 11 is the most surprising number in the piece. Slightly weaker than the top of the range because the mechanism (the GVWR cutoff) needs the lede to land; a reader who only sees the index card gets the hook without the one-pound punchline.

### 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
Citations check out: IIHS October 8, 2026 release, NHTSA FMVSS 127 release, Automotive Fleet October 22, 2026, Nelson Mullins on the FMVSS 127 litigation, NHTSA 2024 overview. No legal claims are made that would need case law. The one soft spot: the BrightDrop production end is stated as fact in the body with a trade-coverage citation; the research file flags it as trade coverage rather than a GM statement, and the body could carry that hedge. The citation is present and the fact is correct, so this is a wording preference, not an error.

### 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution: the 1-pound regulatory cliff (10,000 vs 10,001 lbs), the 9-of-11 substandard rate, the 15.8% death share computed against the 2024 total, the zero-for-four Class 3 pickup result, the $55,000 fleet-economics framing, and the discontinued-good-van observation. Methodology is transparent (counts and ratios shown with their denominators) and the limitations section names the real weaknesses: lab protocols vs real-world outcomes, unloaded testing, involvement vs causation, trade-sourced BrightDrop claim. The strongest counterargument is given a full paragraph, not a sentence.

### 7. Data Presentation — 8.9/10
Every claim carries both the count and the rate, and ratios are anchored so a reader can feel the scale (16% of all deaths, 17 a day, $55,000 a crash). The full 11-vehicle table is rendered accurately in prose, and the pull stat is the most surprising number. One gap: the pedestrian ratings for Transit and ProMaster (Marginal) are implied rather than stated, and the full table lives only behind the IIHS link. The vehicle-to-vehicle table is complete, which is the test the article is about, so this costs a tenth, not a round.
