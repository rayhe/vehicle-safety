## News peg
Euro NCAP published a new scored test in its 2026 protocol: cars are graded on whether **key driving controls have physical buttons**. Results released Oct 12-14, 2026.

## Verified facts
- Euro NCAP updated its 2026 testing procedures to award points for physical buttons on key driving controls: indicators, lights, wipers, washers, mirror adjustment. Rationale: physical controls are safer than routing everything through a touchscreen. (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- Scoring: a percentage for "essential driving tasks" (lights, wipers) and one for "comfort and infotainment" (heated seats, radio). The combined score feeds the overall star rating. (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- Audi Q7: 90% in this category, split 100% essential driving tasks / 80% comfort and infotainment. (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- Xpeng G9: 20% for essential driving controls, 80% for comfort/infotainment; mirrors and lights run through the touchscreen. (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- 2026 scoreboard so far: BMW iX3 and Audi Q7 scored 100% for key driving controls; Cupra Raval 80%; Geely EX2 80% (the only Chinese exception). Xpeng G9, Zeekr 7GT, and Aion UT scored 20%; the Leapmotor B05 scored 0%. Euro NCAP notes "clear differences between European manufacturers and Chinese carmakers." (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- **Every one of those cars still earned the full five-star overall rating**, making up the lost points elsewhere. The Leapmotor B05 scored 92% for crash protection while scoring 0% for key driving controls. (carwow, Oct 12, 2026)
- Audi Q7 also became the first production model to earn 100% in Euro NCAP's post-crash safety category (audi.com press release, Oct 14, 2026). Its other 2026 scores: 88% crash protection, 77% crash avoidance, 72% safe driving (carbike360 / tuningblog, Oct 2026).
- BYD Shark pickup: five stars under the 2026 protocol (Oct 12, 2026): adult occupant 82%, child 86%, vulnerable road users 74%, safety assist 87%. (evmagz; highways.today)
- Context: Sweden's Vi Bilagare re-ran its cabin-distraction test in 2026 across ten new models: completing four basic tasks (temperature, radio, trip meter, interior lights) at 68 mph took an average 2,667 feet, a 7.5% regression from 2022. (The Crash Report, July 2026)

## The novel angle (kill test: PASS)
Cross-tabulate the new 2026 controls scores against the star ratings: the five-star badge tells you a car survives a crash, but the test measuring the thing you touch every 30 seconds of driving now lives in a sub-score ("Safe Driving") almost nobody reads before buying. A car can score **0% on the controls you use constantly and 92% on crash protection and still wear five stars**. That inversion has never been published as a buying-guide story. Actionable: look up any model on euroncap.com and check its Safe Driving sub-score before signing.

## Limitations (for the article)
- Euro NCAP is a European program; model lineups, trims, and equipment differ by market, and several 2026-tested Chinese models are not sold in the US.
- The controls score is one slice of the overall rating; five stars still requires top-tier crash and avoidance performance.
- NHTSA's US NCAP does not score physical controls; American shoppers have no domestic equivalent of the Safe Driving sub-score.
- Touchscreens are not inherently unsafe: voice control, steering-wheel shortcuts, and automation can reduce eyes-off-road time; Euro NCAP's weighting reflects a judgment, not a crash-outcome study.

## Strongest counterargument (for the article)
The five-star system is working as designed: it rewards the things that kill people (crash structure, AEB, post-crash rescue) more than the things that annoy them. A Leapmotor B05 with 92% crash protection and 0% button score will still protect you better in the crash that actually happens than a four-star car with perfect knobs. Buttons are a preference; crumple zones are physics.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Before buying: look the model up on euroncap.com and check the "Safe Driving" sub-score, not just the stars.
- On the test drive: try the wipers, indicators, and hazard lights without looking at a screen. If you cannot, that is the 0%.
- US shoppers: NHTSA does not grade this, so the Euro NCAP page is the only public scoreboard, and only for models sold in Europe.
