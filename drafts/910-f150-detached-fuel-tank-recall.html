Ford's Newest F-150s Can Drop Their Fuel Tanks on the Highway. The Trucks Actually Killing People Are Old Enough to Vote.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 16, 2026

Ford filed recall 26S69 on September 9. The defect reads like a prank. On 223,472 F-150s built between January 2023 and August 2026, the front fuel tank straps may never have been properly seated in the frame rail. Translation: the fuel tank can detach from the truck while it is moving. A box of gasoline, becoming road debris at 70 miles an hour. Sweet dreams.[1][5]

The how is worse than the what. Ford's plants in Dearborn and Kansas City had camera systems to verify the strap assembly. Those cameras were, in NHTSA's delicate phrasing, "not in continuous use" for three and a half years. Quality control was a camera that was not always on. Inspiring. Sixteen warranty claims accumulated globally before Ford connected the dots. Nobody was hurt. Ford estimates one percent of the recalled trucks are actually affected, which still means a couple thousand trucks are driving around with a fuel tank held in by optimism, which is not a recognized fastening method in any engineering discipline I am aware of.[2]

Now pull the fatality data, because this is where it gets properly bleak. FARS codes 9,194 F-150 deaths between 2014 and 2023. Model year 2001 leads the grim parade with 672. Trucks from 2005 and older account for 59 percent of the coded deaths. Everything 2015 and newer accounts for 11 percent. The trucks Ford just recalled, model years 2023 through 2027, barely register in the death record at all. Read that again, because it matters: Ford is recalling its newest trucks for a defect that has hurt nobody, while the F-150's body count keeps coming from trucks old enough to vote, drive, and buy their own insurance.[4]

To be fair to Ford, and I hate doing this: recalls are supposed to target defects, not death counts. A detached fuel tank at highway speed is a genuine catastrophe: fire, stall, a 200-pound road hazard for everyone behind you. Sixteen warranty claims and zero injuries is the safety system working, catching a manufacturing slip through warranty-pattern analysis before it ever became a fatality statistic, which is precisely the outcome every recall is supposed to deliver and that almost none of them actually do. Those old trucks dominating the death record are not defective; they are just old. No ESC, rusted frames, worn suspension, drivers who bought the cheapest truck on the lot. You cannot recall a 2004 truck into being a 2024 truck. Fixing the thing Ford broke is the right move even if the thing Ford broke has not killed anyone.

What this article did not prove: FARS data ends at 2023, so 2023-to-2027 trucks have almost no exposure in the record; comparing their death share to 2001's is not apples to apples. Model-year coding in FARS has gaps: 9,194 total deaths, 9,118 with coded years. The sixteen warranty claims and the one-percent estimate both come from Ford's own filing; there is no independent count of how many tanks are actually loose. And the camera gap ran from January 2023 to August 2026, which means the newest trucks on dealer lots right now are in the recall population. That last one is not a limitation; it is the point.[4]

What you can actually do: If you own a 2023-to-2027 F-150, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today. VINs have been searchable since September 14, and the mailed letters go out September 21.[3] Inspection is free at any Ford or Lincoln dealer. Do not wait for the letter. Seriously. If you are shopping used, understand what the numbers say: the F-150's death record is dominated by trucks built between 2000 and 2008. A cheap 2004 is a statistically different machine from a 2024, and no recall will ever change that.[4]
