82,843 Rams Forgot How Their Own Brake Pedals Work

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 22, 2026

Chrysler just recalled 82,843 Ram 1500 Classic pickups because the software that watches the brake pedal cannot keep up with the pedal itself.[1] Brake pedals drift. Bushings wear, linkages settle, springs weaken, and over four model years the pedal sits a few millimeters from where it started. The ABS module was supposed to track that drift. It did not, and the failure cascades through three safety systems at once.

11,371
Records in FCA's own file tied to this defect before the recall, including 6,001 warranty claims

Here is what one bad adaptation routine actually does. First, the brake lights lie. They can glow while nobody is touching the pedal, which tells the driver behind you that you are slowing down when you are not, and teaches them to ignore your brake lights entirely. Second, the truck can shift out of Park without a foot on the brake, which is the interlock failing at the one job it exists to do. Third, and worst, the module eventually gives up and takes electronic stability control, AutoPark, and forward collision warning down with it.[1] One routine takes three protections with it.

FCA knew, because by October 21, 2026, its own files already held 12 customer assistance reports, 48 field reports, 5,310 other service records, and 6,001 warranty claims potentially tied to the defect.[3] Add them up and the total is 11,371 records, with warranty claims alone outnumbering the other three categories combined. That is not a defect hiding in the dark. That is a defect sitting in the warranty database, filed under routine paperwork, waiting for somebody to notice the pattern. The recall decision came after the count, not before it, and the fix is a software flash, free at any Chrysler dealer.

There is a coda worth your attention if you have ever paid for a repair out of pocket. Chrysler also recalled 669 Mopar ABS control modules sold as replacement parts, campaign 26E084, carrying the same software.[4] Somebody already bought the bug once, installed it in their truck, and now has to have it flashed. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting November 26, when the campaign goes searchable, and set the parking brake every time you park until the flash is done, on flat ground, in your own driveway, everywhere. The Park interlock is the part of this truck you can no longer trust, and the parking brake is the backup that still works.

What this recall does not say matters too. No crashes or injuries are attributed to the defect in the reporting so far, and the exact model-year split inside the 82,843 is not public, so do not go looking for a clean villain model year. And the honest objection, stated at full strength: a software recall is the cheap kind, a download instead of a tow, and FCA found this one in its own warranty data before regulators forced the issue, which counts for something. But a software bug that quietly deletes stability control, the system IIHS credits with cutting fatal single-vehicle crashes roughly in half, is still a safety bug.[5] The Park interlock is not a convenience feature. It is the thing standing between a parked truck and a sloped driveway.
