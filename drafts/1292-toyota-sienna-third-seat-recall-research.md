# Research: #1292 — Toyota's third Sienna seat recall in 20 months

**Slug:** 1292-toyota-sienna-third-seat-recall
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — paradox beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** 2026-10-27

## Kill test
PASS. This is the third separate Sienna seat recall in under two years, each
with a different failure mechanism, and the newest one is a genuine paradox:
the device that is supposed to save you in a crash — the seatbelt
pretensioner — is the trigger that unlocks the seat. NHTSA campaign 26V690
covers 228,367 model-year 2022-2026 Sienna Hybrid 7-passenger vans whose
second-row seatback recliner locks can disengage when the pretensioner fires,
so the seatback may not stay put in the crash the pretensioner is responding
to. Transport Canada goes further than the US notice: it tells owners not to
use the second-row seats to carry passengers until the repair is done — the
row where the kids sit. No prior Crash Report draft covers 26V690 (checked
2026-10-27: no drafts, no queue entries for this campaign; the two earlier
Sienna seat recalls are covered only as background here).

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- NHTSA campaign **26V690000** (report received **October 20, 2026**):
  certain **2022-2026 Toyota Sienna Hybrid 7-passenger vehicles**. "The
  second-row seat backs may fail to remain secured in the event of a crash."
  Consequence: a seat back that is not secured may fail to properly protect
  an occupant, increasing injury risk. Component:
  SEATS:MID/REAR ASSEMBLY:RECLINER. Remedy: dealers update the supplemental
  restraint system (SRS) electronic control unit (ECU) software; for
  vehicles with the "Mobility as a Service" (MaaS) package, dealers replace
  the two second-row seat backs. Free of charge. Owner letters expected
  **December 7, 2026**. Toyota customer service 1-800-331-4331; Toyota
  numbers **26TB24 and 26TA24**. (NHTSA recalls API record, via NHTSA
  database.)
- US population: **228,367 vehicles** (Reuters, October 27, 2026, citing
  NHTSA). Reuters adds the mechanism in plain language: "in certain frontal
  crashes, the force could disengage the seat back from its locked position,
  failing to properly restrain occupants."
- Transport Canada recall **2026505** (published October 20, 2026, updated
  October 23): same defect, same model years, **24,000+ vehicles in Canada**.
  Mechanism stated precisely: "the mechanisms that lock the second-row seat
  recliners in position may disengage when a seat belt pretensioner
  activates." Corrective action: "Toyota advises not using the second-row
  seats to transport passengers until the recall repairs have been
  completed"; dealers update the SRS ECU software that controls the
  pretensioners. (Transport Canada / Canadian Press, October 23, 2026.)
- Recall 1 (February 2025): NHTSA **25V086**, **167,802** model-year
  2021-2025 Sienna Hybrids. Third-row 60/40 seatback bolts may not have been
  tightened to specification at the factory; fails FMVSS 202, 207, 225.
  Remedy: dealers tighten the bolts. Toyota number 25TA05; owner letters
  from March 31, 2025. (NHTSA / Consumer Reports.)
- Recall 2 (October 2025): NHTSA **25V-668**, **54,631** model-year 2025
  Sienna Hybrids built January 14 – July 24, 2025. Second-row seat rails may
  be welded improperly (a welding-machine setting change reduced weld
  penetration); Toyota told NHTSA 100% of vans in that build window are
  affected. Remedy: dealers replace the second-row seat rails. Toyota
  numbers 25TA12 and 25TB12. (NHTSA / Kelley Blue Book.)
- Recall 2 aftermath: a California federal judge ruled **October 15, 2026**
  that warranty claims over the seat-rail recall may proceed; plaintiffs
  alleged that by April 1, 2026 Toyota had yet to repair a single affected
  vehicle and owners were told not to use the seats or install child
  restraints there. (Law Commentary, October 21, 2026; OpenClassActions
  summary of the Vera ruling.)
- Combined three-recall arithmetic: 167,802 + 54,631 + 228,367 = **450,800**
  recall actions across the three campaigns (overlapping populations, not
  450,800 distinct vans — a 2025 Sienna can appear in all three). The
  current-generation Sienna has been hybrid-only since its 2021 redesign.
- What is NOT established: no crash, injury, or fatality count tied to
  26V690 appears in the NHTSA record summary or the Reuters/CP reporting
  found for this article; do not invent one. The US notice as reported does
  not carry the "do not use the second-row seats" instruction — that
  instruction is Transport Canada's; attribute it as such.

## Angles / what to verify in draft
- The paradox: pretensioners fire in milliseconds to pull occupants tight
  against the seat; here that same firing can trip the recliner lock. A
  software retune of pretensioner behavior is the fix for a mechanical lock
  — the lock itself is not being redesigned for most vans.
- The pattern: three seat recalls, three mechanisms (torque, weld, lock
  logic), two rows, one model. The seat system, not one part, is the story.
- The practical sting: second row is the family row. A "do not use" order
  on it turns a 7-seat minivan into a 5-seat one until December-ish
  repairs, and the previous Sienna seat recall's repair delays already
  produced a lawsuit.
- Counterargument, stated fairly: 228,367 is the population at risk, not
  the number of failures; Toyota found this through its own processes
  (Canada notice October 20, US notice the same week); a software fix is
  fast once letters go out; and no injuries are attributed to 26V690 in
  the records found.
