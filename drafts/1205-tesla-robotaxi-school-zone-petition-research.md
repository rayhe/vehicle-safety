## Verified facts (with sources)
1. DP26010 opened Oct 9, 2026; petition received Sep 23, 2026; subject line "Exceeds posted speed limits in active school zones"; covers ~1,000 MY2026 Tesla Robotaxis (Model Y and Cybercab) with ADS engaged. First federal fleet-size number for the Robotaxi program (Tesla does not disclose it). — Electrek, Oct 12, 2026; TeslaNorth, Oct 12, 2026.
2. Evidence base: 3 video links in the petition; ODI resume tallies 4 incidents (1 ODI complaint + 3 social-media reports), 0 from manufacturer, 0 from EWR; 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injury incidents, 0 fatality incidents. — Self Drive News (reprints ODI failure-report summary table); neoTEO.
3. Process: defect petition review under 49 CFR Part 552; NHTSA has 120 days from filing to grant or deny, putting the deadline around **January 21, 2027**. A grant opens a formal defect investigation, which can end in a recall. Opening a petition is the first rung, not a recall, not a finding of defect. — Electrek; CBT News.
4. Context: third federal action against Tesla's driverless fleet in ~5 weeks. Earlier: Audit Query AQ26002 (opened Sep 3) on Cybercab certification (no steering wheel/pedals). Cybercab commercial service began Austin Sep 3; fleet expanded to Dallas and Houston; Cybercab fleet in Austin past 300. — Electrek; TeslaNorth; CBT News.
5. Precedent: January 2023, NHTSA raised concerns about FSD Beta adjusting speed in variable speed zones; Tesla recalled 362,758 vehicles with an OTA update. — CBT News.
6. Texas law: citations for a vehicle with engaged ADS go to the owner or the holder of the driverless operating authorization. — CBT News.
7. NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts, School-Transportation-Related Traffic Crashes 2015-2024 (FARS data): 962 fatal crashes, 1,069 deaths, avg 107/year, 0.3% of all fatal motor vehicle crashes. By person type: 70% occupants of other vehicles, 15% pedestrians, 11% school-vehicle occupants. 162 pedestrians vs 119 school-vehicle occupants (pedestrians "almost 1.5 times" more). 204 school-age children killed (39 bus occupants, 78 other-vehicle occupants, 77 pedestrians, 7 bicyclists, 3 other). 55% of school-age pedestrians killed were ages 5-10. Two-thirds of school-age pedestrian deaths occurred away from intersections; most deaths in the 7-7:59 a.m. hour. — schoolbusfleet.com (summarizing NHTSA report); trafficsafetymarketing.gov; ROSAP PDF (dot/92319).
8. National School Bus Safety Week: October 19-23, 2026. — trafficsafetymarketing.gov.
9. 2000-2024: 63 fatalities involved a driver illegally passing a stopped school bus (avg 2.5/year); ~half (27) were pedestrians 18 or younger. — trafficsafetymarketing.gov.

## Original calculations (show your work)
- Incident density: 4 incidents / 1,000 estimated vehicles = 0.4%. Inputs: ODI resume tally (4), NHTSA population estimate (1,000). This is the thinnest evidentiary base to open a federal file in the ODI ladder's bottom rung, where past escalations typically draw on hundreds of consumer complaints.
- Pedestrian-vs-occupant ratio: 162 / 119 = 1.36x. The school-transportation fatality burden falls more on people walking near the vehicles than people inside them.
- School-zone share of all traffic death: 107 avg/year ÷ ~40,000 annual U.S. traffic deaths ≈ 0.27% (0.3% per NHTSA). The file exists because of who is at risk (5-to-10-year-olds), not how many.
- Fleet-multiplication argument: a human speeder endangers one school zone per trip; an ADS speed-control defect endangers every school zone the fleet traverses simultaneously. Tesla's own 2023 FSD speed-zone recall (362,758 vehicles, one software change) is the empirical precedent that this failure mode scales.

## Limitations (must appear in article)
- DP26010 is a petition review, not a defect investigation; NHTSA has not found a defect.
- The resume names no cities, speeds, dates, or which videos were submitted; the videos are unverified.
- The 1,000-vehicle figure is an agency estimate, not a Tesla count; it may reflect Texas DMV certifications rather than active fleet.
- School-transportation FARS stats cover school buses/school-bus-functioning vehicles, not robotaxis; they contextualize school-zone risk, not this petition's claims.
- Zero reported harm means no crash risk is proven; the petition could be denied on or before Jan 21, 2027.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
The petition system is working exactly as designed, and the low bar is rational: software defects are fleet-wide. One bad school-zone speed map doesn't speed through one school zone, it speeds through every school zone the fleet passes at the same time, which is why the 2023 FSD speed-zone case ended with 362,758 cars recalled by one OTA push. School-zone speeding is the one moving violation treated as a bright moral line, and a system with no driver to ticket is precisely what a public petition process exists to catch early, while the fix is still cheap and nobody has been hurt. If anything, four videos triggering a 120-day federal review is the system catching the problem at the best possible moment: before the body count.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE)
1. Anyone can file a defect petition. NHTSA's ODI petition process is public; the DP26010 petitioner got a federal file (ODI no. 11766676) and a 120-day decision clock with 3 video links. Dashcam and phone video count as evidence. File at nhtsa.gov (ODI defect petition).
2. Know the risk windows: NHTSA's 2015-2024 data puts school-age pedestrian deaths heaviest in the 7-7:59 a.m. hour; the dismissal window (3-4 p.m.) is the other peak in earlier reports. If your kid's school sits near an Austin, Dallas, or Houston robotaxi route, those are the hours that matter.
3. National School Bus Safety Week is October 19-23, 2026. The thing most likely to kill a kid near a school is still a human illegally passing a stopped bus (avg 2.5 deaths/year, half of them minors). Watch the curb, not just the robots.
