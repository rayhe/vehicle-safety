Honda Recalled 1.7 Million Cars for Sticky Steering. The 2021 CR-V Got a Service Bulletin and a $2,200 Bill.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 24, 2026

Before you sign that repair order, you might want to see this. In October 2024, Honda recalled roughly 1.7 million vehicles because their steering could stick mid-turn, a defect federal campaign 24V-744 traced to a swelling worm wheel and an overtightened spring inside the steering gearbox.[1] The recall stopped at the 2023 model year, but the defect did not: steering is the single most-complained-about problem on the 2021 and 2022 CR-V, owners describe the identical failure in their own words, and Honda's published fix for those model years is a service bulletin that owners pay for themselves.

52.7%
Share of all 2022 CR-V owner complaints that are about steering. Zero steering recalls cover the vehicle.

I pulled every owner complaint NHTSA's public database returns for the 2021 and 2022 CR-V, then matched them against every recall on file. For the 2021, steering accounts for 111 of 330 complaints, a full third, with 4 crashes and 4 injuries attached; for the 2022, it is 135 of 256, more than half, and 103 of those complaints use the word "stick" or "sticky."[2] Four recalls exist for the 2021 CR-V, three for the 2022, and not one touches the steering.

Read the owners and the recall boundary starts looking arbitrary. A 2021 owner wrote this July, asking NHTSA to add the model year to 24V-744: "I am experiencing the 'notchy' steering wheel problem described however this model year is currently not included in the recall."[3] A 2022 owner, failure date September 5, 2026, reported "strong mechanical binding" and "noticeable clicking," noted the symptoms were "identical to the failure documented in American Honda's safety recall 24V-744," and filed specifically so NHTSA would "evaluate expanding campaign coverage retroactively." Another 2022 owner, told the repair follows Honda service bulletin 24-011, was quoted $2,200 with 16,500 miles on the odometer and nine months of expired warranty. One more was told to drive the car until it gets worse.

That bulletin is the quiet scandal here: Honda service bulletin A24-011, issued September 2024, documents "steering effort complaint while driving at moderate or highway speeds, with a possible complaint of a clicking noise at slow speeds while turning" for the 2021-2022 CR-V, and parts catalogs list the 5th-generation EPS gearbox assemblies under it.[4] A technical service bulletin is not a recall. Nobody mails you a letter, and nobody pays for the repair. It is the manufacturer publishing the diagnosis and the cure, then handing you the invoice, while the owner of the identical 2023 CR-V gets the same repair free under the federal campaign. Two cars, one symptom, one bulletin, two very different wallets.

Now the part Honda would want read aloud. The 2021-2022 CR-V is the previous generation, and its steering gearbox carries different part numbers from different supplier lots than the recalled units; Honda's defect report pins the recall to specific worm-wheel production, and matching symptoms are not a matched root cause, no matter how loudly 103 owners say "sticky."[5] Honda estimated only 1% of recalled vehicles were actually defective, 111 complaints is a small number against hundreds of thousands of CR-Vs sold per year, NHTSA closed its investigation satisfied in early 2025, and at least one owner admitted mistaking lane-keep-assist torque for the defect. All fair. None of it explains why the fix procedure exists in a bulletin but the free repair stops at an arbitrary model year, or why owners are still filing "please expand the recall" complaints in 2026. Only a teardown of a failed 5th-generation gearbox settles the engineering question; the consumer question is already settled, and it is sitting in service departments at $2,200 a visit.

What this means for you: if you own a 2021 or 2022 CR-V, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and understand that 24V-744 will not appear, which is the entire point. Watch for a notchy or catching steering wheel at highway speed, a clicking noise in slow turns, and a wheel that does not quite return to center on its own. If a dealer diagnoses it, bring up service bulletin A24-011 by number; it is Honda's own repair procedure. File a complaint at nhtsa.gov if it happens to you, because complaint volume is literally what opened this investigation in the first place, 145 Civic drivers back in March 2023, and owners right now are filing with the explicit goal of forcing an expansion. Keep every receipt: a class action over this defect is working its way through federal court into 2026, and recall expansions have a habit of turning old invoices into reimbursements.[6]
