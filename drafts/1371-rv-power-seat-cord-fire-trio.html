Three RV Brands Recalled the Same Recliner Cord. The Fuse Was Supposed to Stop the Fire.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 2, 2026

Three rival RV brands recalled trailers in the same week for the same defect, described in the same federal sentence. Alliance RV recalled 3,686 trailers. Heartland recalled 518 fifth wheels. Ember recalled 259 travel trailers. Add them up and you get 4,463 boxes on wheels whose power-seat cords carry a fuse that may not provide adequate circuit protection. In plain language: if the cord shorts, the thing built to stop a fire might not stop a fire.[1][2]

4,463
Trailers recalled across three brands in one week for the same power-seat cord defect (Alliance 3,686 + Heartland 518 + Ember 259)

A fuse has exactly one job. It sits in a circuit doing nothing until current spikes, and then it breaks the circuit before wire gets hot enough to burn. NHTSA's consequence wording is identical across all three filings: inadequate protection increases the risk of a fire in the event of a short circuit. Nobody has reported a fire, a crash, or an injury tied to any of the three campaigns, and all three are preventive. Still, a fuse that cannot be trusted to interrupt a short is not a marginal defect. It is a safety device failing at the only moment it exists for, inside a vehicle people sleep in.[1][3]

Read the three filings side by side and a shared-component story emerges. Competing manufacturers do not independently write identical defect sentences in the same week. Somewhere upstream, a power seating assembly, or at least its cord and fuse, is common across Alliance Valor and Delta trailers, Heartland Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, and Road Warrior fifth wheels, and Ember E-Series travel trailers. None of the three notices names the seating supplier, so that last link stays an inference, not a fact. What is factual is the pattern: one component family, three brands, one week, one failure mode.[2][4]

One detail separates Heartland from the other two, and it deserves attention. Alliance and Ember will replace the power cord. Heartland will replace the harness and the power cord. Either Heartland's engineers found more of the problem, or its wiring layout exposes more of it. Both readings point the same way for owners: a cord-only fix and a harness-plus-cord fix are not the same repair, and buyers comparing brands should ask which one their trailer got. Fairness also requires the other side of the ledger. All three companies found this themselves, filed promptly, and will fix it free. Zero fires across 4,463 trailers is quality control working before the worst case, not after it.

Owners of 2024-2027 Alliance Valor or Delta trailers, Heartland Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, or Road Warrior fifth wheels, or Ember E-Series trailers should run every VIN through nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not when the letter arrives. Ember letters go out November 23, Heartland letters November 30, and Alliance letters December 7, which means weeks of camping season pass while notification travels by mail. Campaign numbers are 26V680 for Alliance, 26V710 for Heartland, and 26V714 for Ember. All three repairs are free, and Heartland owners can call 1-877-262-8032, Ember owners 1-844-732-4204, and Alliance owners 1-574-218-7165. Anyone shopping for a used trailer from these model years should ask for completion records on the cord recall before money changes hands. A recliner is furniture until its cord shorts. Then it is an ignition source in your bedroom.[1][3]
