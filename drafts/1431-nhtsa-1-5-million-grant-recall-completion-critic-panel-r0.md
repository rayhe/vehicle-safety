Structure is sound: kicker, headline, lede with the key stat bolded, pull stat, seven body paragraphs, references, disclaimer. The headline is the best sentence in the piece and the lede delivers the five-cents punch in the first paragraph. Weaknesses: 972 words is nearly double the 300-500 word guide target and ~15% longer than #1430's 844; the closing "What you can do" paragraph is the softest in the piece, ending on a shrug ("say it to a registration envelope that will never answer back") that is clever but lands flat after the indifference paragraph already made the point harder. ## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Every figure traces to a primary source: grants.gov NOFO (NHTSA-693JJ927R000007-FY27, 5 expected awards, $300k minimum, closing Feb 4, 2027), the NHTSA program announcement (Maryland pilot figures), Autoblog / Tesla North / Free Press for the Nov 5 summit. Pre-panel rigor fix (caught by the critic, not the draft): the NHTSA press release linked as ref-1 is the ORIGINAL program announcement quoting Secretary Elaine L. Chao and Deputy Administrator James Owens, not the November 5, 2026 announcement. The lede citation was moved to the grants.gov NOFO + Autoblog, ref-1 was relabeled with its correct provenance, and the disclaimer was corrected. "Recall repairs are free by law" is correct under 49 U.S.C. section 30120. IIJA section 24103(4) authorization per grants.gov. The "five expected awards" vs the old release's "as many as six States" discrepancy was correctly resolved in favor of the current NOFO. The 68% / 18% completion figures are summit averages, flagged as such in limitations.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution, not synthesis: three calculations nobody has published on this site — $1.5M / 29M vehicles = $0.052 per recalled vehicle; $222,300 / 371,000 repairs = $0.60 per completed repair; 943,000 / 456,000 = 2.07 recalls per affected vehicle, 371,000 / 943,000 = 39.3% repaired inside the pilot window. Limitations are honest and specific (per-campaign counting overstates distinct vehicles; 68%/18% are unpublished summit averages; Maryland ran 2018-2020 when Takata dominated; earlier state grants' total spend is not public). The strongest counterargument is stated at full strength and the piece concedes its force. One gap: the earlier MD/CA/TX/OH grants' total spend is not public, so the "nearly 2 million repaired" figure cannot be costed — flagged, not hidden.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
The pull stat ($0.05) is the right number and the math is shown with inputs in the body ($1.5M / 29M; $222,300 / 371,000). Deduction: two of the research file's computed ratios (2.07 recalls per affected vehicle; 39.3% of open recalls repaired in the pilot window) never surface in the article body — the reader is handed "456,000 vehicles carrying 943,000 open recalls" and "371,000 repaired" and left to divide. The numbers are present; the arithmetic is not. Minor, and the article is already long, so this is noted rather than fixed.
