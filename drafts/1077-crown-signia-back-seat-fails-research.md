# Research Notes — #1077: The Back Seat Is Where 2026 Safety Awards Are Won or Lost

**Slug:** 1077-crown-signia-back-seat-fails
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor) — kicker "The Gap"
**News peg:** 2026 IIHS award rounds, September 2026 (Crown Signia moderate-overlap results published ~Sept 19, 2026)

## The story

Toyota's Crown Signia was a Top Safety Pick in 2025. For 2026, it gets no award at all. The car didn't get worse — the test moved to the second row. IIHS's updated moderate overlap front test (launched 2022, now the gate for 2026 awards) puts a second Hybrid III dummy — sized like a small woman or an average 12-year-old — in the seat directly behind the driver. In the Crown Signia's test (IIHS test ID CEF2621, 2026 XLE 4-door 4wd), the driver was all-Good across every measure, but the rear dummy submarined: the lap belt rode up off the pelvic bone onto the abdomen. Rear passenger restraints and dummy kinematics: Marginal. Rear chest: Acceptable. Overall: Marginal. No TSP.

This is not a one-car problem. It is the defining pattern of the 2026 award year:
- **Hyundai Palisade:** earns TSP+ for 2026, but ONLY for vehicles built after November 2025, when Hyundai revised the rear seat belt buckles. Earlier-build Palisades are ineligible.
- **Chevrolet Traverse / GMC Acadia:** knocked off the 2026 TSP list with merely "acceptable" moderate-overlap results — both would otherwise qualify (per IIHS spokesperson Joe Young).
- **BMW 3 Series (G20):** "acceptable" moderate overlap in the Sept 2026 round; the write-up notes rear-occupant kinematics issues.
- Counter-proof the fix is cheap and known: the **Mazda CX-5** went from Poor to Good in the updated test after Mazda added rear seat belt pretensioners and load limiters — front-row technology adapted for the back.

## The anchor stat (primary, IIHS)

IIHS's own real-world research: **in vehicles from model year 2007 onward, the risk of fatal injury is 46% higher for belted occupants in the rear seat than in the front.** Not because the rear seat got less safe, but because restraint technology (pretensioners, load limiters, airbags) improved up front and was never adapted for the back. Fewer than half of new vehicles have advanced restraint systems in the rear seat. IIHS Senior Research Engineer Marcy Edwards, who led the test update: adapting proven front-seat tech to the rear is "an opportunity to make big gains in a short time."

## Novel angle / kill test

This is genuinely newsworthy, not a data dump: the consumer guidance angle is sharp. Dealers will still show you 2025 Top Safety Pick marketing for the Crown Signia; the 2026 car has the same architecture and no award. Buyers who assume the badge carries over are wrong. And the specific failure mode — submarining — is exactly the mechanism that injures children: the dummy represents a 12-year-old. The seat where your kids ride is the seat the industry left behind.

## Actionable takeaway (hard gate)

Before buying a 2026 family vehicle, check the *updated moderate overlap* rating specifically (not the old test, not a 2025 award). If you own a Crown Signia, Traverse, Acadia, or pre-Nov-2025 Palisade: rear passengers are the vulnerable ones — keep lap belts low on the hips, and put the most vulnerable passengers in the best-rated row you can. If shopping new: ask the dealer whether the rear belts have pretensioners and load limiters.
