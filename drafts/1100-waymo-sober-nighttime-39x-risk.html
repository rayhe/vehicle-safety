Sobriety Report

You're Sober. The Night Doesn't Care.

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
October 8, 2026

According to the toxicology reports, and there are a lot of them, we've been blaming the wrong thing for how dangerous the night is.

Imagine every drunk driver in America vaporized at midnight, no hangover, no designated driver roulette, no interlocks, and the urban fatal crash rate would still fall by only 23%. That's the whole prize: four out of five fatal crashes keep their victims.

3.9x
How much likelier a 100%-sober driver is to die in a nighttime crash than a daytime one

Waymo published the numbers yesterday, and before you roll your eyes at the source, note that MADD co-signed the research. What the safety team built is an exposure reconstruction model: FARS toxicology cross-referenced against telematics, estimating sober-driver miles and a zero-BAC baseline across the 50 biggest U.S. urban areas. The Journal of Safety Research accepted the study, which means peer review, not a press release.

Start with 23%: strip every alcohol-involved driver out of the pool and the average urban fatal crash involvement rate drops from 1.42 to 1.10 incidents per hundred million miles.[1] In Houston the dividend runs 31%; in Minneapolis it's 13%, a dividend that is real and smaller than everything you've been told.

The number that should end the sentence is 3.1 to 3.9: stone cold sober at zero BAC, your nighttime fatal crash risk still runs up to 3.9 times your daytime risk. In some cities one in fifty nighttime drivers is legally drunk, which sounds enormous until you realize the darkness itself is doing triple duty: poor visibility, bad lighting, fatigue, and speeds the geometry never should have allowed, and sobriety fixes none of it.

Waymo's second paper ran 37,654 real 2023 FARS fatal cases through the International Research Council on the Biomechanics of Injury, where in 77% of fatal crashes at least one of three things was true: someone was impaired, someone was speeding, someone was unbelted. Yet only 8.2% involved human behavior alone, because the rest were convergences: an unlit road plus a big speed plus a bad match of metal, proof that single factors rarely kill you while systems do.

Now the honest part, which you'd better not skip: Waymo sells robotaxis, and the same research estimates autonomous systems could address “up to 83%” of these system failures, a number that arrives wearing a company logo and deserves to be treated as marketing until somebody independent replicates it. This exposure model is also a reconstruction, not a measurement: no national database tracks miles by BAC, and it's urban-only, which matters because rural roads, where 41% of traffic deaths happen, play by different rules.[2]

Here is the actionable bit, and it is not the one MADD wanted: the anti-drunk-driving tech the infrastructure law mandated for new cars will do what it's built to do: shave that 23%,[3] while the other 77% is on you and your city. Slow down after dark, wear the belt, fix your headlights, and check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before winter. If your city council is choosing between another DUI checkpoint and actual street lighting, send them this article, because the darkness is the bigger killer. MADD still wants you sober, and so do I; I just want you scared of the dark too.
