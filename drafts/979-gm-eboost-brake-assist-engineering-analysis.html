GM Says Its Brake Failures Happen After You Stop. 745 Drivers Say Otherwise.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 23, 2026

The federal investigation into GM's electronic brakes just went from one model to fourteen nameplates. In late August, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation upgraded its probe of the eBoost brake-by-wire system from a preliminary evaluation to a full Engineering Analysis, expanding the population from the 2023 Cadillac Lyriq to roughly 1.16 million vehicles across GM, plus the Honda Prologue and Acura ZDX.[1]

745
Reported brake-assist incidents in NHTSA's file, including 21 crashes and five injuries

GM has a story about what goes wrong. The company told investigators the failures trace to fractures in the eBoost unit's spindle, and the sequence is almost reassuring: you come to a stop, the warnings appear, the car caps your speed at 43 mph, and ABS and stability control stay functional until you halt,[2] tidy, mechanical, and under control.

The complaints refuse to follow the script. ODI says the reports it kept receiving describe an immediate loss of brake assist while the driver is actively braking, mid-stop, which the agency called inconsistent with GM's description of a spindle failure.[2] That is not a disagreement about root cause: it is a disagreement about physics. When the field data contradicts the manufacturer's version of how a failure unfolds, regulators don't close the file; they widen it.

Which is exactly what happened. An Engineering Analysis is the highest rung on ODI's ladder, the last stop before a recall demand, and it now covers fourteen nameplates: the Lyriq, Blazer EV, Equinox EV, Colorado, Canyon, Enclave, Envision, Traverse, Acadia, Celestiq, Optiq, the Cruise Origin, and, because they ride on GM hardware, the Honda Prologue and Acura ZDX.[3] Your Honda's brakes are GM's brakes. Part sharing is efficient right up until the shared part is the one under federal investigation.

Know the signs: a suddenly hard brake pedal, a "Brake System Failure" or "Service Brake System" warning. If assist drops, the mechanical backup still stops the car, but it takes far more leg. Push through it, don't pump, and expect a longer stop. Then check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, even though there is no recall yet.[4] An Engineering Analysis is where investigations go right before they become recalls. You want to be watching before that happens, not after.

In fairness, GM's position deserves its full weight. The cars still stop, and seven hundred forty-five incidents across 1.16 million vehicles is 64 per 100,000, a fraction of a percent, and some "immediate loss" reports may be drivers misreading the hard-pedal limp mode in a frightening moment. Plenty of Engineering Analyses close with no action at all. The Ridgeline camera probe did exactly that last week.[5]

And here is what this doesn't prove. Complaint counts mix verified failures with confused drivers. The 1.16 million is an investigation population, not a defect count. Nobody has computed a real defect rate, and no fatality has been attributed to this. This article can't tell you your car's brakes will fail. It can tell you the feds and the manufacturer disagree about how they fail, and that the disagreement is now 1.16 million vehicles wide.
