One Person Asked NHTSA to Look at Tesla's Robotaxis. Now There's a Federal File Called DP26010.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 21, 2027

On September 23, one person filed a federal petition claiming Tesla's driverless robotaxis exceed the posted limit in active school zones.[1] On October 9, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened a file on it: DP26010. About 1,000 robotaxis sit in the petition's scope; the evidence runs to three video links and four incident reports, and the body count is zero.

Estimated robotaxis in the petition's scopethe first time the federal government has put a number on the size of Tesla's driverless fleet

This is where the story usually goes sideways, so the procedure comes before the takes. A defect petition is the bottom rung on NHTSA's ladder, and any citizen can file one. Under 49 CFR Part 552, the agency gets 120 days to grant or deny it.[2] A grant opens a Preliminary Evaluation. An Evaluation can grow into an Engineering Analysis, which can end in a recall. NHTSA's own resume says it opened DP26010 "to evaluate the allegations and determine whether to grant or deny the petition." Nothing has been found, and a decision is expected around January 21, 2027.

The evidence file, in full: three video links from the petitioner, one formal complaint to the Office of Defects Investigation, three social media reports folded into the petition. NHTSA counts four incidents in total, with no crashes, no injuries, and no fatalities; and no cities, no speeds, no dates appear in the resume.[3] Tesla has said nothing publicly, and NHTSA has found no defect. If you were hoping for a smoking gun, the cabinet is empty. There is a note on the door.

But this file does not exist in a vacuum, and Tesla's driverless program is having quite the month with the federal government. This is the third federal action aimed at the robotaxi fleet in roughly five weeks.[4] Before it came an audit query and a Special Order demanding sworn proof that the steering-wheel-free Cybercab complies with federal safety standards. Tesla was ordered to prove, under oath, that a car with no pedals and no wheel meets FMVSS. NHTSA has not made the answers public.[5] Meanwhile the older FSD file keeps growing alongside it: a Preliminary Evaluation opened in October 2024 on how the system handles low visibility, escalated to a full Engineering Analysis this March covering about 3.2 million vehicles, plus a separate 2025 investigation into alleged red-light running.

Now, the strongest case against taking DP26010 seriously, because it deserves a fair hearing. Four incidents across a thousand cars, with zero harm recorded. School-zone speeding is a human epidemic: in one 25,000-vehicle study, more than 70 percent of drivers blew past school-zone limits by 20-plus miles per hour during drop-off and pickup,[8] and federal estimates put 50 to 90 percent of motorists over the limit near schools.[9] Measured against the humans currently doing the school run, four videos might be a better record than every minivan in the pickup line. A quiet denial in January is a perfectly plausible outcome.

And yet school zones are the one place where "probably fine" stops being good enough. A child struck at 15 mph has roughly a 5 percent chance of dying; at 35 it is 30 percent.[7] These zones are time-gated and beacon-flashed, the limit blinking on and off with the school day, which makes them the hardest sign-reading problem in everyday driving: not a fixed number on a pole, but a conditional one. A vision-only system has to get that call right a few hundred hours a year, past flashing yellow lights, with passengers aboard. Four videos is a thin file, but it is also exactly how every recall in history started: one person, one complaint, one file number.

If you live in Austin, Dallas, Houston, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, or the Bay Area, robotaxis are running in your metro. You can file your own complaint at nhtsa.gov. That is literally how this file came to exist. And note January 21, 2027: that is the day NHTSA has to decide whether DP26010 gets a real investigation or a quiet denial. I will be reading the resume either way.
