223,472 F-150s Have Gas Tanks That Can Fall Out. The Fix Is a Letter in the Mail.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 17, 2026

Ford recalled 223,472 F-150 pickups on September 9 because the front fuel tank straps may have been bolted on wrong for nearly four years. It can leak gasoline or stall your engine. Or it can detach at highway speed and become a 245-pound projectile for everyone behind you.

223,472
F-150s built from January 2023 through August 2026 with suspect fuel-tank straps (NHTSA campaign 26V578000)

This is not a part that failed; it was never put on right. Ford's own filing says the straps “may have been improperly assembled” — factory language for a step that got skipped. Ford dates the suspect window from January 4, 2023 to August 27, 2026, a forty-four-month span that works out to roughly five thousand trucks a month rolling off the line with gas tanks held on by hope.[1]

A full F-150 tank is a weapon. Depending on configuration, the truck carries 23 to 36 gallons of gasoline at about 6.1 pounds per gallon, plus roughly 25 pounds of steel tank, for up to 245 pounds total. When a 245-pound tank of gasoline lets go at 70 miles an hour, the truck that dropped it is not the vehicle in the most danger. AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety research puts vehicle hardware behind roughly 29 percent of debris-related crashes — and about half the deaths in those crashes come from secondary collisions, when other drivers swerve to dodge the wreckage.[2] Most likely to die from your loose fuel tank: the strangers behind you.

This is a sequel Ford has already lived through: in 2022, its Critical Concern Review Group investigated a 2021 F-150 that left the Dearborn plant with its front fuel-tank-strap bracket missing entirely — shipped, sold, delivered, no bracket. A Field Review Committee approved a field action that June.[3] Four years later, the same component, the same failure mode — this time spread across more than two hundred thousand trucks. A recall fixes the trucks, but it does not fix the process that built 223,472 of them wrong.

Federal standards test what happens when a crash hits the tank. FMVSS 301 demands rigorous fuel-system integrity in barrier tests and limits post-crash leakage to ounces. Nobody audits whether the straps were torqued right on a Tuesday. Build quality rides on warranty claims and, eventually, recalls like this one.[2]

If you own a 2023-through-2027 F-150, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (campaign 26V578000, searchable since September 14).[4] Owner letters start mailing September 21, and dealers inspect and fix it free. And one useful detail from Ford's 2022 filing: a loosening tank announces itself with noise and vibration, so if the truck starts rattling under the bed, that is not a sound to drive home on.[3]

Fairness demands the other side. Ford reports zero injuries across 44 months of production. Most of these straps will hold for the life of the truck. But “improperly assembled” is silent by nature: no dashboard light, no warning, and the failure mode punishes strangers first. You can't see your own tank straps, and neither can the driver behind you.
