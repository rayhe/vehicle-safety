## Proposed angle
NHTSA released its early estimate for the first half of 2026 **today, November 10, 2026**: **16,750 deaths, down 2.5%** from 17,175 in H1 2025, with a fatality rate of **1.02 per 100M VMT, the lowest half-year rate since 2014** and the lowest death count since 2015. The headline is the best in a decade. The second sentence is the story: deaths fell **5% in Q1 but just 0.2% in Q2**. The five-year improvement run that began in Q2 2022 nearly flatlined inside a single half-year. And there is a second number nobody has put next to it yet: the National Safety Council estimated the same six months at **18,460 deaths, rate 1.12** — 1,710 more deaths than NHTSA counts, a 10% gap between two estimates of the same roads in the same months, explained entirely by methodology (NSC counts deaths within 100 days including private roadways; NHTSA counts 30 days on public roads only). The article's original contribution is the within-half-year deceleration arithmetic (Q1 -4.3% to Q2 -0.2%), the counterfactual lives gap, and the NSC-vs-NHTSA methodology gap quantified on the same half-year.

## The numbers (primary sources)
1. **NHTSA H1 2026 early estimates (released Nov 10, 2026):** 16,750 estimated deaths, down 2.5% from 17,175 in H1 2025. Fatality rate 1.02 per 100M VMT, down from 1.05 in H1 2025 and the lowest half-year rate since 2014 (1.01 in H1 2014). Lowest death count since 2015. Deaths fell 5% in Q1 but just 0.2% in Q2. Full-year 2025: lowest death count since 2019, rate 1.10, second-lowest yearly figure in US history. Bicyclist deaths rose 4% to 1,148 in 2025. Sources: Reuters, "US traffic deaths fall in first half of 2026, lowest rate since 2014," Nov 10, 2026 (David Shepardson); Bloomberg Government, "Traffic Death Rates Falling to Lowest Since 2014, DOT Data Show," Nov 10, 2026; NHTSA LinkedIn, Nov 10, 2026 ("second-lowest half-year fatality rate in U.S. history").
2. **NHTSA Q1 2026 (DOT HS 813 833, July 2026):** 7,770 deaths, down 4.3% vs 8,120 in Q1 2025. Rate 0.99, down from 1.05. "16th consecutive quarterly decline in fatalities beginning with the second quarter of 2022." 8 of 10 NHTSA regions down. Source: crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov.
3. **NSC H1 2026 (Sept 9, 2026):** 18,460 estimated deaths, down 2% from revised H1 2025 count of 18,846. Rate 1.12 per 100M VMT, down 3.4% from 1.16. Source: NSC press release Sept 9, 2026; covered by #1402.
4. **NHTSA H1 2025 (DOT HS 813 756, Sept 2025):** 17,140 deaths, down 8.2% from 18,680 (H1 2024), rate 1.06, "largest first-half decline since 2008." Source: crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov.
5. **NHTSA full-year 2025 (DOT HS 813 800, April 2026):** 36,640 deaths, down 6.7% from 39,254 in 2024. Rate 1.10, second lowest ever. Source: NHTSA.
6. **2024 final (DOT HS 813 791, April 2026):** 39,254 deaths, rate 1.19. 2021 peak: 43,230 deaths (+10.8%), most since 2005. Source: NHTSA via Reuters.

## Original calculations (for the article)
- **Q2 implied count:** H1 16,750 minus Q1 7,770 = **8,980 deaths in Q2 2026**. Q2 2025 implied: 17,175 minus 8,120 = 9,055. Raw arithmetic gives -0.8%; NHTSA's release as reported by Reuters says -0.2%, which reflects NHTSA's revised Q2 2025 base. Use the reported -0.2% as the official figure; the 8,980 count is labeled as arithmetic from the two published totals.
- **Deceleration inside the half-year:** Q1 -4.3% (NHTSA DOT HS 813 833) to Q2 -0.2% (NHTSA release via Reuters). The improvement rate fell roughly 95% in three months. Q1's decline was 21 times Q2's.
- **Counterfactual, H1 2025 pace:** had H1 2025's -8.2% pace held, H1 2026 would have come in near 15,767 (17,175 x 0.918). Actual 16,750 means **about 983 more deaths** than that pace implied. Label explicitly as a rough counterfactual, not a forecast.
- **Two-year progress:** H1 2024 18,680 to H1 2026 16,750 = **1,930 fewer deaths (-10.3%)** over two years. The improvement is real; the rate of improvement is what collapsed.
- **The streak:** Q1 2026 was the 16th consecutive quarterly decline since Q2 2022 (DOT HS 813 833). Q2 2026's decline, however thin, makes it the **17th consecutive quarterly decline**. The streak survives; its margin is 0.2%.
- **NSC vs NHTSA gap, same six months:** 18,460 minus 16,750 = **1,710 deaths (10.2% higher)**. Rates 1.12 vs 1.02. Methodology: NSC uses NCHS data, counts deaths within 100 days of the crash, includes private roadways (parking lots, driveways). NHTSA FARS counts deaths within 30 days on public roads only. Neither number is wrong; they measure different things. Never mix the two series in a single sentence without saying which is which.
- **Cyclists, the exception:** 1,148 bicyclist deaths in 2025, up 4% from 1,103 in 2024 (+45). #1453 covered 2024 cyclists (1,103). The 2025 rise is new and runs against the national decline. Reuters notes 2023 was the highest cyclist figure in more than four decades.

## Limitations (must appear in the article)
- All H1 2026 numbers are preliminary statistical projections, subject to revision when the FARS Annual Reporting File for 2025 and the Final File for 2025 land, and again when the 2026 ARF arrives. NHTSA's own Q1 report says the counts "will be slightly revised." A -0.2% Q2 could revise to flat or slightly positive.
- Quarterly slices are seasonally influenced; Q2 is the deadliest quarter of the year by exposure (summer driving), so a thin Q2 decline is not the same signal as a thin Q1 decline. Do not annualize Q2.
- The NSC/NHTSA gap is a measurement difference, not a disagreement about reality. NSC's 100-day window and private-roadway inclusion add roughly 1,700 deaths per half-year. The deceleration claim is built within the NHTSA series (Q1 vs Q2), which is methodologically consistent.
- The Q2 -0.2% figure comes from NHTSA's release as reported by Reuters on publication day; the CrashStats research note (DOT HS number) had not been indexed at research time. The article must date the claim and attribute the quarterly split to the release via Reuters.
- FARS 2014-2023 cannot speak to 2026; the article's comparisons use published early estimates, not FARS model-year data.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
The honest counterargument is that this is still the best half-year in a decade by the only metric that matters: 16,750 deaths is the lowest count since 2015, the rate is the lowest since 2014, the 17-quarter streak is intact, and 2025 was the fifth straight year of declines on pace. A 0.2% Q2 decline after a 4.3% Q1 decline could be noise, seasonality, or regression to the mean — you cannot fall 8% every half-year forever, because the denominator keeps shrinking and the easy wins (post-pandemic normalization, fleet turnover) get used up. The deceleration may be arithmetic destiny, not policy failure. The article must grant this fully, then answer it with the rate math: even if some slowdown was inevitable, losing 95% of the improvement rate between Q1 and Q2 of the same year, while Americans drove more miles, is a materially different regime than the 2022-2025 run — and the one category that should be most sensitive to infrastructure and enforcement, cyclists, went the wrong way while everything else improved.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Do not annualize the headline. The 1.02 rate is a half-year number; Q2's near-flat result means the full-year 2026 rate will land closer to 1.05 than to Q1's 0.99 unless the second half re-accelerates.
- If you ride a bike, the national improvement does not include you: cyclist deaths rose 4% to 1,148 in 2025 while overall deaths fell 6.7%. Daylight visibility and separated infrastructure matter more than the national trend.
- Check your state's trend, not the national headline: #1402 showed 11 jurisdictions got worse by double digits in H1 2026 on the NSC series. The national 2.5% improvement is an average over states moving in opposite directions.
- The streak is 17 quarters long and 0.2% wide. Drive like it ends this quarter, because statistically it nearly did last quarter.
