# Research Notes: Jeep Grand Cherokee Third Coil-Spring Recall (2026-09-08)

**Slug:** jeep-grand-cherokee-third-coil-spring-recall
**Article #:** 847
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; recall/what-to-do beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Proposed headline:** "Your Grand Cherokee's Springs Fell Off. Jeep Fixed It. Twice. They're Recalling Them Again."

## The story

On September 8, 2026, FCA US (Stellantis) recalled **328,381** Jeep Grand Cherokees (2022-2023 Grand Cherokee, 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee 4xe, 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee L) because rear coil springs may be incorrectly installed and detach from the vehicle while driving. The FCA recall number is **89D**; the NHTSA campaign number is **26V-562**.

This is the **third** recall campaign for the same defect.

## The recall chain (original analysis)

1. **June 8, 2023 — 23V-413 / FCA 64A (the original):** 331,401 vehicles (2022-2023 GC, 2021-2023 GC L). Rear coil springs incorrectly installed; estimated **13%** of population with the defect. Safety-risk language in the Part 573 report: springs detaching while driving are "a hazard to operators and occupants of other vehicles which can cause such vehicles to crash without prior warning and/or may result in injury to vulnerable road users." Component supplier: Daewon Kang Up Co Ltd (Opelika, AL). Remedy: inspect and repair spring assemblies. Owner letters July 28, 2023.

2. **January 2026 — 26V-051 / FCA repair-of-repair:** 80,620 vehicles (a subset of the original population — the ones that had already been "repaired"). During the 64A repairs, some dealers had installed springs incorrectly or left repairs incomplete. Triggered by 20 customer complaints, 70 similar incidents, and 284 warranty claims. NHTSA opened an investigation November 2025; recall decision January 22, 2026. At filing: no known accidents or injuries. Remedy: re-inspect and re-position springs.

3. **September 2026 — 26V-562 / FCA 89D (this one):** 328,381 vehicles — the population has expanded back to nearly the original 331K, and now includes the Grand Cherokee 4xe hybrid for the first time. Estimated defect rate this time: **0.1%**. Remedy escalation: no longer just inspection — dealers will **replace both rear coil spring lower isolators**. VINs searchable September 9, 2026; interim owner letters September 29 through October 7.

## The novel angle

The remedies escalate each round: **inspect (2023) → inspect again (Jan 2026) → replace the parts (Sept 2026)**. Each campaign is an admission the previous remedy didn't fully hold. The estimated defect rate collapsed from 13% to 0.1% — the recall net stays enormous while the estimated actual failure count shrinks to roughly 328 vehicles out of 328,381. This is recalls working as designed (over-inclusive by safety design) and also a consumer-comprehension problem: "I already did the recall" no longer means "my car is fixed."

## Kill test

Genuinely newsworthy? Yes — announced today, 328K vehicles, same defect recalled three times in three years, suspension part that detaches at highway speed and becomes a hazard to OTHER drivers (victim-in-other-lane asymmetry, parallel to #846's wheel-off thesis). Novel angle? Yes — the escalation-of-remedies chain is original analysis, not press-release synthesis.

## Actionable insights

- If you own a 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee / Grand Cherokee L or 2022-2023 4xe: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting September 9. Do not wait for the letter.
- **Even if you already had a coil-spring recall repair done** (under 64A in 2023 or the January 2026 re-repair), this new campaign (89D) applies to you. FCA says so explicitly.
- Remedy is free: both rear coil spring lower isolators replaced at a dealership. FCA US customer care: 1-800-853-1403. NHTSA Vehicle Safety Hotline: 1-888-327-4236.

## Limitations

- The 0.1% estimated defect rate is FCA's estimate, not a measured failure rate of the repaired population. The actual number of springs that will detach is unknown.
- At the time of the January 2026 (26V-051) filing, Jeep reported no known accidents or injuries tied to the defect; whether any have since occurred is not stated in public filings.
- We cannot tell from public filings what fraction of the 26V-051 repairs were actually incomplete — the 80,620-vehicle subset was defined by dealer service records, which are themselves the source of the problem.
- Component supplier (Daewon Kang Up) appears in the 2023 filing; the September 2026 filing does not attribute the defect to a supplier in the sources reviewed.

## Strongest counterargument

FCA following the process IS the system working: each campaign widened the net and escalated the remedy, which is exactly what a safety regulator wants to see after a botched repair. Three recalls could be read as diligence rather than failure. And the 0.1% estimate means roughly 328 actual bad installs in a 328K population — the recall is a fire blanket, not an admission that a third of a million cars are dangerous. The "I already fixed it" frustration is real, but the free part replacement is a better remedy than another eyeball inspection.
