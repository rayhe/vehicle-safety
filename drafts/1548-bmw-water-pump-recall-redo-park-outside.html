BMW Fixed This Fire Risk in 2024. The Fix Failed. Now 720,796 Cars Have to Sleep Outside.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

BMW has recalled 720,796 cars and SUVs because the electrical connector on the engine water pump can let moisture in, short-circuit, and start a fire, parked and switched off, in your garage if you let it. NHTSA's advice, issued November 13, is the bluntest sentence the agency writes: park outside, away from buildings and other vehicles, until the car is repaired. There is one problem with that instruction, which is that no repair exists yet.[1][2]

720,796
Vehicles recalled twice for the same water-pump connector, 27 months apart: campaign 24V608 in August 2024, and campaign 26V733 in November 2026.

Same cars, same part, same fire risk. Campaign 26V733 officially replaces 24V608, and every car that already got the 2024 fix has to go back. That is the story in one sentence, and it is worse than it sounds, because the 2024 fix was not a repair so much as a hypothesis with a shield bolted to it. BMW's theory in 2024 was that blow-by fluid dripping from the crankcase ventilation hose was landing on the pump connector. So dealers inspected the pump and plug, replaced them if damaged, and installed a shield to divert the drips, and the paperwork said case closed.[3][4]

It was not closed: in April 2025, a 5 Series outside the United States overheated at the pump connector even though it already had the 2024 remedy. A broader review in early 2026 turned up more incidents in repaired cars, and BMW started pulling parts back for analysis. Lab testing found leak paths near the connector. Thermal analysis found severe embrittlement of the PBT plastic the connector is molded from, and CT scans found cracks. BMW now says it is examining whether the plastic is breaking down through hydrolysis, water molecules snipping the polymer chains apart over years of heat and moisture. A shield keeps drips off a connector, but it does nothing for a connector that rots from the inside out. And BMW still has not determined the root cause.[3]

Run the numbers on the first fix. BMW's own quarterly completion reports show 211,553 vehicles repaired by the end of 2025, about 29 percent of the population. That means 509,243 cars never got the shield at all, and the shield did not work on the ones that did. Meanwhile BMW counts about 10 U.S. incidents possibly related to the defect, with no accidents or injuries known, and estimates the defect affects fewer than 0.1 percent of the population. Do that division and you get at most 721 actually defective vehicles, against 720,796 cars now sleeping outside. Ten incidents in 720,796 cars is 1.4 per 100,000, so your odds are excellent, but a garage fire does not care about your odds.[2][3]

Fairness check, because the counterargument deserves its full weight: no one has been hurt, no crash has been attributed to this defect, and NHTSA issued no do-not-drive warning, so owners can keep driving. BMW told dealers the cars are safe to drive and to schedule the repair when a remedy exists. If the defect really touches fewer than one car in a thousand, then 720,000 park-outside orders is a staggering overreaction to a tiny risk, but that is also the wrong frame. A recall is not a prediction that your car will burn. It is an admission that BMW cannot tell you which one will, cannot fix any of them yet, and cannot even let you check your VIN until December 25.[2][5]

That date is the part that should annoy you most. Dealers were notified November 4, while owners get letters beginning December 25, and VINs become searchable on NHTSA.gov the same day. Fifty-one days between the dealer knowing and the owner knowing, and a 42-day stretch after the public alert during which you cannot confirm whether your car is covered. Three models carry most of the weight: 236,702 3 Series sedans, 163,728 X3s, and 110,102 5 Series cars, together 70.8 percent of the recall. If you own a 2012 to 2018 BMW with the N20 or N26 four-cylinder, check your VIN on December 25, park outside until the remedy exists, and call BMW at 800-525-7417 with questions. And if your 2024 shield repair felt like closure at the time, it was not. It was a dress rehearsal for the recall that is coming now.[1][2][3]
