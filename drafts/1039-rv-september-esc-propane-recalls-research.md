# Research: 1039 — September's 13 RV Recalls (Including Motorhomes With Dead Stability Control)

## Angle
NHTSA published 13 RV safety recalls in September 2026 covering 1,288 vehicles. The defects read like a parody of the vehicle-safety playbook: a stability-control system programmed with the wrong vehicle parameters (47 REV motorhomes, ESC diminished or lost), propane tanks that cannot be shut off and may detach (88 Tiffin Wayfarers), a furnace venting carbon monoxide into the cabin (5 Forest River Vibes), a sleeper sofa that blocks the bunkroom door (132 Cedar Creek fifth-wheels), a rooftop fan PCB catching fire across TWO manufacturers using the same supplier component (Storyteller 26V566, Kibbi/Renegade 26V574), and a motorhome whose top speed exceeds its tires' speed rating (Grand Design Lineage 26V595). The structural point: FMVSS 126 (electronic stability control) only applies to vehicles at or under 10,000 lb GVWR, so the tallest, heaviest, most rollover-prone vehicles on American roads carry ESC only when the builder opts in. One builder opted in, then programmed it wrong.

## Key numbers (verified)
- 13 RV recalls in September 2026; 1,288 vehicles total (Camper Report).
- 26V602 REV RoadWatch: 47 units, ESC diminished/lost (oemdtc).
- 26V557 Tiffin Wayfarer: 88 units, LP tank no remote shutoff + loose bracket; letters by Oct 30 (TPS, Transport Canada).
- 26V563 Forest River Vibe: 5 units, furnace exhaust vents CO into cabin; letters mailed Sep 15 (RV PRO).
- 26V608 Forest River Cedar Creek: 132 units, sleeper sofa blocks bunkroom door (TPS).
- 26V601 Winnebago ARKA: 56 units, wrong GAWR + tire pressure on placard, FMVSS 120 (TPS).
- 26V566 Storyteller MODE/GXV HILTS + 26V574 Kibbi/Renegade (12 model lines): SAME MaxxAir N-Series Maxxfan PCB overheats; two separate recalls, one supplier component (Camper Report).
- 26V595 Grand Design Lineage: top speed may exceed tire speed rating (Camper Report).
- 26V595 remedy "under development"; letters Nov 2 (Camper Report).
- FMVSS 126 applies to vehicles with GVWR of 10,000 lb or less; Class A motorhomes routinely exceed that (49 CFR 571.126, via NHTSA). State as the structural frame.

## The novel calculations
- Hazard cross-tab of the 13 September recalls: fire (2: 26V566, 26V574, same fan) + propane/fire (26V557, 26V586 griddle), crash-handling (26V602 ESC, 26V595 tire-speed, 26V603 coolant, 26V554/26V552/26V601 labels), poisoning/egress (26V563 CO, 26V608 blocked door), false warnings (26V555). The point: the defects cluster in DOMESTIC systems (propane, furnace, sofa, fan, griddle) — the house parts of the house-on-wheels — plus one flagship automotive-system failure (ESC).
- 26V563's population is 5 vehicles. Five motorhomes where the furnace could fill the bedroom with carbon monoxide while you sleep. Smallest recall in the set, most nightmare-inducing.
- The MaxxAir pairing: two manufacturers, same component, two NHTSA IDs. Nobody else has cross-tabbed it.

## Strongest counterargument
RVs are a statistical afterthought in crash data because they are driven fewer miles, by older and more experienced drivers, on highways at moderate speeds; motorhome fatality rates per mile are far lower than passenger cars. Zero injuries are reported across all 13 recalls. The ESC mandate line is real but these are not daily drivers; most sit parked 300 days a year. The honest frame is regulatory-gappiness, not carnage: the system that demands crash tests for a Honda Civic asks nothing of a 30,000-lb Class A.

## Limitations
- No FARS signal: motorhomes are a rounding error in fatal crash data; this article relies on recall documents, not crash outcomes. Say so.
- Unit counts per recall come from secondary recall aggregators (oemdtc, TPS, RV PRO), not the Part 573 PDFs themselves; camperreport supplies the 13/1,288 totals.
- 26V595 (Lineage tire-speed) population count and remedy detail not in hand; describe without counts.
- Cannot verify what share of Class A motorhomes carry optional ESC; RoadWatch is REV-specific. Do not generalize to "all RVs lack ESC" — frame as the mandate gap, not an installed-base claim.
