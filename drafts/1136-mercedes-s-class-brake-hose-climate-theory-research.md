# Research — #1136: Mercedes S-Class brake-hose recall expansion (26V638)

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton | **Kicker:** Investigation
**Slug:** `1136-mercedes-s-class-brake-hose-climate-theory`
**Headline:** "Mercedes Blamed the Weather for Its Leaky Brake Hoses. The Reports From Outside the U.S. Disagreed."

## What happened

- **NHTSA Recall 26V638** (filed October 2026): Mercedes-Benz is recalling **30,078** U.S. S-Class sedans because the front brake hoses may rupture and leak brake fluid, degrading one of the two hydraulic brake circuits and lengthening stopping distance.
- Affected: 2022–2025 S-Class, built **February 2, 2021 – November 28, 2024**, including Maybach S 580/S 680 and AMG S 63 E Performance. U.S. breakdown: S 580 4MATIC 16,881; S 500 4MATIC 7,143; S 580 e 4MATIC 2,277; AMG S 63 E Performance 1,760; Maybach S 680 1,070; Maybach S 580 947.
- Supplier: **Continental Automotive Technologies** (Frankfurt) — hoses did not meet durability specs. Suspect part A2234201800; remedy part A2234203101. Cause: material fatigue from thermal and mechanical stress over time.
- This is an **expansion of 25V116** (filed Feb 2025, ~20,339 vehicles, 2021–2023). The original recall was scoped to specific production periods and hot/humid climates.
- **Why it expanded:** field reports from outside the U.S. (March–August 2025) broke the climate theory. Mercedes pulled hoses for inspection, ran lab and durability tests in July 2026, and by September 2026 acknowledged it could not rule out a safety risk for the larger population.
- Mercedes is aware of **3 U.S. field reports in 2026** (April 2, May 29, August 10) in the expanded population. **Zero crashes, injuries, or property damage** reported in the expanded group.
- Remedy: dealers replace front brake hoses free. Dealer notifications this week; **owner letters start November 27**. Cars built after Nov 29, 2024 already run the improved hose — the production fix predates the expansion by ~11 months.
- Warning behavior: a red brake-fluid warning appears on the dash once fluid is lost. No advance warning before the hose itself degrades.

## Original contribution (the timeline nobody assembled)

Reconstructed 20-month escalation arc:
1. Late 2023: Mercedes becomes aware (individual reports).
2. Feb 2025: 25V116 filed, 20,339 vehicles, scoped to hot/humid climates + specific production windows. Supplier Continental had already corrected the defect in 2022.
3. Nov 29, 2024: production switches to improved hose (running change).
4. Mar–Aug 2025: field reports arrive from outside the U.S., killing the climate-limited theory.
5. July 2026: lab and durability test results reviewed.
6. Sept 2026: Mercedes acknowledges it cannot rule out a safety risk for the wider population.
7. Oct 2026: 26V638 filed, 30,078 vehicles, 2022–2025.

The load-bearing fact: Mercedes fixed the assembly line in November 2024 but did not expand the recall until October 2026 — an 11-month gap during which the "fixed" design existed while ~10,000 additional cars waited for a letter.

## Actionable takeaways (for article)

- If you own a 2022–2025 S-Class (incl. Maybach/AMG): check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — owner letters don't go out until late November; don't wait.
- The repair is free at dealers; do not drive on a red brake-fluid warning.
- Buying a used W223 S-Class: confirm the brake-hose recall is closed on that VIN before purchase; cars built after Nov 29, 2024 already have the improved hose.

## Limitations (for article)

- Zero crashes/injuries in the expanded population; risk is theoretical-empirical (3 field reports), not observed harm.
- FARS cannot isolate brake-hose failures; no fatality-rate cross-tab available for this failure mode.
- Dual-circuit brakes are designed for exactly this failure — one circuit lost, the other still stops the car. Real-world consequence is longer stopping distance, not no brakes.
- Defect is age/stress dependent; a 2024-built car is less exposed than a 2021-built one.

## Strongest counterargument (for article)

The dual-circuit design is doing its job: even a ruptured hose leaves one working circuit, which is why 30,078 cars produced zero known crashes. Mercedes expanded the recall proactively on foreign reports and lab tests, not on bodies. The 11-month production-fix gap is normal recall logistics, not a cover-up — you don't re-notify a population you haven't finished defining.
