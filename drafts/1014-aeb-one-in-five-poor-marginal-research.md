# Research: 1014 — One in Five AEB Systems Rates Poor or Marginal

**Slug:** 1014-aeb-one-in-five-poor-marginal
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; car-buying guidance beat)
**Angle:** Automatic emergency braking is on nearly every new car, but IIHS rates 117 of 524 vehicles Poor or Marginal on front crash prevention. The IIHS test (pedestrian target, darkness, speed) is a rehearsal for FMVSS 127's night-pedestrian requirement, and the industry is simultaneously telling the D.C. Circuit the federal standard is "practically impossible."

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes: fresh data (ForCar published Sep 27, 2026, 2 days ago), consumer-facing paradox (feature present ≠ feature works), live regulatory fight (FMVSS 127 lawsuit in abeyance, DOT proposed 2-year extension March 2026).
- Novel angle on data? Yes. The site has AEB mandate-history and phantom-braking stories but nothing framing the IIHS ratings distribution as the FMVSS 127 rehearsal, and nothing with brand-level failure rates.
- Verdict: **PROCEED**

## Key data (all from ForCar analysis of IIHS ratings, Sep 27, 2026)
- 524 vehicles with a front crash prevention rating: Good 224, Acceptable 183, Poor 69, Marginal 48. Poor+Marginal = 117/524 = **22.3%**.
- Brand failures (Poor or Marginal / rated): Jeep 14/23 (60.9%), Ford 17/34 (50.0%), Chevrolet 14/28 (50.0%), Audi 13/37 (35.1%), Nissan 9/27 (33.3%), Volkswagen 7/19 (36.8%).
- Price does not predict: Audi sits among the worst brands.
- What correlates: hardware generation. Camera-only systems struggle in low light; camera+radar does better. Two model years of the same car can differ more than two brands.
- Model-year trend (failure share): 2024: 41/160 = 25.6%; 2025: 39/182 = 21.4%; 2026: 32/170 = 18.8%. Improving, slowly.
- The test got harder on purpose: early version used a car target in daylight at low speed; current version adds pedestrian target, darkness, speed.

## Regulatory context (FMVSS 127)
- NHTSA finalized FMVSS 127 (April 2024): AEB + pedestrian AEB standard by September 2029 on all passenger cars/light trucks ≤10,000 lb. Must stop/avoid rear-end crashes at up to 62 mph; engage at up to 90 mph when lead-vehicle collision imminent; up to 45 mph when pedestrian detected; detect pedestrians in daylight AND at night.
- Alliance for Automotive Innovation (GM, Toyota, Hyundai, VW, Ford et al.) sued Jan 17, 2025 (D.C. Circuit), calling the speed requirements "practically impossible with available technology." Litigation in abeyance since Feb 2025. March 23, 2026 DOT status report: preparing NPRM to extend compliance lead time by two years.
- The IIHS pedestrian-night-speed test is the same capability the rule demands. 78% of systems already pass it. The industry's "impossible" claim is about the last mile (62 mph avoidance), but the rehearsal data undermines the blanket claim.

## Counterargument (full strength)
- The IIHS test is HARDER than the legal standard: FMVSS 127 requires pedestrian detection at up to 45 mph, and the test scenarios are deliberately adversarial. A Poor rating does not mean the system is useless; it still brakes for stationary cars in daylight.
- Phantom braking is a real cost of aggressive AEB (site has aeb-phantom-braking-causes-crashes.html): the industry's worry about false positives at 62 mph is not fabricated.
- Camera-only physics at night is a genuine hardware constraint; forcing camera+radar raises vehicle cost, which prices some buyers out of new cars entirely.
- The v2v story (aeb-v2v-test-fars-paradox) found NO correlation between AEB test performance and real-world FARS death rates: the Forester aced the test but its drivers die at 2x the CX-5 rate. Lab ratings ≠ road outcomes.

## Limitations
- ForCar is a third-party scrape of IIHS ratings; I did not independently recount all 524 ratings.
- The ratings universe mixes model years and test generations; brand tables are dominated by each brand's oldest-rated hardware.
- IIHS front crash prevention rating measures one scenario family, not overall crashworthiness.
- No FARS cross-tab was run for this article; the null correlation from the v2v story stands as a caveat.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE)
- Check the front crash prevention rating for the EXACT model year at iihs.org/ratings before buying; the feature list means nothing.
- Hardware generation matters more than badge: camera+radar beats camera-only; a 2026 of the same model can be a different system than the 2024.
- On a Poor/Marginal system, treat AEB as a daylight-only backstop, not a substitute for attention. Pedestrian-at-night is exactly where it fails.
