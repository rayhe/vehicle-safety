The Gap

GM Recalled the Sierra's Grille. 21 Repaired Trucks Lost It Anyway.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 21, 2026

You did everything right. The recall letter came. You scheduled the appointment. A dealer touched your truck. And a year later, the grille deflector flew off anyway. That is the experience described in 21 of the 118 complaints NHTSA has logged about GMC Sierra 1500 upper grille deflectors detaching at speed. One in five reports is from a truck that was already "fixed."[1][2]

535,639
Sierra 1500s now under federal review — more than 7x the original recall

On October 9, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened recall query RQ26003, covering an estimated 535,639 GMC Sierra 1500 pickups from the 2022 through 2024 model years. A recall query is not a routine probe. It is NHTSA auditing a recall itself — asking whether the manufacturer's remedy actually worked and whether the recall covered the right vehicles. This one challenges both.[1]

The original campaign, recall 25V-060 filed February 6, 2025, covered only 70,768 trucks: 2022 Sierra 1500s with a chrome deflector built after that year's mid-cycle refresh. Non-chrome 2022 trucks were excluded. Every 2023 and 2024 Sierra was excluded. GM told NHTSA the later trucks used parts outside the supplier's suspect window. The query is now testing whether that cutoff was correct, and whether the repair procedure holds up under real-world driving conditions.[3][4]

The failure

According to GM's own filing, one or more of the eight attachment points holding the deflector can fracture over time. When all eight break, the wide trim bar across the top of the grille separates from the truck while it is moving. Owners may hear a rattle first.[2] ODI describes the detachment as happening "without detectability" and lists the parties at risk as the truck itself, nearby vehicles, motorcyclists, and pedestrians. Complaints describe cracked windshields and drivers swerving to avoid the flying trim.[1] The opening summary lists no crashes, injuries, or fatalities.

GM's internal timeline does the company no favors. A GM employee flagged a detached deflector through the company's Speak Up for Safety program on January 19, 2024. GM opened an investigation on February 27, then closed it on May 23 after finding no defect trend. It reopened the issue on December 11, 2024, when warranty claims started climbing. By the end of 2024, GM had counted 1,225 complaints and four accident allegations.[1][4] GM Authority reports roughly 64 percent of recalled trucks had the repair performed as of January 2026. No newer completion figure has been published.

What this means for you

If you own a 2022, 2023, or 2024 Sierra 1500: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, regardless of whether you already had recall 25V-060 done. The repair is now the subject of a federal review, so a completed repair is not clearance. Listen for a rattle from the front of the truck, which GM identified as the early warning sign. If you hear it, call GMC customer service at 1-800-462-8782 and do not wait for a second recall letter.[5]

NHTSA has not found a defect and the query is not a recall. But 21 trucks walked into a dealer, got the official fix, and lost the same part anyway. That is the whole story of this review in one number. A repair you cannot trust is not a repair.
