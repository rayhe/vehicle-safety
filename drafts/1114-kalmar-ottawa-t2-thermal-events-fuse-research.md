## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Kalmar has recalled its Ottawa T2 terminal tractors, the yard spotters that shuffle shipping containers around American ports and warehouses, because the cabin power distribution board can suffer insulation breakdown and short-circuit into a "thermal event." The remedy is telling: not a redesigned board, but a remote ground stud with a fuse, an engineered escape route for fault current Kalmar cannot fully characterize.

## Original contribution (required)
1. **The denominator problem.** Kalmar's rarity claim is a percentage of an undisclosed total. Without the fleet size, "<0.001%" could mean one unit or dozens. The article will name this explicitly and note what a real disclosure would include (population, event count, injuries).
2. **The fuse as a confession.** A fused ground stud does not prevent insulation breakdown; it gives the resulting fault current a safe path to ground and a fuse to sacrifice. That is the correct engineering control for a failure you cannot fully characterize — and it tells you Kalmar does not yet know which boards will break down, only where the current will go when they do.
3. **The invisible fleet.** Terminal tractors are the least-scrutinized powered vehicles in American logistics: no IIHS ratings, no NCAP stars, no consumer reviews. They operate where the public never goes, so their safety record is whatever the manufacturer files. This recall exists because Kalmar filed it.

## Best counterargument (state at full strength)
- Less than 0.001% is genuinely rare — this is a precautionary recall, not a burning fleet. Kalmar filed voluntarily before any reported injury, which is the responsible move. A fused ground stud is proper defense-in-depth engineering, not a band-aid; fuses exist precisely for faults you cannot predict. And yard tractors operate at low speed inside controlled industrial sites, so a thermal event's harm radius is a cab and a parking lot, not a highway pileup. The prior recalls (cab tilt, brake plumbing) were also caught and corrected.
