# Research: #1290 — Genesis G80 seat-switch park-outside recall (26V664)

**Slug:** 1290-genesis-g80-seat-switch-park-outside-no-fix
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — design-defect beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date researched:** 2026-10-26

## Kill test
PASS. Fresh October 2026 recall, park-outside advisory, and no remedy exists yet.
The defect inverts the usual frame: the danger is not driving, it is parking.
A switch that stays engaged after the driver walks away lets a seat motor run
until it overheats, so the car can start its own fire in a garage overnight.
Novel angles: (1) only ~1% of the recalled fleet actually carries the defect,
about 946 cars, and nobody can tell which ones, so 94,622 owners share the
burden of 946 cars' problem; (2) only non-memory power seats are affected,
so the cheaper trims of a luxury sedan carry the risk the loaded trims do not;
(3) these same nameplates already had a park-outside fire recall in 2021 for a
different system, so this is the second time owners of these cars have been
told to sleep with the car outside.

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- NHTSA campaign **26V664000**, Part 573 report filed **October 9, 2026**,
  publicly disclosed October 15, 2026 (Reuters via NHTSA).
  Hyundai recall **308**; Genesis recall **035G**.
- Population **94,622**: 2015-2016 Hyundai Genesis **54,644** (built Feb 21,
  2014 to Feb 24, 2016) and 2017-2020 Genesis G80 **39,978** (built Mar 17,
  2016 to Nov 7, 2019). Same second-generation DH-platform sedan; the G80 is
  the Genesis sedan rebadged under the standalone Genesis brand. Assembled in
  South Korea. Only vehicles with **non-memory power front seats** are included;
  a model-year match alone does not confirm a car is affected.
- Defect: the front power seat control switch (slider/height adjustment) can
  be damaged or dislodged by external force and stay engaged, so the seat
  motor keeps running even after the occupant leaves the vehicle. Prolonged
  operation overheats the motor and can cause a fire while parked or driving.
  Supplier of the switches reported as Nidec Mobility Korea Corporation.
- Incidents: Hyundai aware of **2 alleged fires** in the US; **0 crashes,
  0 injuries** as of October 1, 2026.
- Defect rate: Hyundai estimates **~1%** of the recalled population carries
  the defect, about 946 vehicles by arithmetic (0.01 x 94,622 = 946.22).
- Interim advice: park outdoors, away from other vehicles and structures,
  until the repair is performed. This is a park-outside advisory, not a
  do-not-drive order.
- Remedy: **still under development** as of October 26, 2026; free when
  available, with reimbursement for owner-paid repairs. Hyundai dealers may
  offer 2015-2016 Genesis owners a loaner or other transport at no cost;
  Genesis retailers are told to offer G80 owners other transport if they are
  uncomfortable driving.
- Timeline: VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from **October 13, 2026**; owner
  notification letters expected mailed **December 7, 2026**.
- Warning signs: burning smell from the seat area, or a front seat control
  switch that stops working.
- Prior fire recall: NHTSA campaign **21V160** (2021) covered 94,646 of the
  same model lines (2015-2016 Genesis, 2017-2020 G80) for an anti-lock brake
  module that could short-circuit and cause an engine-compartment fire, also
  with a park-outside advisory. Different system, different defect; the
  near-identical population size (94,646 vs 94,622, a difference of 24) is a
  coincidence of the same production population, not the same defect.

## Original contribution / calculation
- 1% defect estimate applied to the 94,622 population yields ~946 truly
  defective cars. The recall therefore asks 100 owners to park outside for
  every 1 car that actually has the defect, with no way to identify the 1.
- Post-ignition risk framing: unlike most defects, the hazardous state begins
  after use ends. The motor's runaway condition needs no driver, no key, no
  motion, which is why the garage, not the highway, is the exposure.
- Trim-level inversion: the defect rides on the cheaper seat option. Buyers
  who paid less for the luxury badge get the fire risk; buyers who paid for
  memory seats are excluded.

## Strongest counterargument (stated at full strength)
Two alleged fires across 94,622 cars over a decade of exposure is a tiny
absolute risk, no one has been hurt, and the 1% estimate means 99% of owners
are parking outside for a defect their car does not have. Months of outdoor
parking is a real cost in winter, for apartment dwellers, and for anyone
without a driveway, and it punishes the careful majority for a defect in a
few hundred cars. The honest answer is that the counterargument is mostly
right about the odds and still wrong about the advice: a parked-car fire in
an attached garage is a house fire, and the cost asymmetry (a winter of
outdoor parking vs a house fire at 3 a.m.) is what justifies the advisory,
not the probability.

## Limitations
- No FARS data is involved; this is a recall story, so there is no fatality
  rate to compute and no crash population to analyze. Do not dress it up as
  one.
- The 2-fire count and the 1% estimate are manufacturer-reported figures in
  the Part 573 filing, not independently verified counts. The true defective
  count could differ.
- Remedy timing is unknown; anything about when a fix arrives would be a
  guess and is not claimed here.
- Phone numbers for Genesis customer service differ between secondary
  reports (844-340-9741 vs 844-340-1200); no phone numbers are printed in
  the article to avoid publishing a wrong one. VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls
  is the safe direction.
- The 21V160 population overlaps the 26V664 population but the two campaigns
  are not claimed to cover identical VINs.

## Actionable takeaways
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now; do not wait for the December 7
  letter and do not assume a model-year match confirms inclusion.
- If included, park outside away from structures and other cars until the
  free repair exists, even if the seat works fine today.
- Burning smell from the seat or a dead seat switch: stop using the switch
  and call a dealer or Genesis retailer.
- Shopping used: a 2015-2020 Genesis/G80 needs both recall campaigns checked
  (21V160 and 26V664), not just the latest one.
