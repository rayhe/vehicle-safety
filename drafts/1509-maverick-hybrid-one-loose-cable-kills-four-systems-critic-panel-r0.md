## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10

The lede does its job in one paragraph: truck, part, four systems, one failure. The arc is clean — defect, mechanism, chronology, human case, context, action, honest limits. The final paragraph carries two jobs (limitations plus counterargument) and runs long, but it earns the length; it is the paragraph that keeps the article honest. ## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

No victims to exploit and none are invented: zero accidents, zero injuries is stated twice and never softened into drama. The March 2026 owner complaint is presented as one owner report, explicitly not a NHTSA finding. No owner is named. The article tells readers what to do instead of what to fear, and it refuses the easy scare line even though "loses steering and brakes at once" would have supported one. The one ethical pressure point — publishing a recall story on a truck with zero known injuries — is answered inside the article itself by the counterargument paragraph.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10

Every factual claim traces to the Part 573, the NHTSA acknowledgement letter, or the two cited secondary sources, all linked in the references. The panel caught one real error on the first read: the draft said the recall was filed "five days" after the October 27 committee approval; October 27 to November 2 is six days. Fixed before scoring. A second count tension was flagged and fixed: the draft said "28 recall campaigns in total" while an API rebuild counts 30 covering 2022 trucks; the text now says "28 recall campaigns in NHTSA's count," matching the cited source. Remaining claims verified against the filing: 8,650 vehicles, 1% estimate, Feb 3 2021–May 12 2022 build window, 11 VOQs, 427 claims with 11 reporting loss of motive power, zero accidents/injuries as of Oct 13, 2026. No case law needed; no statutory claims made.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Original contribution is real and itemized in the research file: the sixth-12V-action count, the hydroxy-acetic-acid chemistry angle, the single-point-failure math, the 11-of-427 ratio, and the investigation-to-filing timeline. Limitations are stated as a dedicated paragraph, not hedged inline: the filing does not say whether the replacement cable is redesigned, and gives no per-claim speed or road detail. The counterargument is stated at full strength ("a skeptic could call this a footnote") and answered rather than dismissed. Method is transparent: every number carries its source. The one gap is the 28-vs-30 campaign count, which the research file discloses and the article now attributes.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10

The pull stat (4 systems) is the right number to lead with, and the body embeds the rest cleanly: 8,650 trucks, 1%, 11 of 427, six actions, 28 campaigns. Numbers arrive with context every time (1% is translated to ~87 trucks; 11 of 427 is translated to 2.6%). No table, which is a defensible choice for a narrative investigation but a small miss: a three-row timeline (July 17 investigation, Oct 27 approval, Nov 2 filing) would have made the "Ford moved fast" claim land harder. The research file carries the full table; the article carries the story.
