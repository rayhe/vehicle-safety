## 6. 🔬 Research Rigor — **7.5/10** ⚠️ BELOW THRESHOLD

**Strengths:**
- **Novel contribution:** The core argument — connecting regulatory lag timelines (ESC 2007→MY2012→fleet effect in 2020s) with the simultaneous DOGE cuts and falling fatality curve to argue the coincidence is not causation — is genuinely original. No other article on the site draws this specific pipeline-delay connection with these specific data points.
- **Limitations section:** Present, specific, and honest.
- **Verifiability:** 6 sources, all linked, all real.
- **Methodology transparency:** The ESC → MY2012 → 12-year average fatal crash vehicle age chain is clearly laid out.

**Weaknesses — this is where the article falls short:**

1. **Strongest counterargument NOT stated at full strength.** The article's strongest opposition is: *technology adoption may be market-driven, not regulation-driven.* AEB was already standard on ~93% of new vehicles sold before the FMVSS 127 mandate was finalized, driven by IIHS Top Safety Pick requirements and consumer demand. If the market already adopted AEB without the mandate, the argument that delaying the mandate costs 360 lives/year is weaker — most cars already have it. The article completely ignores this counterargument. This is a hard requirement per STORY_GUIDE.md: "The best case against the article's thesis must be stated at full strength — not strawmanned, not immediately dismissed."

2. **The "survivorship bias" label is technically imprecise.** Survivorship bias refers to focusing on things that passed a selection process while overlooking those that didn't. What the article describes is closer to *post hoc ergo propter hoc* (crediting the cuts for the decline because they happened first) or simply *confusing correlation with causation.* Using the wrong logical-fallacy label undermines the rigor claim.

3. **Missing quantification of the lag model.** The article asserts a "three to five year" lag but doesn't show the math. When did ESC fleet penetration hit 50%? When did ESC-attributable fatality declines become measurable? Those timeline data points would make the lag model credible rather than asserted.

**Specific fixes to reach 8.5:**
1. Add one sentence acknowledging the market-adoption counterargument: e.g., "The strongest rebuttal: AEB was already on roughly 93 percent of new cars before the mandate was finalized, driven by IIHS requirements and insurance incentives, not federal rule. If the market was going to get there anyway, the regulatory delay matters less than this article implies."
2. Either fix the "survivorship bias" label or remove it — "That argument confuses correlation with causation" is more precise and doesn't pretend to be a named fallacy.
3. Optionally: add one data point on the ESC adoption curve to ground the lag model (e.g., "ESC went from 9% fleet penetration in 2004 to 95% by 2017 — a 13-year ramp").
