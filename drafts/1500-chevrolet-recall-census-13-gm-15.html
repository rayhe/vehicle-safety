By The Numbers

Chevrolet Carried 13 of GM's 15 Recalls. Cadillac Got the Other Two.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

General Motors filed fifteen recall campaigns on its 2026-model-year vehicles this year. Chevrolet’s name is on thirteen of them. Two campaigns escaped the bowtie entirely, and both are Cadillac-only, which means GM’s recall year is, to a first approximation, Chevrolet’s recall year, and the brand that sells the most cars in America also carries nearly all of its parent company’s recall load.[1][11]

13 of 15
Chevrolet campaigns out of GM’s 2026-model-year total. The two it escaped, 26V394 and 26V612, are both Cadillac-only.

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again, and they didn’t get better. Method first, because a census is only as honest as its counting rules: NHTSA’s recalls API returns a 400 error when no recall records exist for a model year, which looks exactly like a broken query if you are not expecting it, so I queried all 23 models the federal vehicle database lists for Chevrolet in 2026, plus trim variants like Silverado 1500, Blazer EV, and Equinox EV, 37 queries in all, kept only campaigns numbered 26V filed in calendar 2026, and deduplicated by campaign number. Thirteen survived. Six more campaigns filed in 2025 also touch 2026 Chevrolets, including a second missing-manual recall and an Equinox EV tire recall, but the census counts 2026 filings, and those miss the window.[1][2]

Two campaigns carry nearly everything, and the first one barely touches a 2026 Chevrolet. Recall 26V539 covers 306,511 small SUVs over a rearview camera that can show a distorted image or go blank, after more than 33,000 owner complaints, because a plasma-cleaning step in manufacturing weakened the glue holding the camera housing together. Sounds enormous. It is enormous, for Buick and for 2025. Exactly seven of the 306,511 vehicles are 2026-model-year Chevrolets, all Trailblazers, built in a six-week window in late 2024. Meanwhile the second giant is the one that actually bites: 26V717 covers 67,380 electric vehicles, Lyriq, Celestiq, Vistiq, and Blazer EV, because brake control module software can misread wheel movement and briefly cut braking at low speed, and of those 67,380, only 3,911 are Chevrolets, 1,314 of them 2026 Blazer EVs, all performance all-wheel-drive SS and police pursuit trims. Add the two together and they account for 86.0 percent of the 434,977 campaign-units in this census, which is a number that describes GM’s paperwork far better than it describes Chevrolet’s cars.[7][10]

Here is where the census earns its keep, because NHTSA’s own data is wrong about something the last three censuses in this series quoted as fact. Every campaign in the API carries an over-the-air flag, and all thirteen read false, which is the basis for the line this site has now printed three times: zero of Ford’s forty, zero of GM’s fifteen, zero over-the-air fixes, but GM’s own remedy text says otherwise. Recall 26V129, the Silverado 3500 fuel-pump stall, fixes the engine control module “by a dealer or through an over-the-air (OTA) update.” Recall 26V717 fixes the brake software “over the air or at a dealer.” Two of thirteen are OTA-capable, the flag says zero of thirteen, and the flag is what every census in the series has been counting. I am correcting the record here, and the correction cuts both ways: it means GM’s software recalls are more modern than the database claims, and it means the database field this series trusted is not a remedy count at all.[9][10]

Then the van, which is where Chevrolet’s year actually lives. Four of the thirteen campaigns belong to the Express, and three of them are steering. In May, 26V345 recalled 232 vans because the gear shifter gives false tactile feedback about which gear you selected, while the dashboard display shows the correct gear, which is a defect you can only catch if you trust the screen more than your hand. In June, 26V399 recalled 26,541 Express and Savana vans because a nut in the steering gear assembly was not properly tightened, after a faulty sensor at supplier Nexteer let hand-tightened assemblies pass inspection. In October, 26V718 recalled 12,715 of the same vans for the same nut, because the June inspection list left out 108 suspect steering-gear serial numbers that GM believed Nexteer had contained at its own plant, and a dealer has to read the serial number off the gear to know whether your van is one of the 127 that actually carry the defect. A recall of a recall, four months later, for the same nut on the same vans, and the vans are the only vehicle in the census where the count is driven by repeat failure rather than shared paperwork. March adds the fourth: 26V127, 826 cutaway vans with tires whose sidewalls were damaged in manufacturing.[5][8][12]

Now the small ones, which are the more interesting half of the census, because this is where the counting gets weird. Recall 26V494 covers eleven vehicles in total, across five models, four of them Silverado EVs, because a press machine at GM’s Ramos plant was misadjusted during scheduled downtime and left a battery-module component unsecured, and the remedy is a complete high-voltage battery replacement booked at nine and a half hours of labor. Recall 26V696 covers six vehicles in North America, three Traverses and three Acadias, because a supplier reviewed laser-welding data from more than 94,000 seat components after one Traverse seatback frame was replaced in July and found six suspect parts, all made on November 3, 2025. Recall 26V304 covers 2,464 full-size SUVs because dealers used the wrong bolts on 24-inch wheels, after GM’s own service information and accessory brake-kit instructions failed to specify the correct fastener, and the 2026 Chevrolet share is 86 vehicles, 51 Tahoes and 35 Suburbans. Recall 26V289 covers 66 vehicles because a missing oil pickup tube can lock the wheels without warning, after one 2026 Silverado 1500 locked a wheel in a parking lot, and it is the only campaign of the thirteen that tells owners to park it.[14] Smallest to largest, the census runs from 6 vehicles to 306,511, a 51,085x spread, the widest in the series so far, beating Jaguar Land Rover’s 41,810x, and the spread is the story: Chevrolet’s recall year is simultaneously a rounding error and a third of a million cars.[4][6][13]

Per-model counts sharpen the picture. Express carries four campaigns, Silverado EV three, Tahoe and Suburban three each, Silverado 1500 and 3500 two each, Blazer EV and Traverse two each, and Equinox, Equinox EV, Colorado, Trailblazer, Corvette, and Silverado 2500 one each. Trax carries zero, because the camera recall covers 2025 Trax only, and so do the gas Blazer and BrightDrop. Strip out 26V114, the missing-manual recall, and Chevrolet’s real-defect count drops to twelve, while the Corvette, Equinox, Colorado, Silverado 2500, and Equinox EV all drop to zero campaigns, because a radio that did not download the owner’s manual is the only reason any of them appear. That one campaign touches thirteen Chevrolet models and 2,500 Chevrolets while representing no mechanical danger at all, and GM filed the same defect once before, as 25V815 in November 2025, for the Silverado pickups only.[3]

Where does thirteen put Chevrolet in the series? Sixth, between Hyundai and Toyota at fifteen and Volvo at twelve, behind Ford at 41 and Stellantis at 27, and ahead of Lincoln at ten, Mercedes at eight, and BMW, Nissan, and Jaguar Land Rover at seven. But the ranking counts campaigns, not units, and on units Chevrolet would sit near the top, because 26V539 alone is larger than the entire recall population of most brands in the series, and only seven of its vehicles are 2026 Chevrolets. Both numbers are true, and they describe different years: a quiet year for new Chevrolets, and a very loud year for GM’s paperwork. Chevrolet is the 22nd brand counted in the series, and this is article number 1,500.[11]

What to do, concretely, starts with the brakes. If you own a 2024 to 2027 Blazer EV in SS or police pursuit trim, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V717 first, because it is the only campaign in this census where the defect is a loss of braking, GM has already recalled 21,469 Lyriqs for a similar brake-software fault in 2024 and the patch did not hold, and the fix is a free software update that GM can push over the air if you have accepted its terms. Then check for 26V399 and 26V718 if you own a 2025 or 2026 Express, because a van cleared under the June recall can show up as open again under the October one, and the fix is a serial-number check with a new steering gear on a match. If you own a 2026 Trailblazer, check for 26V539, but know the odds: seven 2026 Trailblazers in the whole country. If you are shopping used, the 2026 Trax and the gas Blazer are the clean sheets in the Chevrolet lineup, and even those carry 2025 filings, so run the VIN regardless, because every fix in this census is free.

Limitations, stated plainly, before anyone mistakes this census for a ranking. This is a filing census, not a danger ranking. A campaign counts as one whether it covers six seat frames or 306,511 cameras, whether the defect estimate is under 1 percent or 100 percent. Campaign-unit totals are GM-wide, not Chevrolet-only: the 434,977 total sums each campaign’s full Part 573 population across all GM brands and model years, and a vehicle can appear in more than one campaign, so it is not a count of distinct vehicles. Against that total, the verified floor is 14,145 2026-model-year Chevrolet units across the nine campaigns where Part 573 reports split by brand; the Chevrolet shares of 26V127, 26V129, and 26V289 are not split in the filings read, so the true count is higher and unknowable from public filings alone. Counts are a snapshot as of November 13, 2026, and campaigns filed later in the year are not included. Scope is 2026-model-year vehicles with 2026 filings, which excludes six 2025 filings that also touch 2026 Chevrolets. Unit counts come from Part 573 reports and NHTSA acknowledgement letters, and where sources disagree, the article uses the Part 573 figure: 26V304 is 2,464 in the report and 2,457 in the API record, 26V129 is 11,787 in the letter and 11,807 in one mirror, 26V496 is 952 total and 911 in the US per GM’s bulletin, 26V696 is six in North America and four in the US. Zero injuries were reported in the verified filings.[1]

Counterargument, at full strength, because the honest reading deserves a hearing. Chevrolet had a competent, even boring, recall year. Thirteen campaigns is sixth in the series, zero injuries were reported in any verified filing, and the two big campaigns are precautionary recalls for defects that have not hurt anyone, with remedies already defined and letters already scheduled. The genuinely-2026 Chevrolet exposure is a few thousand vans and trucks plus a paperwork recall, and the 434,977 campaign-unit total is mostly other brands’ and other model years’ cars. A low count can mean clean manufacturing or quiet reporting, and this census cannot tell which, and the manual recall inflates thirteen models’ counts by one each while representing no danger. All of that is fair, and this article grants it. Counterweight is the shape of the count: three steering recalls on one van, the second one filed because the first one’s inspection list was wrong, is not a quiet year for that van, and a brake-software recall that repeats a 2024 fix that did not hold is not a rounding error. Thirteen campaigns, zero injuries, and two defects that GM has now recalled twice. Both readings fit the same table, which is why the table is in the article.
