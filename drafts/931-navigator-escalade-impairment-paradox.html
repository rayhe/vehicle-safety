Cadillac Drivers Are Drunker. Their SUV Is Still Safer.

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
September 18, 2026

According to the toxicology reports — and there are a lot of them — the Cadillac Escalade is the drunker SUV. In fatal crashes across the decade FARS tracks, 22.0% of Escalade drivers tested positive for alcohol or drugs, versus 17.7% of Lincoln Navigator drivers.[1] The drunker crowd bought the safer truck: the Escalade kills 0.64 people per 100 million miles while the Navigator kills 1.06.[1]

1.66×
How much deadlier the Navigator is per mile than the Escalade — with soberer drivers

Start with the scoreboard, because I treat impairment data like standings and these standings are upside down. Both flagships ran up nearly identical body counts from 2014 through 2023: 211 dead in Escalades, 209 in Navigators. Same mission, same money, same buyer walking into a showroom choosing between Detroit's two answers to one question. One of those answers is two-thirds deadlier per mile.

The obvious excuse is impairment, so I checked it, because that is literally my job. Back out every impaired driver from both columns and recompute the rates: Navigator 0.87, Escalade 0.50. The gap doesn't close; it widens, to 1.75x. Whatever is killing Navigator occupants at a higher rate, it isn't the bottle or the pills. That is the red herring, and I say this as a man professionally invested in blaming substances: sometimes the truck is just the truck.

Some context, since the segment is a rough class and these two are its valedictorians. Their cheaper siblings are far worse: the Expedition sits at 2.31 deaths per 100 million miles, the Yukon at 2.55.[1] And neither flagship gets to hide behind fleet age. Peak death model years land in the early 2000s for both, which means the gap isn't "old Navigator versus new Escalade." It's old versus old, and one still wins by 0.42 deaths per 100 million miles.

Now the honest caveats, because the tox desk doesn't do victory laps on shaky denominators. Fleet sizes and mileage come from sales data and travel surveys, not odometers, and both of these fleets are small enough that denominator noise could eat part of the 1.66x gap. Escalade owners may log more highway miles, which are safer per mile than city duty. The impairment adjustment also assumes impaired drivers account for a proportional share of deaths, which is itself an estimate. FARS also can't tell me whether the dead were inside the SUV or in the smaller car it hit, and big body-on-frame trucks are aggressor vehicles — this dataset doesn't split the bill.[2] The impairment finding survives all of that; the exact size of the structural gap doesn't, quite.

What to do: shopping a used full-size luxury SUV? The per-mile data picks the Escalade, and it isn't close. If there's already a Navigator in your driveway, check your VIN: the 2018–2021 models carry the 26V402 rollaway recall, the 2022–2025s carry 26V165 for camera and driver-assistance loss, and this summer added a roughly 420,000-vehicle seat-belt pretensioner campaign shared with the Expedition.[3][4] Every one of those repairs is free.
