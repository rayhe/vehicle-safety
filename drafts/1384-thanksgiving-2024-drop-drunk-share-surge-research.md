# Research: #1384 — Thanksgiving Deaths Plunged in 2024. The Drunk Share Did the Opposite.

**Slug:** 1384-thanksgiving-2024-drop-drunk-share-surge

## Kill test: PASS (with one honest caveat, carried into the article)

**Proposed angle:** Thanksgiving travel just posted its best year in the
five-year window and its worst drinking number at the same time. In one
consistent holiday-fatality series, Thanksgiving deaths fell from 490 in
2023 to 344 in 2024, a drop of nearly 30 percent. In that same series, the
share of those deaths tied to drunk driving jumped from 35.5 percent to
47.7 percent, the highest in the five years. Fewer people died. A much
larger fraction of the people who did die were killed by a drunk driver.
The good-news headline and the bad-news headline are both true, and the
second one eats the first.

**Challenge:** is this genuinely new after 1,383 articles, given #1280
already covered Thanksgiving? Yes, three ways. (1) #1280 used 2019-2023
NHTSA data (868 drunk-driving deaths, 35 percent share) and the
Blackout Wednesday / Saturday-after split; it had no 2024 data at all.
This piece is built on the 2024 numbers, published in Zutobi's August
31, 2026 holiday report, which postdate #1280. (2) The thesis inverts:
#1280 said Thanksgiving is deadly because of local impaired nights;
this piece says the holiday just got much less deadly overall while
impairment got much more concentrated, so the remaining risk is more
alcohol-driven than ever. (3) The piece carries its own skepticism: the
same report shows Christmas deaths falling 61 percent in 2024, which
smells like incomplete 2024 reporting rather than a safety miracle, so
the article tells readers not to trust the drop and to trust the share
instead. No prior article makes that drop-versus-share argument.

## Key facts (all traced above; shares are this article's arithmetic)

- Thanksgiving deaths, Zutobi/FIRST series: 490 (2023) to 344 (2024),
  down 146 deaths, 29.8 percent.
- DUI deaths in the same series: 174 (2023) to 164 (2024), down only 10
  deaths, 5.7 percent. The total collapsed; the drunk count barely moved.
- DUI share: 174/490 = 35.5 percent (2023) to 164/344 = 47.7 percent
  (2024), up 12.2 points, the highest in the five-year series (2020:
  35.9 percent; 2021: 35.8 percent; 2022: 34.8 percent).
- Speeding share 2024: 132/344 = 38.4 percent, also up from 179/490 =
  36.5 percent in 2023. Young-driver share 2024: 68/344 = 19.8 percent,
  up from 87/490 = 17.8 percent.
- Cross-check: NHTSA's own 2020-2024 Thanksgiving figure is 910
  alcohol-impaired deaths, 36 percent of period fatalities. Zutobi's
  five-year DUI sum is 897 (185+190+184+174+164), close to NHTSA's 910;
  the two tallies use different holiday windows, so small gaps are
  expected and neither is treated as exact.
- NHTSA 2025 national estimate: 36,640 deaths, down 6.7 percent,
  consistent with a broad national improvement that makes the
  Thanksgiving drop less anomalous on its own.

## Original contribution

1. **The drop-versus-share decomposition:** nobody has split the 2024
   Thanksgiving improvement into its parts. The entire 146-death decline
   came from non-DUI deaths (316 to 180, down 43 percent) while DUI
   deaths fell by just 10. Whatever got safer about Thanksgiving 2024,
   it was not the drinking.
2. **The cross-holiday warning:** the same report shows Christmas
   deaths falling 61 percent in 2024 (389 to 153). Two holidays do not
   independently get 30 and 61 percent safer in one year. That pattern
   is consistent with incomplete 2024 reporting in the underlying data
   (FARS final files lag), which means the level of the drop is
   suspect even though the within-year share shift is harder to explain
   away by undercounting alone.
3. **The toxicology-desk verdict:** impairment is now nearly half the
   Thanksgiving problem by this series' count, up from just over a
   third. A holiday campaign aimed at "everyone drive carefully"
   misallocates effort; the marginal Thanksgiving death in 2024 was a
   drunk-driving death.

## Limitations

- Zutobi is a driver-education company, not NHTSA: its tables compile
  NHTSA FIRST data and NSC data, and its holiday windows are not
  stated per holiday, so its totals cannot be stacked against NHTSA's
  910 or NSC's estimates without noting the definition gap.
- The 2024 figures may be provisional: FARS final data lags, and the
  simultaneous Christmas collapse suggests the 2024 counts could be
  revised upward. The article says this outright and does not present
  the 344 as final.
- Shares are arithmetic on published counts, not a FARS finding; if
  undercounting in 2024 fell disproportionately on non-DUI crashes,
  the share jump would shrink on revision. The article flags this.
- No causal claim is made about why non-DUI deaths fell: weather,
  travel volume, enforcement, and reporting all vary, and none are
  measured in the cited tables.

## Strongest counterargument

The whole story could be a data artifact. If 2024 FARS reporting was
simply incomplete when Zutobi pulled it, both the 30 percent drop and
the 48 percent share could be mirages: late-reported crashes tend to be
the routine ones, and an undercount concentrated in sober crashes
would inflate the drunk share mechanically. The Christmas 61 percent
plunge in the same report is the tell. The reply is proportionality,
not denial: even if the 2024 counts revise upward, the 2023-to-2024
comparison uses one series and one method, and a 12-point share jump
requires the undercount to be sharply skewed by impairment status,
which is a stronger assumption than plain incompleteness. And NHTSA's
own five-year figure already puts alcohol at 36 percent of Thanksgiving
deaths, so the direction (impairment over-represented at Thanksgiving)
does not depend on Zutobi at all.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- Do not let the "Thanksgiving got safer" headline change your plan:
  the deaths that disappeared were the sober ones, not the drunk ones.
- The nights that matter are Wednesday and Saturday of Thanksgiving
  week (per #1280's day-level finding, restated here as background):
  set the sober ride before the first drink, for both nights, not just
  Thursday.
- If you are driving home Sunday: assume the driver next to you at
  midnight is the risk, not the highway. Daylight driving, a sober
  driver, and a seat belt are the three controls that actually move
  this number; NHTSA's own campaign runs Nov 23-29, 2026.
- Check your vehicle before the trip at nhtsa.gov/recalls: a holiday
  trip is the worst time to discover an open recall.
