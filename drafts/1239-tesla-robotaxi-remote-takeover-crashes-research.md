# Research: Tesla Robotaxi Remote Takeovers — Five Interventions, Five Crashes

## Story
Number: 1239
Slug: `1239-tesla-robotaxi-remote-takeover-crashes`
Journalist: Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread — the paradox columnist; safety net as hazard is a pure paradox beat)
Title: "Tesla's Robotaxis Got a Human Backup Driver. Every Documented Backup Driver Crashed."

## The Kill Test
Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. Tesla published its first Robotaxi Safety Report Oct 21, 2026 (3.1M driverless miles, zero injuries) — and six days earlier, NHTSA's Standing General Order data release documented that every remote human takeover in Tesla's crash filings ended in a collision. The safety report's headline number INCLUDES miles where a remote operator was assisting or had taken over (per Tesla's own methodology, reported by TeslaNorth). The backup system is statistically invisible and, in the only data we have, 5-for-5 crashing.

## Key Facts (with sources)

1. **Five remote-takeover crashes.** Two new Tesla filings entered NHTSA's September release of Standing General Order (SGO) ADS data (posted Oct 15, 2026), bringing to five the number of robotaxi crashes in which a remote operator was at the controls. Every documented remote takeover in the filings ended in a collision. (Electrek, Oct 15, 2026)
2. **The two new cases (August 2026, both property-damage-only, no passengers):**
   - Dallas, report 13781-16678: Model Y wedged between concrete mixer and parked SUV. "The remote support operator remotely took control and while moving forward, the vehicle made contact with the SUV at approximately 0.40MPH."
   - Austin, report 13781-16679: Model Y in an alley between utility pole and dumpster; remote operator "was in control"; struck the dumpster at ~1 mph. Both coded Driver/Operator Type: "Remote (Commercial / Test)." (Electrek, Oct 15, 2026; AutoGuide)
3. **The other three (surfaced after Tesla unredacted narratives in May 2026):**
   - July 2025, Austin: ADS refused to proceed; teleoperator took over, drove up a curb into a metal fence at 8 mph.
   - January 2026, Austin: teleoperator took over for navigation help; hit a construction barricade at 9 mph.
   - Houston: Model Y stuck on dead-end street; struck a hidden tree stump at 2 mph. (techand.space, Oct 15, 2026)
4. **36 unique Tesla robotaxi incidents on file** in the NHTSA SGO dataset (reports June 16, 2025 – Sept 15, 2026; Austin 26, Dallas 7, Houston 2, Tampa 1). 31 of 36 list pre-crash speed ≤6 mph; no fatalities; no Tesla airbag deployed; two minor injuries alleged, both July 2025, one involving a remote operator. The file carries no mileage, no fault, no fleet size. (theredfire.com, Oct 15, 2026)
5. **Waymo contrast, on the record:** In a February 2026 letter to Senator Ed Markey, Waymo stated it has not used remote driving in which a human performs the Dynamic Driving Task; its remote staff provide guidance the vehicle can accept or reject. (techand.space, Oct 15, 2026)
6. **Tesla's Safety Report (Oct 21, 2026):** 3,112,541 autonomous miles Jan 22 – Oct 18, 2026, no safety monitor aboard; zero injuries, zero airbag deployments, zero pedestrians/cyclists hit. But: the injury metric counts only collisions where the Robotaxi made FIRST contact (human benchmark includes collisions regardless of which vehicle made contact); two events Tesla did NOT count as injuries; the comparison benchmark covers Texas markets only; and the miles count INCLUDES trips where a remote operator was assisting or had taken over. (teslanewswire.com, driveteslacanada.ca via TeslaNorth, Oct 21, 2026)
7. **The selection-bias caveat (must be stated at full strength):** Tesla does not disclose how often remote operators intervene, or how many interventions succeed. Crash reports exist because there was a crash. 5-for-5 is not a failure rate. The remote operators also intervene in the hardest situations (vehicles already stuck, wedged, or refusing to move), so their task difficulty is not comparable to the ADS baseline.

## Novel Angle
Nobody on the site has done teleoperation. The novel finding: Tesla's own safety headline (3.1M miles, zero injuries) explicitly includes remote-operator miles, while the only per-intervention data point we have — five interventions, five collisions, all at walking speed — suggests the backup driver may be the riskiest driver in the fleet. Plus the Waymo design contrast: advise vs. steer.

## Actionable Insight (required)
For readers in robotaxi cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, Orlando, Tampa, Miami): if a robotaxi gets stuck, it may be taken over by a remote human with no direct view of the street. Give stuck robotaxis space. For everyone else: remote driving is the industry's least regulated corner — no FMVSS requirement for teleoperation, and the data that could tell us its true crash rate is not published by any operator.
