# Research: #1415 — The Seat Belt Paradox: 91% Buckle Up, Half the Dead Don't

**Slug:** 1415-seat-belt-paradox-91-percent-half-the-dead
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread columnist — paradox beat)
**Kicker:** Existential Dread

## The story in one line
NHTSA's new fact sheet (DOT HS 813 840, October 2026) puts two numbers side by side that should not coexist: 91.2% of Americans buckle up, and 48% of the people who die in cars were not wearing a seat belt. The two numbers describe two different populations, and the dying one is young, male, in a pickup, at night, in the back seat, and increasingly concentrated in a handful of states.

## Verified facts (all from NHTSA DOT HS 813 840, Oct 2026, unless noted)
1. **The headline split.** 39,254 traffic fatalities in 2024; 22,713 (58%) were passenger-vehicle occupants. Of those, 10,402 restrained (46%), 9,758 unrestrained (43%), 2,553 unknown (11%). Among deaths with known restraint use: 52% restrained, 48% unrestrained. Unrestrained deaths fell 7.4% vs 2023.
2. **A decade of flat.** Unrestrained share of known-restraint PV deaths: 48% (2015), 48% (2016), 47% (2017-2019), 51% (2020), 50% (2021, 2022), 49% (2023), 48% (2024). The share has not moved in ten years while observed belt use rose from 88.5% (2015) to 91.2% (2024) per NOPUS.
3. **The survival gap.** In fatal crashes in 2024, 48% of those killed were unrestrained vs. only 13% of those who survived. Of all PV occupants in fatal crashes with known restraint use, 26% were unrestrained.
4. **Pickups are the outlier.** 59% of pickup drivers killed were unrestrained (1,780 of 3,027 known), vs. 47% of car drivers, 46% of SUV drivers, 42% of van drivers. Pickup passengers: 57% unrestrained.
5. **Young men.** Ages 21-24 and 25-34: 60% of deaths unrestrained, the highest of any age group. Ages 75+: 26%, the lowest. Kids 4-7: 34%. Men: 53% of male PV deaths unrestrained (7,009 of 13,231 known) vs. 40% of female deaths (2,738 of 6,906 known). Nearly twice as many men (15,008) as women (7,672) died as PV occupants.
6. **Night and the back seat.** 41% of daytime deaths unrestrained vs. 56% at night. Front row: 47% unrestrained; second row: 58%. Second-row middle seat: 67%.
7. **Ejection.** 84% of PV occupants totally ejected in 2024 fatal crashes were killed. Only 1% of belted occupants in fatal crashes were totally ejected, vs. 24% of unrestrained occupants.
8. **What belts are worth.** Lap/shoulder belts cut fatal injury 45% (front-seat car occupants) and 60% (front-seat light-truck occupants); moderate-to-critical injury 50% / 65% (Kahane, 2015). Frontal air bags alone cut fatalities 14% with no belt, 11% with a belt. Car seats cut fatal injury 71% for infants and 54% for toddlers in cars.
9. **The law barely moves the needle.** Primary-law states observed 91.7% belt use vs. 89.2% in secondary/no-law states in 2024, a 2.5-point gap. 35 states + DC + PR had primary front-seat laws; 14 secondary; New Hampshire is the only state with no adult belt law (observed use 78.2%, unbelted death share 59%).
10. **State ranking (FARS 2024 ARF, via Fatal Crash Data).** Highest unbelted share of known-restraint deaths: Hawaii 65% (17 deaths), Wyoming 65% (41), Nebraska 64% (97), Missouri 63% (346), Massachusetts 63% (102), Montana 61% (81), Alabama 61% (344), Delaware 60%, Louisiana 59%, South Dakota 59%, Ohio 59% (375). Lowest: Nevada 36% (58), Oregon 37% (103), New York 38% (175), Michigan 39%, Alaska 39%, Maryland 40%, Washington 40%, Minnesota 41%. Median state: 51%. Largest counts: Texas 1,014 unbelted deaths (46%), California 755 (43%), Florida 632 (44%). Puerto Rico: 72% (87 of 121).
11. **The Hawaii paradox, in the fact sheet's own Table 7.** Hawaii: observed use 97.2%, unbelted death share 65%. Wyoming: observed use 73.4% (lowest in the nation), unbelted death share 65%. Massachusetts: observed 84.4% (second-lowest), death share 63%. Nevada: observed 94.8%, death share 36% (lowest). Observed use does not predict who dies unbelted.

## Original calculations (this article's own, method stated in the piece)
- **27 a day:** 9,758 unbelted PV deaths / 365 = 26.7 per day.
- **~5,200 avoidable:** applying NHTSA's effectiveness figures (45% fatal-injury reduction for cars, 60% for light trucks) to the 9,758 unbelted deaths, weighted by the car/light-truck split of 2024 PV occupant deaths (about 47% cars / 53% light trucks), gives ~53% x 9,758 = ~5,170. Framed as a back-of-the-envelope estimate, not a NHTSA figure: roughly 5,000 of the 9,758 would likely have survived had they been belted.
- **The 8.8-point gap:** 91.2% observed use vs. 48% unbelted among the dead is not a contradiction once you condition on the crash: the dead are drawn from the 8.8% who do not buckle, plus the belted minority who die anyway. The 48%-vs-13% killed-vs-survived split is the cleanest statement of the belt's value in the data.

## Counterargument (at full strength)
- The 48% figure conditions on known restraint use; 11% of deaths have unknown restraint status, and unknown is not random (severe crashes destroy evidence, ejection makes belt use hard to determine). If the unknowns skew belted, the true unbelted share is lower.
- The unbelted dead are also disproportionately impaired, speeding, and in older vehicles; the belt is correlated with other risk behaviors, so the 5,200 counterfactual overstates what belts alone would save. NHTSA's effectiveness figures are causal estimates from crash data, but they assume the crash itself is unchanged.
- Observed use (NOPUS) measures daytime front-seat occupants at intersections; the dead skew nighttime, back seat, and pickup. The comparison is apples-to-oranges by design, and the fact sheet says so.
- State rankings on small counts (Hawaii: 17 unbelted deaths) are noisy year to year; Hawaii's 10-year share is 52%, not 65%.

## Limitations, plainly
- FARS 2024 is the Annual Report File, subject to revision when the Final File lands; the fact sheet notes the 2023 final count was revised up from 40,901 to 41,025.
- Fatal Crash Data's national total (9,768 unbelted, 49%) differs from the fact sheet's (9,758, 48%) by 10 deaths, a counting-vintage artifact; the article uses the fact sheet's national figures and Fatal Crash Data only for state shares.
- Vehicle-type classifications from 2020 onward use vPIC and are not comparable to 2019 and earlier.

## Actionable takeaways
- Buckle up in the back seat: 58% of second-row deaths were unrestrained, and the rear-seat reminder rule does not take effect until September 2027.
- Night driving is when the unbelted die: 56% of nighttime deaths unrestrained vs. 41% daytime. If you drive at night, the belt matters more, not less.
- Pickup owners: 59% of pickup drivers killed were unbelted. The truck does not protect you from physics.
- Check your state's number: in 25 states, most people who die in cars were not wearing a belt.
