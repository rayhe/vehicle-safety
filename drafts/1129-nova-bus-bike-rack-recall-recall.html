Nova Bus Recalled 18 City Buses Because the Fix Used Screws That Were Too Short

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 11, 2026

Nova Bus has recalled 18 of its LFS transit buses because the bike-rack mounting screws are too short, and that sentence deserves a second read. This is NHTSA campaign 26V624000, and it is a recall of a recall: it supersedes 24V873, the earlier recall whose repair was supposed to fix the bike rack. The first repair failed at the level of a screw. Too-short screws strip their threads. Stripped threads don't hold. Now the buses that already went in once have to go back.

18
Buses recalled twice for the same bike rack, 2022–2023 Nova Bus LFS

A front-mounted bike rack on a transit bus lives a hard life. It hangs off the nose of a 40-foot box. That box vibrates at idle, slams over potholes, and takes a constant blast of road air. Holding it there demands real thread engagement: a screw long enough to bite deep into the threaded insert so the joint survives thousands of load cycles. Give it a screw that's a few millimeters short and you get stripped threads. Stripped threads give you a rack that works itself loose over weeks, then falls off in traffic. Simple physics. Expensive paperwork.

That's the defect, plainly: somebody specified or installed a screw without checking the stack-up. Somewhere between the engineering drawing, the parts bin, and the dealership torque wrench, the actual engagement length fell short of what the joint needed. These buses already went in under 24V873, which means either the first fix installed short screws or the underlying design margin was so thin that any installation variation killed it. Nova Bus isn't answering that question in the public filing. What matters is the campaign record: vehicles repaired under the previous recall need the new repair completed.

There is a special irony here, and I don't mind pointing at it. This rack exists to make buses safe for cyclists. It's the equipment that says: you don't have to ride in traffic, we'll carry the bike. When it detaches, it becomes a steel projectile on the road in front of the bus, which is a hazard to the very cyclists it was meant to protect, plus everyone behind them.

Fair counterargument: eighteen buses is a small recall. Transit agencies do daily walk-around inspections, and a loosening front rack is exactly the kind of thing a driver notices before it becomes a projectile. No detachments or injuries appear in the public filings. A falling bike rack is a low-energy event compared to the things that kill people in bus crashes.

Still. The pattern is the story. A recall exists because the first engineering solution failed, and a second recall exists because the first repair, installed by trained technicians at authorized dealers, failed at the most basic mechanical level imaginable. Two layers of failure, one short screw. If your agency runs 2022 or 2023 Nova Bus LFS buses, don't trust the 24V873 close-out paperwork. Schedule recall CR5936 and check the rack hardware yourself. And if you're a cyclist loading your bike onto a bus, give the rack a look before you trust it, because the only thing standing between your front wheel and the pavement is a joint that already failed once: wobble means walk away and tell the driver. Owner letters go out November 2, 2026; check your VIN at NHTSA's recall lookup.

With 18 units, no fatality rate or population-level claim is possible.
