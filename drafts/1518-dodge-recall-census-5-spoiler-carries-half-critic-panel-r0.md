## 1. General Editor — 8.5
Lede earns its keep: a spoiler that does not fit becoming nearly half a brand's recall year is the right opening, and the headline delivers on it. Structure is clean, chronological campaign rundown, shares, the defect-rate contrast, the Charger going blind twice, corrections, counterargument, action list. The dock is length. At 1,514 words this sits at the top of the recent census range (1,140-1,521) and the campaign-by-campaign paragraph is a 123-word run that asks a lot of a skimming reader. Five campaigns need room, and none of the paragraphs is filler, but a table would have carried the same numbers in half the space. Still, the piece never sags, and the length is justified by the material rather than indulgence.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0
Mia is present and distinct: technical, judgmental about fit and finish, excited by the engineering detail ("Let us talk about what happens when the parts do not fit" / "That is why the raw total misleads in both directions at once"). Rhythm metrics pass with margin after a pre-score fix that merged the five share fragments into one flowing sentence; the sentence-length spread is genuinely human. Zero banned phrases, zero em dashes, The-starters at 1.8%. Minor tell: "Fairness requires the other side of the ledger" is a phrase that appears in every census in this series and is starting to read like a template seam. One seam does not break a voice. Ship it.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
No victims are named or exploited; no deaths or injuries are tied to any of the five campaigns in the filings, and the article says so rather than implying otherwise. The spoiler-as-road-debris hazard is stated as a risk to drivers behind the truck, which is accurate attribution of the hazard, not gore. Blame lands on the manufacturer, on suppliers, and on a recall system that issued the same fix twice, never on owners, and the call to check VINs is legitimate civic action with a real mechanism behind it. The "third fix is the one that counts" closer is earned by the documented history, not a slogan. No self-congratulation, no moralizing.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.5
The headline is built to travel, and the pull stat (153,660) is the number people will quote. "Dodge's new Charger has two ways to go blind" is the second quotable line and the one most likely to survive a screenshot. The piece loses half a point because the shareable payload is front-loaded: after the pull stat, the article becomes a chronology that rewards reading rather than skimming, and there is no single-sentence pull quote in the back half as strong as the headline. Fine for the site's audience; slightly less so for a drive-by share.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0
Every checkable claim verified against primary documents. Campaign numbers, dates, and Dodge-only populations all match NHTSA filings: 26V262 (8,528 Charger Daytonas of 20,271), 26V510 (40,386 Hornets of 48,777), 26V528 (74,578), 26V531 (16,838 Chargers of 844,027), 26V701 (13,330). FCA recall numbers (39D, 84D, 61D, 78D, A2D) all match the filings. FMVSS citations are correct per campaign (108/126/135/138/208 for 26V262; 111 for 26V531; 108 for 26V701). The three-campaign spoiler total (4,837 + 139,019 + 74,578 = 218,434) matches #1401's arithmetic. One nit: reference [5] links to the MotorSafety tag page rather than a single article URL; the tag page carries the cited piece, so the citation is still verifiable, but a direct article link would be stronger. Arithmetic verified: tally sums to 153,660; shares sum to 100.0%.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0
Original contribution is real and stated: the Dodge-only tally with cross-brand splits from Part 573 filings, the share arithmetic, the 100%-vs-0.8% defect-rate contrast, the Charger-going-blind-twice synthesis, and the third-recall correction (four truly new campaigns, not five). Limitations are explicit and honest: populations are campaign counts not unique vehicles, defect-rate estimates are FCA's own figures, 26V701's defect rate was not published, the API 400s on 2024-2025 Charger/Durango queries are disclosed with the workaround, and the unverifiable Journey camera entry is excluded with the reason stated. The counterargument is genuinely steelmanned rather than strawmanned ("the tally is big, the danger per vehicle is small, and every fix is free"). Methodology caveat is in the disclaimer as well as the body. Seven references, three of them primary NHTSA documents plus the live API.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0
The pull stat is the thesis number and it is correct. Campaign data is presented chronologically in prose with each Dodge-only population attached to its defect mode, which reads better than a table for this story, though a table would have helped the length problem the General Editor flagged. All numbers are consistent between body, pull stat, and disclaimer, and the multi-brand vs Dodge-only distinction is stated in both the body and the disclaimer. The 100% vs 0.8% contrast is presented with the filed estimates attributed, not as the article's own claim. No chart needed: the story is a count, and the count is the chart.
