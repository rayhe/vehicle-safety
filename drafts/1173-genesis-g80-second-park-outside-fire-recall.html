Hyundai Told 94,622 Genesis Owners to Park Outside. Some of Them Already Had to Do It Once.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 15, 2026

Hyundai recalled 94,622 Genesis and G80 sedans on Thursday because a stuck power-seat switch can overheat the seat motor and start a fire, including while the car is parked and turned off.[1] Owners are advised to park outdoors, away from houses and other vehicles, until repairs are performed. According to NHTSA, the remedy is still being developed. There is no fix, only a parking assignment.

2.3 million
Hyundai and Genesis vehicles told to park outside for fire risk since 2021, excluding 1.73 million Kias

But these cars have been here before. I pulled NHTSA's recall database for the exact same population, 2015–2016 Genesis and 2017–2020 G80, and found campaign 21V160000: an ABS module that could short-circuit and start an engine-compartment fire, owner letters mailed July 2021, flagged in the federal data with the park-outside advisory.[2] Same sedans, different component, second exile from the garage five years apart. The luxury line is not exempt from the park-outside list. It has now earned the distinction twice.

I ran the tally, because nobody else seems to be keeping it. Since 2021, Hyundai has issued park-outside fire recalls covering 567,912 Santa Fe and Santa Cruz vehicles in March 2023 for a tow-hitch harness that could short-circuit (23V181000),[4] roughly 1.64 million more in September 2023 for the ABS module fire (23V651000),[3] and now 94,622 Genesis sedans for the seat-switch problem.[1] The sum: 567,912 + 1,640,000 + 94,622 = 2,302,534 vehicles told to sleep outside. That excludes the 1.73 million Kias in the September 2023 campaign and the uncounted 2021 Genesis ABS population, which I left out because NHTSA's API does not expose the unit count and adding it would double-count the same cars. Across both brands, call it four million, and park outside again.

The seat switch is a different animal from the ABS module, and worth understanding. A stuck switch means the seat motor keeps drawing current against a seat that cannot move. A stalled motor gets hot. Wiring that gets hot lives under the seat, surrounded by foam and fabric, which is not where you want a thermal event, and the genuinely unsettling part is the parked-and-off fire: you are asleep, the car is off, and the seat is quietly cooking itself. That is the failure mode Hyundai cannot yet fix, because the remedy does not exist. Until it does, the entire safety plan is geography: put the car somewhere it can burn without taking the house with it.

What this does not prove deserves its own paragraph. The tally counts vehicles, not fires; a 94,622-vehicle recall is not a 94,622-fire problem, and incident counts for the seat recall were not public at publication time. The campaign was announced today and the Part 573 chronology with Hyundai's fire counts is not yet posted, so anyone quoting a fire number on this defect right now is inventing it. FARS, meanwhile, has no Genesis-brand entries at all, which means there is no fatality-rate context to offer, and FARS does not capture non-fatal fires anyway. This article is about recalled vehicles and instructions, not body counts.

The strongest defense of Hyundai is that this is what aggressive self-reporting looks like. After the Theta II engine saga and the ABS fires, Hyundai reports early and wide, and a manufacturer that files fast will always look worse in a tally than one that buries defects and waits for the lawsuits. The two Genesis defects are genuinely unrelated components sharing only a model range and a remedy instruction, and 94,622 is a rounding error next to the recall pace Ford set this year. All granted: the counterargument is correct and the seat is still a fire risk, and both things are true.

If you own a 2015–2016 Genesis or a 2017–2020 G80, here is what to do. Park outside and away from structures starting tonight, not after the letter arrives. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, because the owner letters have not gone out and the fix does not exist yet, which means nobody is coming to help you this week. Register your current contact information with Hyundai so the remedy notice reaches you when it is developed. And if one of these cars is currently in your garage next to the lawn-mower gas can, move it. The seat does not care that you are asleep.
