Audi Recalled 9,048 Q4 e-trons for Battery Fires. None of Them Caught Fire.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2026

Before you charge that Q4 e-tron past 80 percent, you might want to see this. On October 21, Volkswagen Group of America told NHTSA that 9,048 Audi Q4 e-tron and Q4 Sportback e-tron EVs could suffer thermal propagation, the industry's polite term for a battery fire, when charged above 80 percent state of charge. The root cause is unknown. There is no fix. And as far as anyone on the record can tell, not a single Q4 e-tron has ever burned.[1]

9
Vehicles in the 9,048-car recall that VW estimates actually carry the defective cells. The other 9,039 lose 20% of their range anyway.

You have read this story before, because this is the sequel. In September, VW recalled 22,524 ID.4s for the same 80% cap and the same shoulder shrug about the cause (Crash Report #1172).[2] Now the cap has crossed nameplates and crossed an ocean. The ID.4's suspect cells came from SK Battery America in Commerce, Georgia. The Q4's suspect cells come from SK Battery Manufacturing in Komarom, Hungary. Two factories, two continents, one supplier family, one failure mode. The defect is not a factory problem. It is an SK problem wearing two badges.

What triggered the recall is almost better than the recall itself. On September 21, Audi's Product Safety Committee was briefed on high-voltage battery thermal events in other Volkswagen Group vehicles using the same battery type. Nobody's Q4 caught fire. Somebody else's car did, and that was enough to recall 9,048 Audis. This is a recall defined by someone else's smoke.

Run the numbers VW gave NHTSA. The estimated defect rate is 0.1%. One tenth of one percent of 9,048 is about nine cars. Nine actual problem vehicles, and every owner of the other 9,039 gets to surrender a fifth of their advertised range as a precaution. A 2024 Q4 e-tron rated at 258 miles of EPA range keeps about 206 of them until a fix arrives on a date nobody will name. Fifty-two miles of range, held hostage by nine cars' worth of suspect Hungarian cells.

The interim remedy has one detail worth your attention: if your battery data looks sketchy or is unavailable, your dealer can impose a temporary 80% software limiter on your car.[3] Your usable range is now negotiable at the service desk. Meanwhile the actual owner letters do not go out until December 18, a 56-day gap where the only notification channel is press coverage and dealer gossip. Globally, Electrek puts the VW Group total at 85,402 EVs, including 12,734 Q4s in Germany's KBA database, exactly one of which is registered in Germany.[4]

What to do. If you own a 2024-2025 Q4 e-tron or Sportback built between December 2023 and April 2025: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable since October 23), cap your charging at 80% now instead of waiting for the December letter, and ask your dealer whether your battery data has been flagged for the software limiter. If you are shopping used, those build dates are the population. And if you are wondering whether this is the last one: VW's 80% cap has now hit two nameplates, two continents, and 85,402 vehicles. Bet on a third.
