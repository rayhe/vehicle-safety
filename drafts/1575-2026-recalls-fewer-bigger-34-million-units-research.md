# Research: #1575 — 2026 Filed Fewer Recalls Than 2025. It Recalled 8.6 Million More Cars.

**Number:** 1575
**Slug:** 1575-2026-recalls-fewer-bigger-34-million-units
**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (Data Visualization Editor; statistical roundups, methodology pieces)
**Kicker:** By The Numbers
**Date:** November 24, 2026

## Angle (1-2 sentences)

2026 already passed all of 2025's recall total with six weeks to spare, and it did it with fewer campaigns, not more. Five giant recalls are a quarter of every car recalled this year, one Ford trailer-software fix alone is an eighth of the year's total, and the average campaign is 41% bigger than last year. The recall story of 2026 is not that more things broke. It is that the things that broke broke at fleet scale.

## Kill test

- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. Fresh November data: NHTSA's public recall records through Nov 6 total 34.8 million vehicle units, 19% above all of 2025, with about eight weeks of filings still to come. The Weekly Driver's independent tally through Nov 20 puts it at 731 campaigns and 36.0 million units, against 27.2 million a year earlier. This is the biggest recall year by units in the data, and it is happening while the campaign count falls.
- **Novel on site?** Yes. The site's census series (#1473-#1492, #1501) counts campaigns touching 2026-model-year vehicles only, brand by brand. No prior article aggregates the whole year across all model years, and no prior article measures concentration: five campaigns carrying 26% of the year's units, 60 campaigns carrying 84%, 17 carrying more than half. The reconciliation between the two lenses (Ford 41 campaigns for 2026-model-year cars vs 83-84 campaigns across all model years) is itself a new, computed finding.
- **Original contribution:** the concentration arithmetic below (all computed from the two sources' published figures, not quoted): 26V104 = 12.6% of the year's units; top five = 26.4%; 60 campaigns of 100,000+ units = 84.1% of units from 8.5% of campaigns; average campaign size up 40.6% year over year; Ford's 41% of units from 11.7% of campaigns; the site-census reconciliation table; the October paradox (most campaigns, fewest units per campaign); the park-outside warning count up 40%.

### Concentration (computed from the sources' figures)

- **One campaign, an eighth of the year:** 26V104 (4,381,878 units) = 12.6% of all 2026 recalled units.
- **Five campaigns, a quarter of the year:** the five campaigns above 1 million units total 9,171,719 = 26.4% of the year. At the same point in 2025, four such campaigns totaled 5,005,617.
- **Strip the giants and 2026 still leads:** ex-big-five, 25,623,506 units vs 21,135,174 in 2025, a 21% gap.
- **60 campaigns carry 84%:** the 60 campaigns of 100,000+ units hold 29,251,758 units (84.1%) while being 8.5% of the 707 campaigns that list a unit count. The 17 largest alone are more than half the year.
- **The other end:** 400 of 707 campaigns cover fewer than 1,000 vehicles; 523 cover fewer than 10,000. More than half the year's recalls touch almost nobody.
- **Three makers carry 69%:** Ford 14,219,316 (40.9%), Chrysler/FCA US 5,958,659 (17.1%), Honda 3,717,037 (10.7%). Ford filed 11.7% of the campaigns and 41% of the units.
- **Same count, different scale:** GM filed more campaigns than Honda (27 vs 15) but averages 32,028 units per campaign against Honda's 247,802 and Ford's 171,317.
- **Year-over-year by maker:** Ford filed 44 fewer campaigns than the same stretch of 2025 (83 vs 127) and still added ~2 million units. Chrysler's units more than doubled (2,559,715 to 5,958,659, +133%). Honda's rose 4.5x (827,017 to 3,717,037). Toyota went the other way (3,167,812 to 2,395,634, -24%).
- **Ford 2025 vs 2026 (Weekly Driver):** 146 campaigns in all of 2025 vs 84 so far in 2026 — campaign count down 42%, units up.

### Timing and warnings

- February was the heaviest month: 6,430,474 units across 56 campaigns. February through July account for 27,467,731 units, 78.9% of the year. August-October added 5,766,679 units over 212 campaigns.
- **October paradox:** October had the most campaigns of any month (90) for just 1,658,631 units — the year's busiest filing month by count, one of its lightest by units.
- Park-outside warnings: 21 in 2026 vs 15 at the same point in 2025 (+40%). Weekly Driver counts 22 through Nov 20, plus 16 do-not-drive advisories.
- 84 of 2026's campaigns followed a NHTSA defect investigation; 10 followed compliance testing; 637 were manufacturer-opened.

### Reconciliation: why the site's census says Ford 41 and this article says Ford 83

The site's census series counts campaigns touching **2026-model-year vehicles only** (Ford 41, Lincoln 10, GM 15, Toyota 15, Hyundai 15, Honda 4, Kia 4, Stellantis 27, Nissan 7, Subaru 5, VW 4, Mazda 0, Tesla 4, Mercedes 8). The year totals in this article count campaigns across **all model years** — a 2021 F-150 in 26V104 counts here, not there. Both numbers are right; they measure different things. The census answers "how recall-prone is this year's new car." The year total answers "how much of the fleet got recalled." The gap between them is the story: most 2026 recall volume is old cars, not new ones. 26V104 covers model years 2021-2026; 26V237 covers 2015-2017 F-150s; 26V495 covers 2019-2026 Ram 1500s. The biggest recalls of 2026 are, overwhelmingly, recalls of cars sold years ago.

## Actionable insights

- **Check your VIN now, not at the next oil change.** 34.8 million units means roughly one in eight vehicles on American roads was named in a 2026 recall, and the biggest ones cover cars up to 11 years old. Search by plate or VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; a clean result reads "0 unrepaired recalls associated with this VIN." Recall repairs are free at the dealer.
- **If you own a 2021-2026 F-150, Super Duty, Maverick, Ranger, Expedition, Navigator or 2026 E-Transit:** 26V104's trailer-module software fix is free, by dealer or over the air. If you tow, this is the one to close first — it is the single largest recall of the year and the failure mode is losing your trailer's brakes and lights while towing.
- **If you own a 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator:** 26V363 is a park-outside fire recall with no ambiguity in the instruction. Park outside and away from structures until the wiring is repaired.
- **If you own a 2023-2026 CR-V Hybrid or Accord Hybrid:** 26V366's tire repair kit can fire its cap off like a projectile. Do not use the sealant bottle until the remedy is done; Honda's final remedy was expected in fall 2026.
- **A recall can be open before a fix exists.** Honda's June recalls went out with interim letters and a remedy to follow; Ford's 741,195-vehicle rollaway recall (26V402) lists April 2027 for its fix. An open recall with no remedy yet still means: read the letter, follow the interim instruction, and wait for the second letter.

## Limitations

- **Units are not unique cars.** One vehicle can appear in two or more recalls, so 34.8 million units is not 34.8 million cars. The right unit is vehicle units across recalls. The U.S. fleet is roughly 280+ million vehicles, so the "one in eight" framing is units, not cars — stated as such in the article.
- **Two tallies, two cutoffs.** Motoring Chronicle's 709 campaigns / 34.8 million units run through Nov 6 (data pulled Nov 13); The Weekly Driver's 731 / 36.0 million run through Nov 20. The gap between them is two weeks of filings, not a disagreement. Neither is final; the year has about six weeks of filings left at publication.
- **Exclusions differ.** Motoring Chronicle excludes equipment, tire and child-seat campaigns; The Weekly Driver includes every vehicle campaign (trucks, buses, RVs, trailers, motorcycles) and counts each campaign's vehicles once. 28 campaign numbers from 26V001 to 26V737 returned no public record in Motoring Chronicle's pull; two records list no unit count and enter as zero.
- **Counts measure reporting, not danger.** A single over-the-air software fix (26V104) covering 4.38 million vehicles inflates the unit total without 4.38 million dealer visits. Ford's November 2024 consent order pushes aggressive early filing, which lifts its campaign count; other makers may file less aggressively. A big recall can be a sign of a company finding problems, not of cars getting worse.
- **The site census and the year total are different lenses.** The census series' per-brand counts (Ford 41, etc.) cover 2026-model-year vehicles only and understate each maker's total campaign count by design. Do not present 41 and 83 as conflicting; present them as the new-car slice vs the whole fleet.

## Strongest counterargument

The whole story could be an artifact of one software update. Strip 26V104 out and 2026 drops from 34.8 million to 30.4 million units — still above all of 2025's 29.2 million, but the margin shrinks from 19% to 4%, and the "fewer campaigns, more units" frame leans heavily on a single over-the-air fix for a trailer light that most owners will never notice. Campaign counts are also a reporting choice: Ford files aggressively under a consent order, and a maker that quietly fixes the same defect in a service campaign never appears in NHTSA's count at all. And the fleet is not actually getting more dangerous — the traffic fatality rate fell to 1.02 per 100 million miles in the first half of 2026, the second-lowest on record. Recalls are up; deaths are down. The honest reading is that the system is finding and fixing more, faster, and at fleet scale through software — which is the system working, not failing. The counterargument weakens on the breadth: even without the big five, 2026 leads 2025 by 21%, park-outside warnings are up 40%, and Chrysler's units more than doubled on the back of a seat belt anchor defect, not a software push. Concentration is real even if its largest single cause is benign.
