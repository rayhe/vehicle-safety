Mack's Newest Trucks Are Shedding Their Grilles. The Fix Is a Cable Tie.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 10, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. Mack Trucks has filed a federal recall covering 3,096 of its newest Pioneer and Anthem tractors because the intake and grille bezels can detach at speed and become road hazards for everyone else on the highway.[1] Read that again. Not the brakes, not the steering: the face of the truck is falling off.

3,096
2025–2027 Mack Pioneer and 2026–2027 Anthem tractors recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V631

NHTSA's summary is one paragraph of bureaucratic poetry. The intake and grille bezels may detach from the vehicle, and "a detached bezel can become a road hazard for other vehicles, increasing the risk of a crash."[1] The component code tells the rest of the story: STRUCTURE:BODY:BUMPERS:ACTIVE SHUTTERS/GRILL. This is the aero furniture, the sculpted trim ringing the truck's face, the hardware Mack's brochures credit for slipperier aerodynamics and better fuel numbers. On up to three thousand trucks, that hardware is auditioning for a career as interstate debris.

Now the remedy, quoted here because paraphrase would only soften it. Dealers will inspect, replace the intake and grille bezels, and install cable ties as necessary, free of charge.[1] Cable ties. Zip ties. The same fasteners holding your neighbor's Christmas lights to the gutter are now the factory-specified retention upgrade for a flagship Class 8 tractor. Mack's internal number for this campaign is SC0501, and its customer line is 1-800-866-1177 if you own one and would like your grille mechanically persuaded to stay attached.[1]

Laugh, then picture the physics. A detached bezel does not politely pull over. At highway speed it becomes a chunk of molded plastic tumbling through the following lane, and the car behind it has roughly two seconds to choose between braking hard, swerving, or eating it. Swerve wrong and you have traded a grille panel for a multi-vehicle pileup. These are not tired twenty-year-old workhorses either. Mack launched the Pioneer line for 2025; the newest trucks in this population are barely broken in, still smelling like the dealership, already eligible to jettison their own noses.[2]

Here is the strongest case against everything above, stated at full strength, because anger without honesty is just noise. No crashes or injuries are listed in the filing summary, the bezels are lightweight trim rather than structural steel, and 3,096 trucks is a rounding error against the hundreds of thousands of Class 8 tractors on American roads. Mack filed this voluntarily and early, before the pattern had time to graduate from embarrassing to deadly, which is exactly what the recall system is supposed to incentivize. If the worst that ever comes of it is a few thousand zip-tied grilles and one very red-faced engineering team, this column will consider that a win.

On practical matters: if you run a 2025–2027 Mack Pioneer or a 2026–2027 Anthem, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls under campaign 26V631. The VINs have been searchable since October 8, 2026; owner notification letters go out November 30; the bezel replacement and the cable ties are free.[1][4] Everyone else gets the cheaper lesson, the one that costs nothing: if the Mack in front of you has a grille that is hanging by a thread, or half of one, give it room. That thread has a federal campaign number now, and it is one bump away from your lane.
