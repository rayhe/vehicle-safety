300,000+ GM SUVs Just Got Recalled Because the Backup Camera Shows Nothing

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 19, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. This week General Motors recalled more than 300,000 of its best-selling small SUVs, the 2024-2026 Buick Encore GX and Chevrolet Trailblazer, the 2024-2025 Buick Envista, and the 2025 Chevrolet Trax, because the rearview camera can display a distorted image or a blank one.[1] No warning light, no chime. The screen that is supposed to show what is behind you can show nothing at all, and you would only find out by backing over something.

78%
drop in children killed in backover crashes after backup cameras became mandatory in 2018: a gain that only exists if the camera actually works

Federal law has required rear visibility systems on every new passenger vehicle since May 2018, and it was not a symbolic gesture. Regulators had counted roughly 210 deaths and 15,000 injuries a year from backover crashes, with kids under five making up 31 percent of the fatalities.[4] A study out of UTHealth Houston, presented at the American Academy of Pediatrics conference in 2025, found that after the mandate, pediatric backover trauma cases fell by more than half and child deaths dropped 78 percent.[3] That is the single most successful child-safety mandate of the last decade, and it runs on a camera and a screen that must not go blank.

Now look at who bought the recalled cars. The Envista and Trax are among the cheapest new vehicles sold in America, and the Encore GX and Trailblazer are not far behind. Young families, first-time buyers, people stretching to a new car specifically so it comes with every safety feature as standard equipment. They paid for 2025-model-year safety and got 2017-model-year rear visibility, plus a glowing rectangle in the dashboard that presents itself as working equipment.

And GM has been here before, with these exact nameplates. In November 2023 the company recalled the Encore GX, Envista, and Trax because a software fault in the Virtual Cockpit Unit could blank the instrument panel at startup or while driving:[2] blank screens, round two, three model years later. This time there is no over-the-air fix: NHTSA's campaign record flags this remedy as dealer-only, so you have to bring the car in and let a technician inspect and replace the camera, free of charge.[1] Owner notification letters started mailing September 16, 2026, and GM's internal number for the campaign is A262554900.

None of the reporting I have seen ties a specific injury to this defect, and I will not invent one. Consider the failure mode on its own terms: a driver who glances at a blank screen, assumes the camera is between frames or the sun is glaring, and keeps backing up is operating on 2017-era confidence with 2026-era habits. Regulators estimated the mandate would save 58 to 69 lives a year once the fleet turned over.[4] Every one of those saved lives is conditional on the screen lighting up.

What you do is simple and costs nothing. If you own or lease a 2024-2025 Envista, a 2024-2026 Encore GX, a 2024-2026 Trailblazer, or a 2025 Trax, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today instead of waiting for the letter,[5] then book the dealer visit. And until the camera is fixed, reverse the old way: mirrors, shoulder check, the head-turn, because that is not paranoia. That is how everyone drove before 2018, back when 210 people a year died doing it the camera-free way.

What this article did not prove: no injuries tied to this defect are reported in any source reviewed, and none are claimed here. The “more than 300,000” figure is reported by industry briefing coverage of the campaign, not stated in the NHTSA campaign record itself. FARS data is not used for backover attribution in this article; the fatality figures come from NHTSA’s 2014 rulemaking estimates and the 2025 UTHealth study, which covered children under five at a single pediatric trauma center plus public records, not the national fleet.

Strongest case against this article: a camera recall is routine, the fix is free, and nobody has been hurt; treating a dealer camera swap like a safety crisis overstates the case. The 2023 blank-panel recall was software, fixable over the air, while this is a hardware camera replacement, so calling it a “pattern” stretches two different failure modes into one narrative. Fair points. But the counterpoint stands: the screen exists because 210 people a year used to die without it, and a safety device that silently fails is worse than none, because none at least tells the driver to turn around.
