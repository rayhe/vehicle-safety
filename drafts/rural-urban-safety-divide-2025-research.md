# Research: Rural-Urban Safety Divide in 2025

## Source
NHTSA CrashStats DOT HS 813 829, "Early Estimates of Motor Vehicle Traffic Fatalities and Fatality Rate by Sub-Categories in 2025," July 2026.
URL: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813829

## Key Data Points

### Overall
- 36,640 deaths in 2025, down 6.7% from 39,254 in 2024
- Fatality rate: 1.10 per 100M VMT (down from 1.19 in 2024)
- Q1 2026 rate: 0.99 per 100M VMT (second-lowest Q1 ever)

### The Rural-Urban Divergence
- **Urban fatalities: DOWN 10%**
- **Rural fatalities: DOWN only 2%**
- Urban interstates: DOWN 10%
- Urban arterials: DOWN 12%  
- **Rural interstates: UP 1%** (ONLY road type with an increase)
- Rural arterials and local/collector roads drove overall rate decline

### Key Quote from Report
"The trend of the total fatality rate per 100 million VMT in 2025 was strongly driven by the trends in the fatality rates per 100 million VMT on rural arterials, rural local/collector/street roadways, and urban arterials."

### Other Sub-Category Data
- Newer vehicles (<10 yrs): DOWN 12%
- Older vehicles (≥10 yrs): DOWN 9%
- Speeding: DOWN 11% (to ~10,035 deaths)
- Unrestrained: DOWN 13%
- Ejected: DOWN 13%
- Rollover: DOWN 11%
- Pedestrians: DOWN 8%
- Motorcyclists: DOWN 8%
- **Pedalcyclists: UP 4%**
- 65+ age group: DOWN 0%
- 25-34 age group: DOWN 13%
- Passenger fatalities: DOWN 12%
- Driver fatalities: DOWN 5%

## Novel Angle
The 5:1 urban-rural improvement ratio (10% vs 2%) means America's safety revolution has a geography problem. AEB, ESC, lane-keeping assist, better headlights — these technologies deliver their biggest benefits on urban roads where infrastructure supports them (lane markings, lighting, lower speeds, shorter response times for EMS). Rural roads get almost none of this benefit. Rural interstates actually got WORSE.

## FARS Cross-Reference
Pickups dominate rural driving. FARS data shows pickups account for 41,593 deaths across the dataset. The pickup class has a death rate of 8.86 per 100M VMT. Rural pickup driving — high speeds, two-lane roads, longer EMS response times — is the core of the rural safety gap.

## Original Contribution
Calculate the "geography gap" in lives: if rural areas had improved at the urban rate (10%), approximately 800+ additional lives would have been saved in 2025. Rural areas account for ~49% of all traffic deaths despite representing ~19% of the population.
