Ford Recalled 223,472 F-150s Because the Gas Tank Can Fall Out. This Is the Third Time in Fifteen Years.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 12, 2026

Ford would like you to look under your truck. Specifically, at the thirty-gallon container of highly flammable liquid strapped beneath the bed, to confirm it is still attached. On Saturday the company recalled 223,472 F-150s from the 2023 through 2027 model years because their fuel tanks may be improperly secured and could detach while driving.[1] A detached tank becomes a road hazard, and a leaking one becomes a fire, and some of these trucks, according to the federal notice, are both.

223,472
2023–2027 F-150s recalled September 12, 2026, for fuel tanks that may detach while driving

None of this is new. In 2011, Ford recalled 1.1 million F-150s and F-250s after road salt corroded the steel straps holding up their fuel tanks, and the tanks started dropping out of decade-old trucks across the snow belt.[3] Federal investigators had collected 243 reports of tanks falling, including 95 with fuel leakage, nine with sparks, and two fires, one of which destroyed the pickup entirely.[4] In 2022, Ford recalled exactly one F-150, a 2021 model that left the Dearborn plant with its fuel tank strap bracket simply missing, because the frame had arrived from a supplier in Mexico without it and nobody noticed until engineers reviewed the assembly-line camera footage.[2] Now, fifteen years after the corrosion recall, the straps are back, and dealers will inspect and replace them, free of charge, on trucks that in some cases are barely a year old.

Here is the part that should unsettle you more than a repeat defect would: these are three different failures. Salt chemistry on old trucks, a missing bracket on one truck, and now an unspecified strap defect on new trucks. If it were one mistake, you could fix it once and move on. Three unrelated ways for the same component to fail across fifteen years is not a single defect, it is a category. One bad batch of straps was never the through line. It is a component that keeps failing in new ways, which is worse, because you cannot engineer your way out of a problem you keep redefining.

For scale, Ford sold 828,832 F-Series trucks in the United States last year, which means Saturday's recall covers more than a quarter of a model year's worth of sales.[6] The F-150 has been the best-selling vehicle in the country for more than four decades, and its fuel is held aloft by steel straps, a technology older than the moon landing that apparently still contains surprises. From the 2011 investigation, the arithmetic of a dropped tank ran 243 reports to 95 leaks to nine sparks to two fires. Apply that chain to a campaign where the tanks are described as both leaky and improperly secured, and you are looking at the worst pairing of adjectives in the federal recall database.

The honest counterargument deserves its full weight. Road salt dissolving steel on fourteen-year-old trucks is chemistry, not a design flaw, and every manufacturer with steel undercarriages in the snow belt fights the same war. The 2022 recall covered a single truck, which is less a defect trend than a rounding error with paperwork. Ford self-reported all three campaigns, including the one-truck recall, which is the safety system working exactly as designed: detect, disclose, fix. Stacked against that, the trilogy framing is narrative convenience, three different root causes wearing the same headline, and that is fair. It is also true that no other component on the best-selling vehicle in America has needed federal intervention three times for the same failure mode, and that fact survives every caveat.

So check: if you own a 2023 through 2027 F-150, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not at your next oil change. A loose tank announces itself with new noise or vibration from under the bed, according to Ford's own 2022 filing, so treat unfamiliar clunks as information.[2] If you smell fuel, park outside and away from structures before you call the dealer, because the 2011 chain went leak to spark to fire, and sparks do not negotiate. The strap inspection and replacement are free; gravity, as always, is not.
