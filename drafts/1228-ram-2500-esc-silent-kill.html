Your Ram 2500's Rollover Reflexes Can Vanish Without Warning. 8,329 Trucks Just Got Recalled.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 22, 2027

Let's talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds. Your truck starts to rotate somewhere it shouldn't, the ESC computer compares where the steering wheel says you're going with where the truck is actually going, and it brakes individual wheels to pull you back in line. In certain 2026 Ram 2500s, that entire reflex arc runs through one module, and that module can reset mid-drive and sever the loop with no warning at all.[1]

50%
Of fatal single-vehicle crash risk that ESC eliminates, per IIHS. That is the protection these trucks silently lose.

On October 6, FCA US filed NHTSA campaign 26V-651 (FCA recall 90D) covering 8,329 certain 2026 Ram 2500 pickups. Some steering column control modules can reset and disable stability-control communications, which means the trucks fail to comply with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 126, the federal ESC mandate. The warning light only illuminates after the reset occurs. Read that again: there is no warning prior to failure. The dashboard tells you the system is dead only once it is already dead.[1]

Why does one module matter so much? The steering column control module is the hub for driver inputs: turn signals, wheel buttons, and critically the steering-angle sensor. ESC is a closed loop. Without the steering-angle signal, the computer cannot compare intended direction against actual heading, so it does the only safe thing it can: nothing. Your three-quarter-ton truck becomes, in ESC terms, a pre-2012 vehicle. And 2012 matters, because that is when ESC became federally mandatory, precisely because the technology works so well that regulators refused to leave it optional.[2]

How well it works is not a matter of opinion. IIHS studies show ESC cuts fatal single-vehicle crash risk by about half and fatal multi-vehicle crash risk by 20 percent for cars and SUVs, and it reduces fatal single-vehicle rollovers by 75 percent for SUVs and 72 percent for cars.[3] NHTSA's own analysis credits ESC with saving an estimated 4,100 lives in 2010 through 2014, including 1,580 in 2014 alone, more than double the 682 saved in 2010 before the mandate took hold.[3] FARS records 153 deaths in Ram 2500s across 2014 to 2023.[4] Every one of those trucks rolled off the line with ESC as a matter of federal law, and every driver assumed the reflex was there.

One more detail deserves your attention: 26V-651 is an expansion of recall 26V-244 from April 2026. The affected population grew between the two filings. If you own a 2026 Ram 2500 and checked your VIN back in April and cleared, check again. The VIN list on nhtsa.gov was updated October 13, and the second filing exists because the first one underestimated the problem.[5]

What to actually do. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for the owner letter, which FCA expects to mail November 24. If your truck is in the population, schedule the dealer visit: the remedy is a physical module replacement, free, not an over-the-air update, and the NHTSA record lists no home diagnostic. While you wait, drive the truck like ESC might be gone, because it might be: longer following distances, no heroics on wet curves, and particular care when towing, which is the highest-consequence use case for a stability system that prevents rollovers. If you are shopping a used 2026 Ram 2500, run the VIN before you buy; an open 90D needs the module swapped before towing duty.[1]

Limitations, because the data deserves honesty: FARS covers 2014 to 2023, so the 2026 model year trucks in this recall have no observed outcome data at all. The filings describe risk, not a body count; the sources found report no crashes, injuries, or deaths tied to this specific defect. Most 2026 Ram 2500s are unaffected, and the published ESC effectiveness figures come from car and SUV studies, since nobody has published heavy-duty-pickup-specific estimates. To steelman the other side: brakes, steering, and the driver all still work. ESC only intervenes in loss-of-control scenarios, most miles never call on it, and the fix is a simple free module swap with letters already going out. This is a federal-noncompliance recall that is being handled, not a cover-up. But a safety system that fails silently, on a truck built to tow, with the warning arriving only after the fact, is a design choice somebody signed off on. I would like to meet that signature.
