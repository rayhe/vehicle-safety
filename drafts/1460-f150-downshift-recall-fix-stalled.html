Ford Recalled 1.39 Million F-150s. Then the Fix Stopped Showing Up.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 10, 2026

In April, Ford recalled 1,392,935 F-150 pickups because their transmissions can drop from sixth gear to second at highway speed, without warning, without driver input, and sometimes with the rear wheels locked.[1] The fix was a free software update. Seven months later, owners say dealers have stopped installing it.

Start with the part that breaks. Inside the 6R80 six-speed automatic sits a molded wiring carrier called the lead frame, bolted to the valve body, carrying the signal from the transmission range sensor to the powertrain computer. Years of heat cycles and vibration degrade its electrical connections, the signal drops out, and the computer reads a gear position that does not exist. It believes the bad reading and commands a downshift.[1] Ford's own worst case, filed with NHTSA, is a truck doing 35 to 64 mph dropping from sixth straight to second.

43%
Of owners in NHTSA's 329-complaint pool reported at least one rear-wheel lockup: tires locked, screeched, or skidded during an uncommanded downshift.

NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation in March 2025, upgraded it to an engineering analysis in January 2026, and Ford's field review committee approved the recall on April 7.[1] By April 2, Ford had counted 444 warranty claims, 121 field reports, 105 customer-service reports, 316 owner complaints to NHTSA, two injuries, and one crash potentially related to the condition. Its chronology lists related reports going back to April 24, 2015. Eleven years from the first report to the recall, and the trucks in question were built between March 2014 and August 2017, which means some of them spent a decade dropping gears before anyone filed the paperwork.

And then the fix stalled. On October 29, The Autowire reported that F-150 Forum users were relaying dealer statements that recall 26S28 was on hold, while Ford's public recall page still said a remedy became available July 9, with no restart date.[5] On November 10, owners told reporters the software update had stopped being applied at dealerships, and Atlanta News First reported that Ford paused the update because of a parts issue, then agreed to give one affected owner a loaner after the station called the company about his truck.[6] Neither Ford nor NHTSA has published confirmation that the recall is suspended nationwide. Nobody has published a scope, a cause beyond "parts," or a date when it resumes. A recall with a remedy that cannot be installed is a press release with a VIN lookup attached.

Run Ford's own numbers, and the picture gets worse: Ford estimates about 1% of the recalled population actually carries the defect, which works out to roughly 13,900 trucks, but the software is the only protection for all 1.39 million, and the physical lead frame replacement goes only to trucks that have already logged the matching fault codes. A paused rollout leaves the whole population exposed to a defect Ford says affects fourteen thousand of them, with no way for any individual owner to know which side of that line their truck sits on. Meanwhile NHTSA's engineering analysis, EA26-001, is still open, which is exactly the condition an open file exists to catch: the agency keeps the case open while a remedy reaches owners, to see whether it works. A remedy that stops moving is the test case. And NHTSA's own test center found a second failure mode the recall paperwork never mentions: if the sensor signal drops while a truck reverses up an incline, the transmission can go to neutral and the truck can roll forward. Even a perfectly executed 26S28 leaves that one untouched.[1]

The counterargument, at full strength: a pause is not a cancellation, and a parts shortage delaying a software update by weeks is a supply-chain story, not a safety scandal. No owner report of the stall has been independently verified by a regulator, and forum posts plus one local television report are thin evidence for a nationwide claim. Ford has confirmed nothing, NHTSA has confirmed nothing, and the absence of confirmation cuts both ways. Only about 1% of the trucks carry the defect, so the overwhelming majority of owners face no real risk even while the rollout pauses. The reverse-rollaway scenario needs a specific combination, signal loss plus reversing uphill, that may be vanishingly rare in practice. And NHTSA keeping EA26-001 open is routine procedure, not a signal of alarm. All of that is true, and it is also true that Ford has done this before: in June, this site documented 318,367 Ford vehicles re-recalled because a dealer diagnostic tool had been reporting successful software updates that never actually installed.[7] Two different mechanisms, same outcome: owners told their truck is fixed, driving trucks that are not.

What this does not prove: owner reports of the pause are unverified, and neither Ford nor NHTSA has published confirmation of a suspension, so the geographic scope and duration are unknown. Complaint counts are self-reported, and NHTSA's 329-complaint pool is a complaint sample, not a measured failure rate. Ford's 1% defect estimate comes from Ford's own population analysis, not an independent audit. No fatality count is claimed for this defect, because FARS data cannot attribute deaths to a specific transmission fault. Do not read a paused rollout as a cancelled recall, or an open investigation as a finding of wrongdoing.

What This Means for You

If you own a 2015 to 2017 F-150 with the 6R80 six-speed, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now. Check the door sticker or transmission code, because 2017 trucks with the 10-speed are exempt. If campaign 26V237, Ford number 26S28, is open on your truck, call your dealer and ask one specific question: can the powertrain control module recalibration actually be installed this week. Do not accept "the recall is open" as an answer; ask whether the update can be done. If your truck has ever downshifted on its own, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov, because the 329 complaints in NHTSA's file are what forced this recall into existence. And if you reverse up inclines, use the parking brake before shifting out of reverse on a slope. That failure mode is not in the recall at all.
