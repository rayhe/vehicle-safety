## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
Every claim traces to a cited source. "Repairs are free either way" is accurate under federal law (manufacturers must remedy safety recalls at no charge). Do Not Drive and Park Outside are described in NHTSA's own terms. The Carfax figures are attributed to Carfax via DriveDocket, not presented as NHTSA's. The #1123 reference is correctly identified as this site's own queued analysis. No case law, no statutory claims beyond the free-repair rule. One nit, already handled in the disclaimer: the September Park Outside figure is a floor, and the article says so.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Original contributions are real and checkable: (1) the Sept-to-Oct Park Outside movement (3.2M+ to under 3M, at least 200,000 in a month), computed from the tracker editions; (2) the verification of #1123's ~650K/year Takata removal-rate prediction against the new 4.6M count (~700K/year, agreement within ~8%). Limitations are stated in the disclaimer, not buried: Carfax estimates vs NHTSA tallies, the floor caveat, removal rate vs repair rate, open count vs campaign-coverage count. Strongest counterargument is stated at full strength. Deduction: the 700K/year figure depends on the 2024 baseline being "more than 6 million" at an unspecified point in 2024, so the "~2 years" denominator is approximate; the article hedges with "near" and "about," which is the honest handling, but the precision is inherently soft.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0/10
Numbers are consistent and well-framed: 1.3M, under 3M, 4.6M, 6M, 650K vs 700K per year, 83-90% vs 38%, one in five. The 650K-vs-700K comparison is the article's best data moment and is presented as agreement, not false precision. The pull stat matches the lede's headline number. No chart is needed at 406 words; the numbers carry themselves.
