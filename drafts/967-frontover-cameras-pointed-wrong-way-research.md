## Angle (kill test: PROCEED)

Congress made backup cameras mandatory in May 2018 after an 11-year rulemaking slog. While the industry installed cameras pointed backward, child deaths in front of vehicles doubled. Nobody mandated anything facing forward. There is no FMVSS for front visibility. The frontover is the backover's twin, except the law never noticed it.

Zero prior coverage: no "frontover" in any of the 816 published stories or 174 queued drafts.

## Primary sources

1. **DOT non-traffic data via WardsAuto** ("Stopping 'Frontovers' in Their Tracks"): 526 youngsters killed in low-speed frontover incidents in 2020, up from 251 deaths in 2008. In more than 60% of incidents, the child's parent is behind the wheel. Front blind zones on large SUVs/trucks extend to 16 feet. https://www.wardsauto.com/news/archive-wards-stopping-frontovers-in-their-tracks/797284/

2. **Consumer Reports blind-zone testing** (15 models; via GM Authority/CR): full-size pickup front blind zones 11 feet longer than sedans, 7 feet longer than popular SUVs. Hood heights on big trucks up 11% on average (2000–2018); new pickup curb weight up 24% over the same span. KidsAndCars.org tally: 931+ frontover fatalities 1990–2019, victims mostly ages 12–23 months, 80%+ involved truck, van, or SUV. https://gmauthority.com/blog/2021/06/big-trucks-dangerous-for-pedestrians-and-small-car-occupants-says-consumer-reports/comment-page-3/

3. **STOP Frontovers Act of 2024** (Sen. Blumenthal, introduced Dec 16, 2024): directs NHTSA to initiate FMVSS rulemaking within 1 year of enactment and issue a final standard 1 year after initiation. Requires detection of pedestrians including toddlers in a defined "perception zone," driver notification AND active intervention systems. Endorsed by Consumer Reports, Center for Auto Safety, Advocates for Highway and Auto Safety, Kids and Car Safety. https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2024-12-16_bill_text_stop_frontovers_act.pdf

4. **NHTSA NCAP overhaul** (finalized Nov 18, 2024, AP via Tom Krisher): starting with 2026 model year, 5-star program adds pedestrian AEB, blind spot warning/intervention, and rates vehicle front ends on pedestrian injury likelihood. "Not just the safety of people inside vehicles... but how the design of a vehicle could prevent a crash." — Pete Buttigieg. https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/business/2024/11/18/us-to-add-features-such-as-blind-spot-warnings-and-pedestrian-detection-to-vehicle-crash-ratings/

5. **NHTSA pedestrian safety rule proposal** (Sept 9, 2024): Blumenthal statement — the proposed head-to-hood impact rule covers adult pedestrians and older children but "fails to account for children of all ages, including our nation's youngest." Calls out frontover incidents specifically. https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/newsroom/press/release/blumenthal-statement-on-new-nhtsa-pedestrian-safety-rule

6. **Cameron Gulbransen Kids Transportation Safety Act of 2007** (context): mandated rear visibility rule; full compliance May 2018 (delayed 4 times per CR). Rear cameras mandatory on all new vehicles since then. (Background from kidsandcars.org PDF: https://www.kidsandcars.org/document_center/download/how-the-rear-visibility-standard-was-won/No-More-Backing-Blind.pdf)

## Novel contribution (original analysis)

1. **The doubling paradox:** Frontover child deaths went from 251 (2008) to 526 (2020) — a 2.1x increase — during the exact decade America spent installing federally mandated rearview cameras on every new car. The safety apparatus aimed its cameras backward while the front of the fleet grew into a wall. Rear blind zones got cameras; front blind zones got bigger (hood +11%, weight +24%).

2. **Regulatory-timeline arithmetic:** The Gulbransen Act (2008) to full rear-camera compliance (May 2018) = ~10 years, with 4 delays. STOP Frontovers Act (Dec 2024) mandates a 2-year NHTSA timeline. If history rhymes: passage (2027?) + 10-year rulemaking/compliance cadence → fleet-wide front detection ~2037. At ~526 deaths/year, the waiting-room toll is roughly 5,000+ additional child deaths before the mandate bites. Even the bill's own optimistic 2-year timeline plus fleet turnover (average vehicle age ~12.5 years) means driveways stay deadly through the 2030s.

3. **The screen is already there:** Backup cameras are mandatory since 2018, which means every new vehicle already has a dashboard screen and a camera pipeline. CR's Jen Stockburger notes front/trail cameras already exist on many pickups and SUVs (for off-roading). The incremental cost of pointing one forward is trivial relative to the rear-camera fight — yet no standard exists. The industry sells you a trail camera to see rocks; it won't show you toddlers.

## Limitations
- No standardized definition of "frontover" — the STOP Frontovers Act itself requires NHTSA to define the term. DOT's 526 figure (2020) comes from non-traffic surveillance with opaque methodology; NHTSA hasn't published updated non-traffic data summaries recently (last covered through 2015 per Blumenthal's 2019 letter).
- KidsAndCars.org counts are media-scraped, not a national census — they undercount non-fatal injuries.
- CR's 15-model blind-zone test is not a fleet-representative sample.
- Cameras/sensors only help if the driver looks: backover cameras did not eliminate backovers. Technology ≠ attention.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The real cause is inattention, not geometry. A camera doesn't make a rushing parent look at the screen. Frontover tech (surround-view, front sensors) already exists as an option buyers skip; mandating it just taxes every new car for a driveway problem that a 30-second walk-around solves. Backover cameras became mandatory and backovers still happen. And the deaths-per-vehicle-mile framing flatters the panic: these are low-speed driveway incidents in a country with 280M vehicles, and the parents who need the warning most are the ones who won't pay attention to it.

## Actionable insights
- Shopping now? Prioritize models with front-view/surround-view cameras and front parking sensors — they're often optional, not standard, and rarely safety-marketed. Trail cameras on pickups are for rocks, not kids; ask the dealer which cameras are tied to low-speed object detection.
- Every driveway departure: walk around the vehicle first, especially with toddlers (12–23 months is the peak victim age — old enough to run, too young to be seen).
- Aftermarket front cameras/sensors exist and are cheap relative to a new truck.
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — unrelated, but standard.
