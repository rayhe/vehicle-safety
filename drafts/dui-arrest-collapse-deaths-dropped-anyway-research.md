# Research: DUI Arrests Fell Off a Cliff. Deaths Dropped Anyway.

## Story Angle
DUI arrests plummeted during COVID and never recovered. The NORC study documents the enforcement collapse: "DUI arrests went down dramatically, as did traffic stops and speeding tickets." The predictable result was a spike — impaired driving fatalities surged to 13,617 in 2021 (from ~10K/yr pre-pandemic). But then something unexpected happened: deaths started dropping. By 2025, overall traffic fatalities fell 6.7% to 36,640. Q1 2026 hit a fatality rate of 0.99 per 100M VMT. Speeding deaths dropped 11%. Colorado's impaired crash share dropped from 31% to 22% year-over-year.

The paradox: enforcement never recovered. DUI arrests remain well below pre-pandemic levels. So what's actually driving the decline?

**Novel contribution:** Cross-referencing enforcement data with FARS toxicology by vehicle class to test a hypothesis — that vehicle safety technology (ESC, AEB, improved crash structures) is compensating for reduced enforcement. If true, the implication is that engineering is now saving more lives than policing.

## Primary Sources

1. **NORC (James C. Fell, Jan 2025):** "Yes, We Can Substantially Reduce Alcohol-Impaired Driving Fatal Crashes"
   - Since 2009, ~10,000 U.S. residents die in alcohol-impaired crashes per year
   - DUI arrests dropped "dramatically" during COVID
   - Traffic stops and speeding tickets also plummeted
   - 2020: 11,654 fatalities; 2021: 13,617 (all-time pandemic peak); 2022: 13,524
   - U.S. rate "significantly higher" than European countries, Australia, Japan
   - Maryland's SPIDRE unit reduced crash rates; sobriety checkpoints reduce fatalities 20%
   - Source: https://www.norc.org/research/library/yes-we-can-substantially-reduce-alcohol-impaired-driving-fatal-crashes.html

2. **NHTSA 2025 Annual Data (Apr 2026):**
   - 36,640 fatalities in 2025, down 6.7% from 2024 (39,254)
   - Fatality rate: 1.10 per 100M VMT
   - VMT increased 29.8 billion miles (0.9%)
   - 39 states saw declines
   - Source: AASHTO Journal / NHTSA press releases

3. **NHTSA Q1 2026 (Jul 2026):**
   - Fatality rate: 0.99 per 100M VMT (second-lowest quarterly rate in U.S. history)
   - 7,770 deaths, down 4.3% from Q1 2025
   - 30 states + Puerto Rico saw declines
   - Americans drove 11 billion more miles than Q1 2025
   - Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/trumps-transportation-department-reminds-drivers-that-speeding-catches-you

4. **NHTSA Speeding Data (2025):**
   - Speeding deaths dropped 11% to 10,035
   - 2024: 11,288 speeding deaths (29% of all fatalities)
   - 10,035 = return to pre-pandemic levels (2019)

5. **Colorado Task Force Data (Jan-Mar 2026):**
   - 2025: 698 total fatalities, 34% suspected impairment
   - Jan-Feb 2025: 31% of fatal crashes impaired
   - Jan-Feb 2026: 22% of fatal crashes impaired
   - Nighttime crashes: 49% suspected impairment; peak at 2 AM = 77%
   - Unrestrained fatalities: 49% involved impairment

6. **FARS Toxicology (site data, 2014-2023):**
   - Overall impairment rate: ~20% across all classes
   - Sports Cars: 22.4% any impairment (highest)
   - Vans: 18.0% (lowest)
   - Alcohol dominant over drugs in every class

7. **IIHS ESC effectiveness data:**
   - ESC reduces fatal single-vehicle crashes by 56%
   - ESC reduces fatal rollovers by 78%
   - Mandated since 2012 model year
   - By 2025, virtually all vehicles on road have ESC
   - Source: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/life-saving-benefits-of-esc-continue-to-accrue

8. **Ride-hailing context:**
   - Uber/Lyft now available in all major U.S. metro areas
   - 2024 IIHS/HLDI study found ride-hailing reduced alcohol-involved crash fatalities ~6% in urban areas

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? YES. The conventional wisdom is that enforcement drives safety. The data contradicts this — enforcement collapsed and safety improved anyway. This challenges a fundamental assumption about how we prevent drunk driving deaths. It's counterintuitive, data-driven, and has real policy implications.

## Strongest Counterargument
The decline might not be real improvement — it might be a delayed reversion to the mean after an artificial spike. COVID drove fatalities up via behavioral changes (emptier roads → faster driving, stress → more drinking, police → less enforcement). The current decline is just the pandemic hangover wearing off. We're returning to 2019 levels, not making new progress. If you benchmark against 2019 instead of 2021, the improvement is modest or nonexistent.

## Limitations
- FARS toxicology data doesn't capture all drug testing (varies by state)
- Enforcement data is fragmented — no national DUI arrest database with consistent year-over-year tracking
- Ride-hailing effect is studied but not definitively quantified
- VMT data is an estimate, not precise measurement
- COVID behavioral changes confound all trend analysis from 2020-2023
