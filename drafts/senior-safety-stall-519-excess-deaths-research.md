# Research: Senior Safety Stall — 519 Excess Deaths

## Primary Source
NHTSA, "Early Estimates of Motor Vehicle Traffic Fatalities and Fatality Rate by Sub-Categories in 2025," DOT HS 813 829, July 2026.
URL: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813829

## Key Data Points

### Age Group Fatalities 2024 → 2025
| Age Group | 2024 | 2025 | % Change |
|-----------|------|------|----------|
| <15       | 1,037| 918  | -12%     |
| 15-24     | 6,658| 6,113| -8%      |
| 25-34     | 6,956| 6,065| -13%     |
| 35-44     | 6,283| 5,715| -9%      |
| 45-54     | 4,988| 4,672| -6%      |
| 55-64     | 5,272| 5,117| -3%      |
| **65+**   | **8,060**| **8,039**| **0%** |
| **Total** | **39,254**| **36,640**| **-6.7%** |

65+ is the ONLY age group with zero improvement.
65+ is the LARGEST single age cohort for traffic deaths (8,039 > any other group).
65+ share rose from 20.5% to 21.9% of all traffic fatalities.

### Supporting NHTSA Subcategory Data
- Newer vehicles (<10 yrs) occupant deaths: -12%
- Older vehicles (≥10 yrs) occupant deaths: -9%
- Speeding-related: -11%
- Unrestrained occupant: -13%
- Ejected: -13%
- Pedalcyclists: +4% (only person-type increase besides 65+ stall)
- Rural interstates: +1% (only roadway type increase)

### Q1 2026 Extension
From NHTSA press release (July 2026): Q1 2026 fatality rate dropped to 0.99 per 100M VMT — best since 2014, near the record 0.98. 7,770 people died, down 4.3% from same period 2025.

## Original Analysis: Excess Death Calculation

If 65+ had improved at the national average rate (-6.7%):
- Expected: 8,060 × 0.933 = 7,520
- Actual: 8,039
- **Gap: 519 excess deaths**

If 65+ matched the best-performing group (25-34, -13%):
- Expected: 8,060 × 0.87 = 7,012
- Actual: 8,039
- **Gap: 1,027 excess deaths**

## Counterarguments (Strongest Case)
1. **Population growth:** 65+ is America's fastest-growing demographic (Baby Boomers). More people = more deaths even at constant rates. But this doesn't explain 0% when every other group improved.
2. **Fragility:** Older bodies don't survive crashes as well. A crash that a 30-year-old walks away from kills a 75-year-old. This is real and partially explains the high base, but not the LACK OF IMPROVEMENT.
3. **VMT patterns:** Elderly may drive fewer miles but on more dangerous roads (arterials, not highways). Rural interstates (+1%) could correlate.
4. **Vehicle age correlation is not causation:** We can't directly prove from FARS aggregate data that elderly drivers are dying in old vehicles. The cross-reference is suggestive, not conclusive.
