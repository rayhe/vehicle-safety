# Research Brief — #1546: One Corroded Cable Can Kill a Maverick's Steering, Brakes, and Lights at Once

**News peg:** Ford recall filed with NHTSA November 2, 2026 (campaign 26V724 / Ford 26S81). Owner letters mail the week of November 23, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since November 9, 2026.
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (investigations, recalls).
**Slug:** `1546-maverick-one-cable-kills-steering-brakes-lights`
**Working title:** "One Corroded Cable Can Kill Your Maverick's Steering, Brakes, and Lights at Once"
**Kicker:** Investigation

## Core facts (verified)

- **Population:** 8,650 vehicles (NHTSA Part 573). 2022 Ford Maverick Hybrid only, 2.5L HEV engine, built February 3, 2021 – May 12, 2022 at Hermosillo. Gas-powered Mavericks and later model years are not included. Ford's dealer bulletin lists a U.S. population of 8,642.
- **Defect:** The positive 12V battery cable terminal (eyelet C1617E) at the high-current Battery Junction Box (BJB) may become loose or detach from the cable, interrupting the 12V supply to the BJB.
- **Cause:** Hydroxy acetic acid, associated with a dip solder application on the terminal eyelet joint, may corrode the ultrasonic weld attaching the terminal to its cable. The acid left over from soldering eats the weld that holds the terminal on.
- **Consequence (NHTSA Part 573):** Loss of motive power while driving, or a no-crank/no-start condition. The failure may be accompanied by loss of 12V electrical functions, including power steering assist, power brake assist, headlamps and other exterior lighting, and the ability to shift into Neutral. One corroded joint, four safety systems gone at once.
- **Warnings:** Instrument cluster may display "Stop Safely Now," a Powertrain Malfunction / Reduced Power message, a "Shift System Fault" message, a red battery icon, and the Check Engine, wrench, and ABS lamps.
- **Supplier:** Yazaki Inc. (Tier 1 OEM). Cable assembly part number NZ6T-14300-CA.
- **Remedy:** Dealers install a new 12V battery positive cable overlay from the 12V battery located under the rear seat to the underhood Battery Junction Box, free of charge. Owner letters expected the week of November 23, 2026. Ford's Field Review Committee approved the recall October 27, 2026.
- **They knew:** Trucks previously repaired under Customer Satisfaction Program 22B39 are excluded from this recall population, meaning Ford already ran a program on this same eyelet before escalating it to a safety recall.
- **No stop-driving advisory:** NHTSA's record carries no do-not-drive or park-outside flag for this campaign, and the repair cannot arrive over the air. A dealer technician must do it.

## Kill test

- Genuinely newsworthy? YES — filed November 2, letters go out next week, and the failure mode is a total electrical blackout while driving: no steering assist, no brake assist, no headlights, cannot shift to Neutral.
- Novel angle? YES — (a) the poison is in the process: acid from the soldering step itself corroded the weld, so the manufacturing chemistry is the defect; (b) one $0 solder joint takes out four safety systems simultaneously, a single-point-of-failure story; (c) Ford's fix is an overlay, a whole second cable run alongside the rotten one, rather than a repair of the joint; (d) the escalation arc: a customer satisfaction program (22B39) came first, and only now, years later, a safety recall.
- Data-originality: the "1-in-N" framing is weak at 8,650 units, so the article leans on the failure-mode math instead: four safety systems, one weld, zero redundancy. Original calculation: 8,650 is roughly 1 in 12 of the ~100,000+ 2022 Maverick Hybrids sold (verify before claiming; use only if numbers support it, otherwise skip).

## Actionable insights (for article)

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, searchable since November 9, 2026. Do not wait for the letter.
2. If your 2022 Maverick Hybrid flashes "Stop Safely Now" or "Shift System Fault" with a red battery icon, pull over. That cluster of warnings is the tell.
3. The fix is free and takes a dealer visit. The overlay cable runs from the battery under the rear seat to the underhood junction box.
4. If your truck already got the 22B39 customer satisfaction fix, you are excluded from this recall.

## Limitations

- NHTSA's public record lists no crash, injury, or fire count for this campaign. Do not invent one.
- The exact share of the 8,650 trucks that will actually fail is unknown; Ford filed on the defect mechanism, not on a failure rate.
- The 8,650 (Part 573) vs 8,642 (dealer bulletin) population discrepancy is 8 trucks; use the NHTSA figure and note the dealer count differs.
- Whether 22B39 covered the identical eyelet joint is inferred from the Part 573 exclusion language, not stated outright. Frame it as "Ford already ran a program on this eyelet" with the exclusion as evidence, not as a confirmed identical repair.

## Strongest counterargument

8,650 trucks is a small recall, and no crashes or injuries are on the record. A skeptic could call this a paperwork recall for a part that mostly holds. The counter is the failure mode, not the count: when this joint lets go at highway speed, the driver loses steering assist, brake assist, and lights in the same instant, with no ability to shift to Neutral. Small population, catastrophic mode.
