# Research: Motorcyclist Death Share Doubled in 50 Years

## Angle
Motorcyclists' share of US traffic deaths doubled from 7% (1975) to 16% (2024) — from 1-in-14 to 1-in-6. In the same half-century, passenger vehicles got airbags, ESC, AEB, crumple zones, NCAP testing, and dozens of FMVSS standards. Motorcycles got zero federal safety mandates. IIHS filed two petitions for mandatory ABS. NHTSA never acted. Europe, Japan, India, Brazil, Australia — all require motorcycle ABS. The US doesn't.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? Yes:
1. The 2024 FARS Annual Report File was released June 2026 — this is the most current data
2. The 50-year share doubling has never been framed this precisely
3. NHTSA is currently claiming credit for declining traffic deaths while sitting on a 10+ year old petition to mandate motorcycle ABS
4. The international comparison (EU/Japan/India/Brazil all mandate ABS, US doesn't) is a strong policy point

### IIHS Motorcycle ABS Research
Source: https://www.iihs.org/research-areas/motorcycles
- 2021 study (Teoh): ABS reduces fatal crash rates by 22% across 65 models
- Cruiser/standard: 32% reduction
- Sport: 19% reduction  
- Supersport: 12% reduction
- ABS adoption as standard: 20% (2013) → 59% (2023)
- 30-40% of new US motorcycles still lack ABS

### IIHS Petition History
Source: https://iihs.org/news/detail/its-past-time-to-require-abs-on-all-motorcycles-iihs-hldi-petition-argues
- First petition: 2013
- Second petition: November 2023
- Countries that mandate motorcycle ABS: EU (2016), UK, Brazil, Japan, Taiwan, Australia, New Zealand, India
- NHTSA: Has taken no action on either petition

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing IIHS yearly snapshot data 1975-2024 to calculate the 50-year share shift:
- Motorcyclist share went from 7% to 16% (2.3x)
- This happened while total deaths decreased 12% (44,525 → 39,254)
- Motorcycle deaths nearly doubled in absolute terms (+95%)
- Every other category of road user benefited from federal safety mandates
- Motorcycles received zero federal safety mandates in 50 years

Calculation: If motorcycle ABS had been mandated in 2016 (when EU did it), and the 22% fatal crash reduction applied to the 2017-2024 fleet, approximately 5,500+ deaths could have been prevented.

## Counterargument (strongest case against)
Motorcycles are inherently different from cars — they can't be made crashworthy in the same way. No crumple zone, no cage, no airbag (save some Honda Gold Wing experiments). The biggest safety factor for motorcycles is rider behavior: helmets, training, not riding impaired. ABS helps, but the 22% reduction is modest compared to ESC's 33%+ reduction for cars. And riders choose motorcycles knowing the risk — mandating safety features could increase costs and reduce ridership, potentially shifting demand to used bikes without ABS.

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only. Motorcycle injury rates (survivable crashes) may tell a different story
- Share increase partly reflects declining car deaths (denominator effect), not solely increasing motorcycle deaths
- State-level variation in helmet laws creates confounding factors
- VMT data for motorcycles is less precise than for cars

## Actionable Takeaways
1. If buying a motorcycle: choose one with ABS as standard equipment
2. Wear a DOT-certified helmet regardless of state law
3. Check your state's helmet law status — only 18 states + DC require helmets for all riders
4. Support the IIHS petition for mandatory motorcycle ABS at NHTSA
