## 1. General Editor — 8.0/10
The census is the article's spine and the spine is straight, but the piece is too long for the format: 1,479 prose words against #1507's 911, roughly 60 percent over the recent norm, and the tables add another few hundred on top. The lede's first sentence runs 70-plus words before it reaches the point, so the hook ("its biggest recall isn't one of them") arrives in the second sentence instead of the first. The wipers misattribution is a factual error in the body, not a style nit. Deductions: length, lede sentence, one factual error. Fixable in one revision pass.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.5/10
Distinctly Mia: the engineering-first opener echoes her documented catchphrase without quoting it, the judgment lands on design and process ("Year two starts looking like the smart buy"), and the rhythm numbers are the best in the recent run (variance 1541.8, long sentences at 54.4%). Deduction: the counterargument paragraph stacks four "The..." sentence openers in a row, which is the one place the voice flattens into a list, and the lede's opening sentence is a 70-word build before any punch lands. Half a point held back, not a fail on voice alone.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victims are named or sensationalized; the one injury in 26V363 is reported as a count, not a scene. Consumer guidance is honest about what owners should do and does not overstate individual risk (the 0.1% defect estimate is stated next to the 1.08-million total, twice). No self-congratulation, no moralizing about Jeep owners. Deduction: the headline's "biggest recall isn't one of them" framing could read as implying Jeep hid the fire recall, and the article never quite says the exclusion is a method artifact, not a cover-up; the limitations paragraph covers it, but one sentence in the body would close the gap.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10
Headline is the strongest in the census series: a count, a contradiction, and a fire. Pull stat (1,467,098) is a number people screenshot. The 7-vehicle vs 1.08-million-vehicle contrast is a ready-made pull quote, and the tables reward the click. Deduction: at 1,479 words the piece is long for a share, and the middle sags between the two tables; a reader who shares after the lede never reaches the coil-spring trilogy, which is the best anecdote in the piece.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.0/10
Every campaign number, date, and unit count traces to a Part 573 filing, the NHTSA API, or named trade coverage, and all fourteen references link to real pages. Two defects: the wipers misattribution (26V529 is Grand Cherokee, not Grand Wagoneer) is a factual error in the body text, and the "three federal standards" claim for 26V262 is unverified (sources say "multiple"). Both must be fixed before ship. No other citation issues found.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5/10
Original contribution is real and substantial: an independently reproduced 12-count, a 7-campaign excluded set with unit totals, the 1,467,098 excluded-Jeep total, the 558,521 verified counted total, and the 2.63-to-1 ratio, all computed from primary sources. Limitations are honest about both floors, and the counterargument is stated at full strength. Deduction: the headline ratio compares a fully verified excluded total against a partially verified counted total, and while the limitations disclose this, the body presents "2.6 more" as a clean fact; one sentence of hedging in the body would make the rigor match the disclosure.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
Tables are clean, captioned, and sourced; the counted-vs-excluded framing is the clearest in the series; the pull stat carries the piece. Deduction: eight of twelve rows in the counted table read "split unpublished," which is honest but leaves the table looking unfinished, and the per-model table from the research file (Grand Wagoneer 5, Cherokee 4, Recon 0) is summarized in prose instead of shown, where a reader could scan it.

## Verdict: REVISE (round 0 avg 8.50; General 8.0, Legal 8.0 below 8.5)
Required fixes: (1) correct the wipers attribution to Grand Cherokee; (2) soften "three federal standards" to "multiple"; (3) trim ~250 words, chiefly the "rounding out" paragraph, the "put the two sides together" paragraph, and one sentence each from the counterargument and limitations; (4) break the "The..." run in the counterargument paragraph; (5) add one sentence in the body stating the exclusion is a method artifact, not a cover-up. Then re-score round 1.
