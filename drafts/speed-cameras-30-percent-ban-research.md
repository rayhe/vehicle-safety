# Research Notes: Speed Cameras Cut Crashes 30% — States Keep Banning Them

## Angle
The most effective traffic safety intervention in the United States — automated speed enforcement — is being systematically banned by state legislatures and targeted by federal legislation, even as peer-reviewed research quantifies its life-saving effect with unprecedented rigor. A December 2025 PNAS study using NYC's 2,000-camera rollout found speed cameras causally reduce collisions by 30% and injuries by 16% within seven months. Meanwhile, at least 9 states outright ban speed cameras, and a sitting congressman introduced a bill to strip 10% of federal highway funding from states that permit them.

## Primary Sources

### 1. PNAS Study — Stagoff-Belfort, Ben-Menachem, Beck (Dec 2025)
- Title: "Can speed cameras make streets safer? Quasi-experimental evidence from New York City"
- Published: PNAS, Vol 122, No 50
- DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2520328122
- Method: Difference-in-differences design, staggered rollout of 2,000 cameras (2014-2023)
- Data: 700,000 collisions, 200,000 injuries, 18 million speeding tickets
- **Findings:**
  - Cameras reduce collisions 5% per month, injuries 2.5% per month
  - Cumulative: 30% crash reduction, 16% injury reduction over 7 months
  - Effects are persistent and sustained (no fade-out)
- This is the largest causal evaluation of speed cameras in the US

### 2. IIHS Montgomery County Study
- Speed cameras: 59% reduction in 10+ mph speeding
- 19% reduction in fatal/incapacitating injury crashes
- Speed camera corridors (rotated locations) showed even larger gains
- Source: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/speed-cameras-reduce-injury-crashes-in-maryland-county-iihs-study-shows

### 3. NHTSA National Data (2024-2025)
- 2024 final: 39,254 traffic deaths (rate 1.19 per 100M VMT)
- 2025: 36,640 deaths (6.7% decrease, rate 1.10)
- Speeding involved in ~29% of all fatal crashes (NHTSA consistent estimate)
- 2024 speeding-related fatalities: ~11,384
- 2025 speeding-related fatalities (estimate): ~10,626
- First half 2024: speeding-related crashes declined 6%

### 4. City-Level Data
- San Francisco: 72% speeding reduction at 15 sites within 6 months
- Philadelphia: 95% reduction on a major corridor since 2020
- NYC red-light program (30 years): 73% violation decrease, 65% T-bone crash decline
- Portland, OR: 71% speeding decrease, 94% extreme speeding decrease, 43% crash reduction

### 5. Legislative Status
- 16 states + DC have active speed camera programs (per NCSL)
- ~311 communities with programs (GHSA/IIHS data as of April 2025)
- States that BAN speed cameras: Maine, Mississippi, New Hampshire, South Carolina, Texas, West Virginia (all ban both red-light and speed). Montana, South Dakota ban red-light cameras. New Jersey, Wisconsin ban speed cameras.
- Active bills to BAN:
  - Virginia SB 297 (Jan 2026) — repeal speed camera authority
  - Arizona SCR 1004 — ballot measure to ban photo radar statewide
  - Georgia HB 225 — repeal school zone cameras, effective 2028
- Federal: Congressman Pat Harrigan (NC-10) introduced "Freedom from Automated Speed Enforcement Act" — cuts 10% federal highway funding from states that allow cameras (narrow exceptions for school zones and active construction)

## Novel Analysis

### Life-saving calculation
- NHTSA: ~11,384 speeding-related fatalities in 2024
- PNAS causal estimate: 30% crash reduction from camera deployment
- If cameras were deployed nationwide at NYC scale: 30% × 11,384 = ~3,415 lives saved per year
- Reality check: Not all speed deaths occur where cameras would be placed, and the 30% figure is from school zones specifically. Conservative estimate: even 10% reduction = 1,138 lives/year
- Counterpoint: NYC program only covers school zones (active hours). Full deployment on high-risk corridors could approach the 30% figure on those roads.
- IIHS corridor study (Maryland): 19% reduction in fatal/incapacitating injury crashes — a middle-ground estimate

### The ban body count
- States that ban cameras have no automated enforcement deterrent for speeding
- Texas banned red-light cameras in 2019 and is pursuing speed camera bans
- Texas had the 2nd most traffic deaths in the US in 2024

## Strongest Counterargument
- Revenue generation: Some programs ARE operated primarily as revenue sources (e.g., small-town speed traps). The Harrigan bill specifically cites this.
- Due process: Camera tickets typically go to the registered owner, not the driver. Missouri's Supreme Court ruled this unconstitutional in 2015.
- Privacy concerns: Surveillance cameras on public roads, even if triggered only by violations
- Disproportionate impact: Low-income drivers may be hit harder by fixed fines
- These are genuine concerns — some cities HAVE abused camera programs. But the solution is regulation, not prohibition.

## Limitations
- The PNAS study is NYC-only, school zones specifically. Generalizability to other city types, highway corridors, and rural roads is uncertain.
- The 30% crash reduction is cumulative over 7 months — sustained effects beyond that are suggested but not proven to the same rigor.
- Speed camera effectiveness depends heavily on program design (signage, fine structure, camera density). Poorly designed programs may not achieve the same results.
- FARS data attributes speeding as a contributing factor but the exact causal mechanism (speed alone vs. speed + impairment) varies.
