## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** On Saturday, November 7, 2026, volunteers fanned out across the Las Vegas Valley and drew 172 chalk outlines on nine streets, one for every pedestrian killed on those streets between January 1, 2021 and June 30, 2026. The nine streets are Tropicana, Flamingo, Sahara, Charleston, Decatur, Lake Mead, Las Vegas Boulevard North, Las Vegas Boulevard South, and Boulder Highway. Every one of them posts the same speed limit: 45 mph. Flamingo alone accounts for 29 deaths, Boulder Highway 23, Charleston 22, Lake Mead 15. The outlines went down six days after daylight saving time ended, in the middle of the annual Dusk2Dawn campaign, and October 2026 was the deadliest month of the year for valley pedestrians, with 11 killed, roughly one every three days, 8 of the first 10 after sunset.

**Challenge:** is this surprising after 1,462 articles? Yes. (1) Nobody on the site has covered Las Vegas pedestrian deaths or the Dusk2Dawn campaign. (2) The pattern nobody stated out loud: nine streets, one shared speed limit, 172 bodies. The chalk is a data visualization, and the variable it isolates is the street design, not the walker. (3) Timing: the outlines were drawn November 7, three days ago, the day after the NHTSA half-year release showed the national fatality rate at its lowest since 2014 while pedestrian deaths stay elevated. The national story is "roads got safer"; the Las Vegas story is "not on these nine streets, not after dark."

**Newsworthy?** Yes. 172 deaths on nine named streets in one metro area, documented by UNLV's Transportation Research Center, with a fresh public action (Nov 7 chalk outlines) and a fresh monthly record (October deadliest month of 2026). DST ended November 1, so the evening commute now happens in the dark for the first time this season.

## Computations

- **Deaths per street:** 172 / 9 = 19.1 average per street over 5.5 years; one pedestrian death every 11.7 days across the nine streets combined (172 deaths / 2,008 days, Jan 1 2021–Jun 30 2026).
- **Concentration:** top four streets (Flamingo 29 + Boulder 23 + Charleston 22 + Lake Mead 15) = 89 of 172 = 51.7% of all deaths on four of nine streets. Flamingo alone = 16.9%.
- **October share:** 11 October deaths / 64 valley pedestrian deaths in 2026 through Oct 27 = 17.2% of the year's pedestrian deaths in a single month. October ran at roughly one death every 2.8 days, vs the nine-street 5.5-year average of one every 11.7 days.
- **After dark:** 8 of the first 10 October deaths happened after sunset = 80%.
- **Year-over-year:** 64 valley pedestrian deaths through late October 2026, about 8% fewer than the same point in 2025, so the year is improving overall while October spiked. Metro jurisdiction: 47 pedestrian deaths in 2026 vs 51 at the same point in 2025.
- **National contrast:** 7,148 US pedestrian deaths in 2024 (NHTSA), down 4.3% year over year but still 20% above 2016. The national H1 2026 fatality rate (1.02) is the lowest since 2014, yet the pedestrian toll on these nine streets did not follow the national trend.

## Strongest counterargument

The system is responding, and the numbers are not all moving the wrong way. Valley pedestrian deaths in 2026 are down about 8% year over year; the first half of 2026 saw pedestrian deaths fall nearly 40% before the summer climb. Boulder Highway got 11 new crosswalks with rapid flashing lights. Police run Joining Forces enforcement stings, including at dusk. Metro attributes pedestrian error as the leading contributing factor in its jurisdiction, which cuts against a pure road-design story. And the nine streets are the valley's busiest arterials: without per-street traffic volumes, 172 deaths cannot be converted into a per-mile or per-crossing rate, so Flamingo may partly be "deadliest" because it is busiest. The chalk outlines memorialize real people, but they are also an advocacy tactic from a campaign with a point of view, and the 172 count comes from PedSafe Vegas, not from a FARS query I ran myself.

## Limitations

- Counts come from PedSafe Vegas / UNLV Transportation Research Center as reported by local news, not from an independent FARS pull; the Jan 2021–Jun 2026 window and the nine-street list are Breen's figures.
- No per-street traffic volume or pedestrian exposure data was available, so no per-mile fatality rate can be computed; the 45 mph commonality is a design observation, not a proven causal claim.
- October death count differs by report date: 10 as of Oct 27, 11 for the full month per the Nov 10 report. Using 11 for October.
- The 5 p.m. DST multiplier (6x nationally) comes from a New Jersey personal-injury attorney's analysis of 10 years of FARS data, not a peer-reviewed study; the AAA Foundation January 2025 report is cited as consistent, not quoted directly.
- "Five years" in campaign language covers a 5.5-year window (Jan 2021–Jun 2026); computed rates use the full window.
- No breakdown of driver vs pedestrian fault per street; Metro's "pedestrian error leading factor" is jurisdiction-wide, not street-specific.

## Actionable for readers

- **Pedestrians in the Las Vegas Valley:** treat Flamingo Road between Paradise Road and University Center Drive as the most dangerous crossing in the county. Cross at marked, lit crossings, and assume drivers cannot see you after 5 p.m. now that DST has ended. The 17-year-old hit in a marked crosswalk on Lake Mead Boulevard in February shows even marked crossings on these arterials are not safe by themselves.
- **Drivers:** the 5 p.m. hour in the two weeks after the clocks go back is the deadliest hour of the year for pedestrians nationally. Slow down on the nine arterials after sunset, and expect pedestrians where the crosswalks are, because that is where they are dying.
- **Everyone else:** the design variable is visible in the data. All nine streets post 45 mph. Boulder Highway's 11 new flashing crosswalks are the model; ask your county commissioner whether your arterial has them.
