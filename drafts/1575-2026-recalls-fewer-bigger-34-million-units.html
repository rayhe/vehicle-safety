2026 Filed Fewer Recalls Than 2025. It Recalled 8.6 Million More Cars.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 24, 2026

NHTSA's public recall records hold 709 vehicle campaigns filed between January 2 and November 6, 2026, covering 34,795,225 vehicles, already 19 percent above the whole of 2025, with about six weeks of filings still to come, and the campaign count fell. I ran the numbers twice, and they did not get smaller.[1][2]

34.8M
Vehicles named in 2026 recalls through November 6, 19% above all of 2025, from 40 fewer campaigns

Same stretch, both years, and the gap is the story. Through November 6, 2025, NHTSA had received 749 campaigns covering 26,140,791 units, while this year brought 709 campaigns and 34,795,225 units. That is 8,654,434 more vehicles, a 33 percent jump, from 40 fewer filings, and the average campaign grew from 34,901 units to 49,076, up 41 percent in a single year. A second tally, The Weekly Driver's independent count through November 20, lands in the same place: 731 campaigns, 36.0 million vehicles, against 27.2 million a year earlier.[1][2]

Five campaigns did most of the damage. Each of the five million-plus recalls of 2026 clears a million units on its own, and together they total 9,171,719 vehicles, 26 percent of the entire year. One of them, Ford's 26V104 trailer-module software fix, covers 4,381,878 Ford and Lincoln vehicles by itself, 12.6 percent of every car recalled this year, a single over-the-air update with the statistical weight of a mid-size automaker. At the other end of the scale, 400 of the 707 campaigns that list a unit count cover fewer than 1,000 vehicles each. Sixty campaigns of 100,000 units or more carry 84 percent of the year, and seventeen carry more than half. More than half the year's recalls touch almost nobody, and a handful touch almost everybody.[1]

Three makers carry 69 percent of the units. Ford filed 83 campaigns covering 14,219,316 vehicles, 41 percent of the year's total from 11.7 percent of its campaigns. Chrysler, the filer behind Jeep and Ram, filed 35 campaigns for 5,958,659 units, 17 percent, and Honda filed 15 for 3,717,037, 11 percent. Campaign counts and unit counts tell different stories: General Motors filed more campaigns than Honda, 27 against 15, but averages 32,028 units per campaign against Honda's 247,802 and Ford's 171,317. Ford filed 44 fewer campaigns than the same stretch of 2025 and still added about 2 million units, while Chrysler's units more than doubled. Honda's rose four and a half times, and Toyota went the other way, down 24 percent.[1][2]

Regular readers will notice a number that does not match this site. Our census series counted Ford at 41 campaigns, not 83. Both numbers are right, because they measure different things: the census counts campaigns touching 2026-model-year vehicles only, while the year total counts every model year, and most 2026 recall volume is old cars. Campaign 26V104 covers model years 2021 through 2026. 26V237, the second-largest recall of the year at 1,392,935 units, covers 2015 to 2017 F-150s. 26V495, the Ram seat belt recall at 1,271,294 units, covers 2019 through 2026. October sharpens the point from the other direction: it had the most campaigns of any month, 90, for just 1,658,631 units, the year's busiest filing month by count and one of its lightest by cars.[1][3][5]

Now the case against my own headline, stated at full strength. One software update is an eighth of the year. Strip 26V104 out and 2026 drops from 34.8 million to 30.4 million units, still above all of 2025, but the margin shrinks from 19 percent to 4 percent, and the "fewer campaigns, more cars" frame leans on a trailer light most owners will never notice. Counts also measure reporting, not danger: Ford files aggressively under a November 2024 consent order, and a maker that quietly fixes the same defect in a service campaign never appears in NHTSA's count at all. Meanwhile the traffic fatality rate fell to 1.02 per 100 million miles in the first half of 2026, the second-lowest on record, so recalls are up and deaths are down, which is the system finding and fixing more, faster, at fleet scale through software, which is the system working, not failing. What keeps the headline standing is the breadth: even without the big five, 2026 leads 2025 by 21 percent, park-outside fire warnings are up 40 percent to 21, and Chrysler's doubling rides on a seat belt anchor defect, not a software push.[1][2]

What to do with this: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, by plate or by the 17-character VIN, because 34.8 million units means roughly one in eight vehicles on American roads was named in a 2026 recall, and the biggest ones cover cars up to eleven years old. If you own a 2021-2026 F-150, Super Duty, Maverick, Ranger, Expedition, Navigator or 2026 E-Transit, close 26V104 first: the trailer-module fix is free, by dealer or over the air, and the failure mode is losing your trailer's brakes and lights while towing. If you own a 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator, 26V363 is a park-outside fire recall with no ambiguity in the instruction, so park outside and away from structures until the wiring is repaired. If you own a 2023-2026 CR-V Hybrid or Accord Hybrid, do not use the tire repair kit sealant bottle from 26V366 until the remedy is done. And know that a recall can be open before a fix exists: Ford's 741,195-vehicle rollaway recall, 26V402, lists April 2027 for its repair, so read the letter, follow the interim instruction, and wait for the second one.[1][6]
