Mini’s Entire 2026 Recall Is Four Cars. One of Them Was Built on a Single Day.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 15, 2026

Mini filed exactly one recall in 2026. It covers four cars. One is a 2025 Cooper S built on March 26, 2024, a Tuesday, and that single day is its entire production window.[2] Three more are 2025 Countryman S ALL4s. All four share one problem: a brake fix they received in 2024 may never have been performed correctly, so BMW is recalling them again to check the work.

4
Minis in the brand’s entire 2026 recall footprint: one Cooper S and three Countryman S ALL4s, inside a 428-vehicle BMW-group campaign

Numbers this small usually mean somebody miscounted. Nobody miscounted. NHTSA campaign 26V422, filed July 1, 2026, covers 428 vehicles across BMW, Mini, and Rolls-Royce, and Mini’s share is four.[1] Four out of 428 is 0.93 percent. BMW’s X5 accounts for 92. A single Rolls-Royce Spectre accounts for one, which means a $400,000 electric coupe and a Mini Cooper S are now statistical equals, each representing exactly one car in the same filing.

Here is the part that should bother you more than the count. This is the third recall of the same integrated brake module. BMW first recalled it in February 2024 under campaign 24V104, a 266,716-vehicle action covering BMWs, Minis, and a Rolls-Royce, because the module could malfunction and cut power brake assist or disable the anti-lock brakes and stability control.[4] Seven months later came 24V697, 309 cars, after broken servomotor welds were found in the same module.[4] Now, 870 days after the first recall, comes the third: not a new defect, but an audit. BMW spent late 2025 reading its own warranty claims paperwork, formed a team in January and February 2026, spent March through June evaluating every car that had received the brake fix, and concluded that a small number of them may not have received a remedy performed in accordance with specified procedures.[2] Translation: somebody fixed the brakes, and BMW is no longer sure the somebody did it right.

Run BMW’s own numbers through the arithmetic and the recall gets stranger. BMW estimates 1 percent of the 428 cars actually has the defect, which works out to about four cars across the whole campaign.[2] Mini’s expected share of that is 0.04 cars. Read that again: the most likely number of actually defective Minis in this recall is zero. Four cars are being recalled because BMW cannot prove their dealers did the first job correctly, not because any of the four is known to be broken. That is a paperwork audit with a brake module attached.

Scale the thing and it shrinks further. From 266,716 cars in 24V104 to 428 in 26V422 is a 99.84 percent reduction, and the 428 are the residue of the first recall, the cars whose service and warranty records did not check out. Mini sold 28,749 cars in the United States in 2025, up 9.3 percent from the year before.[7] Four recalled cars is 0.014 percent of one year’s sales. Polestar’s 213-car recall, the smallest vehicle population this series had covered, is 53 times bigger than Mini’s. Porsche filed zero campaigns in 2026. Mini filed one campaign covering four cars, which in vehicle terms is barely more than zero, and the difference between zero and four is a single Cooper S built on a Tuesday.

Mini’s recent history is not a story of one quiet year. It is a story of a small brand borrowing its parent’s problems. In 2024 the brand filed five campaigns, including two for the Cooper SE’s high-voltage battery and the two brake actions above. In 2025 it filed two, and both were seat belt retractors shared with BMW’s X1 and X2: front retractors in September, 1,571 vehicles across the group, and rear retractors in October, 1,024 across the group, both traced to parts damaged during production.[6] Every Mini recall in the last two years except the battery ones is a BMW recall that happens to contain some Minis. Mini does not have its own defects anymore. It has BMW’s defects, in miniature.

Credit where it is due. Zero accidents and zero injuries are attached to this issue, BMW found the problem by auditing its own paperwork rather than waiting for a crash, and the fix is a free dealer inspection with a replacement if needed.[2] A manufacturer that re-checks its own recall work is doing what the system asks. But the defect is a brake system that can lose power assist and take the anti-lock brakes and stability control with it, the supplier is the same UMOVIO Germany module in all three campaigns, and the third recall exists because the first fix may not have been done right on 428 cars. A remedy that has to be re-verified two years later is not a rounding error in paperwork. It is a manufacturer that does not know, car by car, whether its dealers did the job.

What to do: own a 2025 Mini Cooper S or Countryman S ALL4? Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and look for campaign 26V422. Only four Minis are in it, one Cooper S built March 26, 2024 and three Countryman S ALL4s built March 21 to May 27, 2024, but the whole point of this recall is that a car which already had the 2024 brake work done can still be in it. Own a 2025 or 2026 Cooper, Cooper Convertible, Countryman S ALL4, or Countryman SE ALL4? Check for 25V616, the front seat belt retractors from September 2025, and for a 2026 Countryman S ALL4, 25V717, the rear retractors from October 2025. Both are free dealer replacements, and both are federal safety standard failures, since the retractors may not lock in a crash. Shopping a used 2025 Mini? Verify all three brake campaigns, 24V104, 24V697, and 26V422, are closed on the actual VIN. A car that shows the 2024 fix as complete may still have the 2026 recall open. That is precisely why the 2026 recall exists.

What this does not prove: the 1 percent defect estimate covers the whole 428-vehicle campaign, not Mini specifically, so do not quote a Mini defect rate. The four Minis in 26V422 were all previously counted in the 2024 brake recalls, so campaign-vehicle counts double-count the same cars across years. And the census counts filings through November 15, 2026, so a late filing changes the totals. Mini also had a China-only mirror recall in November 2026, 714 electric Minis built by its joint venture with Great Wall, which is a separate regulatory regime and is not part of this count.[8]
