Genuinely newsworthy and novel: a recall whose entire US population is 7 cars, 100% defect rate per Koenigsegg's own estimate, triggered by a single rollaway, fixed over the air, and exposing a gap in NHTSA's own static parking-brake tests. No prior Crash Report article covers Koenigsegg or CC850 (grep clean, Oct 27 2026). Not a data dump: the story is the scale inversion (smallest recall, total fleet) plus a test-regime blind spot.

## Verified facts (3+ primary sources)

1. **Population: 7 cars, 100% estimated defect rate.**
   - NHTSA recall 26V694000, "Unintended Vehicle Movement from Disengaged Electronic Parking Brake," Koenigsegg CC850, model years 2025-2026 (ConsumerAffairs NHTSA weekly roundup, Oct 26, 2026).
   - All 2025-2026 CC850s built Sept 28, 2025 - Aug 2, 2026; every one of the seven US cars is on the list and Koenigsegg figures all of them have the bug (Autoblog, Oct 27, 2026).
   - For the CC850, seven cars IS the entire American fleet (Autoblog).

2. **The defect: engine running + Reverse + parking-brake button.**
   - When the engine is running and the transmission is in Reverse, pressing the electronic parking brake button will not keep the brake engaged unless the driver continuously holds the button down (Autoblog; The BRAKE Report, Oct 26, 2026).
   - An over-the-air software update fixes it (The BRAKE Report).

3. **Trigger: one rollaway, Oct 12, 2026, no injuries.**
   - Oct 12: Koenigsegg learned a CC850 had rolled away after the driver tried to set the parking brake with the engine running, Reverse engaged, on a slope, and got out (Autoblog).
   - Koenigsegg opened an investigation the same day; real-world testing and failure-rate assessment ran through Oct 19; then it decided on a voluntary safety recall (Autoblog).

4. **Remedy and timing.**
   - Free software update delivered over the air; went live the week of Oct 20-26; owner letters to mailboxes Oct 29, 2026 (Autoblog).
   - If an owner cannot complete the OTA update, an authorized Koenigsegg dealer performs the same repair; Koenigsegg will reimburse owners who paid to remedy the condition before the recall (Autoblog).

5. **The test gap (the angle beyond the recall itself).**
   - NHTSA notes this glitch is not covered by the usual static parking brake tests (Autoblog). The failure needs a running engine and Reverse selected: a dynamic state the static test never creates.

6. **Price/context.**
   - Price tag over $3 million (Autoblog). Swedish hypercar, limited-run (Autoblog: "only a few CC850s in existence").

## What is NOT established (do not claim)
- No crashes, injuries, or deaths: Autoblog reports only that nobody got hurt in the rollaway incident. Do not inflate to "no crashes ever" beyond the single incident account.
- Worldwide CC850 total: NOT stated in the sources used; do not claim a global production number. Say only that seven is the entire US fleet.
- Cause beyond software logic: sources describe a software issue, not a failed part. Do not invent a supplier or hardware cause.
- Whether the OTA update has already reached all seven cars: sources say it was supposed to go live the week before Oct 27; do not claim completion.

## Actionable takeaway (required gate)
- CC850 owners: accept the OTA update before parking on any slope; until it is installed, do not rely on a tapped parking-brake button with the engine running in Reverse. Put the car in Park (or neutral with foot brake) and keep the button held if you must use it. Any owner: the general lesson is that electronic parking brakes are software, and software has modes your test regime never tried.
