# Research Notes: Bilstein Off-Road Shock Corrodes Off-Road

## Angle
Bilstein supplies premium remote-reservoir shock absorbers to both Ford (Bronco Badlands/Sasquatch) and Toyota (Tacoma TRD Off-Road). The identical defect — corrosion at the mounting flange causing external oil reservoir detachment — has triggered recalls at both automakers. These are premium off-road upgrades that corrode from the conditions they're marketed for.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Ford Bronco Recall — NHTSA 25V025000
- **Filed:** January 17, 2025
- **Vehicles:** 149,449 units (2021-2024 Bronco)
- **Production dates:** September 23, 2020 – July 23, 2024
- **Trims affected:** Badlands, Sasquatch package (HOSS 2.0 suspension)
- **Defect:** Rear shock absorber crevice corrosion at retention interface between long-flange damper tube and external reservoir. Jacking force from corrosion → material erosion + weld failure → reservoir separation
- **Supplier:** Bilstein ESCV monotube shocks with external piggyback bypass reservoirs
- **Warranty claims:** 551 (Mar 2023 – Nov 2024)
- **Injuries/crashes:** None reported
- **Fix:** Replace rear shock absorbers (parts availability anticipated Q3 2025)
- **Ford estimated ~30% of recalled vehicles affected**
- Source: NHTSA recall database, autoevolution, The Drive, CarBuzz, MotorTrend

### 2. Toyota Tacoma Recall — 26TB14 / 26TA14
- **Filed/Announced:** August 6-8, 2026 (NHTSA listing Aug 18, 2026)
- **Vehicles:** 48,280 units (2024-2025 Tacoma TRD Off-Road + 2024 Tacoma Hybrid)
- **Defect:** Metal flange on front AND rear shock absorbers corrodes → external oil reservoir detaches
- **Supplier:** Bilstein monotube remote-reservoir shocks
- **Fix:** Inspect front and rear shock absorber assemblies, replace with improved ones as needed
- **Notification:** Early October 2026
- **Injuries/crashes:** Not specified
- Sources: Toyota press release (Aug 6, 2026), NHTSA, USA Today, Autoblog, Carscoops, GearHead Daily

### 3. Toyota Tacoma PRIOR Recall — 25V058000
- **Filed:** February 6, 2025
- **Vehicles:** 106,061 units (2024-2025 Tacoma 4WD)
- **Defect:** Rear brake hoses damaged by mud and dirt buildup inside rear wheels → brake fluid leak → reduced braking ability
- **Fix:** Replace both rear brake hoses
- Source: NHTSA recall database

## Novel Analysis — The Off-Road Paradox

The Tacoma TRD Off-Road has now been recalled for:
1. **Shock absorbers** that corrode from environmental exposure (48K, Aug 2026)
2. **Brake hoses** that fail from mud/dirt buildup (106K, Feb 2025)

Both systems critical for off-road safety. Both fail from off-road conditions. The truck is marketed specifically for these conditions.

Combined affected vehicle population across both Ford and Toyota Bilstein recalls: **197,729 vehicles**.

## Cross-Supplier Pattern

Ford's investigation found that the "long-flange" rear shock absorber design was more susceptible to corrosion than "short-flange" designs. This suggests a design-level vulnerability specific to how Bilstein welds the reservoir mounting flange to the damper body.

Toyota's recall covers FRONT AND REAR shocks (broader than Ford's rear-only recall), suggesting either:
- Bilstein didn't fully fix the design between the two OEM programs
- The corrosion mechanism affects multiple flange configurations

## Counterargument (at full strength)
These may be genuinely independent defects. The Bronco uses Bilstein ESCV (Electronically Controlled Solenoid Valve) dampers specific to HOSS 2.0, while the Tacoma uses standard Bilstein remote-reservoir monotubes. Different part numbers, different engineering teams, potentially different manufacturing facilities. Crevice corrosion at weld joints is a well-understood metallurgical failure mode — two suppliers could independently produce the same defect without it being a systematic company-level problem. Ford already identified that short-flange designs resist the corrosion better, which implies the root cause is geometry-specific, not company-wide.

## Limitations
- FARS data for the Bronco (25 deaths) is too small for meaningful rate analysis
- We don't know Toyota's pre-recall warranty claim count
- Bilstein may supply shocks to other OEMs with the same vulnerability; we only know about Ford and Toyota
- The NHTSA recall for the Tacoma was not yet on the NHTSA website as of the initial filing, so some details are preliminary
