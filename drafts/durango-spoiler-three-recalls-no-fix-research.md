# Research: Dodge Durango Rear Spoiler — Three Recalls, No Fix

## Story Angle
Stellantis has recalled the Dodge Durango for the exact same rear spoiler detachment defect three times in five years. The third recall, issued August 2026, doesn't even have a remedy. The prior two "fixes" — inspect and replace — failed. The same assembly tolerance mismatch at Jefferson North keeps producing spoilers that sit too flush to the roof, get damaged by liftgate contact, and eventually detach at highway speed.

## Three Recall Campaigns

### Recall #1 (October 2021)
- NHTSA Campaign: 21V842000
- Chrysler Internal: Y73
- Vehicles: 4,837 (2021 Dodge Durango only)
- Issue: Over-flush rear spoiler contacts roof when cycling liftgate
- Remedy: Inspect and replace spoiler, free of charge
- Owner letters: December 21, 2021
- Notes: Chrysler introduced updated spoiler design (parts 7DY53TZZAB and 7DY56TZZAB) into production Oct 2020, but the updated parts ALSO had assembly issues

### Recall #2 (March 2023)
- NHTSA Campaign: 23V115000
- Chrysler Internal: 20A
- Vehicles: 139,019 (2021-2023 Dodge Durango, built Oct 24 2020 – Feb 16 2023)
- Issue: Identical — over-flush rear spoiler
- Field data: 8 customer assistance records, 56 warranty claims, 1 field report
- Remedy: Inspect and replace spoiler, free of charge
- Owner letters: April 14, 2023
- Source: Autoblog, Jalopnik, Carscoops, NHTSA

### Recall #3 (August 2026)
- NHTSA Campaign: 26V528000
- Chrysler Internal: 61D
- Vehicles: 74,578 (2021-2023 Dodge Durango, built Jul 13 2020 – Mar 28 2023)
- Issue: Identical — assembly tolerance mismatch, over-flush rear spoiler
- Assembly Plant: Jefferson North
- Field data: 116 warranty claims, 8 customer assistance records, 37 service records (May 10 2022 – Jun 30 2026)
- 161 total instances reported as of July 29, 2026
- Estimated affected: 0.8% of recalled vehicles
- **Remedy: UNDER DEVELOPMENT — no fix available at time of recall**
- Investigation opened: March 2026, after post-recall detachments continued
- Dealer notification: August 20, 2026
- Owner letters: September 9-10, 2026
- VIN lookup: Live August 20, 2026
- Source: Carscoops, Autoblog, CarBuzz, USA Today, AutoSpies, Consumer Reports

## Cumulative Numbers
- Total recall actions: 4,837 + 139,019 + 74,578 = 218,434
- Note: Many vehicles overlap between campaigns 2 and 3
- Zero crashes or injuries reported across all three campaigns

## Original Contribution
1. Cumulative recall count across three campaigns for identical defect
2. Stellantis issued recall #3 WITHOUT having a remedy — they admitted the problem persists but have no solution
3. The "inspect and replace" fix from recalls 1 and 2 was the same fix both times, and it clearly didn't work
4. Stellantis's own revised spoiler design (introduced Oct 2020) was also defective
5. This is now a five-year-old unresolved manufacturing defect on a vehicle platform that's been in production since 2011

## Strongest Counterargument
- No crashes or injuries reported in any of the three campaigns
- Only 0.8% of vehicles estimated to have the defect
- A detached spoiler is road debris, not a primary collision risk to the Durango's occupants
- Stellantis identified the problem quickly each time and issued recalls

## Limitations
- NHTSA recall data doesn't track whether vehicles recalled in campaign 1 or 2 were re-recalled in campaign 3
- "161 instances" is self-reported by Stellantis; actual detachments could be higher
- We don't know the completion rate for recalls 1 and 2
- Spoiler detachment as "road hazard" is the stated risk, but we have no data on secondary crashes caused by road debris from Durangos specifically
