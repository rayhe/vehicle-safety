# Research: #1482 — The Queue Was the Trap: Eight Dead on I-85

**Article #1482 | Journalist: Rex Driverton | Date: 2026-11-12 | Kicker: Investigation**

**Working title:** "Eight People Sat in a Traffic Queue on I-85. A Semi Hit Them at 65 MPH."

## Kill test

**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** On October 13, 2025, a 2005 Western Star semi plowed into the back of a stopped 2014 Dodge Grand Caravan at the end of a traffic queue on I-85 near Commerce, Georgia, at 64-66 mph. All eight people in the minivan died, including five children and a pregnant woman. The NTSB's factual report (HWY26FH001, published 2026) shows the minivan did everything right, the truck had no crash-avoidance technology at all, and the queue itself was the kill zone.

**Challenge:** After 1,481 articles, is this just another fatal crash retelling? No. Four reasons: (1) site grep 2026-11-12: zero hits for "end-of-queue", "Hammock", "Jackson County", or "Commerce" as a crash story — the end-of-queue crash pattern has never been covered in 867 published stories; (2) the numbers are new and primary-sourced from the NTSB factual report, not recycled FARS tables: 64-66 mph impact speed, 56.0 mph delta-V, five sequential impacts, six vehicles; (3) the regulatory hook is live and unresolved: the truck was a 2005 with zero crash-avoidance tech, and as of September 2026 there is still no federal rule requiring AEB on heavy trucks — the 2023 proposal was never finalized and the agencies are restarting it as a supplemental NPRM; (4) the inversion is the story: the victims were stopped, belted, and in a queue. The danger was not their driving. It was being last in line.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Verified facts (all sourced)

1. **The crash.** Monday, October 13, 2025, ~4:06 p.m. EDT. I-85 northbound, right lane, ~1.3 miles south of exit 147, Commerce, Jackson County, GA. A traffic queue had formed in the right lane. The 2005 Western Star 4900EX (Detroit Series 60 14L, Class 8) with a 2009 Warren 38-ft tank trailer loaded with bulk grain, operated by Clermont Trucking Inc., approached the queue and did not slow. [NTSB factual report, NTSB page]
2. **The impact.** Calculated impact speed 64-66 mph into the rear of the stopped 2014 Dodge Grand Caravan SE. The Caravan's EDR shows 2-3 mph in the five seconds before impact, brake off, a slight acceleration to 7 mph in the final second. Maximum longitudinal delta-V: 56.0 mph. Rear bumper and cargo area intruded into the passenger compartment. [NTSB factual report]
3. **The chain.** Five sequential in-line impacts plus a sideswipe by a second combination vehicle. Six vehicles total: Western Star, Grand Caravan, 2020 Ford Transit cargo van (2.4 mph at impact, delta-V 50.7 mph), 2024 Toyota Tacoma (3.7 mph), 2017 Dodge 4500 service truck, 2020 Freightliner Cascadia (Walgreens, unit 62062). The Caravan was shoved into the Transit; the Transit was sideswiped by the Freightliner. [NTSB factual report]
4. **The fire.** After the impacts, the Caravan and the Western Star left the roadway right and were "immediately engulfed in a postcrash fire." Fire damage consumed the entire Caravan. The tank trailer (bulk grain) was largely unaffected. [NTSB factual report]
5. **The dead.** Eight occupants of the Caravan: three adults and five juveniles, including one pregnant occupant. All sustained fatal injuries. The truck driver sustained minor injuries; other drivers minor to serious. [NTSB page, GA DPS]
6. **The belts held; the van didn't.** Front driver and passenger lap/shoulder belts were still latched in their buckles; EDR confirmed both front belts buckled. A Graco child safety seat was found partially melted, affixed to the second-row left seat. The interior seating configuration could not be definitively confirmed due to intrusion, thermal damage, and extrication. [NTSB factual report]
7. **The truck.** 2005 Western Star, manufactured 2005, "was not equipped with crash avoidance technologies." Any onboard video or data recording systems, if present, were destroyed in the fire. Seat belt use by the truck driver could not be determined due to thermal damage to the cab. [NTSB factual report]
8. **The driver.** 33-year-old male, valid Georgia Class A CDL issued August 2019, expiring December 2027. Cell phone records obtained from the provider show no texts or phone calls at the time of the crash. The driver told Georgia State Patrol he "may have looked at his phone" prior to the crash. [NTSB factual report]
9. **The charges.** Kane Aaron Hammock, 33, Gainesville, GA: 8 counts vehicular homicide (2nd degree), 1 count feticide by vehicle (2nd degree), following too closely, no registration, driver to exercise due care. Arrested and booked into Jackson County Jail. [GA DPS]
10. **The cats.** The Ford Transit van was transporting 37 rescue cats in multiple cages to a facility in Vermont. The van's driver was belted; curtain and side-impact airbags deployed. [NTSB factual report]
11. **The weather and road.** Clear, dry, 71-73 F. Six-lane divided interstate, 70 mph limit. Nothing wrong with the road. [NTSB factual report]

## Original contributions (show the work)

1. **The closing-speed ledger.** 64-66 mph into a 2-3 mph minivan is a ~62 mph closing speed — the energy equivalent of the Caravan hitting a concrete wall at highway speed, except the wall was moving. Delta-V 56.0 mph is the number that converts "rear-ended" into " unsurvivable": NHTSA's own crash-test regime tops out around 35-40 mph delta-V for occupant protection design. Nothing in a 2014 minivan is engineered for a 56 mph rear delta-V plus fire.
2. **The queue as the trap.** The Caravan was last in a slow-moving queue, doing 2-3 mph with its brake off, creeping forward. Every safety behavior the site ever recommends — buckle up, drive sober, keep distance — was satisfied by the victims, and none of it mattered, because the risk was positional: being the last vehicle in line when a 20-year-old truck with no automatic braking arrives at 65 mph. The site has never covered end-of-queue crashes; the pattern deserves a name in the taxonomy.
3. **The AEB gap, dated.** The truck was built in 2005, before crash-avoidance tech existed on Class 8 trucks. But the gap is not historical: FMVSS 127 (April 2024) mandates AEB only for vehicles up to 10,000 lbs GVWR, by September 2029. A Class 8 tractor (33,001+ lbs) is outside it entirely. The June 2023 joint NHTSA/FMCSA heavy-vehicle AEB proposal was never finalized; as of September 2026 the agencies are restarting it as a supplemental NPRM. A brand-new 2026 Western Star is no more required to brake itself than the 2005 one was. The Commerce crash is what that rulemaking gap looks like from inside a minivan.
4. **The phone that wasn't.** The driver's cell records show no texts or calls at the moment of impact, and he still told troopers he "may have looked at his phone." The factual report leaves the distraction question open — the data recorder burned. The honest read: a glance is enough at 65 mph, and the absence of a text record does not exonerate the glance. Do not overclaim; the NTSB has not issued probable cause yet.

## Limitations (for the article)

- The NTSB has not yet issued a probable cause finding for HWY26FH001; the factual report is evidence, not a verdict. The criminal charges are allegations; Hammock is presumed innocent until proven guilty.
- No EDR/ECM data survived from the Western Star (fire destroyed any onboard recording). Impact speed 64-66 mph is a reconstruction calculation, not a recorder readout. Whether the driver braked at all before impact is unknown.
- The "may have looked at his phone" statement is the driver's own account to GSP; cell records show no texts/calls at the crash time, but a glance at a phone leaves no carrier record.
- FARS rates use VMT estimates, not odometer readings (±15% uncertainty for low-volume models). The Caravan's 1.33 rate is a 2014-2023 fleet average; the 2014 SE in Commerce is one model year of a long production run.
- The 37 rescue cats: reported in the factual report; their fate is not stated. Do not invent it.

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)

The NTSB has not blamed the truck's age, the lack of AEB, or the queue. The probable cause may land squarely on the driver: following too closely, inattention, a phone glance at 65 mph. A 2005 truck with a perfect driver does not hit a stopped minivan, and a 2026 truck with AEB still relies on a driver who is supposed to be watching the road. The regulatory framing risks laundering a straightforward case of a distracted truck driver into a systems story, and the AEB mandate would not have retrofitted the 2005 Western Star anyway — the agencies explicitly declined to propose retrofits. The counterargument is correct on the retrofit point and partially correct on the driver point; the article must carry both. The systems claim that survives: a new truck sold today still isn't required to have the system that would have caught this driver's glance, and the queue pattern (stopped vehicles at highway speed, invisible until too late) is a recurring NTSB theme, not a one-off.

## Actionable takeaways

- **If you are last in a queue on an interstate:** you are the crumple zone for everything behind you. Keep your foot on the brake, hazards on, and watch the mirror — a stopped queue at highway speed is the most dangerous place on the road, and the vehicle behind you may be a 20-year-old truck with no automatic braking. If you can, leave an escape gap and an exit path (shoulder side), not just following distance.
- **If you drive a pre-2012 minivan or any older family vehicle:** the Commerce Caravan was a 2014 with belts buckled and kids in seats, and it still could not survive a 56 mph rear delta-V. Rear-impact protection is the weakest-tested direction in the fleet; check IIHS rear-crash ratings for your specific model year, not just the frontal stars.
- **If you buy or operate a heavy truck:** ask whether it has forward collision warning and AEB, because the federal government does not require it yet. The 2023 proposal is still a proposal. Fleet buyers are the only mandate that exists right now.
- **Check your VIN** at nhtsa.gov/recalls — unrelated to this crash, but the Caravan family carries open recalls in other model years, and the habit is the point.
