Your Navigator's Stereo Can Move Your Seat. Ford's Fix Is to Delete the Feature.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 7, 2026

Ford recalled 36,994 Lincoln Navigators this month because the rear audio control module can command the front passenger seat to slide forward, slide back, or recline without anyone touching a control.[1] Transport Canada adds the detail NHTSA left out: the seat may keep moving even if something is in the way.[3]

36,994
2025-2027 Lincoln Navigators recalled because the rear audio module can drive the front passenger seat (NHTSA 26V691, October 20, 2026)

Chauffeur mode is the feature at the center of it. Rear passengers adjust the front passenger seat from a second-row touchscreen, sliding it forward to stretch out, the kind of thing that sells a six-figure SUV to people who ride in back.[4] Except the module that runs the rear audio controls, the RACM, can fire that command on its own, with no passenger input at all. NHTSA's stated consequence is blunt: a seat that moves unexpectedly can crush into second-row passengers, or shove the front passenger into an unsafe seating position. Ford's remedy is a dealer software update to the RACM and the infotainment module that disables chauffeur mode entirely, no new parts, no repaired logic, just a feature deleted.

March already ran this play, when Ford recalled 1,189 model-year 2026 Navigators after the driver's seat module started reversing the driver's seat toward the rearmost position at speeds under 3 mph, moving the driver away from the pedals, because the software never learned to cancel the automatic reversal when the driver grabbed the manual switch.[2] That fix was also a deletion: update the module software to remove the automatic reversal feature. Two recalls in one calendar year, both front seats, both software, both "the seat moves by itself," both fixed by taking a feature away. Add them together and 38,183 Navigators have been recalled in 2026 for seats that move on their own, and the October recall alone is 31 times the size of the March one.

Credit where it is due, because the other side of this story is a manufacturer doing the unglamorous thing right. Ford found both defects, filed promptly, named the exact modules, and the remedy is free and software-only. No crash or injury has been reported for either recall, and the March record says so explicitly. Deleting chauffeur mode is the honest engineering call: a convenience that lets a rear passenger slide the front seat is a luxury, not a safety system, and removing it eliminates the hazard instead of patching around it. Also fair: the two defects involve different modules, different seats, and different failure modes, so treating them as one systemic cause would overreach. What they share is a manufacturer willing to delete a feature rather than defend it, which is arguably the right instinct, and a car where the stereo and the seats share a network, which is how a rear audio module ends up with the authority to move furniture in the first place.

What this piece did not prove, stated plainly. No crash, injury, or field-report count tied to the October recall appears in the NHTSA record or any source consulted, and that absence is not a claim of zero incidents, so do not read it that way. Ford has not published the precise software failure inside the RACM, and neither has any regulator; the record describes what the seat does, not why the module commands it, and this article will not invent a root cause. And 36,994 is the population of potentially affected vehicles, not the number with the defect, because NHTSA does not publish a defect rate for this campaign.

What you can actually do with this. If you own a 2025, 2026, or 2027 Navigator, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and look for campaign 26V691, Ford 26S77, because the new recall covers every model year of the redesigned Navigator and a different model year will not save you. If you own a 2026, check for the March campaign too, 26V120, since the two recalls touch different seats and one fix does not cover the other. Owner letters for the October recall are expected in November 2026, and the update is free. Until then, second-row passengers should know the seat in front of them is not guaranteed to stay where it is.
