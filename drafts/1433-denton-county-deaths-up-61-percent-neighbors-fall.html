Denton County’s Crashes Barely Moved. Its Deaths Jumped 61%.

Axle McScatter • Data Visualization Editor
November 8, 2026

Denton County, Texas logged 10,075 crashes in the first nine months of 2026, forty-six more than the same stretch of 2025.[1] Deaths went from 31 to 50, a 61 percent jump. Same roads, same drivers, essentially the same number of crashes. Crashes did not get more frequent in Denton County. They got more lethal.

61%
Increase in Denton County traffic deaths, Jan–Sep 2026 vs. Jan–Sep 2025, while reported crashes rose 0.5% (TxDOT CRIS)

Divide deaths by crashes and the story sharpens. In 2025, Denton recorded 3.09 deaths per 1,000 crashes. In 2026, that figure was 4.96, a 60 percent rise in lethality per crash. Fatal crashes went from 0.31 percent of all crashes to 0.49 percent. Forty-nine fatal crashes produced the fifty deaths, which means all but one killed exactly one person: no single pileup explains the jump, just forty-nine separate crashes that ended worse than the year before.

Winter did the damage, and the first quarter tells the whole story in miniature. January through March produced the fewest crashes of any quarter, 3,164, and the most deaths, 20, against six in the first quarter of 2025. Fourteen of the year’s nineteen extra deaths arrived before April. At 6.32 deaths per 1,000 crashes, first-quarter crashes ran about 49 percent more lethal than third-quarter crashes, which recorded 4.25 per 1,000 despite logging the year’s highest crash, injury, and serious-injury totals. Whatever made Denton deadlier in 2026, it showed up hardest when the roads were emptiest.

Cross the county line and the picture flips. Tarrant County, immediately to the south, recorded 142 deaths against 151 a year earlier, down 6 percent.[2] Collin County, to the east, went from 52 to 49, down 5.8 percent.[3] Denton alone added nineteen deaths while its two neighbors combined saved twelve, and the three-county total barely moved, 234 to 241. Trucks are not the explanation either: commercial-vehicle crashes in Denton rose 5.7 percent, yet commercial-vehicle deaths fell from four to three, which puts the entire surge in passenger-vehicle crashes, where deaths climbed from 27 to 47, up 74 percent.

Give the small-numbers objection its full weight, because nineteen deaths is a small absolute number and county-year comparisons are noisy by nature. Denton’s 2025 base of 31 may have been a lucky year, and the neighbors’ declines, nine and three deaths, could be noise running the other way. Growth is a real confounder too: crashes in Northlake rose 29 percent and in the Fort Worth portion of the county 54 percent, which tracks new development and more driving. But growth explains volume, and volume is not the story here. Lethality per crash rose 60 percent, and no amount of new rooftops makes each individual crash more likely to kill. Whether 2026 was a deteriorating county or a bad draw is a question the next two quarters will answer.

What you can do with this. If you drive in Denton County, the corridor to respect is I-35E, which logged 1,503 crashes in nine months, followed by US-380 and SH-121. January through March was the deadliest quarter despite the lightest traffic, so winter driving conditions deserve more respect than the calendar suggests. And the pattern is a severity problem, which means the usual levers are the ones that matter: belts on, speed down, sober behind the wheel, because in a county where crashes stayed flat and deaths jumped 61 percent, survival is decided after the crash starts, not before. Texas crash data is public through the TxDOT CRIS portal.[7]
