# Research — #1614: Hit-and-Run Pedestrian Deaths

**Slug:** `1614-hit-and-run-one-in-four-pedestrians-driver-kept-going`
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (design/engineering beat: darkness, lighting, street design, pedestrian AEB)
**Kicker:** Body Count
**Working title:** "One in Four Pedestrians Killed in 2024 Was Hit by a Driver Who Kept Going"

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy: YES. Zero prior Crash Report articles with "hit-and-run" in the title (queue search, 822 entries). The story is not "deaths fell" — it is a structural break: the hit-and-run *share* of pedestrian deaths jumped from ~20% (2015–2019) to 25% in 2020 and has not come back down, even as total deaths fell in 2024.
- Novel angle: the improvement is in volume, not behavior. Total traffic deaths fell 4.3% in 2024 and hit-and-run deaths fell 5.3%, yet one in four pedestrian deaths still involves a driver who fled. Plus the seasonality hook: November is the deadliest month for pedestrians (732 deaths), October second (695), December third (685) — the article is written at the start of the darkest, deadliest stretch of the year, and its queue ship date (Dec 2028) lands in that same stretch.
- Original calculations (mine, from cited counts): 1,749 pedestrian hit-and-run deaths / 365 = ~4.8 per day, nearly five pedestrians a day killed by a driver who did not stop. 2,758 total hit-and-run deaths / 365 = ~7.6 per day. Pedestrian hit-and-run deaths up 55.7% from 1,123 (2015) to 1,749 (2024).

## Verified facts (primary sources only for counts)

### NHTSA, Overview of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes in 2024 (DOT HS 813 791, April 2026)
- 39,254 people killed in 2024, down 4.3% from 41,025 in 2023 (1,771 fewer).
- Fatalities in hit-and-run crashes: 153 fewer, 5.3% decrease (2024 vs 2023).
- Pedestrian fatalities in hit-and-run crashes: 95 fewer, 5.2% decrease.
- Pedalcyclist fatalities in hit-and-run crashes: 45 fewer, 16% decrease.
- Pedestrian fatalities overall: 287 fewer, 3.9% decrease.
- Older-driver fatalities rose 115 (1.4%); SUV occupant fatalities rose 112 (1.6%).
- Source: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813791

### NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts 2024 Data: Pedestrians (DOT HS 813 818, June 2026)
- 7,080 pedestrians killed in 2024, down 3.9% from 7,367 in 2023.
- 71,635 pedestrians injured in 2024, up 5.0% from 68,241 in 2023.
- A pedestrian was killed every 74 minutes and injured every 7 minutes in 2024.
- Pedestrian deaths = 18% of all traffic fatalities.
- Nearly 1 in 4 pedestrians killed (24%) were struck by hit-and-run drivers.
- 76% of pedestrian fatalities occurred in the dark; 84% in urban areas; 73% at non-intersections.
- Alcohol involvement (driver and/or pedestrian BAC ≥ .01) in 43% of fatal pedestrian crashes.
- Source: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813818

### IIHS, Fatality Facts 2024: Pedestrians (posted June 2026, FARS analysis)
- 25% of pedestrian deaths in 2024 occurred in hit-and-run crashes. Share was ~20% in 2015–2019, jumped to 24% in 2020, and has sat at 25% since.
- Hit-and-run pedestrian deaths by year: 2015: 1,123 (20%); 2024: 1,749 (25%). Non-hit-and-run: 4,363 → 5,331.
- Pedestrian deaths up 72% since the 2009 low of 4,109. 2024 total 7,080 = 18% of all crash deaths.
- 6 p.m.–midnight alone = 47% of pedestrian deaths (24% 6–9 p.m., 23% 9 p.m.–midnight). 73% between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m.
- Deadliest months: November 732, October 695, December 685.
- Pedestrian AEB: vehicles with automatic braking that detects pedestrians had a 27% lower rate of pedestrian crashes (Cicchino, 2022).
- Engineering countermeasures named: sidewalks, refuge islands, overpasses/underpasses, barriers, increased illumination, improved signal timing, crosswalk beacons.
- Source: https://www.iihs.org/research-areas/fatality-statistics/detail/pedestrians

- Hit-and-run deaths rose an average of 7.2% per year since 2009.

- Source: https://newsroom.aaa.com/2018/04/hit-run-deaths-hit-record-high/

## Cross-checks performed
- 2,758 total hit-and-run deaths (White analysis) reconciles with NHTSA's 153-fewer / 5.3% decline.
- 1,749 pedestrian hit-and-run deaths appears identically in NHTSA Overview, NHTSA Pedestrians fact sheet, and IIHS Fatality Facts (25% vs 24% rounding difference between NHTSA fact sheet text and IIHS table: 1,749/7,080 = 24.7%).
- 7,080 pedestrian deaths confirmed across NHTSA Overview Table 3, NHTSA Pedestrians fact sheet, and IIHS. GHSA preliminary count (7,148) differs slightly — preliminary state-reported data vs FARS final; use FARS 7,080 as the canonical count and note the GHSA figure only if needed.

## Original contribution
1. The share-break finding: hit-and-run share of pedestrian deaths stepped from ~20% to 25% in 2020 and never reverted — a behavioral ratchet, not a blip.
2. The volume-vs-behavior split: 2024's declines (total deaths -4.3%, hit-and-run deaths -5.3%) look like progress, but the share held at 25% — the system got smaller, not better at keeping drivers at the scene.
3. Per-day toll: ~4.8 pedestrians/day and ~7.6 people/day killed by fleeing drivers.
4. The 94% figure (GHSA): the fleeing vehicle is the striking vehicle — the driver who hits is the driver who leaves.

## Strongest counterargument
- The share jump could partly reflect better hit-and-run coding in FARS/police reports rather than a pure behavior change; classification practices vary by state and have improved.
- 2024's absolute declines (hit-and-run deaths down 5.3%, pedestrian hit-and-run down 5.2%) are real progress; the story is not "getting worse" in volume terms.
- FARS records no motive field. Panic, unlicensed/uninsured status, outstanding warrants, and impairment are all plausible drivers of flight, but the data cannot apportion them — any motive claim would be speculation.
- The Brian White analysis is a plaintiff personal-injury firm's work, not peer-reviewed research; its city-level claims (e.g. Houston +61.5%) should be treated as directional, not canonical.

## Limitations
- FARS counts deaths within 30 days of a crash on a public trafficway; state counts using 12-month windows or private roads will differ.
- Hit-and-run classification depends on police reports and varies by state.
- No motive data in FARS; no data here on conviction rates or sentencing for fleeing drivers.
- Pedestrian injury count (71,635) is a CRSS estimate, not a count; fatality counts are actual FARS counts.

## Actionable takeaways (for the article)
- For pedestrians: reflective gear (500 ft visibility vs 55 ft in dark clothing), cross at lit intersections, assume the 6 p.m.–midnight window is the danger zone.
- For buyers: pedestrian-detecting AEB cuts pedestrian crash rates 27% (IIHS/Cicchino 2022) — check IIHS ratings for the specific system before buying; it is not standard on every trim.
- For drivers: dashcams — a fleeing driver is only caught if someone records the plate; and the legal point that leaving the scene turns a crash into a felony in most states, which is the opposite of the self-preservation math fleeing drivers seem to be doing.
- For cities: illumination and refuge islands are the named engineering fixes; November–December is when they matter most.
