# Research: #1041 — The Emergency Exit That Doesn't Tell You How to Open It

## Topic
Volvo Bus Corporation recall **26V600000** (NHTSA report received 2026-09-18): the emergency exit label on the entrance door of certain **2009-2025 Volvo 9700 motorcoaches** is missing the exit operating instructions, failing FMVSS 217. **1,236 buses** affected. Prevost remedy SR26-84 (label replacement, free). Owner letters mailed November 3, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since September 25, 2026.

## Primary sources (4+)
1. **NHTSA recalls API, campaign 26V600000** (fetched 2026-10-01): summary, consequence ("The missing information may prevent or delay the use of the emergency exit, increasing the risk of injury"), remedy, dates. Component: EQUIPMENT:OTHER:LABELS.
2. **FMVSS 217 text (govinfo.gov, 49 CFR 571.217)**: S5.5.3(b) requires "concise operating instructions describing the motions necessary to unlatch and open the emergency exit, in letters at least 1 centimeter high of a color that contrasts with its background, located within 15 centimeters of the release mechanism on the inside surface of the bus."
3. **NHTSA TP-217-06 compliance test procedure (carsandracingstuff.com archive)**: examples of compliant instructions — "Lift to Unlatch, Push to Open"; "Turn Handle, Push Out to Open".
4. **NTSB Special Investigation Report SIR-99/04 (Bus Crashworthiness Issues, ntsb.gov)**: motorcoach travel among safest modes, but average ~4 motorcoach passenger fatalities/year (older figure); rollovers disproportionately lethal.
5. **NHTSA motorcoach NPRM (stnonline.com, agency filing)**: fatal rollover crashes = ~1/3 of fatal motorcoach crashes but >1/2 of occupant fatalities; ~2/3 of rollover deaths are ejections; emergency exits must remain closed during rollover and operable after.
6. **NHTSA final rule, seat belts on motorcoaches (ohsonline.com, Nov 2013)**: average 21 motorcoach/large-bus occupants killed, 7,934 injured annually.
7. **NHTSA Motorcoach and School Bus Fire Safety Analysis (rosap.ntl.bts.gov)**: avg 2.5 fatalities/year in motorcoach fires; Wilmer, TX 2005 fire killed 23.

## Novel finding / contribution
- Site has **zero** motorcoach/coach-bus coverage (checked queue + stories/: no 'coach', 'motorcoach', 'prevost' hits). First coach article.
- The 16-model-year run: same defective label shipped 2009-2025. The defect is informational, not mechanical — the exit works; the *instructions* are what failed. FMVSS 217 requires two things at every exit (designation + operating instructions within 15 cm, 1 cm letters); these buses carry the designation but not the instructions.
- Framing: a federal safety label that fails the federal safety label rule. The recall IS the instruction manual.
- When exits matter most: rollover (exits must be operable after crash) and fire (2.5 deaths/year; smoke + darkness + panic = the moment 1-cm letters earn their keep).

## Numbers for the article
- 1,236 buses; 2009-2025 model years (16 years)
- Campaign 26V600000; report received 2026-09-18; letters 2026-11-03; Prevost SR26-84; VIN search from 2026-09-25
- FMVSS 217: 1-cm-high letters, within 15 cm of release mechanism
- 21 motorcoach/large-bus occupants killed, 7,934 injured annually (NHTSA 2013)
- Fatal rollovers: 1/3 of fatal crashes, >1/2 of occupant fatalities; 2/3 of rollover deaths = ejection
- 2.5 fire deaths/year; Wilmer TX 2005: 23 dead

## Counterargument (full strength)
This is a label-only recall. The exit hardware works. Nobody has been hurt, and the "risk" is that a passenger might fumble a handle for a few seconds in an emergency they will probably never experience. The entrance-door exit is at the front of the bus, steps from the driver, who can open it. FMVSS 217's 1-cm-letter, 15-cm rule is prescriptive to a degree that makes engineers laugh; a missing instruction line is a paperwork defect, not a death trap. Fixing it means mailing stickers.

## Limitations
- FARS light-vehicle data on this site does not cover motorcoaches; all bus stats come from older NHTSA/NTSB publications (1999-2013 vintage), not current FARS.
- No evidence any passenger has actually been confused or delayed by these specific labels; the consequence is NHTSA's stated regulatory judgment, not an observed event.
- Affected population is fleet operators, not retail owners; recall completion data is not public.
- Cannot verify whether the designation marking (5-cm letters) was present and correct; the recall concerns only the operating instructions.

## Actionable
- Charter/tour operators: check fleet VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls; ask Prevost about SR26-84 completion before the Nov 3 letters.
- Riders: you cannot check a bus VIN, but you can ask the company whether recall SR26-84 is done on the coach you're boarding. Know where the emergency exits are before the bus moves; the federally required instructions should be within arm's reach of every exit handle.
- If you drive a coach: the 1-cm instruction label is the cheapest safety device on the vehicle. Verify it exists at every exit.
