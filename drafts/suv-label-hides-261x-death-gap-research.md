# Research: The SUV Label Hides a 261x Death Rate Gap

## Angle
FARS data reveals that the "SUV" category — the single label NHTSA, IIHS, and the industry apply to everything from a Chevrolet Tracker to a Tesla Model Y — hides a death rate gap of 261x between the worst and best. The gap correlates strongly with construction type (body-on-frame vs unibody/crossover), but also with vehicle age, weight, and rollover propensity.

## Key Data (FARS 2014-2023)

### Body-on-frame SUVs (18 models in FARS dataset)
- Weighted average death rate: **1.45 per 100M VMT**
- Total deaths: **19,464**
- Worst: Chevrolet Tracker (7.83), Toyota Land Cruiser (6.27), Chevrolet TrailBlazer (2.83)
- Best BOF: Jeep Grand Cherokee (0.51), Dodge Durango (0.54)

### Unibody/Crossover SUVs (103 models in FARS dataset)
- Weighted average death rate: **0.38 per 100M VMT**
- Total deaths: **26,978**
- Worst unibody: Jeep Cherokee (1.73), Mercury Mountaineer (1.71)
- Best unibody: Tesla Model Y (0.03), Porsche Macan (0.03), Kia Telluride (0.04)

### Aggregate gap: **3.8x** (weighted average), **261x** (widest individual gap: Tracker vs Model Y)

### Same-manufacturer gaps (stunning):
- **Ford:** Expedition (2.31) vs Bronco (0.05) = 46x
- **Chevrolet:** Tracker (7.83) vs Traverse (0.20) = 39x
- **Toyota:** Land Cruiser (6.27) vs RAV4 (0.19) = 33x
- **Lexus:** RX (0.43) vs NX (0.05) = 9x
- **GMC:** Yukon (2.55) vs Terrain (0.29) = 9x

## Why this matters
1. When consumers shop for an "SUV," the label tells them nothing about safety. A Tracker and a Model Y are both "SUVs."
2. IIHS Top Safety Pick+ awards don't capture this — they test current models, but the fleet includes 15+ years of vehicles.
3. Body-on-frame vehicles have inherently higher rollover risk and different crash energy management.
4. The "SUV is safer" narrative applies only to modern unibody crossovers, not to the construction type that defined the category.

## Sources

## Limitations
- Fleet age confound: many high-rate BOF SUVs are older designs with older drivers. The Tracker was discontinued in 2004.
- VMT estimation introduces ±15% uncertainty for low-volume models.
- Construction type is not the only variable — weight, driver demographics, usage patterns all contribute.
- Some classifications are debatable (e.g., 4Runner could be either category; Navigator is BOF).
- The 261x gap compares the extreme outliers — the weighted average gap of 3.8x is the honest comparison.
