# Research: Toyota Solara vs Camry — Same Platform, Double the Death Rate

## Core Finding
The Toyota Solara (1999-2008) shares the Camry platform (XV20/XV30) but has a fatality rate of **4.25 per 100M VMT** vs the Camry's **2.03** — more than double. Only **4.1%** of Solara drivers in fatal crashes were impaired, vs 19.2% for Camry drivers. This is the lowest impairment rate in the entire FARS dataset for vehicles with 200+ deaths.

## FARS Data
- **Toyota Solara:** 642 deaths, rate 4.25, fleet ~131,250, class: Sedan
  - Toxicology: anyPct=4.1%, alcPct=2.6%, drugPct=2.6%
- **Toyota Camry:** 6,328 deaths, rate 2.03, fleet ~2,712,500, class: Sedan
  - Toxicology: anyPct=19.2%, alcPct=14.5%, drugPct=8.4%

## Why the Rate Differential
1. **Structural differences:** Coupe/convertible body loses the B-pillar and rear door frame reinforcement. Side impact protection is significantly reduced.
2. **Roof crush resistance:** Convertible variants have no fixed roof structure. Even the coupe has a longer roof span without the structural break of rear doors.
3. **Smaller fleet, older average age:** The Solara was discontinued in 2008. By the FARS period (2014-2023), the average Solara on the road was 10+ years old — older vehicles have degraded safety systems.
4. **Demographics explain the LOW impairment:** Solara buyers skewed older (50+), suburban, female. This demographic has the lowest impairment rates nationwide. The 4.1% impairment rate reflects the buyer, not the car's safety.
5. **The demographic mismatch IS the story:** These are the safest, most cautious drivers in America, dying at 2x the rate of Camry drivers who are 5x more likely to be impaired.

## NHTSA Records
- 1999 Solara: No Overall Safety Rating, 0 recalls, 0 investigations, 2 complaints
- 2007 Solara: IIHS moderate overlap test result = "Good" rating
- The Solara was never tested in IIHS side impact, small overlap, or roof crush tests

## Novel Contribution
This is a natural experiment in automotive safety: same engine, same transmission, same chassis — just remove two doors. The impairment data isolates the engineering variable. When 96% of fatal crash drivers are sober, you can't blame the driver.
