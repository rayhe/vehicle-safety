The Night Light That Unplugs Itself on the Interstate

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 9, 2026

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute. Light Boy America is recalling its GBX12SK towable light tower under NHTSA campaign 26V564 because the unit may detach from the tow vehicle while being towed.[1] The affected model year is 2020. At 65 miles per hour, 1,288 pounds of detached light tower carries roughly 247 kilojoules of kinetic energy, the equivalent of a 14-story drop.[3]

A light tower exists for one reason: to make darkness survivable. Construction zones, crash scenes, highway repairs at 2 a.m. It is the machine we bring to the most dangerous stretches of road so the workers there can see and be seen. This week one model earned a federal recall for the opposite service, becoming, briefly, a projectile. There is a word for a safety device that introduces a new hazard, and it is this one.

To see the company it keeps, take the four years the AAA Foundation studied: between 2011 and 2014, road debris factored into more than 200,000 police-reported crashes in the United States, injuring about 39,000 people and killing 500, according to the AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety.[2] Debris crashes were four times as likely on Interstates. Tow trailers separating from their tow vehicles are explicitly named among the most common debris types. And here is the cruelest line in the whole study: nearly 37 percent of those 500 deaths came not from the debris striking a car, but from a driver swerving to avoid it. The dodge kills more often than the object. Panic, it turns out, is the more reliable weapon.

To be fair, a six-year-old construction trailer getting recalled is the safety system doing its job. An equipment maker files the paperwork, NHTSA publishes the campaign, fleet owners check their serials, nobody is accused of a cover-up. No population count or failure mechanism is stated in the public announcement I could find, which limits how much blame to assign, and the universe does not grade on intent anyway. But physics does not read filings, so if something large and metallic appears in your lane at highway speed and your hands itch to make heroes of themselves, remember what the data says: let them not.

What to do: if you operate a 2020 GBX12SK, check NHTSA campaign 26V564 at nhtsa.gov/recalls before the next tow, and inspect the coupler, safety chains, and mast stowage before every haul.[5] For the rest of us sharing the Interstate with towed construction equipment: leave extra following distance, because debris crashes concentrate there, and if debris appears in your lane at speed, brake in a straight line instead of swerving. The object is usually survivable; the swerve, 185 times out of 500, was not.
