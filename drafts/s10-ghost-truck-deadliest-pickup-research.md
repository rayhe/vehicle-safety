# Research Notes: The S-10 Ghost Truck

## Angle
The Chevrolet S-10 Pickup was discontinued in 2004. Twenty-two years later, it remains the **deadliest pickup truck in FARS** by a massive margin — and one of the deadliest vehicles of any type on American roads. Its replacement, the Chevrolet Colorado, kills at 1/17th the rate. The S-10 is a fleet ghost: a truck that stopped being built but never stopped killing.

## Self-Critique Gate
**Propose:** A discontinued compact truck from 2004 still ranks #1 in pickup death rate, #8 overall, with a 17x gap to its replacement.
**Challenge:** Is this genuinely surprising after 618 articles? Yes — we've done zombie vehicle stories (Buick Century) and platform comparisons, but never a head-to-head discontinued-vs-replacement analysis with this magnitude of gap (17x). The S-10 has 1,427 deaths and has never had its own story.
**Verdict:** Proceed.

## Primary Data (FARS 2014-2023)

### Death Rate
- **S-10: 4.83 deaths per 100M VMT** — #1 among ALL pickups
- **Colorado (replacement): 0.28 deaths per 100M VMT** — 17.3x safer
- For comparison:
  - Ford Ranger: 2.91 (#2 pickup)
  - Dodge Dakota: 2.62 (#3 pickup)
  - Silverado: 1.25
  - F-150: 1.04
  - Tacoma: 0.80
  - Ram 1500: 0.13

### Deaths
- S-10 total: 1,427 deaths (142.7 per year)
- Colorado total: 348 deaths (34.8 per year)
- S-10 ranks #8 in death rate among ALL vehicles with 200+ deaths

### Toxicology (impairment is NOT the explanation)
- S-10: 20.5% any impairment (alc 15.4%, drug 8.6%) — right at fleet average
- Colorado: 19.0% any impairment (alc 14.4%, drug 8.3%)
- The impairment rates are nearly identical — the truck, not the driver, is the difference

### Fleet Age
- S-10 death-weighted average fleet age: **21.0 years** — one of the oldest in FARS
- Peak death model year: 2000 (178 deaths), 2003 (149), 1998 (143)
- Last model year produced: 2004 (only 16 deaths for 2004 MY — partial year production)

### Fleet Size
- Estimated remaining S-10 fleet: ~218,750 vehicles (FARS estimate)
- This fleet of under 220K vehicles produced 1,427 deaths in a decade

### Model Year Death Distribution
1983: 8, 1984: 6, 1985: 8, 1987: 14, 1988: 23, 1989: 28, 1990: 12, 1991: 59, 
1992: 46, 1993: 58, 1994: 72, 1995: 78, 1996: 83, 1997: 103, 1998: 143, 
1999: 104, 2000: 178, 2001: 118, 2002: 113, 2003: 149, 2004: 16

## Context
- The S-10 was GM's compact pickup from 1982-2004 (three generations)
- Built on a body-on-frame platform with rear-wheel drive
- No ESC (Electronic Stability Control) — ESC wasn't mandated until 2012
- No side airbags, limited crash structure by modern standards
- The Colorado replaced it in 2004 on a modernized platform
- 2023 NHTSA estimated 36,640 total US traffic deaths — 142.7/yr from S-10 alone = ~0.4% of all US traffic deaths from one discontinued truck

## Limitations
- FARS captures fatal crashes only, not injury-only crashes
- VMT estimates for aging vehicles carry uncertainty (±15-20%)
- Fleet size estimates for 20+ year old vehicles are rough
- Some S-10 deaths involve multi-vehicle crashes where the S-10 occupant wasn't at fault (struck by larger vehicle)
- We cannot separate S-10 design deficiency from general small-truck vulnerability

## Strongest Counterargument
The S-10's death rate is partly a survivor bias problem: the remaining S-10s on the road are disproportionately owned by people who can't afford a newer truck — lower income, rural, younger drivers. These demographics correlate with higher crash risk independent of vehicle. However, the toxicology data directly undercuts this: the S-10's impairment rate (20.5%) is essentially identical to the Colorado's (19.0%) and the fleet average. If the S-10's drivers were a uniquely risky population, their impairment numbers would show it. They don't.
