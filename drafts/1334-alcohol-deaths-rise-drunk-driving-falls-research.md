# Research: Drunk Driving Deaths Fell. Alcohol Deaths Rose 29% Anyway. (Same Molecule.)

**Article #1334 — Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk)**
**Researched 2026-10-30. Dateline Oct 30, 2026.**

## The angle
NHTSA's June 2026 fact sheet (DOT HS 813 815) put 2024 alcohol-impaired crash deaths at 11,904, down 3.9% from 2023, the third straight annual decline, about 30% of all traffic fatalities. Same decade, CDC alcohol-attributable mortality sits near 178,000 a year, up 29% from 2016-2017. A state-by-state Lantana Recovery analysis (reported Oct 22, 2026 by the Staten Island Advance) makes the split concrete for New York: alcohol-impaired road deaths fell 3.12% (2015-2024, 1.55 to 1.50 per 100,000) while alcohol-induced deaths rose 18.46% (6.5 to 7.7 age-adjusted per 100,000). Dale's toxicology framing: the molecule is the same; the country built a system for the BAC that produces a defendant and nothing for the BAC that produces a hospital chart. Alcohol kills roughly 15x as many Americans through the body as through the windshield.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: NHTSA June 2026 fact sheet is fresh federal data; Lantana/SI Advance story is 8 days old; the NY rank gap (15th-best improvement on roads vs 43rd-worst growth in alcohol-induced deaths) is a genuinely new cross-dataset comparison.
- Novel vs prior Crash Report coverage? Yes: 43 prior impairment-related stories cover cars-with-drunk-drivers, interlocks, 0.15 cohorts. None cover the alcohol-induced/alcohol-attributable mortality divergence or the 15:1 ratio.
- Data with sources? Yes, five primary/federal sources below.

## Primary data

1. **NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts 2024: Alcohol-Impaired Driving, DOT HS 813 815 (June 2026), FARS 2024 ARF**
   - 11,904 alcohol-impaired driving fatalities in 2024; down 3.9% from 12,382 in 2023; third consecutive yearly decline.
   - ~30% of all traffic fatalities; in 1982 it was 48%.
   - 7,247 impaired drivers (61%), 1,483 passengers riding with impaired drivers (12%), 1,858 occupants of other vehicles (16%), 1,316 nonoccupants (11%).
   - 68% of alcohol-impaired deaths involved a driver at BAC 0.15+ (8,097 deaths).
   - Ages 21-24 highest impairment rate in fatal crashes: 28%.
   - Children 14 and under: 1,032 traffic deaths, 221 (21%) in alcohol-impaired crashes.
   - One death every 44 minutes. 10-year average 2015-2024: over 11,500/year.
   - Source PDF: http://business.cch.com/plsd/2024Alcohol-ImpairedDrivingTrafficSafetyFacts082026.pdf

2. **NHTSA, State Alcohol-Impaired-Driving Estimates 2024, DOT HS 813 813 (July 2026)**
   - 30 states + DC declined 2023-2024; 19 states increased; 1 unchanged.
   - New York: 1.55 to 1.50 per 100,000, -3.12%, 15th-largest decline.
   - Source PDF: https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92320/dot_92320_DS1.pdf

3. **CDC alcohol-attributable mortality**
   - ~178,000 deaths/year from excessive alcohol use (2020-2021), ~5% of all US deaths, up ~29% from 2016-2017.
   - Breakdown: ~117,000 chronic conditions (cancers, heart disease, liver disease, AUD) vs ~61,000 acute events (crashes, overdoses, poisonings, suicide).
   - Deaths rose 35% among girls/women vs 27% among boys/men between periods; leading causes include breast and colorectal cancer.
   - Average 24 years of life lost per death; ~4 million YPLL.
   - Cited via the Oct 22, 2026 analysis piece.

4. **Lantana Recovery state analysis (reported by Staten Island Advance, week of Oct 22, 2026)**
   - NY alcohol-induced deaths (underlying cause wholly attributable to alcohol: alcoholic liver disease, poisoning, cardiomyopathy, dependence, etc.): 6.5 to 7.7 per 100,000 age-adjusted, +18.46%, rank 43rd for growth.
   - Same dataset class as vital statistics (death certificates), different from FARS crash records.

5. **JAMA Network Open (Jan 2026): 3,153 counties, ~22,000 county-years**
   - Share of counties with at least one substance-use-disorder facility offering medications for AUD (naltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram) rose 34% (2017) to 44% (2021), then flat through 2023.
   - Concentration in metro counties with larger populations and lower uninsured shares; rural/smaller/disadvantaged counties less likely to have any.

6. **NIAAA**: alcohol played a role in ~16% of 2022 opioid overdose deaths; at least 8.4% of ED visits. NY overdose deaths down 44.9% since 2022 (Governor's office, provisional CDC data), opioid-related down 51.9% — the falling curve absorbs alcohol's contribution into a different ledger.
