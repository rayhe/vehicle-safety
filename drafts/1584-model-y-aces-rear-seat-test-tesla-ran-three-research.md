## Story in one line
The redesigned 2026-27 Tesla Model Y earned IIHS Top Safety Pick+ on September 3, 2026, with Good ratings in every crash test including the updated moderate overlap front test that sank the RAV4 (Acceptable), the RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid (Marginal), and the CR-V (Poor) — and three of the four crash tests behind the award were conducted by Tesla itself under IIHS's verified manufacturer testing program, while IIHS ran the one test that decides the award.

## Original computations (show work in article)
- Rate ratios: RAV4 0.19 / Model Y 0.03 = **6.3x**; CR-V 0.53 / 0.03 = **17.7x**; Forester 0.26 / 0.03 = **8.7x**. The Model Y's real-world death rate is roughly one-sixth the RAV4's and one-eighteenth the CR-V's.
- Self-test share: 3 of the 4 crashworthiness tests (driver small overlap, passenger small overlap, side) carry Tesla-conducted notes; 1 of 4 (moderate overlap) does not. The moderate overlap is the test that gates the 2026 awards — and it is the one IIHS ran.
- The award math: the Model Y needed Good in 3 crash tests + Good/Acceptable headlights + Good pedestrian FCP + Acceptable-or-Good vehicle-to-vehicle FCP. It got Good in all six. The only sub-Good marks on the whole page (seat belt reminders M, LATCH A) are the two evaluations IIHS explicitly excludes from award criteria.

## Strongest counterargument (must appear at full strength)
Three of the four crash tests were run by the company selling the car. IIHS's verification program is decades old and includes audit testing, and the institute scores the data itself — but the detailed test data is submitted confidentially and destroyed after a final rating is determined, so outsiders cannot re-run Tesla's numbers. A skeptic is entitled to note that the one test IIHS will not outsource, the moderate overlap, is also the one test where Tesla's rivals keep failing, and that the Model Y's pass there is the only fully independent result on the page. Separately, the FARS rate (0.03) covers pre-redesign Model Ys (2020-2023 model years) and reflects who buys the car as much as how it is built: newer, wealthier, ADAS-equipped fleets with sober drivers (17.6% impairment vs ~25-30% fleet-wide) will always post lower rates. Lab Good plus a 0.03 real-world rate is the strongest combination on the site, but neither number alone proves the Juniper redesign is the safest family SUV you can buy — it proves the outgoing car was, and the new one passed the lab.

## Limitations (must appear in article)
- FARS 2014-2023 covers the pre-redesign Model Y (model years 2020-2023); the Juniper redesign has no real-world fatality data yet. The 0.03 rate is the old car's record, not the new car's.
- IIHS ratings apply to 2026-27 Model Ys only. A used 2021-2025 Model Y carries different ratings; check iihs.org/ratings before buying used.
- IIHS does not publish the detailed manufacturer-submitted test data; the verification program's audit tests are occasional, not per-model. The article states what IIHS discloses and does not claim independent verification of Tesla's three tests.
- The RAV4/CR-V/Forester comparison uses 2026-model-year IIHS ratings; the RAV4 PHEV is tested separately from the RAV4 Hybrid and scored worse (Marginal vs Acceptable).
- NHTSA 5-star overall for the 2026 Model Y is secondary-sourced (trade roundups, Sept 2026); verify against nhtsa.gov/ratings at DRAFT or drop it.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- If you are shopping for a family SUV and anyone rides in the back seat: check the *updated moderate overlap* rating specifically, not the old test and not a 2025 award. The Model Y and Forester earn Good there; the RAV4 earns Acceptable, the CR-V Poor, the RAV4 PHEV Marginal. That single rating is the difference between the 2026 award winners and the rest of the segment.
- If you own a 2026 RAV4 or CR-V: the weak point is the rear seat in a frontal crash. Keep lap belts low on the hips, put the most vulnerable passengers in the best-protected row, and do not assume a 5-star NHTSA rating covers rear-seat protection — NHTSA does not test a rear dummy in frontal crashes.
- If you are buying a used Model Y: the TSP+ applies to 2026-27 cars only. A 2023 Model Y is a different car with different ratings and a different FARS record.
- The seat-belt reminder Marginal is the one fixable blemish and it is a software problem (alert duration 8-89 seconds, no second-row startup alert). If Tesla pushes an update that lengthens the alert, the page gets cleaner without a single bolt turning.
