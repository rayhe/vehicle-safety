# Research: #1559 — The Guard Was Never There

**Slug:** 1559-mac-trailer-missing-rear-guard-liquid-tanker
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Investigation)
**Angle:** MAC Trailer recalled 60 liquid tank trailers built 2024-2026 because the rear underride guard was never installed. Not a failed part. Not a worn part. A federally mandated safety device that simply was not there. These trailers rolled off the line AFTER the upgraded FMVSS 223/224 compliance date (July 15, 2024) requiring guards strong enough to stop a compact car at 35 mph. The guard on these 60 trailers stops a car at 0 mph, because it does not exist.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: a safety device mandated since 1998, strengthened in 2022, still missing from new trailers in 2026. A compliance failure, not a component failure, which is a different and rarer recall species.
- Novel? Yes: zero prior Crash Report coverage of a recall for a guard that was never installed. #991 and #1192 covered side underride (unregulated) and the undercount; this is the REAR guard, which IS regulated, and it was missing anyway. The "never installed" framing is original to this treatment.
- 3+ primary sources? NHTSA recall 26V723000 (Part 573 report, Nov 11, 2026); NHTSA 2022 final rule upgrading FMVSS 223/224 (Federal Register 2022-14330); IIHS 2011 rear-guard crash-test study (Brumbelow, ESV proceedings); GAO-19-264 underride undercount. VERDICT: PROCEED.

## Verified facts
1. **The recall:** NHTSA campaign 26V723000, reported October 30, 2026. MAC Trailer Mfg., Inc. recalling certain 2025-2027 Liquid Tank Trailers. "The trailers may be missing the rear impact guards. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard numbers 223, 'Rear Impact Guards,' and 224, 'Rear Impact Protection.'" Potentially affected: 60 vehicles. Production range per NHTSA applicability records: June 26, 2024 to August 4, 2026. Remedy: dealers install rear under-ride bumpers, free of charge. Owner notification letters expected December 29, 2026. MAC customer service 1-800-795-8454. NHTSA consequence statement, verbatim: "A missing rear impact guard increases the risk of injury during a crash."
   Sources: NHTSA recall record (nhtsa.gov/recalls?nhtsaId=26V723000); The Trucker, "NHTSA: Massive recall of trucks and trailers"; Fleet Maintenance, "NHTSA issues truck and trailer recalls for safety defects across nine brands"; TruckCodex 26V723000 page; RecallsNearMe campaign page (production dates).
2. **The rule it violates:** FMVSS 223 (equipment standard: guard strength and energy absorption) and FMVSS 224 (vehicle standard: trailers with GVWR 10,000 lbs or more must be equipped with a compliant guard) have required rear impact guards since 1998. NHTSA's July 15, 2022 final rule upgraded both standards, adopting Transport Canada's requirements: guards must protect occupants of compact and subcompact cars impacting the rear of a trailer at 56 km/h (35 mph), center or 50% overlap. Compliance date: July 15, 2024. The recalled trailers were built June 26, 2024 to August 4, 2026, entirely after the upgraded standard took effect.
   Sources: Federal Register 2022-14330 (Docket NHTSA-2022-0053); NHTSA press release, "Underride Protection on Truck Trailers" (June 2022); NTEA, "Trailer rear impact protection upgrades."
3. **Why the guard matters (IIHS):** IIHS crash-tested three semi-trailer rear guards certified to US FMVSS 223. In a full-width test at 56 km/h (35 mph), the guard built only to US requirements "failed catastrophically at the points of attachment to the trailer, allowing severe underride and trailer contact with the dummy's head." A second guard failed in 50% overlap tests at 40 and 56 km/h. IIHS's analysis of the federal Large Truck Crash Causation Study found underride in nearly 80% of the 115 crashes where a passenger vehicle struck the rear of a heavy truck, and in 23 of 28 fatal cases the underride damage was severe or catastrophic. NHTSA has estimated about 423 people in passenger vehicles die each year when their vehicles strike the backs of large trucks.
   Sources: Brumbelow, "Crash test performance of large truck rear underride guards," 22nd ESV Conference proceedings, 2011 (iihs.org); IIHS petition coverage, Expediter Online.
4. **The undercount (context):** GAO-19-264 (2019): FARS recorded 189-253 underride deaths per year 2008-2017, but GAO found the count is likely low because police crash forms often lack an underride field and officers document inconsistently. IIHS told GAO it uses general rear-impact crash data as a proxy because FARS undercounts underride.
   Source: GAO-19-264 (gao.gov/assets/700/697656.pdf). Also covered in #1192 research.
5. **The cargo:** liquid tank trailers haul liquid cargo, fuel and chemicals among them. The recall record does not specify cargo. Do NOT claim these specific 60 trailers carried fuel. The point that survives sourcing: a rear-end underride into any tanker is a bad day; into a liquid tanker, the trailer's cargo adds a second hazard on top of the intrusion. Keep the cargo point general and clearly framed as context, not as a claim about these 60 units.
   Source: recall record (vehicle type: Liquid Tank Trailer); general trailer classification.

## Novel calculation (show math in article)
- The 2022 rule requires a guard to protect a compact car at 35 mph. A missing guard protects at 0 mph. Protection delivered: 0% of the standard.
- Production window: June 26, 2024 to August 4, 2026 is 770 days, about 25 months. 60 trailers over 770 days is roughly one guard-less tanker every 13 days leaving the factory.
- IIHS math: NHTSA's estimate of ~423 rear-impact deaths per year, with underride in ~80% of rear strikes, means roughly 340 deaths a year involve a car sliding under the back of a truck. A missing guard converts a survivable rear-end crash into an intrusion crash, the exact mechanism IIHS documented.
- Assumptions stated: the 80% figure comes from the LTCCS sample of 115 rear-strike crashes, not from all rear impacts nationally; the 423 figure is NHTSA's estimate, not a FARS count; the "one every 13 days" rate assumes even production across the window, which is illustrative, not measured.

## Limitations (dedicated paragraph in article)
- 60 trailers is small. This is not a fleet-wide scandal; it is a manufacturing and quality-control failure at one builder. Do not inflate it into an industry-wide claim.
- NHTSA has not reported crashes, injuries, or deaths tied to these 60 trailers. The risk is prospective, not realized, as far as the public record shows.
- The recall record does not explain HOW the guards went missing: whether a production-line omission, a supplier shortfall, or a paperwork error. MAC's Part 573 report may say more; the public summary does not. Do not speculate on the mechanism.
- The cargo point is context, not a finding about these trailers. The recall says "Liquid Tank Trailers" and nothing about what they carried.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
Sixty trailers is a rounding error against the roughly 260,000 new trailers built each year, and MAC caught it, reported it, and is fixing it free. The system worked: the defect was found, the recall was filed, the remedy is a bolt-on bumper. A reader could fairly say this is a story about a recall process functioning as designed, not a scandal. The honest reply: the process worked only after two years of production. The trailers were on the road, hauling liquid cargo, with no guard at all, for up to 25 months before the recall. The recall system catches failures; it does not prevent them. And the failure mode here, a required device simply absent, is the one quality control is supposed to make impossible.

## Actionable insights
- Fleet operators and owner-operators: check any 2025-2027 MAC liquid tank trailer against NHTSA campaign 26V723000. The fix is a free bumper install; letters go out December 29, 2026, but the VIN lookup works now at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
- Drivers: a trailer's rear guard is the only thing between your windshield header and the trailer floor in a rear-end crash. If you cannot see a guard bar hanging below the trailer's rear, do not tailgate it. That is true for every trailer, not just these 60.
- Everyone: rear guards are the one underride direction federal law actually covers. Side guards still have no federal standard (see #991, #1192). The rear is supposed to be the solved problem.
