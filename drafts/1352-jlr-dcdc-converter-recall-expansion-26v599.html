JLR Found 23,677 More SUVs That Can Go Dark and Die in Ten Seconds

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2026

In April 2026, Jaguar Land Rover recalled 170,169 mild-hybrid SUVs because a failing DC-DC converter can kill drive power and every exterior light at the same time. That recall missed cars. On September 17, 2026, JLR filed NHTSA campaign 26V599, adding 23,677 more vehicles with a later converter specification, and the company estimates 100 percent of the added population carries the defect.[1][2] No maybe. No probably fine. One hundred percent.

The failure has a script, and it runs in about ten seconds. A red warning reading "Stop Safely Electrical Fault Detected" appears on the dash. The transmission drops into neutral on its own. A transmission fault message follows. Then the exterior lights cut out, the climate control dies, and the engine shuts down.[3] That is not a stall. That is your six-figure SUV becoming an unlit, unpowered obstacle on the road you were just driving.

194,000
Vehicles now covered across both DC-DC converter recalls, making this one of the largest electrical recalls any luxury SUV brand has issued in 2026.

How did 23,677 cars slip through the April net? JLR says it found them with field-data analysis plus AI modeling: a model trained on the known failure population, run at scale on September 8, 2026, which flagged vehicles with the later-generation converter as likely to fail the same way.[4] The investigation also turned up a separate software bug that can stop the converter when the vehicle starts. JLR built a model to find which of its own cars the first recall missed. The answer was twenty-three thousand.

JLR has no permanent fix. Owners get an interim software update now, and a second letter whenever engineers finish a lasting repair.[3] Owner notification letters are expected to begin November 13, 2026.[5] Between April and September, JLR received 393 claims and field reports tied to this failure, with no accidents or injuries reported so far.[2] Read that again. Three hundred ninety-three field reports. Zero crashes. That is luck, not safety engineering.

Drive a 2019-2024 JLR mild hybrid? Run your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now, not when the November letter arrives.[6] If the red "Stop Safely Electrical Fault Detected" warning ever appears, pull over immediately; you have roughly ten seconds before drive power and lights go. Take the interim software update the moment your dealer offers it, and treat the second letter for the permanent fix as urgent, not routine. JLR customer service: 800-637-6837, campaign E008.[2] Shopping a used 2019-2024 Range Rover Sport or Defender? Verify both 26V248 and 26V599 are closed on that VIN before money moves.

What this article did not prove: the AI model's inputs, training data, and miss rate are undisclosed, so "AI modeling" is JLR's characterization, not an independently verified method. The interim software update is a mitigation, not a cure. Three hundred ninety-three field reports against 23,677 vehicles is a small numerator, and reporting lag means the real count is likely higher.

Fair point, stated plainly: an expanding recall can be read as the system working. JLR found the later-spec population with a new method, filed a second campaign before any reported injuries, and warned owners while engineering the real fix.[2][4] It is also the second attempt at scoping the same defect.
