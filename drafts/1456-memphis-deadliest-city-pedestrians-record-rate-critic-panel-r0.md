## 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Lede opens with the record, not the methodology, and the pull-stat lands on the second screen. Structure is clean: record, body count, counterfactual math, national divergence, rankings-lie, disparities, vehicle front end, crash shape, counterargument, action. The counterfactual paragraph is the spine and earns its length. Deduction: the 86-word national-divergence sentence and the 72-word front-end sentence are dense even after the rhythm fix; a reader could lose the thread mid-sentence. No structural rewrite needed.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.9/10
Distinctly Clara: direct, practical, angry on behalf of the people walking. "Count the bodies and the record gets worse." "A ranking is a sorting exercise, but the morgue is the measurement." Could not swap this byline with Rex or Dale without rewriting. Banned phrases zero, rhythm PASS with real variance. Deduction: "That is not an opinion, it is a measurement" is a deliberate comma splice for voice, and it works, but the same trick repeats twice more in the piece ("it is the risk", "it is a verdict on the streets") and the third landing is the weakest. One fewer would be sharper.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.8/10
Fair to Memphis where fairness is owed: the counterargument paragraph is at full strength (small-number volatility, the national decline is partly a gift of comparison, "designed to kill" overstates intent) and the limitations are explicit in the research file and the disclaimer. No victim-blaming; the pedestrian advice (cross at signals, wear reflective clothing) is framed as data-driven rules, not blame, and it sits alongside the driver ask and the city ask. Disparities are reported factually without moralizing. Deduction: the pedestrian advice still puts some onus on the person at risk; the balance holds only because the driver and city asks are longer and more specific.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.7/10
Pull stat 5.5 is the shareable number, and "roughly 225 people... a small neighborhood, erased" is the line that travels. "The morgue is the measurement" is quotable. The Memphis-record hook is local-news friendly and the national-vs-local divergence gives it a second audience. Deduction: the headline is long and does not compress to a single viral phrase as cleanly as some prior pieces; the strongest line ("a small neighborhood, erased") is buried in paragraph three.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.9/10
Every figure traces to SGA, GHSA, NHTSA, IIHS, or William's Walk, all linked. No statutes or case law are invoked, so there is nothing to misstate there. The 30.77 figure was caught unsourced before the panel and is now cited to FARS [6]. Deduction: "about 1.3 million people" for the metro is implied by the rate math rather than directly sourced; it is consistent with SGA's rate (369 deaths over 5 years at 5.5 per 100,000 implies ~1.34M), but the article does not show that derivation.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Original contribution is real and shown with math: the ~225 excess-death counterfactual (369 vs ~145 at the national rate), the 67% five-year jump, the 2.4x state and 2.8x national gaps, and the rankings-lie framing applied to this report's own warning. Limitations are honest (2020–2024 window already two years stale, GHSA 2025 figures preliminary and not FARS-comparable, metro boundaries vary, counterfactual is illustrative not causal) and the counterargument is stated at full strength in the article itself. Verifiability: 6 references, all hyperlinked, with DOT HS numbers where they exist. Deduction: the excess-death figure mixes a five-year count with a five-year average national rate, which the disclaimer discloses but the body does not flag in-line.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10
Pull-stat 5.5 with a clear label; every number arrives with its denominator (per 100,000, of five years, of all traffic deaths). The 72%, 96,615, 43.6%, and 10.1% figures are each contextualized rather than dropped. Deduction: the reader has to hold three rates at once (5.5 metro, 2.26 state, 1.97 national) across two paragraphs; the comparison is restated clearly, but a small table would have done the work in one glance.
