## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
Counterargument is stated at full strength (tiny recalls are cheap to file, the audit was a random check, Ford sat on it five weeks, do not mistake a cheap recall for a working system) and not strawmanned. The zero-crash claim is handled honestly: Ford's zero is explicit in the Part 573 report, while the Mack and Autocar zeros are labeled as inference from the absence of reported figures, in the article body and again in the disclaimer. Scope is defined (Nov 1–13 surfacing window) and the census is not oversold. Nothing tells the reader what to think about any manufacturer as a company, only what to do as an owner.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
All three campaign numbers (26V725, 26V719, 26V722), dates, populations, and defect descriptions trace to the three NHTSA documents cited in the research file, two of which were opened live this run (26V719, 26V722 letters) and the third verified against the Part 573 report text (26V725). One nit: the article says owner letters for the Bronco "go out November 16 through 20," which is the remedy-letter window in the Part 573 schedule; phrased correctly.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Original contribution is real: the first cross-manufacturer census of November 2026's smallest recall campaigns, ranked by units affected, plus the original contrast arithmetic (1,539 combined = 0.21% of BMW's 720,796) and the finding that all three were caught by internal quality processes rather than the complaint pipeline. Math is shown with inputs: 330 + 151 + 1,058 = 1,539; 1,539 / 720,796 = 0.21%; Sep 22 to Oct 27 = 35 days; 1,058 / 7 model years ≈ 151 per year. Limitations are explicit in the article and the disclaimer (Nov 1–13 window, inference vs. stated zero, conditional wiper risk, no completion rates yet). Counterargument full-strength. Four sources, anchored by three primary NHTSA documents.
