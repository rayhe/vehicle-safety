A Smudge on the Touchscreen Can Drive Your Navigator's Seat

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 22, 2026

Ford just recalled 36,994 Lincoln Navigators because a smudge on the rear-seat touchscreen can move the front passenger seat, and the cause is not a broken part or a crash, just residue on a screen, the kind every hand leaves behind, registering as a finger touch and telling a hundred-plus-pound seat assembly to slide forward, slide back, or recline.[1] Nobody touches a control, and the seat moves anyway. Lincoln markets this truck as a sanctuary on wheels, and the sanctuary keeps rearranging its own furniture.

38,183
Lincoln Navigators recalled in 2026 for seats that move on their own, across two campaigns

Mechanics first, because the filing is unusually specific about how a smudge becomes a safety defect. Chauffeur mode lets a rear passenger slide the front seat forward for extra legroom, controlled through a second-row touchscreen console Ford calls the RACM.[1] Ford's Part 573 report names two triggers. One: residue left on the RACM screen can be registered as finger touch input, moving the seat with no human involved. Two: pressing the home screen button at the same time as the seat movement button can send the seat to its full travel limit without any additional input. A moving seat can strike a second-row passenger or shove the front passenger into a seating position that makes a crash worse. If the RACM display starts making erratic menu selections on its own, that is the warning sign, written into the federal filing like a confession.

This is the second time in 2026, and that is the story. Back in March, Ford recalled 1,189 model year 2026 Navigators because the driver seat's obstacle detection software could get stuck in a loop and reverse the seat to the full back position while the truck was moving under 3 mph, with the module refusing to cancel the reversal even when the driver hit the manual switch.[3] Two different modules, two different triggers, one failure mode. Do the math and the October recall is 31 times the size of the March one, spanning nearly three years of production against a three-week window. Add them together and 38,183 Navigators carry a seat with a mind of its own.

Both fixes tell you something Ford will not say out loud. March removed the automatic reversal logic from the driver seat module. October disables chauffeur mode entirely.[1] When a feature keeps hurting people, or threatening to, the remedy is not better software for the feature, it is deleting the feature. A touchscreen that cannot tell a fingerprint from a finger is now a safety-critical input device, and the honest fix is admitting the input was never trustworthy.

Fair objection, stated at full strength: nobody has been hurt as far as the record shows, the fix is a free software update, and chauffeur mode is a gimmick most owners will never miss. All true. Also true: the March version moved the driver's seat while the truck was moving, which is a loss-of-control defect by any honest name, and the October version can drive a seat into a second-row passenger, possibly a child in a car seat, with nobody touching anything. A vehicle that rearranges its own interior while occupied is a safety defect even when the fix is a download and the body count is zero so far.

Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, where campaign 26V691 has been searchable since October 26, and the free dealer update disables chauffeur mode.[2] Until the appointment, wipe the rear touchscreen clean and watch for erratic menu selections, because that is the truck telling you the screen is hallucinating. If you own a 2026 Navigator built in late November or early December 2025, check for the March recall, 26V120, too. That production window sits inside both campaigns, which means one truck can carry two different ghost seats.
