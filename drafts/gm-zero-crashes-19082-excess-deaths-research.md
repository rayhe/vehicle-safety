# Research: GM's "Zero Crashes" Mission vs 19,082 Excess Deaths

## Angle
GM published the 8th installment of its UMTRI safety study (May 2026), examining 12 million MY2020-2024 vehicles matched to 700,000+ police-reported crashes across 18 states. The study proves GM's safety tech works: AEB reduces rear-end crashes 57%, backing tech reduces backing crashes 86%, pedestrian braking cuts injury-producing pedestrian crashes 35%. GM's stated mission: "a future with zero crashes."

But FARS data (2014-2023) tells a different story. GM vehicles have generated **19,082 excess deaths above class average** — more than any other manufacturer by a wide margin. That's 46% more excess deaths than Ford (13,093), nearly 4x Stellantis (4,914), and 4.5x Toyota (4,283).

## Novel Calculation: Excess Deaths by Manufacturer
Excess deaths = actual deaths minus expected deaths if the vehicle performed at its class average rate.

## Key Tension
GM claims "zero crashes" while:
1. Having 19,082 excess deaths — worst of any manufacturer
2. 6 of the top 15 excess-death vehicles industry-wide are GM
3. The UMTRI study only examines MY2020-2024 (very recent vehicles), not the millions of older GM vehicles still on the road killing people
4. GM's ignition switch scandal (Cobalt, 1,100 excess deaths) was a known defect
5. The Impala — America's fleet queen — has 2,673 excess deaths (2.4x what you'd expect for a sedan its size)

## Limitations
- FARS covers 2014-2023; most deaths involve older vehicles (pre-2020), not the MY2020-2024 vehicles in the UMTRI study
- GM's newer vehicles DO have safety tech that likely works
- Excess death calculation uses estimated fleet/VMT data, which has ±15% uncertainty for some models
- Fleet size matters: GM sells more vehicles than most, so higher absolute numbers are partly a volume effect (excess calculation controls for this via VMT)
- Autoevolution skepticism piece already published ("Don't Trust GM's Promise") but didn't have this FARS cross-tab

## Strongest Counterargument
GM could argue: "Of course our historical fleet has high death counts — we're one of the largest manufacturers, and these are overwhelmingly older vehicles built before our current safety tech was available. The UMTRI study proves our *current* vehicles are dramatically safer." This is partially valid — the excess death metric is backward-looking and can't capture the trajectory of improvement. But it's also the point: GM declared "zero crashes" as a mission while sitting atop a mountain of 19,082 excess deaths from vehicles that are still on the road, still in FARS, and still killing people. The mission statement runs years ahead of the fleet reality.
