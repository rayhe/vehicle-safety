# Research — #1170: The Recall That Didn't Recall (GMC Sierra grille deflector RQ26003)

**Slug:** `1170-sierra-grille-deflector-recall-query`
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate — "the recall fix didn't hold" outrage fits her register)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Dateline target:** ship_date 2027-09-24

## Angle
NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened Recall Query RQ26003 on Oct 9, 2026 — a federal audit of GM's own February 2025 recall (25V-060) for detaching upper grille deflectors on the GMC Sierra 1500. The query asks two questions: did the recall cover enough trucks, and does the fix actually hold? 21 of the 118 complaints in the record allege the deflector came off AFTER the recall repair was performed. The recall may have been too narrow, and the remedy may not work. A recall can fail twice: scope and fix.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Federal action opened 5 days before this run (Oct 9, 2026). Recall queries are rare instruments; NHTSA publicly auditing a recall's adequacy is a genuine news event.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. The Crash Report has covered dozens of "recall announced" stories (Ford Mach-E, Nissan Cube, Toyota RAV4 PHEV). None has covered a recall *audit* — a recall under investigation for not working. This flips the genre.
- **Data-grounded?** Yes. Primary-source NHTSA ODI opening resume, quoted verbatim below.

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA ODI opening resume, RQ26003** (verbatim via oemdtc.com/investigation/RQ26003/, which mirrors the NHTSA resume):
   - Opened 2026-10-09. Subject: Upper Grille Deflector Detachment. Component: STRUCTURE:BODY:BUMPERS:ACTIVE SHUTTERS/GRILL.
   - "ODI has received 118 complaints alleging detachment of the upper grille deflector on 2022-2024 model year (MY) GMC Sierra vehicles. 21 of these 118 complaints allege that the upper grille deflector detached after Recall 25V-060 was performed."
   - "The complaints allege that the upper grille deflector detaches while driving. In some cases, leading to a cracked windshield or surrounding vehicles needing to conduct evasive maneuvers."
   - "On February 6, 2025, GM submitted Recall 25V-060 addressing upper grille deflector detachment for 2022MY GMC Sierra vehicles manufactured with a chrome grille deflector. 2022MY GMC Sierra vehicles which were not manufactured with a chrome grille deflector and 2023-2024MY GMC Sierra vehicles were not included in the recall."
   - Purpose: "ODI is opening this Recall Query (RQ) to assess the adequacy of the scope and remedy for Recall 25V-060."
   - Estimated population under review: 535,639 trucks (2022-2024 MY Sierra 1500).
2. **NHTSA recall filing for 25V-060 (Feb 2025):** GM's original recall covered ~70,768 2022MY Sierra 1500s built with chrome grille deflectors after the 2022 mid-cycle refresh. Defect description: attachments holding the deflector can fracture over time; if all eight attachment points break, the piece may detach while driving. Remedy: dealers reinforce or repair the attachment points free of charge. GM said owners might notice a rattle from the front of the truck before complete separation. (Via autopostglobal.com and motorbiscuit.com summaries of the filing.)
3. **Detroit News, Summer Ballentine, Oct 13, 2026** (akronnewsreporter.com mirror, Tribune Content Agency): "Four accidents related to the issue were reported to GM, but there were no reported injuries." GM spokesperson Bill Grotz: "The safety and satisfaction of our customers are the highest priorities for the entire GM team... we continue to cooperate with NHTSA's evaluation of this matter." Also: only Sierras "with chrome grille deflectors produced after GM's 2022 model year mid-cycle enhancement" were in the original recall; "Non-chrome grilles on the trucks were designed differently and have not had similar problems."

## Original contribution (required)
1. **Scope-coverage math nobody ran:** The original recall covered ~70,768 trucks. The population now under review is ~535,639. That means the first recall covered roughly **13% of the population NHTSA now says may be affected** (70,768 / 535,639 = 13.2%). Whatever happens next, GM's first answer covered one truck in eight.
2. **The dual-failure framing:** The RQ is two audits in one — scope AND remedy. The scope question (why only chrome 2022s when complaints now span 2022-2024?) and the remedy question (why did deflectors detach on trucks that already had the fix?) are independent failure modes. One recall can fail twice.
3. **Denominator discipline:** 21/118 = 17.8% of *complaints* are post-repair, but the repaired population size is unknown, so no true failure rate can be computed from the resume. Say so explicitly — this is the number GM's lawyers will attack, and getting it right is the point.

## Strongest counterargument
- A Recall Query is not a finding. NHTSA has not declared anything defective, and RQs sometimes close with no action. The 21 post-repair complaints could reflect repairs not yet performed properly at the dealer level, owner misreporting (was the recall actually done?), or a different failure mode — not a defective remedy.
- 118 complaints against 535,639 trucks is a 0.022% complaint rate — tiny in absolute terms. Complaint volume alone doesn't establish a defect trend; ODI gets noise.
- GM did the right thing first: it self-filed 25V-060 in February 2025. Auditing your own recall is what the system is supposed to do.
- Fair version: this is regulators doing their job, not GM caught hiding something. The story's tension is about recall *adequacy*, which is a legitimate open question, not a conviction.

## Limitations
- NHTSA complaints are unverified consumer reports; they allege, they don't prove.
- We don't know how many of the 70,768 recalled trucks actually received the repair (completion rate unknown), so the post-repair denominator is unknown.
- The opening resume lists no crashes, injuries, or fatalities (Detroit News cites 4 accidents reported to GM, no injuries — slightly different from the resume; attribute each).
- The defect affects chrome deflectors per the original filing; whether non-chrome 2023-2024 complaints involve the same failure mode is exactly what the RQ is testing — we can't pre-judge it.

## Actionable insights (required gate)
- If you own a 2022-2024 Sierra 1500: listen for a rattle at the front of the truck (GM's own pre-separation symptom), check whether your VIN is under 25V-060 at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and file a complaint at nhtsa.gov if your deflector is loose or missing — ODI complaint volume is literally what opened this query.
- If you had the 25V-060 repair done: the deflector coming off anyway is a known-reported outcome; re-check your front end and re-file.
- For used-truck shoppers: a 2022 chrome-grille Sierra is worth a front-end inspection regardless of recall status.
- If your windshield cracked from a flying deflector: the complaint record shows you're not alone, and the RQ docket is where to add yours.
