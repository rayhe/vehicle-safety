## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10
Lede lands the paradox in two sentences and the piece never loses the thread: inversion,
math, who, rebuttal, limits, action. Structure is clean and the close earns its preach
by having done the math first. Dings: 781 words runs past the guide's 300-500 range
(in line with the recent 720-word draft, so a guide drift to note, not a fail), and the
73-word profile-list sentence is a mouthful even though rhythm metrics pass. Readable,
not bloated.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victim-blaming and no shaming of individuals: the "young man in a pickup" passage is
a data-grounded profile, attributed to AAA, not a moral judgment. The piece refuses the
easy moral ("just buckle up and deaths halve") by stating the confounding case at full
strength. The generic "drunk driver at 2 a.m." hypothetical names no real person.
No kids, no real names, no grief exploitation.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.5/10
Headline is the share: a 9%-vs-half paradox with a number in it. The 50% pull stat is
labeled with source and scope, so screenshots of it stay honest. Actionable close gives
readers something to do, not just something to feel. Ding: no single quotable one-liner
as strong as the headline itself; the piece shares as a headline-plus-stat, which is
enough but not exceptional.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
No statutes, regulations, or legal claims are asserted, so there is nothing to misstate.
Every external claim carries attribution (AAA Foundation study, NHTSA 2022 TSF, AAA
release) and the reference list links the primary documents, not aggregators. The
"flags, not profile" fix removed the one overclaim. No defamation exposure: AAA and
NHTSA are quoted, not paraphrased into new claims.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10
Genuine original contribution: the crude 10x share-ratio with inputs shown
((50/9)/(50/91)), two cross-checks from the same sources (28%-vs-9%, 50%-vs-14%), and
an explicit contrast with NHTSA's controlled 45%/60% effectiveness estimates, including
why the two differ. Limitations are a dedicated paragraph, not hedging: fatal-only
scope, known-restraint-use restriction, sample mixing, self-report bias, 28% wording
caveat. Strongest counterargument (shared causes, not just shared outcomes) is stated
before the rebuttal, not strawmanned. Judgment call endorsed: the secondary "14,955
lives saved" figure was left out of the article because it was not verified against the
primary TSF PDF this run. Ding: the 28% item's survey construction remains genuinely
opaque in the summary; quoting AAA's wording is the right move, but it caps this at 9.0.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
Pull stat, inline superscripts, linked references, and in-text methodology all present.
The calculation paragraph shows its inputs instead of asserting a result. Ding: the
headline's "nearly half" (AAA, all traffic deaths) and the pull stat's 50% (NHTSA 2022,
passenger-vehicle occupants killed, known restraint use) measure different populations;
both are labeled, but a skimming reader could conflate them. Acceptable, flagged for the
record rather than fixed, since both labels are already in the text.
