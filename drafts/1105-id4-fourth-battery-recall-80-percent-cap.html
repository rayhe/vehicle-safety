Volkswagen Recalled the ID.4 a Fourth Time. The Fix Is “Charge It Less.”

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
July 20, 2027

Before you buy a used ID.4, you might want to see this.[1] On October 6, Volkswagen told NHTSA it is recalling 22,524 ID.4s, model years 2023 through 2026, because charging the high-voltage battery past 80% can trigger thermal propagation, the industry's polite term for a fire starting inside the battery, and the root cause is still unknown. Two years into this saga, the official safety plan is a charging habit.

4
US battery-safety recalls for the Volkswagen ID.4 since December 2025. Running total: 67,704 vehicles, all SK Battery America cells, still no confirmed root cause.[1]

The roll-up nobody has published in one place: campaign 25V836 (December 2025, 629 cars, misaligned electrodes), campaign 26V028 (January 2026, 670 cars), campaign 26V030 (January 2026, 43,881 cars, battery overheating, software update plus module swaps), and now campaign 26V630. Four recalls, the same battery, the same supplier, the same unanswered question about why it burns.

This is what is genuinely new about 26V630. Volkswagen says the recall follows "another field study as part of an ongoing review of Volkswagen vehicles not included in previous battery recall campaigns."[1] Read that plainly: cars Volkswagen had already cleared are back in the recall, and the model-year range now runs to 2026, including the new ones rolling off lots right now.

Each of the first three campaigns shipped with something: module replacements, a software update, an inspection protocol. 26V630 ships with nothing, no remedy and no fix date. Owner risk letters go out November 27, and Volkswagen will send a second letter "once a fix is available."[1] Twenty-two thousand electric crossovers, and the entire safety plan is a dashboard setting, for now, indefinitely.

Now the fair version, at full strength: twenty-two US claims over two and a half years against roughly 45,000 previously recalled cars is a low incidence rate. Volkswagen found this through its own field study, which is the surveillance system catching something early, the way it is supposed to work. EV battery fires remain rare next to gasoline-car fires. An 80% daily charge cap is ordinary battery hygiene; most EV owners should be doing it regardless. And a recall that widens is evidence of a manufacturer still looking, not one hiding.

All of that is true, and it does not excuse two things. First: nearly two years after the first fire, the root cause is still "unknown," which means every interim fix so far has been aimed at a target nobody can see. Second: a class action filed in September alleges Volkswagen sat on this defect for two years before the first recall.[3] When the fourth recall for the same battery arrives with no remedy, the owner is not being protected; the owner is being managed. Buy the used ID.4 if you want one, but price in the possibility that the fifth letter brings another habit, not a fix.

What to do: If you drive a 2023–2026 Volkswagen ID.4, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today; set your charge limit to 80% in the car's charging menu and keep it there. Expect a risk letter around November 27, 2026, then a second letter when a remedy exists. Volkswagen customer service: 1-800-893-5298, recall number 93EX. Shopping used? Verify all four campaigns are complete (25V836, 26V028, 26V030, 26V630) and get the recall-completion paperwork in writing before you sign. And if your car showed reduced range or performance, treat it as urgent: earlier incidents included self-discharge warning signs.[4]
