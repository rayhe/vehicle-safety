# Research — #1259 "Tesla's Robotaxi Had Its Worst Crash Month Ever. Seven of the Nine Happened Under 3 MPH."

## News peg
NHTSA's Standing General Order (SGO) crash data update posted **Thursday, October 15, 2026** (ADS reports through September 15). Tesla filed **nine** ADS crash reports with an August 2026 incident month — the most for any single incident month since the current SGO version took effect June 16, 2025. Next highest: 5 (January 2026). All nine list property damage, **zero injuries**. The release also contains the **first-ever Cybercab report in federal data**: an Austin parking lot, "proceeding straight... as fast as 5MPH and drove into a large pothole filled with water," safety monitor aboard, no passengers, August — before public Cybercab rides opened Sept 4.

## Verified facts
- 36 total Tesla ADS reports in SGO data since June 16, 2025 (15 months). 35 = Model Y robotaxi; 1 = Cybercab.
- August 2026 breakdown: 5 Dallas, 3 Austin, 1 Tampa (Dallas launched April 18, 2026; Tampa July 21, 2026; Austin June 2025).
- **7 of the 9 August reports list a pre-crash speed of 3 mph or less** (Tesla News Wire's read of the CSV; spot-verifiable per narrative list below).
- August incident narratives (Tesla's own): remote operator took over → touched dumpster at ~1 mph (Austin alley); remote operator → parked SUV at 0.40 mph (Dallas); nav route into low-hanging chain across parking lot exit (Tampa, 4 mph) and entrance (Dallas, 3 mph); low-speed contact turning: utility meter 0.45 mph, guardrail 0.25 mph (Dallas); pickup ran stop sign into robotaxi (Dallas: robotaxi at 27 mph slowed to 3 mph, steered right, truck hit its left side); SUV rear-ended robotaxi stopped at yield sign with 3 passengers (Austin).
- Texas records (Oct 14): 769 Tesla Robotaxi vehicles, including 349 Cybercabs. Tesla does not publish robotaxi mileage with the reports.
- Concurrent federal pressure: NHTSA auditing Tesla's Cybercab self-certification (steering-wheel-free vehicle), response due **Oct 30**; defect petition **DP26010** (school-zone speeding) open since Oct 9.

## Kill test
Notable? First-ever Cybercab crash in federal data + fleet's record crash month = yes. Novel angle? The "36 robotaxi crashes!" headline is a trap: 78% of the record month happened slower than a brisk walk, and the third-amended SGO (June 2025) deliberately added a property-damage reporting threshold for ADS — so a fleet with Tesla-grade telemetry reporting 9 fender-benders is partly an artifact of a wider net, not a safety regression. Nobody has run the speed distribution. Distinct from #1239 (remote-takeover angle) and #1240 (Cybercab sales during audit). PROCEED.

## Novel contribution (original analysis)
1. **Speed distribution**: 7/9 August reports (78%) list pre-crash speed ≤3 mph. The record month is a parking-speed month.
2. **Injury ledger**: 0 injuries across all 36 reports in ~15 months — including a 27→3 mph side impact and a rear-end with 3 passengers aboard.
3. **Reporting-artifact math**: the third-amended SGO took effect June 16, 2025, *days before* Austin launch — the entire robotaxi reporting history exists under the expanded property-damage threshold, and NHTSA itself says telemetry asymmetry makes high counts expected for Tesla specifically. The "record" is partly the reporting regime working as designed.

## Limitations (must state)
- NHTSA warns the data is **not exposure-normalized**: no fleet size or VMT per month, so "August was the worst month" cannot be stated as a rate — fleet grew fast through 2026 (769 vehicles by Oct 14) and Dallas only launched April 2026.
- Tesla's narratives are self-reported; "as fast as 5MPH" is the car's own account.
- Speeds are pre-crash speed, not impact speed; delta-v unknown.
- 36 reports is a small sample; the injury-free streak proves little statistically.
- Tampa July 21 launch is new-market data; August's geography is confounded by expansion.

## Strongest counterargument
"36 crashes and counting is a real number. Human drivers in these same geofences at the same hours might do better, and we can't know because nobody publishes robotaxi mileage. The 0-injury streak survives because these cars mostly operate at speeds where injuries are impossible — that proves geofencing discipline, not driving skill. And the Cybercab hit a pothole: the vehicle with no steering wheel can't even see a puddle."

## Actionable insights
- Before citing any "robotaxi crash count" headline, check three things in the SGO data: pre-crash speed, whether the report is property-damage-only under the June 2025 threshold, and whether a fleet-size/mileage denominator exists (it won't — NHTSA doesn't collect one).
- For riders: low-speed, property-damage crashes dominate robotaxi federal filings; injury risk in current robotaxi service sits with other road users' behavior (running stop signs, rear-ending stopped cars), not ADS speed events.
- Cybercab watch: Dallas launch next month (November 2026) + NHTSA self-certification audit response due Oct 30 — the audit answer matters more than the pothole.
