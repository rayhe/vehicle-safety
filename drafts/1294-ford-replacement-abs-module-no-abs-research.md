# Research: #1294 — Ford's replacement ABS modules that cannot do ABS

**Slug:** 1294-ford-replacement-abs-module-no-abs
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — car-buying guidance beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date researched:** 2026-10-27

## Kill test
PASS. This is not a recall of a car. It is a recall of the repair. Ford's
NHTSA equipment campaign 26E073 (Ford 26S76, report received October 20,
2026) covers 3,376 replacement ABS service modules, built April 8 to July
10, 2026, that may be programmed with the wrong software, so the module
cannot perform anti-lock braking at all. The owner did everything right:
their ABS failed, a dealer installed a genuine Ford service part, and the
part itself was defective. Consequence per the NHTSA record: loss of ABS
plus degraded brake balance, meaning longer stopping distances. No prior
Crash Report draft covers 26E073, 26S76, or these part numbers (checked
2026-10-27: greps for 26E073, service-module ABS, and the five part
numbers returned zero prior drafts). Killed angles this run: IIHS Oct 8
commercial-vehicle ratings (covered #1110, #1121, others), IIHS Oct 24
Kicks/Q3 round (covered #1278), Ford F-Series cluster 26V653 (covered
#1223), Hyundai 26V692/26V693 (covered #1291), Cummins L9 fuel-hose
26V699 Autocar follow-on (parent equipment recall 26E066 covered #1046),
Toyota Sienna seat-back 26V690 (covered #1292), Lexus NX display 26V687
(covered #1277), Audi Q4 fire 26V698 (covered #1268), Jaguar camera
(covered #1251), Ram 2500 ESC 26V651 (covered #1228, #1262), VW ID.4
battery 26V630 (covered #1194), Rosenbauer rim weld 26V640 (covered
#1162), Ram ProMaster steering 26V621 (covered #1148), Ford F-150 seat
bolts (covered #1234, #1265), Porsche center-lock 26V682 (covered
#1245).

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- **NHTSA equipment campaign 26E073 / Ford 26S76** (report received
  October 20, 2026; recall type: Equipment, not vehicle): Ford is
  recalling certain ABS modules with part numbers EB5Z-2C219-B,
  DB5Z-2C219-B, CB5Z-2C219-C, DL1Z-2C219-A, or DT4Z-2C219-B. "The
  replacement ABS module may be programmed with the incorrect software,
  preventing the ABS from functioning as intended." (NHTSA record via
  OEMDTC.)
- **Population and build window:** 3,376 modules, manufactured April 8,
  2026 to July 10, 2026. Component description: "ABS service module for
  certain Ford Expedition, Explorer, and Edge vehicles and Lincoln
  Navigator and MKX vehicles." (NHTSA record via OEMDTC.)
- **Consequence:** "An incorrectly programmed ABS may cause a loss of
  ABS and degrade brake balance, increasing the distance needed to stop
  and the risk of a crash." Base brakes are not described as failed;
  the loss is anti-lock function plus brake balance. No do-not-drive
  advisory, no park-outside advisory. (NHTSA record via OEMDTC.)
- **Remedy and contacts:** "Dealers will inspect and replace the ABS
  module as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters are
  expected to be mailed November 30, 2026. Owners may contact Ford
  customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is
  26S76." (NHTSA record via OEMDTC.)
- **Root-cause reporting:** The BRAKE Report (Oct 26, 2026) describes
  the same campaign as 3,376 replacement ABS service modules for older
  Ford and Lincoln SUVs, after "a supplier chipset change left modules
  unable to accept the correct software, disabling ABS."
- **Why this is a parts recall, not a car recall:** because the
  defective item is a service part, the affected owners are the people
  who had an ABS module replaced (or whose dealer stocked these part
  numbers) during the April-July 2026 build window — not every owner
  of an Expedition, Explorer, Edge, Navigator, or MKX. That distinction
  is the consumer trap: a VIN lookup for the vehicle may not flag a
  parts recall the way it flags a vehicle recall, so the repair invoice
  is the document that matters.

## Original contribution
1. **The repair is the defect:** every other recall in this week's
   wave is a factory defect. This one only exists because someone
   already paid to fix their brakes.
2. **The identification gap:** vehicle recalls are found by VIN.
   Equipment recalls are found by part number and service history,
   which most owners never see unless they kept the invoice.
3. **The consequence is quiet:** no warning that says "your ABS is
   gone" is stated in the record; a driver finds out in a panic stop,
   when wheels lock and stopping distance grows, instead of in the
   driveway.
