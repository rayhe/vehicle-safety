# Research: #1128 — Ford Ranger 26V605 Counterfeit Capacitor Black Screen
Journalist: Rex Driverton | Date: 2026-10-10

## The facts
- **NHTSA campaign 26V605** (Ford 26C42), filed Sept 22, 2026; 11,405 units: 2026 Ford Ranger with 10.1-inch center stack screen.
- Failure mode: a cracked multi-layer ceramic capacitor (MLCC, part GRM32ER71H106KA12L) on the display PCBA shorts, blows the display fuse, screen goes blank. SYNC 4 black = no rearview camera image in reverse.
- These vehicles fail FMVSS 111 "Rear Visibility" (S5.5.1, S5.5.3, S5.5.6). A radio screen is now legally mandated safety equipment.
- Component analysis: affected MLCCs show out-of-spec dissipation factor; Ford calls them **suspected counterfeit components** that fail to meet engineering and material specs.
- Supplier named in the Part 573: Suzhou Dongshan Display Inc., Suzhou, China (Tier 2).
- Chronology: Aug 14, 2026 — Ford's Pre-Investigation Review opened after early warranty claims flagged by the Michigan Assembly Plant vehicle team (black screens at low time-in-service, low mileage). Aug 20 — Critical Concern Review Group reviewed teardown of six warranty returns, confirmed cracked-capacitor short, identified one bad batch.
- 46 additional service replacement screens covered under 26E065000 (same root cause, parts shipped as replacements).
- Remedy: still under development as of filing. Interim owner letters mailed starting Oct 5, 2026. VINs searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls since Sept 25, 2026. No crashes or injuries cited in the filings found.
- Ford customer service: 1-866-436-7332.

## The angle (kill test: PASS)
A two-cent capacitor, possibly counterfeit, slipped into Ford's display supply chain and took down a federally mandated safety system on 11,405 brand-new trucks. This is not a bulb story and not a software story. It is a supply-chain integrity story: the part that died is so cheap and so ubiquitous that nobody audits it until 11,405 backup cameras go dark. Novel because prior coverage (Autoblog, oemdtc) treated it as a routine screen recall; nobody connected the FMVSS 111 angle (the infotainment screen IS the safety device now) with the counterfeit-electronics angle (the most dangerous part in the truck may be the cheapest).

## Original contribution
- Connecting three facts nobody has put in one place: (a) FMVSS 111 makes the SYNC screen a compliance device, (b) the counterfeit MLCC is a sub-$0.10 commodity part, (c) Ford caught it through warranty analytics at low mileage before any crash report. Plus the arithmetic of exposure: back-over crashes kill ~200+ people/year in the US (NHTSA backover stats), and a blank screen removes the single intervention FMVSS 111 was written to provide.

## Limitations
- No crashes or injuries are attributed to this defect in the filings I could verify; the risk is regulatory and probabilistic, not demonstrated.
- "Suspected counterfeit" is Ford's language in a Part 573; the counterfeit determination is preliminary and could be a batch quality escape rather than deliberate fraud.
- Backover crash baseline (~200+ deaths/year nationally per NHTSA) is not Ranger-specific; I do not have Ranger-attributable backover counts.
- Remedy status may have changed since the Sept 22 filing; verify before ship.

## Strongest counterargument
Ford's system worked: warranty analytics flagged the trend within weeks of production, the CCRG confirmed root cause in six days, and the recall went out with zero reported crashes. Counterfeit components are a supplier-crime problem, not a Ford engineering problem. And every recalled truck still has mirrors and a rear window; the camera is a supplement, not the driver's only eyes. If the remedy lands quickly, this is a success story with a scary headline.

## Actionable takeaway
Own a 2026 Ranger? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now (searchable since Sept 25); interim letters mailed Oct 5. There is no fix yet, so until the remedy lands, treat reverse like it's 1995: mirrors, windows, turn your head. Everyone else: this is why you check the screen every time you start the car, not just the tires.
