Dodge Has Recalled the Durango’s Rear Spoiler Three Times. The Fix Keeps Failing. They Still Don’t Have a New One.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
August 25, 2026

October 2021: Stellantis recalls 4,837 Dodge Durangos because the rear spoiler sits too flush against the roof, gets whacked every time someone opens the liftgate, and eventually rips free at highway speed.[1] Dealers inspect, dealers replace, and Stellantis closes the file as if the problem is solved.

3
Recalls for the same defect on the same vehicle in five years

March 2023: Stellantis recalls 139,019 Durangos for the exact same defect, covering 2021 through 2023 model years built between October 2020 and February 2023.[2] Fifty-six warranty claims had piled up. Same diagnosis. Same remedy. Inspect the spoiler, replace if damaged, send the customer home.

August 2026: Stellantis recalls 74,578 Durangos for the exact same defect a third time, under NHTSA campaign 26V528.[3] But something is different now. After 116 warranty claims, 8 customer assistance records, and 37 service reports accumulated between May 2022 and June 2026, many on vehicles that had already been through the prior recall remedy, Stellantis opened an internal investigation in March 2026 and arrived at a conclusion that should make every affected owner livid: the fix they had been deploying for five years does not actually fix the problem, and a replacement remedy is still under development.

Not "fix coming soon," not "fix in testing," but remedy under development, which is corporate for admitting they issued a federal safety recall and told 74,578 owners to sit tight while the engineering team figures out what the engineering team should have figured out in 2021.

The root cause has not changed in five years. Durangos rolling off the Jefferson North Assembly Plant line get their rear spoilers installed in what NHTSA filings describe as an "over-flush" position relative to the roof. Every time the liftgate cycles, the spoiler's forward edge contacts the roof's trailing edge. Do that a few hundred times and the mounting loosens. Do it a few hundred more and you're watching your spoiler cartwheel across I-95 in your rearview mirror, becoming road debris for whoever is drafting behind your three-row family hauler. Stellantis's 2020 redesign of the spoiler, which introduced revised part numbers 7DY53TZZAB and 7DY56TZZAB, was supposed to solve the tolerance mismatch.[4] It didn't. The revised parts had the same assembly problem.

Add up the three campaigns and you get 218,434 recall actions for one plastic panel on one vehicle over five years. Stellantis claims only 0.8% of the recalled population actually has the defect, which at face value means roughly 596 vehicles in the latest batch.[3] That math deserves scrutiny. If the defect rate is truly that low, why did 116 warranty claims and 37 additional service records pile up after the second recall was supposed to have resolved everything? If the prior fix worked on 99.2% of vehicles, what changed for the ones where it didn't?

Stellantis reports zero crashes and zero injuries across all three campaigns, and that's worth noting. A flying spoiler is a road debris hazard for trailing vehicles, not typically a direct collision risk for the Durango's own occupants. But NHTSA classifies detaching body panels as safety defects for a reason: a chunk of painted plastic landing on a motorcyclist's windscreen at 70 mph is not a cosmetic issue. The strongest case in Stellantis's favor is also the most uncomfortable one for the company to make aloud: the defect hasn't hurt anyone yet, so the urgency hasn't matched the frequency.

If you own a 2021 through 2023 Durango built between July 2020 and March 2023, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls immediately, because VIN lookups went live August 20. Listen for the spoiler striking the roof when you open or close the liftgate and for rattling at speed. If you had the spoiler replaced under either of the two prior recalls, you are not in the clear; this campaign specifically targets vehicles where the earlier remedy may have failed. Owner notification letters start going out September 9. When they arrive, your instructions will be to bring the vehicle in for a repair that does not exist yet, and anyone who wants to express their feelings about that in the meantime can reach Stellantis customer service at 1-800-853-1403.
