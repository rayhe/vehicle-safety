# Research Notes — #952: Ford Edge Coolant Intrusion Defect Petition DP26008

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**News peg:** NHTSA opened Defect Petition DP26008 on September 3, 2026 (petition received August 5, 2026).

## The facts

- NHTSA ODI opened **Defect Petition DP26008** on 09/03/2026 re: **2015–2018 Ford Edge, 2.0L EcoBoost**, est. population **499,603 vehicles**.
- Subject: "Loss of Motive Power" — engine coolant intrusion into cylinders → sudden significant power loss + "Engine Coolant Over Temperature" warning; after cool-down, rough running + check-engine light for cylinder misfire.
- ODI failure-report summary at opening: **1,563 incidents, 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injuries, 0 fatalities**. Investigator David Engel; reviewer Peter Kivett; approver Tanya Topka. Petition reviewable at NHTSA.gov under ODI number 11754989.
- NHTSA generally completes defect-petition review within ~4 months → grant/deny decision expected roughly January 2027. Opening a petition is NOT a defect finding and NOT a recall.

## Ford already knew (the paper trail)

- **Ford TSB 19-2346 (Dec 20, 2019, superseding 19-2208; later superseded by 22-2133, Apr 2022):** covers 2015–2018 Edge + 2017–2019 Fusion/MKZ/Escape/MKC with 2.0L EcoBoost exhibiting low coolant, white exhaust smoke, rough running, DTCs P0300/P0301–P0304/P0316/P0217/P1285/P1299. Verdict in Ford's own words: "THIS MAY BE DUE TO COOLANT INTRUSION INTO THE CYLINDER. TO CORRECT THE CONDITION, FOLLOW THE SERVICE PROCEDURE STEPS TO **REPLACE THE LONG BLOCK ENGINE ASSEMBLY**."
- Technical root cause (open-deck cooling design): premature head-gasket failure and cracks forming **between cylinders**; coolant seeps into combustion chambers → corrosion, misfires, overheating, dead engine.
- **Class action: Miller et al. v. Ford, E.D. Cal., Case No. 2:20-cv-01796-DAD-CKD** (consolidated from Reed, Lund, Miller suits). Covers 2013–2019 Escape, 2013–2019 Fusion, 2015–2018 Edge, 2016–2019 MKC, 2016–2019 MKZ with 1.5L/1.6L/2.0L EcoBoost. Alleges Ford knew of the defect before first sale, concealed it, and applied "superficial stopgap, 'Band-Aid' remedies such as installing coolant level sensors" instead of replacing the engine block. Alleges replacement engines are equally defective — owners pay thousands, then pay again. (A related 2.3L EcoBoost suit, Nelson v. Ford, had its nationwide class claim dismissed; keep it out of the article except as a footnote if needed.)

## The novel angle (Rex's paradox)

**Ford wrote the fix down in December 2019 and never recalled the cars.** The TSB is an internal confession with a parts number: the correct remedy is a whole new long block. Dealers got a step-by-step procedure; owners got nothing — unless they were inside warranty, they paid for Ford's design error themselves. Seven years later, a private citizen had to file a federal defect petition to get NHTSA to even *look*. The defect outlasted the warranties by the better part of a decade.

Second paradox: 1,563 documented incidents, zero crashes/fires/injuries in ODI's summary. The safety case rests on the physics — sudden motive-power loss at highway speed — not on a body count. NHTSA's standard says no complaint-count threshold is required; severity and frequency against population decide.

## Original calculations

- **Complaint density:** 1,563 ÷ 499,603 = **0.313%** — roughly **1 in 320** vehicles has a documented ODI incident. ODI complaints are a known fraction of field failures (owners must find NHTSA's portal, file, and describe the defect); the true failure rate is higher, unknowable from public data. State the 0.31% as a floor, not a rate.
- **Timeline math:** last affected model year (2018) rolled off ~8 years before the petition; Ford's TSB documenting the fix predates the petition by 6 years 8 months. The defect aged into the used-car market before the feds opened a file.

## What the article must NOT claim

- Do NOT say a recall was issued or is certain. The petition may be denied; most defect petitions historically are. Say "NHTSA is deciding whether to investigate further."
- Do NOT claim injuries/deaths from this defect — ODI lists zero.
- Do NOT present the class action's allegations as adjudicated facts — attribute them ("the lawsuit alleges").
- The TSB covers Fusion/Escape/MKC/MKZ too, but the petition is Edge-only. Keep the article Edge-centered; mention the sibling models as context.

## Kill test

Genuinely newsworthy? Yes: half a million SUVs, highway-speed power loss, a seven-year-old internal fix that never became a recall, and the rare procedural vehicle — a citizen petition — that can force the federal government's hand. Novel: the "Ford documented the confession in 2019" framing plus the 1-in-320 complaint-density floor. Passes.

## Actionable takeaways (for the draft)

- Own a 2015–2018 Edge 2.0L? Watch coolant level like a hawk; white exhaust smoke + rough idle + P030x misfire codes = the TSB symptom set; pressure-test + borescope before it strands you.
- File at NHTSA.gov or call 1-888-327-4236 if it happens to you — petitions live on complaint volume.
- Shopping used? A 2015–2018 Edge 2.0L with a replaced long block (post-2019 redesigned block) is arguably safer than one with the original engine — ask for the service records, specifically engine replacement history.
- Decision watch: NHTSA's grant/deny on DP26008 expected ~Jan 2027.
