Your Lexus Can Lose Its Oil Pressure Without Telling You. 68,356 NXs Just Got Recalled.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 1, 2026

Imagine every gauge on your dashboard going on strike at once. Toyota just recalled 68,356 model-year 2022 through 2024 Lexus NX crossovers because the instrument cluster combination meter may fail to display certain warnings or indicators, and a dashboard that cannot warn you is not a dashboard. It is a decoration.[1]

68,356
2022-2024 Lexus NX250, NX350, NX350 Hybrid, and NX450h+ crossovers recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V687000

Campaign 26V687000, filed under Toyota's internal number 26LA10, covers the NX250, NX350, NX350 Hybrid, and NX450h+.[1] The defect is a software fault in the combination meter, the digital cluster that shows your speed, fuel, and every telltale from the check-engine light to the low-oil-pressure warning. When it glitches, the warnings simply do not appear. Oil pressure can collapse, coolant temperature can climb, the charging system can quit, and the driver gets the one thing no safety engineer ever wants: silence.

Which is why this one is filed as a federal noncompliance, not a courtesy. FMVSS No. 101, "Controls and Displays," exists precisely because a telltale you cannot see is a safety system that does not exist.[2] A broken sensor lies to you once, about one thing. A broken display lies about everything, simultaneously, and the lie is invisible, because the thing that is broken is the thing that was supposed to tell you something was broken. Lexus built a single point of failure out of the instrument that monitors all the other points of failure, and software did the rest.

Here is the honest version of the risk. The recall summary we verified cites no crashes or injuries tied to the defect, and a missing telltale only hurts you if something else goes wrong at the same time, which is, admittedly, the entire premise of every warning light ever made. But owner letters do not go out until December 7, 2026, so if your NX is in the population you get five more weeks of driving a car whose dashboard may or may not be telling you the truth. Do not wait for the letter. Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, and if it matches, call Toyota at 1-800-331-4331 and reference 26LA10. The fix is a free combination meter software update at the dealer, no parts, no tow truck, just a reflash.[1]

Zoom out and the pattern is the actual story. Cars replaced a dozen dumb gauges with one brilliant screen, and now the screen is the recall. Backup cameras, speedometers, and now the entire warning system have all been taken down by software in the past year, fixed by updates instead of wrenches. That is progress, until the update is the thing that broke it.

What this story does not prove: NHTSA's campaign summary does not say which specific telltales are affected, only that "certain warnings or indicators" may fail to display, so we cannot rank which faults a driver might miss. Toyota has not published the root cause of the software fault or the failure rate across the 68,356-vehicle population, and the December 7 notification date means the data on owner uptake will not exist for months.
