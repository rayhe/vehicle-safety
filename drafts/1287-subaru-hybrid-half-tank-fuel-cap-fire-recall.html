Subaru's Hybrid SUVs Leak Gas From the Cap. The Prescription Is Half a Tank.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 26, 2026

Park a 2025 Forester Hybrid with a full tank in a warming garage, walk away, and come back to gasoline on the floor, because nothing broke while you were driving and nothing had to break at all. Under NHTSA recall 26V-106, roughly 70,000 Forester Hybrid and Crosstrek Hybrid SUVs can leak fuel straight out of the filler cap when a near-full tank heats up while parked, and the fire risk arrives with the car switched off and nobody in it.[1]

33
U.S. fuel-leak reports before Subaru filed, with zero fires and zero injuries

Heat does the work here, not impact, and that inversion is what makes this recall strange. Fuel expands as it warms, tank pressure climbs, and the rising fuel pushes up the filler neck until it finds the one seal that cannot hold it. Subaru's own letter to owners says it plainly: a parked car with a near-full tank, exposed to a significant rise in ambient temperature, may leak fuel from the filler cap, and a leak near an ignition source raises the risk of fire.[1] A gasket with an added O-ring is the entire fix, free, in about ten minutes.[2]

Eight months after Subaru told owners, the instruction still stands, and that is the part worth pausing on: a February recall with a March letter is easy to forget by October, when the car drives perfectly and the only symptom is a smell you might blame on a lawnmower. Anyone who never got the letter, or never booked the ten-minute gasket swap, is still under the same rule today: half a tank or less, and outside away from buildings if it goes above half.[1] For apartment and condo owners with no driveway, that second clause is the one that actually costs something.

Two cautions before anyone panics or shrugs: first, Subaru's own headcount for this recall moved three times across its filings, from 69,153 to 66,332, with secondary summaries citing 71,207, so treat "roughly 70,000" as a range and let the VIN decide coverage. Second, zero fires across 33 leak reports means the hazard is a leak that still needs a spark, not a car that bursts into flame on its own, and the ten-minute gasket is the system working, not a cover-up. But a defect that needs no crash and no key in the ignition is exactly the kind of quiet failure owners never notice until they smell it, which is why the boring advice matters: run the VIN under the hybrid nameplate, not the gas model, because the filing covers hybrids only.[3]

Three moves cover it, starting with a VIN check at nhtsa.gov/recalls under Forester Hybrid or Crosstrek Hybrid. Until the gasket is swapped, hold the tank at half or below, and park outside whenever it goes above half. Book the free fix through any Subaru dealer; Subaru customer service is 1-844-373-6614. Same month, Subaru also told just eight Crosstrek and Forester owners not to drive at all over a loose ball-joint nut, for scale: eight cars you cannot drive versus 70,000 cars you cannot fill up.[4]
