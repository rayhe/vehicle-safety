## Kill test
**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** In September, NHTSA's defect office sent GM a six-question information request in the L87 6.2L V8 investigation (EA26005) demanding every post-remedy failure record on a VIN-by-VIN basis — and it opened with a new number: **946 reports of engine failure** in vehicles that already had the recall repair, nearly double the 499 at the EA's opening seven weeks earlier. GM asked for more time on October 23; NHTSA granted a partial extension on October 25; the records are now due **November 13, 2026**.

**Challenge:** After 1,366 articles — and after #1025 already covered EA26005's opening — is this just a rehash? No. #1025 (Mia, August-era sources) was about the remedy's engineering: thicker oil as a fix for a crankshaft defect. This is about the *paper trail since then*: two new federal documents (Sept 15 IR letter, Oct 25 extension memo) that did not exist when #1025 shipped, a new complaint count (946, up from 499), a hard deadline (Nov 13, two weeks out), and GM's own quoted reason for the delay. Nothing in #1025 covers these documents. The news peg is fresh (documents posted Oct 23–25, 2026) and the deadline is 11 days from the dateline — timely in a way queue articles usually are not.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Verified facts (read directly from sources)

From gm-trucks.com (Zane Merva, Nov 1, 2026 — reporter read both docket documents page by page):
1. **Sept 15, 2026:** NHTSA ODI sent GM formal information request letter **EA26005-01**. Opening number: ODI has received **946 reports of L87 engine failure** in vehicles that already went through Recall 25V-274 or were built after its production window.
2. **Scope:** 2021–2026 Chevrolet Silverado 1500, Tahoe, Suburban; GMC Sierra 1500, Yukon, Yukon XL; Cadillac Escalade and Escalade ESV with an L87 built after March 1, 2021. Alleged defect: engine seizure due to internal engine component failure. Investigation covers ~997,743 vehicles.
3. **Six numbered requests:** (1) VIN-level production data — every subject vehicle, engine part number and design version at factory, build date, recall completion date, remedy type; (2) counts of every complaint, field report, crash, fire, property-damage claim, arbitration, lawsuit tied to the defect; (3) per-incident detail (VIN, mileage at failure, recall done, which remedy); (4) every underlying document; (5) every paid warranty claim with labor op numbers, DTCs, replacement part numbers, repeat repair dates, mileage at recall completion; (6) GM's own assessment: causal factors, failure mechanism, failure modes, safety risk, and what warning a driver gets before the engine lets go.
4. **Requests 1, 3, 5 pattern:** recall completion dates, remedy types, repeat repair records tied to individual VINs — ODI is building the dataset to answer whether trucks fail after the remedy and whether the remedy branch matters. Engine part number + design version fields test whether failures cluster in specific L87 versions.
5. **Teeth:** failure to respond exposes GM to civil penalties up to **$27,874 per violation per day**, ceiling just shy of $140 million.
6. **Oct 23, 2026:** GM requested more time. **Oct 25, 2026:** ODI posted a memo granting a *partial* extension. Request 1 (VIN-level production data) remained due Oct 30. Requests 2–6 (all field-data-built) now due **November 13, 2026**.
7. **GM's reason, verbatim per the memo:** "GM is still analyzing the field data due to the expanded interpretation of the Alleged Defect from the RQ to the EA." (When the probe was Recall Query RQ26001 in January the defect question was narrow; the August upgrade to Engineering Analysis widened the definition, and GM is still re-running its data against it.)
8. **Decision timeline:** ODI must digest a VIN-level dataset covering ~1M vehicles, cross-reference 946 failure reports against remedy types, and weigh GM's own account. Realistic decision on the remedy — including a possible recall expansion — lands in **2027**. An EA can end in a recall or close with no defect finding; nothing in these documents tips NHTSA's hand.
9. **Owner guidance (reporter's, from the docket's field list):** keep every repair order (recall completion date, remedy type, mileage, labor op numbers, part numbers); document oil changes if the truck got the 0W-40 remedy; warning signs: knocking/banging, check-engine light, hesitation, reduced propulsion, no-start; if the engine already failed after the recall repair, file a complaint at nhtsa.gov — the 946 reports are what pushed the investigation forward.

## The complaint-count trajectory (original contribution material)
- Jan 2026 (RQ26001): 36 owner complaints of post-remedy failures.
- Aug 20, 2026 (EA26005 opening): 499 ODI complaints.
- Sept 15, 2026 (IR letter): 946 reports.
That's a ~90% increase in seven weeks between the EA opening and the IR letter — while the vehicles were already under a federal investigation. The reporting rate is *accelerating under scrutiny*, not tapering.

## Novel angle / original contribution
1. **The acceleration arithmetic:** 36 → 499 → 946. Nearly doubling between Aug 20 and Sept 15 while under active federal review. The remedy is failing faster the harder the feds look.
2. **GM's own sentence is the story:** "still analyzing the field data due to the expanded interpretation of the Alleged Defect" — the manufacturer admits its internal analysis lags the regulator's widened question. The defect definition moved faster than GM's data team. For owners, that's the detail: the company still can't tell the government what the failure mechanism is, 18 months after the recall.
3. **The field list as a checklist:** requests 1/3/5 read like a VIN-level autopsy protocol — remedy branch vs failure outcome, engine design version vs failure cluster. This tells owners exactly which paperwork will matter if there's ever a remedy-recall.

## Limitations (must state in article)
- The 946 reports are **owner complaints to ODI, self-reported and unverified** — not 946 confirmed engine seizures; some may be unrelated failures.
- The IR letter and extension memo are **demands for data, not findings**; they establish what NHTSA wants to know, not what it has concluded.
- **No crash/injury counts** in these two documents (unlike the EA opening resume); don't import August's numbers as if they're in the new documents.
- GM's reason for the delay is quoted **from NHTSA's memo**, i.e., the agency's paraphrase of GM's position — not GM's own public statement.
- An Engineering Analysis routinely closes with no new recall; the Nov 13 deadline is a paperwork milestone, not a verdict date.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
946 complaints across roughly a million vehicles is under 0.1% — a small fraction of a huge population, and complaint counts inflate as press coverage tells owners the filing exists. Two-week extensions are routine in federal investigations; reading panic into a partial paperwork extension is over-reading. The "expanded interpretation of the Alleged Defect" is standard regulator caution — ODI widening the net before deciding, which is the process working, not GM stalling. And the widened definition cuts both ways: if the defect gets defined broadly enough, GM's existing data will look thin no matter how fast it analyzes it. NHTSA is asking questions; it has not found an answer, and it may never demand one.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- **Check your VIN** at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 25V-274 (GM campaign N252494000); this is an engine investigation, not a model investigation — a Silverado with the 5.3L L84 is not in it. Confirm the RPO **L87** on your glovebox sticker.
- **Keep the paperwork NHTSA just demanded from GM, for your own truck:** recall completion date, which remedy branch (oil change vs engine replacement), every repair order with labor op numbers and part numbers, dated oil-change receipts if you got the 0W-40 switch.
- **Know the warning signs** from GM's own filings: knocking or banging, check-engine light, hesitation, reduced propulsion, no-start. A knocking L87 gets towed, not nursed.
- **If your engine failed after the recall repair, file the complaint yourself** at nhtsa.gov — the 946 reports are the entire reason this EA exists, and post-remedy failures are exactly what request 2 asks GM to count.
- Nov 13 is GM's homework deadline, not a verdict. Expect the actual decision in 2027 — and keep records until it lands.
