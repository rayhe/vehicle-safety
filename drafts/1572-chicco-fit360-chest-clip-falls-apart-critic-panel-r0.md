## 6. Research Rigor — 8.0/10
The original contribution is real: the three-year lag, the false-latch second failure mode, and the quiet per-customer replacement economy are all assembled from primary sources and none of them appear in the existing coverage. But the timeline framing — the article's central contribution — contains a date conflation that overstates the case against ODI. The body says a parent "reported the exact same failure on October 2, 2023." October 2, 2023 is the incident date; complaint 11685775 was not filed with NHTSA until September 8, 2025. The agency's window for seeing that complaint was about fourteen months, not three years, and the "it took three years to collect them" line inherits the error. The pull-stat label ("first documented clip disassembly") is accurate; the body sentence is not. The piece also never says that only four of ODI's five reports are individually identifiable in the public file — the fifth is unaccounted for. Limitations are otherwise honest, and the FARS gap is stated explicitly. Fix the date sentence and the collection line, add the filing-date nuance, and this is a 9.0.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
The pull stat picks the right number: 3 years, not 5 reports, because the tension of the piece is the lag, not the count. The timeline is laid out in date order and every date traces to a cited complaint. Minor deduction: the article contains no computed figure beyond the quoted counts and the 3-year arithmetic; the $20-vs-$10 price comparison is the only ratio, and the filing-date correction (14 months vs 3 years) belongs in the text, not just the research file.
