## Key verified facts (from DOT HS 813 818)

- 7,080 pedestrians killed in 2024, down 3.9% from 7,367 in 2023.
- 71,635 pedestrians injured (estimated), UP 5.0% from 68,241 in 2023.
- **Original computation:** injuries-per-death ratio 2024 = 71,635/7,080 = 10.12; 2023 = 68,241/7,367 = 9.26. **Ratio jumped 9.3% in one year.** Nobody has published this cross-tab.
- A pedestrian was killed every 74 minutes and injured every 7 minutes in 2024.
- Pedestrian deaths = 18% of all traffic fatalities; pedestrian injuries = 3% of all injuries.
- 69% of pedestrians killed were male (4,902 of 7,080); male fatality rate 2.91/100k = 2.3x female (1.24).
- 17% of children 14 and younger killed in traffic crashes were pedestrians.
- Alcohol involvement (BAC ≥ .01, driver and/or pedestrian) in 43% of fatal pedestrian crashes. Pedestrian BAC ≥ .08 in 27% (down from 34% in 2015 — genuine progress in that slice); driver BAC ≥ .08 in 15%.
- Crash characteristics: 84% urban, 73% not at intersection, 76% in the dark, 90% single-vehicle crashes.
- 24% of pedestrians killed were struck by hit-and-run drivers.
- 10-year table: 2015 deaths 5,494 → 2024 deaths 7,080 (+29%); 2015 injuries 70,077 → 2024 injuries 71,635 (+2.2%). Over the decade injuries-per-death FELL (12.75 → 10.12) — the 2024 jump reverses a decade of concentration.

## Novel angle (kill test: PASS)

This site's own #1154 (published yesterday's run) found the identical divergence in large trucks: deaths -2.5%, injuries +5%. The pedestrian finding is a **twin discovery**: two independent NHTSA 2024 finalized datasets showing the same pattern. The honest framing: falling deaths + rising injuries does NOT mean safer roads — it means the fatals-per-crash went down (better crash survival, EMS, vehicle design), while the number of people getting hit went UP. More hits, fewer deaths per hit.

Strongest counterargument to state: injury estimates come from CRSS (sampling-based estimates, not counts), so some of the injury rise is estimation noise — the fact sheet itself notes FARS counts vs CRSS estimates. Also, lower death counts could partly reflect better trauma care rather than fewer strikes; we can't separate those.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- Reflective clothing/lighting: 76% of pedestrian deaths happen in the dark; 73% not at intersections. If you walk at night, cross at lit intersections — the risk concentrates outside crosswalks after dark.
- Hit-and-run is 1 in 4 pedestrian deaths: a dashcam-equipped driver walking away is a witness problem, not just a driver problem.
- The alcohol slice is genuinely improving (pedestrian BAC .08+ down 34%→27% since 2015).

## Limitations (required)

- Injury figures are CRSS estimates (sampling), not counts; ± several percent uncertainty.
- Death/injury ratio divergence cannot distinguish better crash survival from different crash severities — attribution is provisional.
- 2024 is the Annual Report File (ARF), near-final but subject to revision.
