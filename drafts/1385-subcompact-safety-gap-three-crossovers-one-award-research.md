## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** Three sub-$30,000 subcompact crossovers, same showroom
class, same IIHS lab, three different verdicts. The 2026 Hyundai Kona
earns Good in all three crash tests and takes Top Safety Pick+. The
2026 Toyota Corolla Cross earns Good, Acceptable, Good and takes
nothing. The 2026 Kia Seltos earns Good, Acceptable, Marginal and
takes nothing. The gap is not one bad car. It is three different
failures: the Toyota fails in the back seat, the Kia fails in the
side, and the Kia fails a second time in crash prevention, where it
is the only Poor in the whole comparison. A shopper cross-shopping
these three on price or looks would never see any of this. The lab
sees it all.

**Challenge:** is this genuinely new after 1,384 articles? Yes, four
ways. (1) No prior article runs this three-way cross-tab. The Kona
article (kona-cheapest-tsp-three-recalls, July 2026) is a recalls
thesis: cheapest TSP+ car recalled three times. This piece never
mentions those recalls; its thesis is comparative lab performance.
(2) The Corolla sedan article (corolla-rear-seat-two-fixes-still-
marginal, July 2026) is a different vehicle (Corolla sedan, FARS
fatality-rate thesis), not the Corolla Cross crossover. (3) The
Seltos article (#1203, salt-fire recall twice) is a recall thesis,
not an IIHS comparison. (4) The closest format precedent, #1354
(CX-5 vs CR-V compact SUV safety gap), is a different segment
(compact, not subcompact) and different models. The novel
contribution here is the cross-tab itself: of the nine crash-test
cells, Kona takes 3 Good, Corolla Cross 2 Good plus 1 Acceptable,
Seltos 1 Good plus 1 Acceptable plus 1 Marginal, and the failures
sit in three different tests, so there is no single fix that would
rescue both losers. The comparison itself is fresh: Autoblog
published it November 1, 2026, three days before this run.

## The cross-tab (this article's original analysis)

Crash tests, 2026 models, IIHS ratings as reported Nov 1, 2026:

| Model | Small overlap | Moderate overlap | Side | Award |
| Hyundai Kona | Good | Good | Good | Top Safety Pick+ |
| Toyota Corolla Cross | Good | Acceptable | Good | None |
| Kia Seltos | Good | Acceptable | Marginal | None |

Crash prevention, same models:

| Model | Vehicle-to-vehicle | Pedestrian | Headlights |
| Hyundai Kona | Good | Good | Acceptable |
| Toyota Corolla Cross | Not tested | Good | Acceptable |
| Kia Seltos | Poor | Acceptable | Acceptable or Poor (trim) |

Counting the grid: Kona sweeps all six rated cells at Good or
better (headlights Acceptable is the only non-Good, and it still
clears the award bar). Corolla Cross has one soft cell (moderate
overlap Acceptable) and one blank cell (vehicle-to-vehicle not
tested). Seltos has three problem cells across two categories
(moderate Acceptable, side Marginal, vehicle-to-vehicle Poor) plus
trim-dependent headlights. No single engineering fix rescues both
losers: Toyota needs rear restraints, Kia needs side structure AND
crash-prevention software/sensors.

## Limitations (carried into the article)

- IIHS ratings are controlled lab scenarios (40 mph frontal, 37 mph
  side rig), not real-world fatality rates. The 2026 Kona has no
  meaningful FARS crash history yet, so lab excellence is not yet
  confirmed by body counts.
- "Not tested" for Corolla Cross vehicle-to-vehicle prevention is
  missing data, not a failure. Do not score it as a fail.
- IIHS itself cautions that larger, heavier vehicles generally
  protect better than smaller ones, so a TSP+ subcompact is not
  automatically safer than a non-award midsize SUV.
- The Seltos verdict covers the outgoing generation only. The
  redesigned 2027 Seltos is unevaluated, and shoppers buying it are
  buying an untested car.
- Prices: only the Kona's $25,500 starting MSRP is sourced (IIHS,
  Mar 2026). No Cross or Seltos prices are claimed in this piece.

## Strongest counterargument (carried into the article)

A skeptic could fairly say a lab award is a weak reason to pick a
car: real crashes are not 40 mph barrier tests, weight still wins
in multi-vehicle crashes, and the Seltos is already replaced by a
redesigned model this comparison does not cover. Fair, and the
article concedes it. But the counterargument does not erase the
pattern: all three cars faced the same barriers at the same speeds,
and only one passed everything. When the test is identical and the
results are not, the difference is the car, not the test.

## Actionable takeaways (carried into the article)

- Shopping subcompact crossovers on safety: Kona, HR-V, and CX-30
  are the only mainstream subcompacts carrying TSP+ for 2026.
- Buying a Corolla Cross: the back seat is the weak point. Check
  rear belt fit for your actual passengers, especially smaller
  adults and kids in boosters.
- Buying an outgoing Seltos: know the side-impact and crash-
  prevention gaps before signing, and check whether the 2027
  redesign (unevaluated) changes the calculus.
- Every shopper: check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before
  purchase, since IIHS awards say nothing about recall status.
