## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Tarrant County is the Big Six county that cannot decide what it is. Per resident it is the second-safest big county in Texas (8.56 deaths per 100,000, only El Paso lower). Per crash it is the second-deadliest (6.11 fatal crashes per 1,000, only Travis higher). And in 2026 the split is widening in real time: deaths fell 6% in the first nine months while crashes and injuries both rose.

## Verified facts

### Tarrant County, 2025 (TxDOT Crashes and Injuries by County, CR-3 processed April 7, 2026)
- **186 fatal crashes, 190 deaths, 930 suspected-serious-injury crashes, 1,076 serious injuries, 30,433 total crashes.**
- **Fatal-crash rate: 186 / 30,433 = 6.11 per 1,000 crashes — second-highest of the Big Six** (Travis 8.53, Dallas 5.87, Harris 4.44, Bexar 3.89, El Paso 3.75).
- Deaths per 1,000 crashes: 190 / 30,433 = 6.24. Serious injuries per 1,000 crashes: 1,076 / 30,433 = 35.4.

### Tarrant County, 2025 (Reyes deadliest-counties study, CRIS export processed Sept 25, 2026)
- **191 deaths, population 2,230,708; 8.56 deaths per 100,000 — #5 of the Big Six, second-safest** (Travis 10.71, Dallas 10.54, Harris 10.32, Bexar 9.16, El Paso 8.22).
- Fort Worth city: 113 deaths in the Reyes table — 59.2% of the county total.
- The one-death wobble: Reyes counts 191 county deaths vs 190 in TxDOT's April county table; Fort Worth 113 vs 112 in the Cities table. Same pattern as #1441's Travis piece (146 vs 143). Rates here use the April county table consistently; the per-capita ranking uses the September export.

### The split-brain table (Big Six, 2025)
| County | Deaths/100k (rank) | Fatal crashes/1,000 (rank) |
|---|---|---|
| Travis | 10.71 (#1) | 8.53 (#1) |
| Dallas | 10.54 (#2) | 5.87 (#3) |
| Harris | 10.32 (#3) | 4.44 (#4) |
| Bexar | 9.16 (#4) | 3.89 (#5) |
| Tarrant | 8.56 (#5) | 6.11 (#2) |
| El Paso | 8.22 (#6) | 3.75 (#6) |
- Tarrant is the only Big Six county whose two ranks are four places apart. Every other county's ranks differ by at most one place. Travis is #1 on both; El Paso is #6 on both. Tarrant is the outlier by construction: few deaths per resident, but a crash here is 1.38x as likely to be fatal as one in Harris County and 1.57x as likely as one in Bexar.

### Fort Worth city, 2025 (TxDOT Cities and Towns, processed April 7, 2026)
- **109 fatal crashes, 112 deaths, 475 suspected-serious-injury crashes, 562 serious injuries, 15,680 total crashes — fatal-crash rate 6.95 per 1,000**, second-highest of any major Texas city after Austin (8.52), ahead of Dallas (6.69) and Houston (4.53).
- Fort Worth accounts for 109 of the county's 186 fatal crashes (58.6%) and 112 of its 190 deaths (58.9%) in the April table — the county's lethality IS the city's lethality, the same pattern #1441 found in Austin/Travis.
- Deaths per 1,000 crashes in Fort Worth: 112 / 15,680 = 7.14, vs 6.24 countywide — the city is deadlier per crash than the county around it.

### Arlington city, 2025 (same Cities table)
- **31 fatal crashes, 31 deaths, 188 SSI crashes, 210 serious injuries, 4,985 total crashes — 6.22 fatal crashes per 1,000.**
- Arlington holds 16.7% of the county's fatal crashes on 16.4% of its total crashes. It is not the problem and not the solution; it is the county average with a stadium district attached.

### The 2026 decoupling (Wooley Law Firm analyses of TxDOT CRIS)
- **First half 2026:** 16,398 crashes (+1.9% vs 16,100 in H1 2025), 8,128 injured (+4.4% vs 7,784), but **94 deaths vs 104 (-9.6%)** and serious injuries 511 vs 528. One death roughly every 46 hours, down from every 42.
- **January-September 2026:** 24,868 crashes (+0.8% vs 24,664), 12,495 injured (+3.7%), but **142 deaths vs 151 (-6.0%, -9)**. Crashes up, injuries up, deaths down — the three series are moving in different directions for the first time in the county's recent record.
- **Commercial vehicles cut the other way:** CMV crashes flat (1,770 vs 1,774) but CMV deaths rose 13 to 18 in the nine-month window, even as overall deaths fell. The improvement is entirely in passenger vehicles.
- **Fort Worth Jan-Sep 2026:** 12,754 crashes (51.3% of the county), up 3.5% vs 12,318. Arlington 3,801. I-35W remained the top roadway label with 1,787 crashes in nine months.

### The ten-year view (NHTSA FARS 2015-2024, via fatalcrashdata.com)
- Tarrant averaged **9.0 road deaths per 100,000 residents per year, 2015-2024 — below the Texas rate of 13.6** and 21st of the 46 U.S. counties averaging 1M+ residents, 14% above the median county (7.9).
- **2024: 205 deaths, 193 fatal crashes, 9.2 per 100,000** vs 13.3 statewide. Dallas County, at 12.0, had the highest ten-year rate of the six Texas million-plus counties; Tarrant sat in the middle of the Texas pack.
- The long view agrees with the 2025 per-capita ranking: Tarrant has been an average-to-safe county by population for a decade. The per-crash lethality is the new finding, not the per-capita safety.

### Fort Worth's pedestrian problem (Patterson Law Group, TxDOT CRIS 2021-2025)
- Fort Worth city, 2021-2025: **80,773 crashes, 603 deaths, 2,679 suspected serious injuries.**
- **Pedestrians were 2% of crashes and 26% of deaths: 1,426 pedestrian crashes, 157 deaths.** At intersections: 506 pedestrian crashes, 31 deaths.
- Worst intersection: Henderson St at Belknap/Weatherford, downtown edge — 197 crashes in five years, 73 with injuries, and 51 crashes in 2025 alone, the most at any Fort Worth intersection in a single year.
- Fort Worth motorcycle deaths, 2025: 19 inside city limits (TxDOT CRIS preliminary fatal injury summary, viewed Nov 2, 2026).

## Original contribution
Three firsts for this site: (1) the split-brain table — Tarrant is the only Big Six county whose per-capita rank (#5, second-safest) and per-crash rank (#2, second-deadliest) are four places apart, while every other county's ranks differ by at most one; (2) the Fort Worth/Arlington decomposition showing the county's lethality is the city's lethality (6.95 vs 6.11), with Arlington sitting almost exactly on the county average (6.22); (3) the 2026 decoupling — deaths down 6% in nine months while crashes rose 0.8% and injuries rose 3.7%, with commercial-vehicle deaths moving the opposite direction (13 to 18).

## Strongest counterargument
The per-crash lethality gap could be a reporting artifact: counties that under-report minor crashes inflate their fatal-per-1,000 rate, and Tarrant's denominator (30,433) is far smaller than Harris's (107,472). But Tarrant's total-crash count is not small in absolute terms, and its rate lead over Harris (6.11 vs 4.44, a 38% gap) is too large for plausible under-reporting of fender-benders to explain — and the same artifact would have to leave the per-capita ranking untouched, which it does, since population is not a crash report. The sharper challenge is the 2026 decoupling: nine deaths is a small absolute number, and a single bad quarter can erase it. The honest reading is that Tarrant's 2026 improvement is real in the public record but unproven as a trend; the next two quarters decide whether it is a turnaround or a lucky draw. Same caveat #1433 applied to Denton's surge in the other direction.

## Limitations
- Counts come from TxDOT's published county and cities reports (CR-3 processed April 7, 2026), a law-firm compilation of the CRIS statewide export (processed Sept 25, 2026), and law-firm analyses of CRIS query exports for 2026 — not from a direct TxDOT query by this newsroom. Processing dates differ; Tarrant deaths read 191 (Reyes, Sept) vs 190 (TxDOT county table, April); Fort Worth deaths read 113 (Reyes) vs 112 (Cities table). The article uses the April county table for cross-county comparisons and discloses the alternates.
- Fatal crashes per 1,000 total crashes is a derived rate, not a TxDOT-published metric; it conflates exposure (VMT), speed, and reporting completeness.
- The 2026 figures are preliminary CRIS extracts and will be revised; the FARS Final File for 2025 lands in early 2027.
- The pedestrian share (2% of crashes, 26% of deaths) is a five-year Fort Worth city figure, not a Tarrant County figure; the article does not claim it holds countywide.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you live in Tarrant County: your odds of dying on the road per year are lower than in any other big Texas county except El Paso. Your odds of dying if you do crash are the second-highest. Buckle up like the crash is the dangerous part, because here it is.
- If you drive I-35W through Fort Worth: it logged 1,787 crashes in nine months of 2026, the most of any roadway label in the county. The interstate is where the county's lethality lives.
- Pedestrians in Fort Worth: you are 2% of the crashes and 26% of the deaths. Cross at intersections, and after dark assume you are invisible — the five-year data says the risk is not evenly spread, it is concentrated on people on foot.
- Commercial-vehicle deaths rose while total deaths fell. If you drive a truck or work around them in Tarrant County, the improvement everyone else is enjoying has not reached you yet.
