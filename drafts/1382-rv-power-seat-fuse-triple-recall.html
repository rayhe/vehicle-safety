Three RV Brands, One Bad Fuse: 4,463 Trailers Recalled for Power Seats That Could Catch Fire

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 3, 2026

Three unrelated RV manufacturers just recalled trailers for the identical defect, word for word: a power-seat cord fuse that may not provide adequate circuit protection. Alliance, Heartland, and Ember filed separately, weeks apart, and each filing describes the same part, the same failure, and the same consequence. Add the three populations together and you get 4,463 trailers where the device standing between a short circuit and a fire might not do its job.[1][2][3]

4,463
Trailers recalled across three brands for the same power-seat cord defect

Start with the biggest one, because it also gets warned last. Alliance recalled 3,686 Valor and Delta trailers from model years 2025 through 2027, which alone accounts for 82.6 percent of the combined total, and its owner letters are not expected until December 7. Heartland recalled 518 fifth wheels across its Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, and Road Warrior lines from 2022 through 2024, with letters expected November 30. Ember, the smallest of the three at 259 E-Series travel trailers from 2024, mails first, on November 23. Same defect, same free fix, three different mail dates spread over fourteen days.[2][3]

A power seat in a trailer is living-room furniture bolted to a vehicle frame, and its cord runs where people sleep, which is what turns a boring electrical spec into a fire risk. Fuses exist for exactly one moment, the short circuit nobody planned, and a fuse that cannot clear that fault is not a minor imperfection. It is the safety device failing at the only job it has. Dealers will replace the cord, or the harness and cord in Heartland's case, free of charge, and that remedy tells you something useful: this is a parts swap at a dealership, not a warning label, and not a fuse an owner can pull and replace in a driveway.[1][2]

What this analysis does not prove: public summaries of the three filings do not name a shared seat or cord supplier, so this article does not claim one common vendor behind all three recalls, and the identical wording may reflect similar commodity hardware described the same way rather than one bad batch. No crash, fire, injury, or complaint counts appear in the summaries reviewed, which means "no fires cited" is not the same as "no fires happened." Each filing also limits itself to trailers "equipped with certain power seating assemblies," so not every Valor, Delta, Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone, Gravity, Road Warrior, or E-Series trailer is affected, and only a VIN check settles an individual trailer. Model years differ across the three campaigns, so this is not one production run gone wrong. It is the same component description failing the same way across different years.

Strongest counterargument. Four thousand trailers is a rounding error against the millions of RVs on the road, the remedy is a free cord swap, and three small recalls with similar wording may be nothing more than three companies buying similar seats and writing similar defect descriptions. It is also possible the fuse rating was marginal rather than absent, meaning most cords would never see a short circuit in the first place. All fair, and all beside the point that matters most: a fire inside a sleeping space does not care how small the recall was, the protection device is the only thing standing between a short and that fire, and the largest group of owners waits until December 7 to be told.

Before you sign anything, or sleep in anything, check. If you own a 2025-2027 Alliance Valor or 2026-2027 Delta, a 2024 Heartland Torque, Bighorn, or Cyclone, a 2022-2023 Heartland Gravity or Road Warrior, or a 2024 Ember E-Series, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for the letter, then call the brand: Alliance at 1-574-218-7165, Heartland at 1-877-262-8032, Ember at 1-844-732-4204.[2][3][5] Until the cord is replaced, treat the power seat like a suspect appliance. Do not run it unattended. If the seat stops moving, smells hot, or trips anything, leave it unplugged and call the dealer. Shopping used? Ask whether the power-seat cord recall was actually performed, because a free remedy nobody claimed is still an open fire risk.
