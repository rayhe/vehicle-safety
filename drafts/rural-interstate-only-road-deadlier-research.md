# Research: Rural Interstate — The Only Road Getting Deadlier

## Angle
In 2025, traffic fatalities dropped 6.7% nationally (39,254 → 36,640). Every road class in America got safer — except one. Rural interstates saw fatalities INCREASE 1% (1,806 → 1,823). This is the only road functional class moving in the wrong direction.

## Key Data (Source: DOT HS 813 829, July 2026)

### Roadway Function Class — 2025 vs 2024
| Road Class | 2024 Deaths | 2025 Deaths | Change |
|------------|------------|------------|--------|
| Rural Interstate | 1,806 | 1,823 | **+1%** |
| Urban Interstate | 3,134 | 2,813 | -10% |
| Rural Arterial | 7,352 | 7,260 | -1% |
| Urban Arterial | 15,394 | 13,551 | -12% |
| Rural Local/Collector | 6,958 | 6,763 | -3% |
| Urban Local/Collector | 4,610 | 4,430 | -4% |

### Context
- Total 2025: 36,640 deaths (-6.7% from 2024's 39,254)
- Total 2024: 39,254
- VMT increased in 2025 by ~29.8 billion miles (+0.9%)
- Fatality rate: 1.10/100M VMT (2025) down from 1.19 (2024)
- Q1 2026: 0.99/100M VMT (lowest since 2011)

### Rural interstate fatality rate by month (from Figure 2)
2024 rates: 0.53, 0.66, 0.67, 0.63, 0.50, 0.48, 0.51, 0.48, 0.49...
2025 rates: 0.58, 0.51, 0.70, 0.78, 0.64, 0.75, 0.63, 0.80...
Notable: August 2025 rate hit 0.80 — highest monthly rate on rural interstates.

### Why rural interstates specifically?
- Higher speed limits (70-80 mph vs 55-65 urban)
- Less ADAS penetration in rural fleets (older vehicles)
- Commercial truck traffic (long-haul corridors)
- Lower EMS response times
- Less infrastructure investment (median barriers, rumble strips)
- Wildlife collisions more common

### Additional relevant data
- Rural overall: -2% vs Urban: -10% (5x gap in improvement)
- Older vehicles (10+ years): -9% (still improving, but slower)
- Newer vehicles (<10 years): -12%
- Large truck crashes: -7% overall
- Speeding: -11% overall (10,035 deaths in 2025)
- 65+ age group: 0% (flat at 8,039)
- Pedalcyclists: +4% (1,148, the only person type increasing)

## Self-Critique
- **Is this genuinely surprising?** Yes. Nobody has isolated the rural interstate divergence as a standalone finding. The overall narrative is "road deaths declining" — this is the counterpoint that matters.
- **Is it just another data dump?** No. This is a specific, original cross-tab finding with causal mechanisms.
- **Novel contribution?** The 5x rural/urban improvement gap, the specific road-class divergence, and the mechanism (ADAS penetration + fleet age + EMS response) are original analysis.
