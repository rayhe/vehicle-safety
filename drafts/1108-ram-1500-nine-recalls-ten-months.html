Nine Recalls in Ten Months: The 2026 Ram 1500 Is a Statistical Outlier

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 9, 2026

I ran the numbers, then I ran them again, and they didn't get better. Nine separate NHTSA recall campaigns name the 2026 Ram 1500, all filed between December 2025 and September 2026.[1] That is one federal safety action every five weeks for a truck that has been on sale barely a year, and the count is still climbing.

9
NHTSA recall campaigns naming the 2026 Ram 1500 in ten months

The inventory runs brake lights, headlight wiring, and an instrument cluster recalled twice, once for a screen that goes blank while driving. A rearview camera got recalled twice in three weeks, the second time for the same radio software implicated the first time.[4] Seat-belt anchors on 1.27 million trucks,[2] tire-pressure sensors that cannot sense pressure,[3] and on September 29, two recalls in a single day: side-airbag connectors that may not be secured, plus the aforementioned blind TPMS.[1]

Now the part that broke my spreadsheet. Our FARS data for 2014 through 2023 puts the Ram 1500 at 0.13 deaths per 100 million miles of travel, the lowest rate of any model with more than 200 deaths.[5] Next-closest is the Toyota RAV4 at 0.19, nearly fifty percent higher. When a Ram 1500 is involved in a fatal crash, its occupants walk away far more often than the occupants of a Chevy Cavalier, whose death rate per crash is two and a half times worse. So the truck that rarely kills anyone cannot stop being recalled, and both facts are true at the same time.

Recall counts do not measure danger. They measure manufacturing chaos, and to be fair, they also measure a company catching its own problems before the bodies pile up. FCA filed the seat-belt campaign voluntarily, and across all nine, the FARS numbers suggest the defects are being caught before they turn into bodies. Nine recalls indict the assembly line, not the crash structure. A truck can be badly built and still be the safest thing on the road, which is a sentence I never expected to type.

If you own a 2026 Ram 1500, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today. Triage the nine by what kills you: the side-airbag connectors (Chrysler recall 94D, notification letters go out October 27) and the seat-belt anchors decide whether you survive a crash, so those go first. Brake lights matter too. The TPMS and the blank cluster display are annoyances, though a dead cluster also takes your warning lights with it. Ask the dealer to clear every open campaign in one visit, because at this rate there will be a tenth.
