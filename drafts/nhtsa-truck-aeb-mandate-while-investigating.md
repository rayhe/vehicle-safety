# Research Notes: NHTSA Is About to Mandate Truck AEB While Investigating Whether It Causes Crashes

## Angle
NHTSA opened an investigation into false AEB activations on Daimler heavy trucks in May 2023. In October 2025, they upgraded it to a full Engineering Analysis (EA25006). The investigation has found 347 reports, 24 crashes, 13 injuries, and ONE FATALITY linked to false AEB activations. The investigation remains OPEN.

Meanwhile, NHTSA and FMCSA are about to publish a joint supplemental NPRM — as early as August 2026 — to MANDATE AEB on all heavy trucks (Class 3-8, over 10,000 lbs). The original 2023 proposal called for compliance in 3-4 years.

The paradox: the same agency investigating whether the technology kills people is about to require every truck in America to use it.

## Self-Critique Gate
**Is this genuinely surprising after 687 articles?** YES. We have zero articles on truck AEB. We've covered car AEB extensively (phantom braking, AEB lawsuits, pedestrian AEB gaps) but never applied it to heavy trucks. The regulatory paradox — investigating AND mandating simultaneously — is genuinely novel. The one-fatality-from-false-activation data point is devastating.

**Kill test: Is this genuinely newsworthy?** YES. A commercial truck slamming its brakes on a highway at 70 MPH with no obstacle is categorically different from a Tesla phantom-braking at 65. A Freightliner Cascadia weighs 80,000 lbs loaded. A full emergency stop from highway speed can be unsurvivable for following traffic.

## Key Data Points
- 347 false activation reports in Daimler trucks
- 24 crashes caused by AEB activating when it shouldn't have
- 13 injuries from AEB-caused crashes
- 1 death from AEB doing exactly what it wasn't supposed to do
- 250,000 trucks under investigation
- ~1.08 million Class 7-8 trucks on US roads (FMCSA data)
- AEB mandate would cover ALL trucks >10,000 lbs

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing NHTSA's own investigation data (EA25006) with its simultaneously active rulemaking. Nobody has asked: how can you mandate technology you're actively investigating for causing a fatality? The one AEB fatality in trucks isn't a random bug — it's a systemic issue with radar+camera sensor fusion misidentifying objects (overpasses, road signs, metal plates) as obstacles.

## Limitations
- The investigation covers only Daimler trucks (Freightliner/Western Star). Other manufacturers' AEB systems may perform differently.
- The 347 reports likely undercount incidents — many truck drivers may not report false activations that don't result in crashes.
- The one fatality has not been conclusively attributed to false AEB; the engineering analysis is still determining causation.
- The revised NPRM may include updated test procedures that address false activation concerns.
- AEB in heavy trucks unquestionably PREVENTS more crashes than it causes — the question is whether the mandate should wait until the investigation concludes.

## Strongest Counterargument
AEB's net safety benefit is overwhelmingly positive. IIHS estimates that AEB reduces front-to-rear crashes with injuries by 56%. Even with occasional false activations, the system prevents far more crashes than it causes. Delaying the mandate to "perfect" the technology means more truck-involved fatalities in the meantime. The 24 crashes from false activations are dwarfed by the thousands of rear-end crashes AEB would prevent. A mandate with performance standards (including false activation testing) could actually ACCELERATE improvements in the technology by forcing manufacturers to fix their systems.
