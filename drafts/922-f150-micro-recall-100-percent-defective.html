37 Trucks, 100% Defective: The Recall So Small Ford Named Every Victim

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 17, 2026

Everyone covered the big one. On September 9, Ford recalled 223,472 F-150s over fuel tanks that can fall off, which is dramatic enough to drown out anything else. But buried in the same filing were two recalls so small that Ford's own Part 573 report could account for every single truck, and one of them carries a number you almost never see in recall paperwork: an estimated defect rate of 100 percent.[1]

100%
Estimated defect rate in Ford recall 26S63: 37 out of 37 trucks got the wrong rear axle bearings.

First up: micro-recall 26S63, NHTSA campaign 26V576. Thirty-seven 2026 F-150s built on May 27 and 28, all with the Max Tow package, left the factory with 8.8-inch rear axle tail bearings where 9.75-inch bearings were specified. An undersized bearing in a max-tow axle is a time bomb with a polite demeanor: it can seize after extended service, lock the rear wheels, separate the driveshaft, or let the truck roll away in the driveway if the parking brake is not set. A worker at Ford's Sterling Axle Plant spotted the mismatch on May 26. Dearborn issued a stop-ship on June 3 for 38 vehicles, and 37 had already escaped to customers.[1][4]

Then there is the Raptor recall, 26S66, seventeen trucks. On those seventeen trucks, the rear Panhard rod fasteners may not have been tightened to spec because the error-proofing device, the machine whose entire job is verifying that torque was applied, was in bypass mode. Read that again, because the anti-mistake machine was turned off while the mistakes were being made. Ford found out because of a February warranty claim about a rattle, which is to say the first QA inspector on this line was a customer with sensitive ears.[3]

For perspective on the week Ford was having: before this wave, its 2026 recall tally stood at 71 campaigns, versus 33 for Chrysler, 23 for Toyota, and 22 for GM.[7] And the same Sept 9 batch contained the fuel-tank recall, where assembly-line cameras meant to verify strap installation were, per the NHTSA report, "not in continuous use."[2] Two different automated quality layers, one truck line, one week. Cameras off, torque checker bypassed. Somewhere in Dearborn, a meeting about this must have been genuinely uncomfortable.

Here is the case for the defense, and it deserves a fair hearing: this is the system working. A line worker caught the bearing swap; two warranty claims caught the Raptor bolts. Dearborn's stop-ship caught 38 of 38 before they could all vanish into the wild. Nobody crashed, nobody got hurt, and that part genuinely matters. And a company that files 71 campaigns is, at minimum, a company that is looking for problems, which is more than you can say for a company that files 22 and hopes for the best. Prevention had a bad week; detection, on balance, did its job.[2]

What to do, since the letters go out September 21. If you own a 2026 F-150 with the Max Tow package built May 27 or 28, or a 2026 Raptor built between late December and late January, do not wait for the mail. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today; both campaigns were searchable by September 14.[8] And if the rear end of your truck starts making new noises, the Part 573 report says the bearing failure announces itself with increasing noise as the condition worsens. A howling axle is not a personality quirk. It is a recall symptom wearing a costume.

What this did not prove

No crashes or injuries have been reported for either micro-recall, and no bearing has been reported to have actually seized in the field. That 100% figure is Ford's estimate over a population it defined by tracing plant records to specific VINs, not a measured field failure rate. Fifty-four trucks combined is too small for any fleet-wide risk math. Ford's Part 573 report for the Raptor recall (26S66) was not in the public NHTSA document index at the time of writing, so the bypass-mode detail rests on Carscoops' and Autoblog's reading of Ford's filing; the claim about Ford's traceability of the axles likewise takes Ford's filing at face value. The fourth recall in the wave, a seat-belt reminder issue, was not detailed enough in public reporting to include.
