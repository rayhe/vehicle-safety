# Research: Urban Arterials Shed 1,843 Deaths — No Infrastructure Changed

## Angle
Urban arterials (stroads, suburban boulevards, multi-lane surface roads) are America's deadliest road category. In 2025, they dropped 12% — the largest absolute decline of any road subcategory (1,843 fewer deaths). No new lanes were built. No speed limits changed. Technology is saving people FROM bad road design.

## Key Data Points
- Urban arterials account for 37% of all traffic deaths (13,551 / 36,640)
- Urban arterials produced 70.5% of the entire national decline
- The 12% decline is 1.8× the national average decline of 6.7%
- Rural interstates were the ONLY road category that increased
- Vehicle tech context: Newer vehicles (<10yr) saw -12% decline; AEB, FCW, LDW primarily effective at urban arterial speeds (35-55 mph)
- Speeding-related: -11% nationally — speeding is more common on arterials
- Roadway departure: -9% — less relevant to arterials (on-roadway crashes more common)

## Why This Matters
70% of America's safety improvement in 2025 came from ONE road type. Not from better roads. Not from lower speed limits. From vehicles overriding bad road design. The roads didn't change. The cars did.
