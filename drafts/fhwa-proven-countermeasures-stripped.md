# Research: FHWA Strips 5 Proven Safety Countermeasures From Its Own List

## Angle
The Federal Highway Administration quietly removed 5 countermeasures from its 28-item "Proven Safety Countermeasures" list in July 2026, after Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called bike lanes "DEI bike lanes" on X (July 7). Two days later (July 9), an internal FHWA email directed staff to remove the items as an "urgent request from leadership." The removals were not publicly announced.

## The 5 Removed Countermeasures
1. **Bicycle lanes** — while cyclist deaths hit a 40-year high of 1,148 in 2025 (+4% YoY)
2. **Speed safety cameras** — speed-related fatalities: 10,035 in 2025 (29% of all traffic deaths)
3. **Variable speed limits** — work zones, school zones
4. **Road diets (road reconfiguration)** — reducing travel lanes to add turn lanes and bike facilities; documented 19-47% crash reduction
5. **Speed limits appropriate for vulnerable road users** — 62% of cyclist deaths and 65% of pedestrian deaths occur on roads with 40+ mph speed limits

## Key Data Points (Original Analysis)

### Deaths in categories the removed countermeasures protect against:
- Speed-related fatalities 2025: ~10,035 (11% decline from 2024 — but still 29% of all deaths)
- Cyclist fatalities 2025: 1,148 (40-year high, up from 1,105 in 2022)
- Cyclist fatalities trend: 623 (2010 low) → 1,148 (2025) = +84% increase
- Pedestrian fatalities remain elevated (exact 2025 figure pending, but 2022 was a 40-year high)
- Total traffic deaths 2025: 36,640 (down 6.7% from 39,254 in 2024)

### The divergence:
- Overall deaths: DOWN 6.7% year-over-year
- Cyclist deaths: UP 4% year-over-year to 40-year high
- Policy response: Remove the countermeasures that protect cyclists

### IIHS data on cyclist deaths by helmet use (2024):
- 68% no helmet (727 deaths)
- 13% helmet use (141 deaths)
- 19% unknown (207 deaths)
- 18% of killed cyclists age 16+ had BAC ≥ 0.08%

### Speed camera effectiveness (from IIHS):
- Speed cameras reduce fatal and injury crashes by 20-54% depending on implementation
- Removed from "proven" list despite FHWA's own data showing effectiveness

### Road diet effectiveness:
- FHWA's own research (before removal): 19-47% reduction in crashes
- Converting 4-lane undivided roads to 3-lane with turn lane + bike facilities

## Kill Test
**Is this genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. A federal agency removing evidence-based safety measures from its own recommendations — while the specific death categories those measures address are at historic highs — is a legitimate data story. The original contribution is cross-referencing the removed countermeasures against the specific death categories they address: collectively, the five removed countermeasures are associated with crash types that kill ~12,000+ Americans per year (speed + cyclist + pedestrian on high-speed roads).

**Novel angle after 690+ articles?** Yes. We've covered cyclist deaths and the divergence from overall trends, but never the policy machinery — a federal agency delisting its own proven solutions. The parallel between "cyclist deaths at 40-year high" and "bike lanes removed from proven countermeasures list" is the kind of paradox The Crash Report lives for.
