America's Safest SUVs Just Got Recalled for a Bolt That Can Rust Off

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 18, 2026

The numbers don't lie, but they do occasionally smirk. Volkswagen announced today that it is recalling 208,724 vehicles because a single bolt fastening the steering rack to the subframe can corrode, snap, and leave you holding a steering wheel that no longer steers.[1] Affected: certain 2018 Tiguans, 2018-2019 Atlases, and 2019-2021 Audi Q3s, all of them held together at that joint by one bolt.

2,087
Estimated recalled vehicles actually carrying the defect (1% of 208,724)

Our FARS 2014-2023 data ranks the Atlas 9th-safest of 121 SUVs, and the Tiguan 26th.[5] Two of the statistically safest SUVs in America, both now recalled for a failure mode with a horror-movie name: sudden, total loss of steering control at speed.[3] Death-rate tables measure the past. Recalls measure the future.

Volkswagen estimates that 1% of the recalled vehicles actually carry the defect, which works out to roughly 2,087 cars with a bolt on a slow rust timer.[2] Those cars are five to eight years old now, and corrosion doesn't care about your odometer. It cares about your winters, and salt-belt drivers know exactly who they are.

This is the second time in thirteen months that a Volkswagen bolt has threatened the steering. Last August, NHTSA logged recall 25V526: a loose tie rod bolt on 2025 Tiguans, same consequence, loss of vehicle control.[4] That one covered 15 vehicles; this one covers 208,724. Somewhere in Wolfsburg, a fastener engineer is having a bad year.

While it was at it, VW recalled another 49,958 2025 Tiguans today for a body control module that can reset itself mid-drive, killing the ignition, the exterior lights, and the rearview camera.[2] NHTSA estimates 100% of those vehicles carry the defect. That is not a recall; it is a confession.

If you drive a 2018 Tiguan, a 2018-2019 Atlas, or a 2019-2021 Q3: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. VINs became searchable September 16, the fix is free, and the owner letters go out November 10.[3] If you drive a 2025 Tiguan, check yours twice. No part of the car is less allowed to be decorative than the steering wheel.

What this did not prove: the FARS rates describe 2014-2023 outcomes with VMT-estimated denominators, so treat the 9th and 26th SUV rankings as approximate rather than exact. VW's 1% defect estimate is the manufacturer's own figure, and NHTSA has not reported injuries tied to the bolt. Stated fairly, the counterargument runs like this: with 99% of the recalled fleet unaffected, the low death rates suggest steering-loss crashes are rare rather than lurking, so a recall on safe cars does not make the cars unsafe; it makes them recalled.
