Ram Is Recalling 265,512 ProMasters Because Water Got Into the Steering Wiring. Again.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
July 19, 2027

Let's talk about what happens when water meets copper inside a steering column.[1] NHTSA campaign 26V621000 covers 265,512 Ram ProMaster vans whose electric power steering wiring harness can admit water, corrode, and disable steering assist. The sentence NHTSA chose not to soften: drivers may receive no warning before it happens.

265,512
Ram ProMaster vans recalled, model years 2022–2026, for a wiring harness that lets water in and kills power steering with no warning.[1]

If this smells familiar, it should. In 2016, Chrysler recalled the ProMaster City under campaign 16V202000 (Chrysler S24) because a low-voltage harness connector by the driver seat was susceptible to water intrusion and corrosion.[3] Corrosion killed communication with the transmission control module, and the van shifted itself into neutral without warning the next time it stopped. Water plus wiring harness plus no warning: a decade ago, same van family.

Here is why "no warning" is the tell. Hydraulic power steering dies politely. It whines, it gets heavy in spots, it fights you and tells you why, all the way down. Electric power steering dies the way electronics die: a corroded connection goes from carrying the signal to not carrying it, and assist goes from all to nothing between one ignition cycle and the next. There is no in-between to warn you about, because the failure mode is digital. In this design, the entire safety margin was a connector seal, and the seal was rubber.

This one did not materialize from nowhere. NHTSA opened Preliminary Evaluation PE26002, and the PE grew into a recall.[1] That escalation is the agency's way of saying the complaint cluster was real enough to act on. Stellantis told the Detroit News it knows of one non-injury accident linked to the problem,[2] one crash across a quarter-million vans. The surveillance system caught it before the body count, which is the system working exactly as designed.

Now the fair counterargument, at full strength. Two recalls a decade apart do not prove Chrysler never learned a thing. The 2016 campaign hit the smaller ProMaster City's transmission connector; this one hits the full-size van's steering harness. Different platform branch, different supplier era, different engineers, different decade. Water intrusion is also the most boringly common electrical failure in the auto industry. And one non-injury crash across 265,512 vans is, by the numbers, a success story for early detection rather than an indictment. The decade-long pattern might be coincidence wearing a narrative.

The decade-long pattern might be coincidence wearing a narrative, but a manufacturer that shipped a van that put itself in neutral over a wet connector in 2016 and then shipped a van that drops its steering over a wet connector in 2026 does not get the benefit of the doubt from this desk. Seal the connector, then seal it again.

What to do: If you drive a 2022–2026 Ram ProMaster or a 2024–2026 ProMaster EV, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today; the recall is live and VIN-searchable now, and interim owner letters go out November 17. Watch for steering warning lights or sudden heaviness, especially in wet weather. If assist vanishes, pull over; a van with no assist is a wrestling match at parking-lot speed, and at highway speed the surprise is the dangerous part. Fleet managers: check every van, not just the loud ones. And if it happened to you, file with NHTSA at 1-888-327-4236. PE26002 became a recall because people filed.
