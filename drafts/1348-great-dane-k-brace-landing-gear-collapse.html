Great Dane's Trailers Started Collapsing Inside Its Own Factory

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

Let's talk about welds. Not the pretty kind you see on show-truck roll cages, but the load-bearing kind: the welds holding up tens of thousands of pounds of trailer while a forklift drives in and out of it, day after day, in weather nobody would stand around in.

Great Dane is recalling 6,810 semi-trailers because the welds on the landing-gear K-brace can fail and let the whole trailer drop. The campaign, NHTSA 26V708, was filed October 27 and covers 2026-2027 Champion dry vans and Everest reefers, and Great Dane estimates that 10 percent of them carry the defect.[1]

6,810
Trailers recalled; an estimated 1 in 10 has K-brace welds that can fail and collapse the landing gear

Here is the part that made me sit up: no fleet mechanic found this defect, and no roadside inspector did either. On September 30 and October 1, two K-brace assemblies failed inside Great Dane's own production facilities and collapsed on the factory floor. When your quality-control process involves the product falling over in the building where you built it, the process has opinions about your supplier.[1]

And the supplier is the whole story, because these K-braces were welded by a robot, which sounds reassuring until you read the failure analysis: Workers held the parts in the robotic fixture with manual clamps, and fixture variability let components shift mid-weld, producing off-seam weld beads, poor fusion, and parts that did not sit flush, which is exactly the kind of defect a visual inspection should catch and evidently did not until assemblies started falling over. A robot welder is only as repeatable as the clamp holding the workpiece, and somebody saved money on the clamp.[1]

Now the failure mode, because this is where it gets dangerous instead of merely embarrassing. A K-brace only matters when the trailer is parked without a tractor attached and without safety stands, which is exactly the configuration of a trailer being loaded or unloaded at a dock, with workers and a forklift inside it. A bump from another trailer backing into the next slot can be enough, and when the brace lets go, tens of thousands of pounds come down on whoever is underneath or inside.[1] Landing gear does not get a lot of engineering glory. It gets all of the load.

Timing worth noting: on October 23, four days before this recall was filed, NHTSA opened defect petition DP26012 evaluating landing-gear collapse on 2017 Great Dane reefers with Jost gear: different hardware from a different decade, but the same failure family. Great Dane is also fresh off a 69-trailer recall for coupler kingpins that could prevent proper coupling, which means it has been a rough month for things that hold trailers up and hook them together.[2][3]

What to do with this: fleet owners should check VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for the letter, which Great Dane says goes out by December 22. The fix is an inspected-and-replaced K-brace, free of charge, and the company line is 1-877-369-3493.[4] Dock rule, and this one is non-negotiable: never load or unload a dropped trailer without safety stands in place, recall or no recall, because a failing K-brace gives no dashboard warning before it lets go. If a trailer settles, shifts, or makes a sound it should not make, get out from under it and get everyone else out too. Welds are supposed to be boring. When they stop being boring, distance is the remedy.
