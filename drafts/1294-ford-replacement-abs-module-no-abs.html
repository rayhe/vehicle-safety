Ford Recalled 3,376 Replacement ABS Modules. The Replacement Could Not Do ABS.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

Your brakes already failed once. That is why a dealer installed a new ABS module, and why the replacement part sitting in your Expedition, Explorer, Edge, Navigator, or MKX matters more than most recall notices. Ford just recalled 3,376 of those replacement modules because they may carry the wrong software, which means the module cannot perform anti-lock braking at all. A repair already completed left the safety system it was supposed to restore silently inoperative.

3,376
Replacement ABS service modules recalled under NHTSA equipment campaign 26E073, built April 8 through July 10, 2026

NHTSA equipment campaign 26E073, Ford number 26S76, covers ABS service modules under five part numbers: EB5Z-2C219-B, DB5Z-2C219-B, CB5Z-2C219-C, DL1Z-2C219-A, and DT4Z-2C219-B.[1] An incorrectly programmed module can lose ABS function and degrade brake balance, which stretches stopping distance.[1] Nothing in the record says base braking itself is lost. What disappears is the anti-lock part and the balance, and a driver discovers that in a panic stop rather than in the driveway.

Vehicle recalls get found by VIN. Equipment recalls get found by part number and repair invoice, and most owners never see either unless they kept the paperwork. Ford expects to mail owner letters November 30, 2026, and dealers will inspect and swap the module free of charge.[1] If your SUV has had an ABS module replaced, your invoice is the recall notice until that letter arrives.

The BRAKE Report attributes the failure to a supplier chipset change that left the modules unable to accept the correct software.[2] Ford's own filing uses blander language: the module "may be programmed with the incorrect software."[1] Either way, the part shipped wrong, and the only fix is another module swap, not a software patch beamed to the car.

What you can actually do: dig out the repair invoice for any ABS module work on a Ford Expedition, Explorer, or Edge, or a Lincoln Navigator or MKX, and check it against part numbers EB5Z-2C219-B, DB5Z-2C219-B, CB5Z-2C219-C, DL1Z-2C219-A, or DT4Z-2C219-B. Match found, or no invoice at all after recent ABS work, call Ford at 1-866-436-7332 and cite recall 26S76.[1] Until the module is inspected, drive like your ABS is already gone, because for 3,376 modules, it is.

What this article did not prove: no crash, injury, or fatality count is tied to these modules in the sources found, and no model years are stated for the host vehicles, because the NHTSA component description names models without years. And the 3,376 figure counts modules, not vehicles, so it is not converted into a car count here.
