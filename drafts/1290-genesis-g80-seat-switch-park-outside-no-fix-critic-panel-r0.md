## Scores

2. **Voice Coach — 9.0.** Distinctly Mia: mechanism-first, judgmental about bad design ("the cheaper trims carry a risk the loaded trims do not"), no banned phrases, no throat-clearing. 3. **Ethics Reviewer — 9.0.** No victim-blaming, no inflated danger: the two-fire count and zero injuries are stated plainly, and the counterargument is given real weight instead of a strawman dismissal. Owners are told what to do, not scared for clicks.
4. **Social/Shareability — 8.5.** The hook shares itself: a car that can catch fire after you park it. Pull stat (946) is the kind of number people repeat. Docked half a point because the headline is long for a card, though it is accurate.
5. **Legal Accuracy — 9.0.** Campaign numbers (26V664, 21V160), filing date, VIN-searchable date, letter date, and recall numbers (Hyundai 308, Genesis 035G) all check against the Part 573 reporting. Prior recall explicitly distinguished as a different defect. Genesis phone numbers deliberately omitted because secondary reports disagree.
6. **Research Rigor — 9.0.** Original contribution is real: the 1% x 94,622 = ~946 calculation reframes the whole recall, plus the post-ignition risk framing and the trim-level inversion. Limitations are a dedicated paragraph, not hedging. Weakness disclosed, not hidden: incident counts are manufacturer-reported.
7. **Data Presentation — 9.0.** Population split (54,644 / 39,978) is exact, the pull stat shows both inputs (1% of 94,622), and the 24-car gap between the two recall populations is presented as coincidence of the same production population, not as a conspiracy.
