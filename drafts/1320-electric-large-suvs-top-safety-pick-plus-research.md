## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? YES — fresh (4 days old), paradoxical (the vehicles buyers fear for battery fires are the only ones that aced the crash tests), and actionable (large-SUV shoppers). **Original tabulation (novel contribution):** of the 8 gas-powered large SUVs, **6 failed a required award category outright** (Q7: Poor V2V 2.0; Q8: Marginal moderate overlap; Sequoia: Marginal moderate overlap; Grand Wagoneer: Marginal moderate overlap; Tahoe: Poor moderate overlap + Acceptable small overlap; Expedition: Marginal in both front tests). The other 2 (Armada, QX80) are incomplete — key categories not yet tested. **Zero gas models earned TSP+.**

**2026 TSP+ criteria** (primary: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/iihs-pushes-improvements-in-crash-avoidance-with-2026-awards): Good in small overlap, moderate overlap, side; Acceptable/Good headlights; Good pedestrian front crash prevention; Acceptable/Good vehicle-to-vehicle 2.0.

**Rollover stakes** (primary: https://www.iihs.org/research-areas/fatality-statistics/detail/passenger-vehicle-occupants): 6,250 passenger-vehicle occupants died in rollover crashes in 2024; rollovers were 28% of all occupant deaths, **32% for SUVs**; 47% of single-vehicle crash deaths involved a rollover.

**Mechanism** (primary: https://www.iihs.org/topics/vehicle-size-and-weight): heavier vehicles protect their occupants better in two-vehicle crashes; low-mounted battery packs lower the center of gravity, cutting rollover risk — the deadliest failure mode for tall SUVs. EVs are also newer designs carrying the latest crash-avoidance tech the 2026 criteria reward.

**Prices** (secondary, Oct 25, 2026: https://allmotornews.com/volvo-ex90-rivian-r1s-earn-iihs-top-safety/): EX90 starts $78,090; R1S starts $85,885.

## Counterargument (full strength)
The "only two" framing is partly a testing-coverage artifact: Armada and QX80 have untested categories and could still earn TSP+ (they already hold regular TSP). The award criteria heavily reward the newest crash-avoidance tech, and the two winners are among the newest designs in the class — newness, not just batteries, explains part of the gap. EVs' extra weight protects occupants but makes them more dangerous to everyone else (IIHS's own size/weight research; see site's #1063). And real-world battery-fire data remains limited — the lab tests don't measure that risk.

## Limitations
- IIHS ratings are controlled lab tests, not real-world fatality rates; FARS driver-death-rate data lags by years and won't show these 2026-27 models.
- 2026 award cycle still in progress; table will change as IIHS tests the Armada/QX80 fully.
- Price barrier: both winners start near $80k — this is a luxury outcome, not a mass-market one.
- No claim about battery fire risk either way; IIHS tests monitor battery temps but don't rate fire outcomes.

## Actionable takeaways
1. Shopping a large SUV: read the full IIHS class table, not the badge — "Top Safety Pick" (Q7, Armada, QX80) is a tier below "Plus."
2. Tahoe, Expedition, Sequoia, Grand Wagoneer, Q8 buyers: your model failed a required category — check which one before you buy.
3. The moderate-overlap rear-seat test (tightened 2024) is what's flunking most of them — put your kids' safety above the badge.
4. Weight cuts both ways: the safest big SUV for your family is the most dangerous thing on the road for everyone else's.
