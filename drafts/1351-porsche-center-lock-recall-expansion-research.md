# Research: #1351 — Porsche's Wheels Keep Falling Off (Center-Lock Recall Expansion 26V682)

## Event
On October 16, 2026, Porsche Cars North America filed NHTSA campaign **26V682000** (Porsche internal: ATA1), expanding the October 2024 center-lock wheel bolt recall **24V809**. **548 additional 2024-model sports cars** now carry a Do Not Drive advisory: the center-lock wheel bolt may fracture, letting a wheel detach at speed. Owner notification letters go out December 15, 2026; VINs became searchable October 20, 2026.

## The recall facts (primary sources)
- **NHTSA 26V682000**, filed Oct 16, 2026; 548 vehicles potentially affected.
- **Models:** 2024 718 Cayman GT4 RS, 911 Turbo S, 911 Turbo, 911 S/T, 911 GT3 RS, 911 GT3, 911 Carrera GTS, 718 Spyder RS. The per-model split Porsche reported: 911 Carrera GTS 168, 911 GT3 50, 911 GT3 RS 41, the rest across Turbo/Turbo S/S/T/Cayman GT4 RS/Spyder RS.
- **Defect:** center-lock wheel bolt may fracture; wheel detachment = loss of vehicle control. NHTSA do-not-drive advisory on the campaign record.
- **Component:** wheel lugs/nuts/bolts/studs; suspect part number **992601119B**.
- **Cause (now named, was "under investigation" in 2024):** a batch of out-of-spec bolts from German supplier **Hirschvogel**. Testing found **lead and bismuth levels outside specification** in the alloy — metallurgical defect, not assembly error.
- **Why they were missed in 2024:** after two more field fractures (May and August 2026, no injuries), Porsche and Hirschvogel reopened the investigation. Supply-chain review found **unrecorded parts transfers within the supplier's manufacturing and logistics facilities** — defective bolts moved off the books, so the original recall boundary (1,851 vehicles) was drawn from incomplete paperwork. Matching supplier delivery records to component and vehicle build dates identified the extra cars; suspect parts went into 911s and 718s **built June 7 to July 19, 2024**.
- **Original campaign context (24V809, Oct 2024):** 1,851 vehicles told to park; four U.S. incidents that fall including **two crashes where a wheel came off**; no injuries. After a December 2024 amendment it covered 2,251 vehicles (2021–2024 911, 2024 718, 2025 Panamera). The new expansion drops the Panamera — this is a surgical addition from the same batch.
- **Defect rate:** Porsche's filing carries a 1.4% defect rate — roughly 8 of the 548 cars, per The Auto Wire's math. The other ~540 park their cars as a precaution.
- **Remedy:** dealer inspects the center-lock fastening system, replaces bad parts free. Remedy notification date: December 15, 2026. Porsche will contact owners by phone, email, and MyPorsche app within 1–2 weeks; letters within 60 days. No reimbursement for pre-notification repairs (all cars still under new-car warranty).

## The novel angle (kill-test verdict: PASS)
1. **The recall boundary was drawn on bad paperwork.** Unrecorded supplier logistics moves meant the 2024 recall scoped a defect population that didn't match reality. Two years later, the same bad bolts are still showing up. A recall is only as good as the supplier's paper trail — and the paper trail had gaps.
2. **Lead and bismuth.** The failure is metallurgical: the alloy chemistry was out of spec. These aren't lug nuts that loosened; these are bolts that crystallize and fracture. It's a supply-chain chemistry problem discovered two years after the cars shipped.
3. **The affected cars are the track cars.** GT3 RS, GT3, Turbo S, S/T, Cayman GT4 RS, Spyder RS — the exact Porsches bought to run at redline on circuits, where a wheel departing at 150+ mph is catastrophic. A Porsche tells its fastest customers: don't drive.
4. **The 1.4% paradox.** ~8 cars of 548 actually carry bad bolts. Porsche can't tell which eight without inspecting every car, so 548 owners all park theirs until mid-December. That's the right call — but it's also a measure of how little Porsche knew about its own supply chain.
5. **Recall of a recall, Porsche edition.** 24V809 was supposed to close this out in 2024. It didn't. The expansion is an admission the first net was cast from an incomplete parts list.

## What owners should do (actionable)
- Check the 17-character VIN at **nhtsa.gov/recalls** — VINs searchable since Oct 20, 2026.
- If affected: **do not drive** until a Porsche dealer inspects the center-lock system. Call Porsche customer service at **1-800-767-7243** and reference campaign **ATA1** (NHTSA 26V682000).
- Buying a used 2024 GT3/GT3 RS/Turbo S? Verify the recall is closed on that VIN before money changes hands; some of these cars sat for sale during the gap.
