# Research: #1371 — Three RV brands, one recliner cord, one fire risk

**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** 2026-11-02

## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** Three competing RV makers recalled trailers in the same
week for the same defect, described in the same NHTSA sentence: the power
cord for the power seating assembly "may not provide adequate circuit
protection," so "in the event of a short circuit, inadequate protection
increases the risk of a fire." Alliance RV (3,686 trailers), Heartland
(518 fifth wheels), Ember (259 travel trailers). Total: 4,463 units.
Three brands, three campaigns, one component family, one failure mode:
the fuse that is supposed to stop a fire is the part that cannot be
trusted to stop a fire. And the stakes are not a car-seat recliner: people
sleep in these boxes, far from a fire station, often with the power seats
wired into the same 12-volt system as everything else.

**Challenge:** is this surprising after 1,370 articles, given the site's
RV bench and its power-seat fire history (Kia Telluride power-seat
contagion stories)? Yes, three ways. (1) Nobody connected the three
campaigns: the trade press lists them as separate line items in a weekly
roundup, but the defect wording is identical across all three filings,
which points at a shared seating supplier or shared cord design rather
than three independent mistakes. (2) The paradox is clean: the safety
device IS the defect. A fuse exists for exactly one job, interrupting a
short before it becomes a fire, and these fuses cannot be counted on to
do it. (3) The remedy split is telling: Alliance and Ember replace the
power cord; Heartland replaces the harness AND the cord, which reads like
Heartland found more of the problem than its competitors did.

## Verified facts (3+ primary / primary-adjacent sources)

### The three campaigns
- **Alliance RV, 26V680:** ~3,686 model-year 2025-2027 Valor and
  2026-2027 Delta recreational trailers equipped with certain power
  seating assemblies. The power cord fuse may not provide adequate
  circuit protection. Dealers replace the power cord free of charge.
  Owner letters expected Dec 7, 2026. Alliance customer service
  1-574-218-7165.
- **Heartland RV, 26V710 (Heartland no. 990182):** 518 units: 2024
  Torque, Bighorn, Cyclone and 2022-2023 Gravity, Road Warrior fifth
  wheels with certain power seating assemblies. The power cord fuse may
  not provide adequate circuit protection. Dealers replace the harness
  AND the power cord free of charge. Owner letters expected Nov 30,
  2026. Heartland customer service 1-877-262-8032.
- **Ember RV, 26V714:** 259 units: 2024 E-Series travel trailers with
  certain power seating assemblies. The power cord fuse may not provide
  adequate circuit protection. Dealers replace the power cord free of
  charge. Owner letters expected Nov 23, 2026. Ember customer service
  1-844-732-4204.

### The tally
- Combined: 3,686 + 518 + 259 = **4,463 trailers** across three brands,
  all announced in NHTSA's week-of-November-2 roundup.
- Identical consequence wording in all three notices: "In the event of
  a short circuit, inadequate protection increases the risk of a fire."
- No crashes, injuries, or fires are reported in any of the three
  notices. All three are preventive.

### Context (framing, not new claims)
- RV fires are disproportionately dangerous because the vehicle is also
  the bedroom: occupants sleep inside, egress is through a narrow door,
  and the structure is lightweight paneling around a 12-volt system.
  (General framing; no specific stat claimed.)
- The site's own power-seat history shows the pattern: a powered seat
  is a motor, a switch, and a cord running through furniture, and when
  any of those shorts, the fuse is the only thing between a warm wire
  and a fire. Here the fuse is the recall.

## Actionable takeaway
Owners of 2024-2027 Alliance Valor/Delta, Heartland Torque/Bighorn/
Cyclone/Gravity/Road Warrior, or Ember E-Series trailers: run the VIN
at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for letters (Ember Nov 23,
Heartland Nov 30, Alliance Dec 7). The fix is free. If your trailer is
affected, get the cord (and harness, for Heartland) replaced before the
next trip where anyone sleeps in it.
