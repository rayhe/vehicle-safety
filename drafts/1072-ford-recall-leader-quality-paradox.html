Ford Issued 78 Recalls This Year. That's the Good News.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
October 4, 2026

Fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: the automaker that recalled the most vehicles this year is Ford, and Ford would like you to know this is actually excellent news. Through October 1, Dearborn issued 78 recalls. Stellantis managed 35. General Motors, 25. Ford's two biggest campaigns are also the two biggest of 2026: 4,381,878 vehicles for a trailer-module software glitch, 1,392,935 for another. Somewhere in Michigan, a quality executive is reading these numbers the way a doctor reads good cholesterol.[1]

78
Ford recalls issued through Oct 1, 2026 — more than GM and Stellantis combined

Absurd as it sounds, the logic holds together. Ford says it doubled its safety and technical teams and adopted an "intensive strategy to quickly find and fix" defects, including ones no customer had reported yet.[4] Consider the 4.38-million-vehicle trailer recall: Ford reopened its investigation in January after a December sit-down with NHTSA. Reuters counted 407 related incidents and zero crashes.[3] Find it yourself before the feds find it for you. That is the strategy.

Now, the dread: it works. By July 21, 2.88 million of those 4.38 million vehicles were already fixed, mostly through an over-the-air software update. No dealer visit. No waiting-room coffee. Two-thirds of the largest recall of the year, resolved in five months by a patch beamed through the air.[2] Under the old model, a recall meant a letter, a dealership appointment, and a completion rate you could set your watch to. Somewhere around 30 percent. Forever.

65.6%
Share of the 4.38M-vehicle trailer recall remedied by July 2026 — mostly via over-the-air update

But read the defect-rate line of the filing before you applaud. Ford estimated 1% of those 4.38 million vehicles actually carried the defect. Forty-three thousand real problems, 4.34 million precautionary notices. Headline recall counts measure the size of the net, not the size of the catch.[2] And every one of those 78 campaigns is also, technically, an admission that something shipped broken.

So which is it. Worst quality in the industry, or the most honest quality program in the industry? These are the same animal wearing different jackets. A company that ships fewer defects issues fewer recalls. A company that hunts its own defects issues more. You cannot tell them apart from the campaign count alone, which makes the campaign count a perfect modern statistic: precise, official, and useless.

What this means for you

Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not when the letter arrives. If your car offers over-the-air updates, turn them on; the 26V104 campaign is proof the fix can reach you faster than the mail. When a record-recall headline crosses your feed, read past the first paragraph to the defect-rate line. A company that recalls 4.3 million cars over a 1% defect it found itself is telling you something very different from a company that recalls 40,000 because NHTSA opened an investigation. The safest car company may be the one most willing to admit its cars are broken.
