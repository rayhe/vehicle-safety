Toyota's New Electric C-HR Can Lose Power Because You Braked

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
September 10, 2026

Your brakes are supposed to slow you down. In 8,521 brand-new Toyota C-HR EVs, they can also shut the car off. Under recall 26TA17, Toyota admits that when the battery sits at a high state of charge and the driver keeps using regenerative braking, a software error in the charge-limiting function can overcharge the pack and trigger a protective shutdown of the electric drive system. Warning lights, a buzzer, and then no propulsion: power steering and power brakes keep working, but forward motion does not.[1]

8,521
2026 C-HR EVs recalled (26TA17), built between February 6 and July 10, 2026, because braking can overcharge the battery

Let us talk about what the charge limiter is, because this is the part that should bother you. Regenerative braking turns the motor into a generator, converting kinetic energy into electrical energy that gets stuffed back into the high-voltage pack. A full pack has nowhere for that energy to go, so every battery management system on earth has one job it must never flub: stop charging when the pack is full. Preventing lithium-ion overcharge is the entire reason charge-limit logic exists. Toyota's version had a programming error in exactly that function, which is the electronic equivalent of a smoke detector that sets fires.[2] Toyota's own language understates it politely: "A loss of motive power while driving at higher speeds can increase the risk of a crash."[2]

This is also the second Toyota EV software-driven power-loss recall of the summer. In July, Toyota recalled roughly 21,000 Toyota, Lexus, and Subaru EVs over a Denso battery ECU where one integrated circuit could overwrite the other's data in shared memory, producing the same failure class: drive system shutdown, crash risk.[4] The backstory there is worse than the bug. Toyota's engineers investigated that one starting in August 2025, concluded in September 2025 that its battery-electric models could not be affected, reopened the investigation in April 2026, and discovered they were wrong.[4] That is not a supplier issue anymore; it is a software QA pattern.

0
Over-the-air updates supported by the 2026 C-HR. Every one of the 8,521 software fixes requires a dealership visit.[5]

And now the part where I judge the vehicle design, because someone has to. This is a pure software defect: no bolts, no brackets, no wiring harnesses, just a code patch. Toyota announced the recall on September 2, and owner notification letters are not scheduled until October 18, with the fix available only at a dealership because the 2026 C-HR does not support over-the-air updates.[1][5] That means more than six weeks of owners driving a car that can lose propulsion on a downhill off-ramp, with the official interim advice being: keep the battery away from full and don't brake hard with regen.[6] The defect is software, the fix is software, and the delivery method is a service appointment.

So here is the checklist for owners: if you have a 2026 C-HR EV, check Toyota.com/recall or call Toyota at 1-800-331-4331 now instead of waiting for a letter in October. Until the battery ECU software is updated, avoid charging to full and leaning on strong regenerative braking on descents, which is a miserable sentence to have to write about a car that cost around $37,000.[6] If you are shopping EVs, ask whether the car supports over-the-air updates, because the gap between "fixed" and "fixed for you" is now measured in dealership waiting rooms. And the broader lesson from Toyota's summer: the most dangerous component in a modern EV is not the battery. It is the software that manages the battery.

To be precise about what this article did not prove: no crashes, injuries, or fatalities are documented in the recall filings; the risk is the documented shutdown mode, not a body count. The 8,521 figure covers the US; roughly 1,310 additional vehicles are affected in Canada, and whether the European C-HR+ shares the defect is still unclear.[1][3] Only the 2026 model year is affected; the 2027 C-HR now on sale is not part of the recall. The July Denso recall is a separate defect, linked here as pattern context rather than the same failure.
