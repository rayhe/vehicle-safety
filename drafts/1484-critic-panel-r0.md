## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

No victims are named or exploited; there are no crashes or injuries in this story, and the article does not invent any. The Audi May 2026 belt modification is labeled as reported by secondary coverage, never as an IIHS-verified fact, in both the article and the disclaimer. Toyota and Audi are given their context at full strength: the RAV4's front crash prevention beat the Kicks, and the Q3 earned Good everywhere else. No self-congratulation, no moralizing about manufacturers, no dunking on owners. The limitations paragraph states plainly that nobody was injured in these tests and the stakes are prospective.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.7/10

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10

Every rating, test ID, population, and date was verified against the IIHS ratings page read directly during research (CEF2619 Marginal at 4.0 kN shoulder belt tension on pre-July 2026 builds; CEF2631 Good at 3.6 kN on post-June 2026 builds; V2V Marginal with the motorcycle-target failure; pedestrian Good; headlights Good; LATCH G+), and against the AutoGuide article read in full (Q3 lap-belt slip, RAV4 Acceptable/Marginal split, Trailblazer shortfalls, test history 2022/2024). The "five small SUVs" framing follows Apex News; the article names four and never invents the fifth. The Palisade post-November 2025 build-date claim is verified against its IIHS ratings page. No unverified price or sales claims: the RAV4 is described as "one of America's best-selling vehicles" per SlashGear, not with a sales figure. One point off: the Q3 May 2026 modification rests on a single secondary source (WiseVoter) and is presented as reported, which is honest but thinner than the rest of the piece's sourcing.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Original contribution: the Poor → Marginal → Good two-revision trajectory with test IDs and kilonewton figures pulled from IIHS's own page (the secondary coverage flattened this to "Poor to Good in one revision," and this article corrects it); the build-date lottery quantified across two nameplates; the RAV4 powertrain split; the motorcycle-target detail behind the Marginal V2V rating. Limitations are stated in the article's own paragraph, not just the disclaimer (no FARS track record for the redesigned Kicks, five-SUV sample, prospective stakes). Counterargument is given at full strength and partially conceded, with the honest admission that both readings hold. Methodology is transparent: test IDs and build-date boundaries are in the references. One point off: the piece does not state the Kicks' pre-fix 2025 Poor test ID, because IIHS's current page supersedes it and the 2025 test record was not independently pulled.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.6/10

The two-test comparison (Marginal at 4.0 kN vs Good at 3.6 kN) is in prose with test IDs, and the pull stat carries the trajectory. The RAV4 split and the Q3 failure mode are each stated with their specific mechanism. Deduction: the CEF2619-vs-CEF2631 comparison would scan better as a small table (build window, overall rating, rear restraints, belt tension), and a reader hunting for "which build is mine" has to read the paragraph rather than a table. Consistent with series precedent (no tables in recent articles), so no revision demanded.
