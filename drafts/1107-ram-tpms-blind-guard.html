The Gap

The Tire-Pressure Monitor Can’t Detect Low Tire Pressure. 16,385 Rams.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
July 22, 2027

Your 2026 Ram 1500 has a tire pressure monitoring system that cannot monitor tire pressure. Federal regulators recalled 16,385 of the trucks because the TPMS sensors may fail to detect low tire pressure, and the warning light may fail to turn on anyway.[1][2] A blind, mute TPMS is not a monitoring system; it is dashboard decoration.

16,385
2026 Ram 1500s recalled under NHTSA 26V623000, because the tire pressure monitoring system fails to comply with FMVSS 138. The sensors may not detect low pressure, and the light may not tell you.[1]

Late-1990s Firestone tires were linked to more than 100 rollover deaths after tread separation. Congress answered with the TREAD Act, mandating TPMS on every light vehicle sold in America after September 2007.[3] Its entire job description is one sentence: tell the driver the tires are low. Ram’s version cannot perform it, and NHTSA says the trucks fail to comply with FMVSS 138, the federal TPMS standard.[1]

This is also the second time in two model years that Ram has gotten tire pressure wrong, and the two failures are mirror images. In 2025, Ram recalled 10,396 Ram 1500s because a software bug in the instrument cluster told drivers to inflate their tires to 50 psi, far above spec, trading under-inflation for blowout risk.[4] FCA told you to pump them up too far; now the sensor cannot tell when they are down too far. Ram’s relationship with tire pressure has been wrong at both ends of the gauge.

The fix, to FCA’s credit, is straightforward and free: dealers inspect the sensors and replace the bad ones. FCA filed the recall voluntarily, and no crashes have been blamed on the defect so far.[1][2] But owner letters do not mail until October 27, and the sensor lives inside the wheel, so this is a dealer visit with a tire dismount, not an overnight software patch. VINs became searchable on NHTSA.gov on October 6. If you own a 2026 Ram 1500, run your VIN now and do not wait for the letter.[1]

The counterargument, stated fairly: a recall is the system working. FCA filed voluntarily, no crashes are attributed to the defect, and the remedy costs you nothing. All true. But the owners who most need to know will learn about it last, by mail, weeks after the company knew. The guardian was blind for a while before anyone filed the paperwork. That is the gap.

What to do: If you own a 2026 Ram 1500, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now; do not wait for the October 27 letter.[1] Buy a five-dollar tire gauge and check pressures monthly. TPMS was always a last-resort light, not a gauge, and even healthy tires bleed three to nine psi a year sitting in the driveway.[3] Under-inflated tires are tied to 40,000 crashes and more than 650 deaths a year.[3] Shopping used? Confirm recall 51D is marked complete before money changes hands.
