# Research Notes — #1053: "Road Deaths Hit a Decade Low in 2026. Bicyclist Deaths Hit a 40-Year High."

**Slug:** `1053-decade-low-deaths-vulnerable-divergence`
**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (national trends / statistical roundups beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy? YES. Three primary sources from the last ~3 weeks (Sept 2026): NHTSA Q1 2026 CrashStats early estimate, NSC H1 2026 analysis, and the TRIP Sept 23, 2026 national report ("Addressing America's Traffic Safety Crisis"). Novel angle: the national decline is real (on pace for a fifth straight down year, lowest Q1 rate since 2014), but it is a story of divergence — pedestrian, bicyclist, and motorcyclist deaths did NOT decline, and vulnerable road users now account for 21% of all deaths. The original contribution is counterfactual arithmetic: the vulnerable-user backslide erased ~8% of the entire post-2021 decline, and the pandemic-era driving surge cost ~15,700 excess lives vs. the 2019 baseline.

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA, "Early Estimate of Motor Vehicle Traffic Fatalities and Fatality Rate for the First Quarter of 2026," CrashStats** (rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92423/dot_92423_DS1.pdf): Q1 2026 = 7,770 deaths, -4.3% vs Q1 2025 (8,120); fatality rate 0.99/100M VMT, lowest Q1 rate since 2014 and near the all-time quarterly record (0.98). Full-year 2025 = 36,640 deaths, -6.7% vs 2024 (39,254); annual rate 1.10, second-lowest ever recorded. Quarterly table 2021-2026.
2. **National Safety Council, H1 2026 preliminary analysis** (reported via ISHN, Sept 2026; carriermanagement.com): 18,460 deaths Jan-Jun 2026, -2% vs revised H1 2025, despite +0.8% VMT; mileage fatality rate 1.12 (-3.4% vs 2025, -10.4% vs 2024). State divergence: 13 states down >10% (Hawaii -30%, Illinois -24%, West Virginia -24%, Kansas -21%, Oklahoma -20%, Idaho -18%, New Hampshire -16%, Nevada -14%, Maryland -13%, Wisconsin -13%, Tennessee -12%, Utah -11%, Massachusetts -10%); 10 states + DC up >=10% (DC +133%, Delaware +24%, Rhode Island +22%, Vermont +18%, North Dakota +18%, Kentucky +18%, Alabama +17%, Iowa +16%, Wyoming +14%, California +14%, Mississippi +12%). NSC CEO Lorraine Martin quoted.
3. **TRIP, "Addressing America's Traffic Safety Crisis"** (news release Sept 23, 2026, via tripnet.org and AASHTO news brief; roadsbridges.com summary Sept 27, 2026): fourth straight year of decline in 2025 (36,640 deaths); still +3% above 2015 (35,484). Pedestrian deaths +5% from 2020 to 2025 (6,236 -> 6,529). Bicyclist deaths +29% (891 -> 1,148). Pedestrian + bicyclist fatalities = 21% of all 2025 US traffic deaths; +8% combined from 2020 to 2025. Motorcyclists: 5,758 deaths in 2025 (+2% vs 5,620 in 2020); 0.7% of VMT but 16% of deaths; 2024 rate 28/100M mi vs 0.78 for passenger car occupants = 36x. Work zone crashes: 4,481 deaths 2020-2024. Societal harm $1.75T ($435B economic + $1.32T quality-of-life, NHTSA methodology). Helmet non-use among fatally injured motorcyclists: 17% (2019) -> 29% (2021) -> 18% (2023).
4. **Carscoops (July 2026, HT: Reuters)** on NHTSA Q1 2026: confirms 7,770/-4.3%, rate 0.99; bicyclist deaths rose 4% in 2025 to 1,148, "remaining near the highest levels seen in more than four decades"; NHTSA crash cost study: $30B taxpayer / $340B direct / $1.37T with quality of life.
5. **League of American Bicyclists** (data.bikeleague.org): 65% of pedestrian deaths with reported speed limits occurred on roads posted >=40 mph; most common posted limit in a fatal pedestrian crash = 45 mph. 62% of bicyclist deaths on roads >=40 mph; most common = 55 mph overtaking crashes. Fewer than 20 ped or bike deaths per year on 20 mph roads.

## Key numbers (verified above)
- Q1 2026: 7,770 deaths (-4.3%), rate 0.99 — lowest Q1 since 2014.
- 2025: 36,640 deaths (-6.7%), rate 1.10 — second-lowest annual rate ever; lowest full-year total since 2019.
- 2021 peak: 43,230. 2021->2025 decline: 6,590 deaths (-15.2%).
- Bicyclists 2025: 1,148 (+4% YoY, +29% vs 2020), near 40-year high.
- Pedestrians 2025: 6,529 (+5% vs 2020).
- NSC H1 2026: 18,460 (-2%); rate 1.12.

## Original contribution (Axle's math, show work)
1. **Pandemic-era excess:** 2019 baseline = 36,355. 2020-2022 actual = 39,007 + 43,230 + 42,514 = 124,751. At 2019 levels: 3 x 36,355 = 109,065. Excess = 124,751 - 109,065 = **15,686 deaths** attributable to the 2020-2022 surge.
2. **Vulnerable-user backslide vs. the national decline:** ped+bike 2020 = 6,236 + 891 = 7,127. Ped+bike 2025 = 6,529 + 1,148 = 7,677. Backslide = +550 deaths. Post-peak decline 2021->2025 = 43,230 - 36,640 = 6,590. 550 / 6,590 = **8.3% of the entire decline erased** by rising ped/bike deaths.
3. **Motorcycle arithmetic:** 0.7% of VMT, 16% of deaths -> overrepresentation factor 16/0.7 = **~23x**; per-mile rate 28 vs 0.78 = **35.9x** (round: 36x, per TRIP).
4. **2026 pace check:** NSC H1 -2% + NHTSA Q1 -4.3% -> a fifth consecutive annual decline is the base case if H2 tracks.

## Limitations (must state in article)
- Early estimates are revised: 2025 figures carry the preliminary dagger; 2026 FARS final not until 2028.
- NHTSA Q1 2026 report gives no breakdown by road-user type; whether pedestrians/cyclists shared the H1 2026 decline is unknown.
- NSC and NHTSA use different scopes/methodologies (NSC counts differ from NHTSA's); do not mix the two series in one trend line.
- The excess-death calc uses a flat 2019 baseline; a trend-extrapolated baseline would change the number.

## Strongest counterargument
The celebration is mostly regression to the mean. 2025 (36,640) is still 3% above 2015 (35,484) — a year nobody called a golden age of safety. The "decline" is really a return to the long-run plateau after a three-year spike driven by emptier roads, reduced enforcement, and riskier behavior. On the rate basis, the whole story is VMT normalization plus fleet turnover/ADAS, not policy victory. Also, 10 states + DC are moving the wrong way at double digits — the national number is an average that hides DC's +133%.

## Actionable insights
- If you walk or bike: the national trend is not your trend. Route selection dominates your risk: <20 ped/bike deaths per year happen on 20-mph roads vs the majority on >=40 mph arterials (most common fatal speed limit: 45 mph ped, 55 mph bike). Choose the slow street.
- If you ride a motorcycle: helmets. Non-use among the fatally injured fell from 29% (2021) back to 18% (2023) — the decline in deaths tracks the gear, not luck.
- For everyone: the gains are fragile and enforcement-linked; the states that fell (Hawaii -30%, Illinois -24%) did something the states that rose didn't — ask what, before copying the celebration.
