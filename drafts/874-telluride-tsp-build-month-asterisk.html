The 2027 Telluride Won IIHS's Highest Award. Yours Might Not Have.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 10, 2026

Before you sign that lease, open the driver's door and read the little sticker on the jamb. Last week the IIHS gave the redesigned 2027 Kia Telluride its highest honor, Top Safety Pick+, alongside the Tesla Model Y.[1] But the award carries an asterisk that will never appear on a window sticker: it applies only to Tellurides built after June 2026, when Kia improved the headlights.[1] Buy a May build, same model year, same badge, and the headlights rate Marginal instead of Good.

June 2026
Why your Telluride's build month decides whether its headlights rate Good or Marginal

Is this a scandal? No: it is how IIHS ratings work, where a running production change gets its own rating and the improved lamps earned their Good. Kia deserves credit for fixing it mid-year instead of waiting for the 2028 model year. What bothers me is the gap between the award press release and the dealership lot, where "IIHS Top Safety Pick+" gets quoted at shoppers with zero mention of build months. Nobody at the finance desk is checking your door jamb.

Here is the part the FARS data actually backs up. The outgoing Telluride generation recorded 31 deaths across a 612,500-vehicle fleet from 2014 to 2023, a fatality rate of 0.04 per 100 million vehicle miles traveled.[4] That is one of the ten lowest rates in the entire dataset among high-volume models, and the Model Y sits even lower at 0.03.[4] So the lab award and the real-world record agree: the Telluride is genuinely one of the safest shapes on the road. The asterisk is not the car; it is the month it rolled off the line.

Now the pattern the batch exposed, because the five failures tell you where the award bar moved. Seven models entered the fourth testing wave of 2026; five left without an award.[3] Three of them, the BMW 3 Series, the BMW i4, and the GMC Canyon, flunked the updated vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention test, the one that grades your automatic emergency braking.[1] The Canyon also earned Marginal on the toughened moderate overlap test that now seats a dummy in the back row.[1] The Lexus IS stumbled on that same rear-seat test, and the Toyota Crown Signia missed on moderate overlap alone.[1] Read that list again. Nobody failed on crumple zones; they failed on software and sensors and back-seat dummies, which means the 2026 award is a test of your car's code as much as its steel.

Credit where it is due: Marginal headlights are a nighttime-visibility grade, not a death sentence, and a May-built Telluride is still a structurally Good SUV. Kia fixed the lamps in production within months instead of dragging it out, which is the system working.[2] IIHS has always rated running changes separately; this is granularity, not favoritism. But granularity that lives only on a ratings PDF helps nobody standing on a dealership lot on a Saturday.

So here is the consumer checklist. Shopping a 2027 Telluride: read the build date on the door-jamb sticker, then look up that exact build on iihs.org/ratings before you negotiate.[5] Cross-examine any "award-winning safety" claim against the build date, not the model year. Test the driver-assist stack on your test drive, because the disqualifiers in this batch were AEB software, not sheet metal. And run every used VIN through nhtsa.gov/recalls before money changes hands.[6] The award is real, which is why you should make sure it is yours.

What this article did not prove. FARS data ends with 2023, so the 0.04 rate belongs to the first-generation Telluride (2020-2025), not the redesigned 2027 that won the award. The new car's real-world record does not exist yet; for the 2027, the award is lab evidence only. FARS rates use estimated vehicle miles traveled, not odometer readings, and inherit roughly 15% uncertainty for low-volume models. The Lexus IS, BMW i4, and Crown Signia have too few FARS records for a real-world cross-check. And a Marginal headlight rating measures nighttime visibility, not crashworthiness. Stated plainly.
