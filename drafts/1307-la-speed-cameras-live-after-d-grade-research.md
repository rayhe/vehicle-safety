## Primary sources (3+)
1. **LAist via thelalocal.org, October 26, 2026.**
   https://thelalocal.org/transportation/start-watching-your-speed-la-speed-cameras-are-now-online/
   - About one-fifth of LA's 125 cameras issuing warnings at launch; rest
     slated to go live later in 2026.
   - 60-day warning period per camera before fines.
   - Fines start at $50 for 11 mph over, up to $500 over 100 mph.
   - Pilot signed into law three years ago; five other California cities
     in the program, including Glendale and Long Beach.
2. **Angeleno Daily, October 13, 2026 (reporting LADOT Oct 1 announcement).**
   https://angelenodaily.com/articles/la-launches-speed-cameras-oct-26-with-fines-starting-at-50-mv61a5as
   - 125 systems total, all 15 council districts; most districts eight,
     Districts 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 get nine each; all running by end of 2026.
   - Trigger: 11+ mph over the posted limit; rear plate photo only, no
     driver face; data cannot be shared with law enforcement without a
     subpoena; photos deleted within five days if no violation.
   - 60-day grace of written warnings per camera; after grace, first
     11-15 mph-over offense still a warning, repeats start at $50;
     16+ mph over draws a civil citation starting at $100.
   - Fine revenue cannot enter the general fund; must fund street-safety
     infrastructure (per Streetsblog LA).
   - Speeding contributed to roughly 20% of LA fatal collisions in 2024
     (LADOT); 290 people died in LA traffic incidents in 2025, more than
     the city's homicide total that year (LAPD data).
   - AB 645 signed October 2023; pilots run through January 1, 2032;
     LADOT must submit a safety and economic evaluation to the legislature.
   - SF saw more than a 70% decline in speeding after activating cameras
     (LADOT); SF also saw a 42% drop in traffic fatalities in 2025
     (Damian Kevitt, Streets Are For Everyone).
   - Location list at LAspeedsafety.org.
3. **LA Weekly, October 2026 (program live).**
   https://www.laweekly.com/las-speed-cameras-are-live-save-our-google-map-of-all-the-locations/
   - Two cameras per location, both directions; notice by mail within
     15 days; civil penalties, no license points; 30 days to request review.
   - Fine schedule: 11-15 mph $50; 16-25 mph $100; 26+ mph $200;
     100+ mph $500.
   - Low-income drivers: payment plan or community service in place of fine.
   - Program cost about $7.95 million annually; citation revenue first
     covers operating expenses; surplus must go to traffic-calming
     projects within three years.
   - SF, first California city to activate, reports speeding reductions
     of more than 70% at camera locations.
4. **Streetsblog California, December 9, 2025 (SAFE report card).**
   https://cal.streetsblog.org/2025/12/09/report-speed-camera-programs-working-in-san-francisco-floundering-in-bureaucracy-in-l-a
   - SF earned A+: all 33 cameras permitted by state law installed and
     issuing citations; 72% average reduction in speeding at 15 key
     camera locations; 400,000+ warning notices in the first six months,
     violations dropping week over week.
   - LA scored the worst of the seven pilot cities, a D grade, in SAFE's
     December 2025 AB 645 implementation report.
5. **GrowSF, July 17, 2026 (SFMTA one-year report).**
   http://growsf.org/news/2026-07-15-supervisors-back-more-speed-cameras/
   - SFMTA one-year report: share of drivers going at least 10 mph over
     fell 79% at camera locations, from 11% to 2%, roughly 40,000 fewer
     speeding incidents each day.
   - Citywide traffic deaths fell from 43 to 25 between 2024 and 2025,
     alongside speed cameras and other safety measures.
   - SF Board of Supervisors voted 10-1 to ask the state to lift the
     33-camera cap and allow up to 80 more.

## Verified facts used in the article
- Launch: October 26, 2026; 26 of 125 cameras issuing warnings at launch;
  all 125 expected running by end of 2026.
- Trigger threshold: 11 mph over the posted limit; rear plate only.
- Grace: 60 days of warnings per camera; after grace, first 11-15 mph
  offense still a warning, repeats $50; 16-25 mph $100; 26+ mph $200;
  100+ mph $500. Civil penalties, no license points.
- Privacy: no face images; non-violation photos deleted within five days
  (five business days per LA Weekly); no sharing with agencies without
  a court order/subpoena.
- Cost: about $7.95 million per year; surplus revenue to traffic calming
  within three years; revenue barred from the general fund.
- LA safety case: speeding in roughly 20% of fatal collisions in 2024;
  290 traffic deaths in 2025, above the city's homicide total.
- SF preview: 72% speeding reduction at camera sites (SAFE/Streetsblog);
  SFMTA: 10+ mph-over share 11% to 2% (79% drop), ~40,000 fewer speeding
  incidents per day; citywide deaths 43 to 25 (2024 to 2025).
- LA's prior grade: D, worst of seven pilot cities, SAFE December 2025.
- AB 645 signed October 2023; six pilot cities plus Malibu (added via
  SB 1297); pilots run through January 1, 2032.

## Caveats stated in the article
- SF's fatality decline came alongside other safety measures; the
  article does not credit cameras alone.
- The 42% SF fatality figure is an advocate's statement (Kevitt); the
  SFMTA citywide count (43 to 25) is the harder number and is consistent
  with it.
- LA's own results do not exist yet; the 60-day warning window means
  the first fine data arrives in late December 2026 at the earliest.
- Fines are civil penalties against the registered owner, not moving
  violations against the driver.
