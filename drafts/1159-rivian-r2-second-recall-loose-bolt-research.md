**Working title:** "Rivian's Make-or-Break Car Has a Loose Bolt. A Human Caught What the Robot Missed."

**Kill test:** PASS. Second recall of the R2 (NHTSA 26V625, filed Sept 30, 2026) — 4 months into deliveries of the vehicle Rivian's profitability depends on. Novel angles nobody else is running: (1) the defect was caught by a human operator AFTER it passed Rivian's automated torque station — the machine said fine, the person said no; (2) Rivian quietly grounded some R2s in September for HV circuit fastener inspections and never publicly commented; (3) the NHTSA filing admits there is NO warning before the connection fails. Pipeline covered a different Rivian recall (#1133 toe link), not this one.

## Primary sources

1. **NHTSA Recall 26V625 (oemdtc mirror, filed 2026-09-30):** Rivian Automotive, LLC recalling certain 2027 R2 vehicles. High-voltage battery fastener may be improperly tightened, causing loss of drive power. Potential units affected: 14. Manufacturer recall number FSAM-1888. Remedy: inspect and repair connection or replace damaged components, free of charge. Owner notification letters expected mailed November 28, 2026. Rivian customer service 1-888-748-4261. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov November 28, 2026. Components: ELECTRICAL SYSTEM: PROPULSION SYSTEM: CRITICAL FASTENERS. https://oemdtc.com/recall/26V625000/

2. **TechCrunch, Oct 2, 2026:** "Rivian issues R2 recall for poorly tightened battery packs." 14 vehicles affected. Rivian told TechCrunch all affected vehicles are at service centers being inspected. Second recall for the R2, which started shipping to customers this year; the R2 is "the first Rivian EV meant for a mass market and is supposed to help it reach profitability for the first time." First R2 recall (September) was a rearview camera visibility issue addressed with an OTA software update. Filing: defect first spotted September 17, when a "manufacturing operator" at the Normal, Illinois factory noticed an under-torqued fastener after it had gone through the automated torque station. Station reprogrammed to reject improperly torqued fasteners. Rivian told NHTSA it "is not aware of any customer reports, accidents, injuries, or fatalities related to this issue." Some Reddit users reported the issue; one owner said service told them to stop driving immediately and the vehicle was towed. https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/rivian-issues-r2-recall-for-poorly-tightened-battery-packs/

## Verified facts

- NHTSA campaign 26V625; 14 model year 2027 Rivian R2s; built May 18–August 27, 2026 at Normal, Illinois.
- Defect: improperly tightened fastener on the high-voltage battery connection; can cause sudden loss of motive power with no prior warning.
- Discovered Sept 17, 2026 by a manufacturing operator who spotted the under-torqued fastener AFTER it passed the automated torque station. Rivian investigated through Sept 24, then voluntarily recalled.
- Rivian reprogrammed the automated station to reject improperly torqued fasteners. NHTSA filing does not explain how the station passed bad fasteners.
- Zero customer complaints, accidents, injuries, or fatalities known (per Rivian's filing). One owner reported being told to stop driving; vehicle towed.
- Owner notification letters: November 28, 2026. VIN search opens same day.
- This is the R2's SECOND recall in ~4 months of customer deliveries (deliveries began June 2026). First: 2,481 R2s, backup camera display, fixed via OTA.
- Separately in September: Rivian grounded some R2s for HV circuit fastener inspections with no public comment (per Rivian Wave/Reddit reports).

## Original contribution

1. **The automation parable:** Rivian's automated torque station — the machine whose entire job is catching this — passed the bad fastener. A human on the floor caught it. In an industry racing to automate quality control, the safety net that worked was a person looking at a bolt.
2. **The silence ledger:** Rivian never publicly commented on the September R2 grounding for HV circuit fastener inspections. Two HV fastener incidents in two months, one disclosed only via NHTSA filing, one via Reddit.
3. **Two recalls, two failure modes, four months:** one software (OTA), one physical (service visit). The mass-market launch vehicle — the one that must carry Rivian to profitability — has a recall rate of 0.5 campaigns per month since deliveries began.

## Limitations

- 14 vehicles is a tiny population; nothing here says anything about the R2 fleet's overall defect rate.
- Rivian's claim that the torque station is reprogrammed is unverifiable from outside.
- No crashes, injuries, or fatalities are tied to this defect — this is a near-miss story, not a body count.
- The September grounding is reported via Reddit/RivianTrackr, not confirmed by Rivian; treat as reported, not established.

## Strongest counterargument

Fourteen cars. Zero incidents. A voluntary recall filed within a week of discovery, with every affected vehicle already at a service center. A defender would call this the system working exactly as designed — and note that Ford's recall machine (#1131) filed 146 campaigns in a year. Rivian's two recalls in four months is diligence, not crisis. Fair. But diligence that needs a human to overrule the robot on the line is worth one headline.

## Actionable insight

- If you own a 2027 R2: your VIN becomes searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls on November 28, 2026. Rivian says all affected vehicles are already at service centers.
- If any EV loses drive power at speed: hazards on, coast to the shoulder, do not expect the car to help you. This failure mode has no warning per the NHTSA filing.
- The broader takeaway for shoppers: early-production vehicles from any maker carry first-run risk. A 2027 R2 built in May 2026 is a first-batch car. That's not a reason to avoid it — it's a reason to check your VIN the day the recall database updates.
