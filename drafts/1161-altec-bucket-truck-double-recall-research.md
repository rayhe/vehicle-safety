# Research: 1161-altec-bucket-truck-double-recall
Journalist: Clara Rollover | Kicker: Investigation
Status: RESEARCH (2026-10-14)

## The story
Altec Industries recalled its aerial-device trucks TWICE in seven days in October 2026, for two unrelated defects on the same work machines. Recall one (week of Oct 5): the boom stow auto-neutral may not engage, so a lineworker can drive off with the boom still elevated. Recall two (week of Oct 12): the platform access step may not be welded on properly, so the step a lineworker stands on to climb into the bucket can separate or go wobbly mid-climb. One defect sends you through a bridge. The other drops you.

## Verified facts
1. **CSN-3374 (filed Oct 2026, week of Oct 12):** 2020-2026 AA Series, 2021-2026 AN Series, 2023-2026 AM Series aerial devices; 2024-2026 Digger Derrick; 2024-2025 A-Class. Turntable-mounted platform access step may be improperly welded between step tube and mounting plate. Step may separate or become unstable in use. 6,780 units. Remedy: replace the steps. Owner letters mailed Nov 27, 2026. Altec 877-462-5832. [fleetmaintenance 10/13/26; truckpartsandservice 10/12/26]
2. **CSN-3370 (filed Oct 2026, week of Oct 5):** 2021-2026 Digger Derrick and 2023-2025 TA Series aerial devices. Improper wiring may prevent the boom stow auto-neutral from engaging; truck can be driven with the boom out of stow, exceeding recorded travel height. 143 units. Remedy: correct wiring in power distribution module. Letters Nov 20, 2026. Same Altec contact. [fleetmaintenance]
3. **The overlap:** Digger Derrick appears in BOTH October recalls for DIFFERENT defects. Same machines, two ways to get hurt in the same month.
4. **Recurring weld theme at Altec:** Aug 2022, 22V613000 (DT65/DT85, weld between base boom and auger stow bracket could crack). ~2020: pedestal-to-turntable bolts (head marking 5185) could fail, 157 units. May 2026: e-stop may not stop hydraulics in ePTO mode, 213 units. The company keeps finding its metal joints and safety interlocks wanting.
5. **Who uses these:** electric utilities, telecom, tree care, sign contractors, municipalities. The access step is the literal first step of every bucket-truck workday.

## Novel angle
Nobody has tied the two October recalls together. The finding: within seven days, Altec admitted two independent failure modes on the same class of trucks, and the Digger Derrick got hit by both. Plus the welds: a weld defect (2022) followed four years later by another weld defect (2026) on the same product family suggests a process problem, not a batch problem. That is the original contribution: pattern, not just recall summary.

## Kill test
PASS. Fresh (this week), never covered on the site (altec = zero prior slugs), genuine news hook with a cross-recall finding that requires reading both weekly roundups. Not a rehash of the "fix failed" theme; it is a "two different things failed at once" theme.

## Limitations
- NHTSA "potentially affected" counts are estimates; actual defect rate unknown. Neither roundup cites complaint or incident counts for these two recalls.
- FARS tracks roadway fatalities, not occupational falls from bucket trucks; fall data lives in BLS CFOI, which we did not query. No fatality numbers are claimed here.
- Altec is an upfitter; the recalls cover the aerial equipment, not the truck chassis. Fleet managers should check both the chassis OEM and Altec.

## Strongest counterargument
Two recalls in a week is arguably the system working: Altec found both defects, filed with NHTSA, and is replacing parts and rewiring before the letters even go out. 6,780 units spread across seven model years is a modest footprint. The step recall in particular covers a lot of metal to catch a bad batch of welds.

## What the reader should do
If your fleet runs Altec AA/AN/AM (2020-2026), Digger Derrick (2021-2026), TA Series (2023-2025), or A-Class (2024-2025): inspect the turntable platform access step welds NOW, verify the boom stow auto-neutral engages before rolling, and call Altec at 877-462-5832 referencing CSN-3374 and CSN-3370. Do not wait for the November letters.
