# Research Notes — #1455: Motorcycle Deaths Fell in 2024. Injuries Didn't. A Third of the Dead Weren't Licensed to Ride.

**Slug:** `motorcycle-deaths-fell-injuries-rose-unlicensed-third`

**Number:** 1455
**Date researched:** 2026-11-10

## Kill test
PASS. NHTSA's full 2024 motorcycle fact sheet (DOT HS 813 824, July 2026) has not been covered as a standalone article on this site. #1451 covered only the left-turn slice; #1426 covered the FMVSS 127 petition; #1272 covered the DC helmet-repeal bill. None took the whole fact sheet. The novel angle: the "good news" headline is a mirage. Deaths fell 2.2%, but the fatality rate per registered motorcycle actually ROSE (66.89 to 67.25 per 100,000) because registrations fell faster than deaths, and injuries jumped 5.2%. Riders are dying slightly less often in absolute terms and getting hurt more often per bike on the road. Plus the behavioral triad nobody wants to say out loud: 35% unlicensed, 37% speeding, 26% alcohol-impaired.

## Key verified facts
- **6,228 motorcyclists killed in 2024**, down 2.2% from 6,366 in 2023. 16% of all traffic fatalities; 20% of all motor-vehicle occupant fatalities. 96% riders (5,950), 4% passengers (278).
- **86,887 injured (estimate), up 5.2%** from 82,567 in 2023. Deaths down, injuries up: the divergence is the story.
- **Per-mile fatality rate 28.00 per 100M VMT, almost 27x the passenger-car occupant rate (1.05)** and 44x the light-truck rate (0.63). Injury rate 391 per 100M VMT, nearly 5x cars.
- **Per-registered-vehicle fatality rate ROSE**: 67.25 per 100,000 registered motorcycles in 2024 vs 66.89 in 2023, because registrations fell to 9,261,249 from 9,516,910. Deaths fell; the risk per bike did not.
- **Motorcycles are 3% of registered vehicles, 0.7% of all VMT, and 16% of the dead.**
- **35% of riders in fatal crashes had no valid motorcycle license** (vs 14% of passenger-vehicle drivers in fatal crashes). Riders in fatal crashes were 1.3x more likely than car drivers to have prior suspensions/revocations (15.4% vs 11.8%).
- **37% of riders in fatal crashes were speeding** (vs 22% passenger cars, 15% light trucks, 8% large trucks). Ages 15-20: 56% speeding, the highest of any age group. Ages 21-24: 52%.
- **26% of riders killed were alcohol-impaired (BAC ≥ .08)** — 1,529 of 5,950. Another 8% had BAC .01-.07. Riders and car drivers tied for highest impairment share of any vehicle type (25% each in fatal crashes).
- **40% of riders who died in single-vehicle crashes were alcohol-impaired** (vs 18% multi-vehicle). Weekend single-vehicle: 43%.
- **Night vs day alcohol gap: riders killed at night were 2.6x more likely to be alcohol-impaired** (37% vs 14%).
- **Helmet-law gap: in states without universal helmet laws, 48% of motorcyclists killed were unhelmeted, vs 11% in universal-law states.** Only 17 states + DC + PR require helmets for all riders. Overall DOT-compliant helmet use 73.8% (2023, last available). Helmet use among killed riders 67%; passengers 48%.
- **Young riders getting worse while the total improves:** deaths among under-15s rose 114% (14 to 30); ages 15-20 up 20% (508 to 609); ages 21-24 up 15% (613 to 705). Average age of a rider killed: 40.
- **Crash shape:** 60% of motorcycles in fatal crashes collided with a motor vehicle in transport; 24% hit fixed objects (vs 16% cars). In two-vehicle fatal crashes, 78% of bikes were struck on the front; 45% involved the other vehicle turning left. 66% urban, 62% not at intersections, 56% daylight, 91% non-interstate.
- **Engine size:** 37% of the dead rode 501-1,000cc bikes; 26% on 1,501cc+; small bikes (≤500cc) up 5% (705 to 739).
- **State leaders (2024, NHTSA state data):** Florida 642, Texas 583, California 522.
- **Decade context:** motorcycle deaths 5,029 (2015) → 6,228 (2024), up 24% over ten years, even as 2024 ticked down.

## Angle / structure
Lede: the good-news number (6,228, down 2.2%) and the immediate undercut — injuries up 5.2%, risk per registered bike up, and the dead were disproportionately unlicensed, speeding, and drunk. Dale's voice: treat impairment/licensing like box scores. Three behavioral stats as the spine (35/37/26), helmet-law split as the policy kicker, young-rider surge as the sting. Actionable: get the endorsement (35% didn't), DOT helmet every ride, and the left-turn stat from #1451 as a cross-reference, not a repeat.

## What this is NOT
- Not a repeat of #1451 (left-turn geometry). That stat appears once, attributed, as context.
- Not a helmet-law polemic; the 48%-vs-11% split is presented as data with the 17-state fact.
- Not a "motorcycles are dangerous" shrug; the per-mile 27x figure is the quantification, and the licensing/speeding/alcohol triad is the actionable part.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- If you ride without a motorcycle endorsement, you are in the 35% cohort that dominates the fatal-crash file. Get licensed; the MSF Basic RiderCourse is the usual path and many insurers discount for it.
- A DOT-compliant helmet is the single cheapest intervention in the whole sheet: unhelmeted share of the dead is 4x higher where the law doesn't require one.
- Riders 15-24: more than half of fatal-crash riders your age were speeding. The bike will do 150; the road will not forgive it.
- Drivers: the left-turn kill is still the most common two-vehicle fatal shape (45%). Look twice, specifically for bikes, before turning across oncoming traffic.
