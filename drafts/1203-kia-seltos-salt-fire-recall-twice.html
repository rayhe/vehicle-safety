Kia Recalled 44,000 Seltos for Salt-Corrosion Fires in 2024. Then It Built 48,070 More of Them.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 28, 2027

Kia Canada is recalling 48,070 Seltos crossovers because the all-wheel-drive control unit, mounted under the driver's seat, corrodes when road salt and winter moisture get tracked into the cabin, and the corrosion can short-circuit the module and start a fire even when the vehicle is parked and turned off.[1] Owners are told to park outdoors, away from homes and other cars, until a dealer inspects the module and installs a protective cover. A plastic lid is the entire engineering solution to a fire risk.

Seltos crossovers recalled in Canada for the same salt-corrosion fire defect, across two recalls 2.5 years apart

Let me tell you what happens in the first 150 milliseconds of this failure, because the packaging decision is what offends me. An electronic control module lives bolted to the floorpan directly beneath the driver's seat, in a vehicle sold overwhelmingly in the road-salt belt. Every winter, drivers drag slush and de-icing brine into the footwell on their boots, and the mixture migrates under the carpet until it reaches an unsealed circuit board carrying 12 volts. Salt water plus voltage plus time equals corrosion, corrosion equals stray current paths, and stray current paths inside a control module equal a short that does not need the ignition on to start heating.[2] This is not a subtle failure mode. This is first-semester automotive packaging: do not put unsealed electronics where the salt goes. February 2024 brought the identical story back around: Kia Canada recalled more than 44,000 Seltos crossovers from the 2021 through 2023 model years for the identical defect chain, same module, same under-seat location, same salt-and-moisture contamination, same corrosion, same short circuit, same fire-while-parked risk, announced through the same Transport Canada notice process.[3] Read the two notices side by side and the cause paragraphs are nearly interchangeable. So here is the question the 2026 recall does not answer: if the fix is a protective cover, and the cover existed as a remedy concept in 2024, why did the 2024, 2025, and 2026 model-year cars roll off the line without one? Either the lesson from the first recall never reached the production line, or the design team looked at a known salt-corrosion fire defect and kept building it unsealed for three more model years. Pick whichever version of that story you prefer; neither is flattering.

Now the part that should worry American owners. I queried NHTSA's recall API for the 2025 and 2026 Kia Seltos on October 17, 2026, and the result was zero recalls.[4] There is no equivalent US action, which the Canadian press notes is a rare occurrence.[1] Meanwhile New York, Michigan, Minnesota, and all of New England salt their roads exactly the way Ontario and Quebec do. A 2025 Seltos AWD in Buffalo has the same module, the same floorpan location, the same winters, and none of the recall. The Canadian recall population and the American non-recall population are the same cars separated by a border. If you own one south of that border, you are driving the recall without the recall.

Context worth keeping: this is not Kia's only fire program running right now. Kia Canada recalled more than 20,000 Telluride SUVs in July 2026 for overheating power-seat motors.[1] That is the same seat-motor failure mode behind Hyundai's October 2026 Genesis recall of 94,622 vehicles, which this site covered in October 2026.[6] The two sister companies are running parallel fire recalls on unrelated components in the same quarter, which tells you something about the state of Hyundai-Kia's electrical validation pipeline. The Seltos is the corrosion fire while the Telluride and the Genesis are the seat-motor fires: different components, same season, same park-outside advisory.

What this article does not prove, stated plainly. My sources contain no fire or incident counts for the 2026 Seltos recall; anyone quoting a fire number on this defect is inventing it. I also could not retrieve the 2024 recall's remedy details, so I cannot prove whether the 2024 fix failed on the 2021-2023 cars or whether the unsealed design simply carried into 2024-2026 production untouched. The recurrence is a fact; the mechanism of the recurrence is the open question, and Kia has not answered it. The "no US recall" claim is a snapshot from an October 17, 2026 query; Kia or NHTSA could act tomorrow. And FARS has no Seltos-specific entries, so there is no fatality-rate context to offer. Non-fatal fires are invisible to FARS by design.

The strongest defense of Kia deserves its full weight. Canada-only recalls frequently reflect Canada-specific conditions: heavier road-salt application, longer winters, and Transport Canada running its own defect process on its own timeline. Kia may reasonably judge the US exposure lower, and demanding a US recall on Canadian salt data could be the overcautious move rather than the missing one. Regulators also do not generally require a recall remedy to be retrofitted into future production; they require a design change, and the redesigned 2027 Seltos, explicitly excluded from this recall with its overhauled mechanicals, may be exactly that change arriving on schedule.[1] Finally, a recall built on engineering analysis before the body count exists is the safety system working as designed. All granted. The module was still unsealed under the seat in the salt belt for three more model years, and a plastic lid is still the fix.

Canadian owners of a 2024-2026 Seltos AWD: park outside and away from structures starting tonight, not when the letter arrives, and get the module inspected and the cover installed. American owners of the same car in the salt belt: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now and re-check monthly, because the Canadian defect exists whether or not the American recall does, and a burning smell from under the driver's seat is not a wait-and-see symptom. Used-car shoppers eyeing a 2021-2023 Seltos: verify the 2024 recall was completed through Transport Canada's database before money changes hands. And the general rule, free of charge: any vehicle with electronic modules mounted low in the cabin, under seats or in footwells, in a road-salt state, deserves a glance at the recall database. Salt does not only eat body panels; it eats the computers bolted underneath your feet.
