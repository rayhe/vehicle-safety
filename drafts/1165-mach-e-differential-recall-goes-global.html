Ford Doubled Its Mach-E Differential Recall. The Fix Still Doesn't Exist.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 14, 2026

Before you sign that lease renewal, you might want to count. In July, Ford recalled 42,784 Mustang Mach-Es in the United States because the rear differential pinion shaft can fracture, killing propulsion or letting the car roll away in Park.[1] On October 9, Germany's motor vehicle authority published the same recall for the world. The count is now 96,840.[2] Ford found this defect in March. The replacement parts still do not exist.

2.26x
The global Mach-E differential recall is 2.26 times the size of the US recall filed in July

Seven months, no fix: let that land. Ford's own development team found a failed rear differential shaft in a 2023 Mach-E in Europe back in March. By June the company had logged 62 warranty claims, a pile of quality-system reports, and two European white alerts, and its field review committee approved a field action.[3] The American recall went out in July under NHTSA campaign 26V417000, with interim letters warning owners of a defect that can snap the driveline or send a parked car rolling if the parking brake is off. The German authority's October publication lists the program under reference 16878R, status "under investigation," and notes the one thing owners most need to know: no corrective action has been specified.[2]

According to German press reporting on Ford's own recall portal, the planned remedy is a full replacement of the rear differential, but the parts are currently unavailable and expected from the fourth quarter of 2026, which is right now, and Ford has not answered journalists asking whether that timeline holds.[4] This is the part that should make you angry. A recalled car is supposed to be a problem with a solution attached. These owners have been driving since July, and in Europe since March, on a shaft that fatigue-fractures with no warning, waiting for a part Ford says it will replace but cannot yet hand them. Every hill they park on is a pop quiz.

Credit where due: Ford is not hiding the danger. The recall documents describe the failure modes plainly, loss of propulsion, unintended movement in Park, and the interim instruction is free and immediate: set the parking brake, every time, every hill, every flat driveway, every grocery run.[1] It is a two-second habit that converts a rollaway into a non-event while the parts pipeline catches up. Nobody will send you a letter reminding you of this, which is why I am saying it in print.

There is also a pattern here worth naming, because Ford keeps starring in it. The US slice of this recall was one of the headline campaigns in a summer where Ford filed recalls at a historic pace, and this site noted at the time that every single Mach-E model year had been recalled.[5] Now the same differential defect has gone global at more than double the American headcount, and Germany's count alone is 6,598 vehicles, which is 6.8 percent of the world total.[2] Filing early and often is the behavior regulators want after a $165 million penalty for slow recall handling[6], and I will take transparent and parts-less over silent and parts-less. But "we found it, we filed it, please hold" is not a remedy; it is a waiting room with wheels.

What to do: drive a 2020 through 2023 Mach-E? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for campaign 26V417000 in the US, or your national authority's database in Europe, and apply the parking brake every single time you park until the differential is replaced. If you got an interim letter in July, watch for the follow-up; the parts are allegedly arriving this quarter. Shopping used? A 2020-2023 Mach-E is a fine car once the new differential goes in. Before that, it is a car you cannot safely park on a slope without remembering a workaround.
