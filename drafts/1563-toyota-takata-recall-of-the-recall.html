Toyota Recalls the Recall: 314,029 Takata Airbags Hit the End of Their Safe Life

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 22, 2026

Toyota is recalling 314,029 vehicles because the Takata passenger airbag inflators inside them have reached the end of their estimated safe service life, and many of those inflators went into the cars as replacements during the original Takata recall, the largest in American history. Toyota fixed the cars, and now the fix is the defect.[1][2]

1,124,621
US vehicles recalled in a single week by Toyota (314,029) and Honda (810,592) for the same failure mode: aging desiccated Takata inflators.

NHTSA campaign 26V748, filed November 12, covers seven nameplates built between 2002 and 2017. Inflators contain desiccated phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate propellant, and years of humidity, heat, and temperature cycling can degrade it until a deployment ruptures the inflator and throws metal fragments through the bag. Crucially, the filing covers inflators "installed as original equipment or replaced under a prior recall." If your airbag was already swapped once, that swap is now the thing being recalled.[1]

Three out of four cars in this recall are the same car. Corollas account for 232,174 of the 314,029 vehicles, 73.9 percent, followed by 35,949 4Runners, 25,174 Tundras, 10,736 Sequoias, 8,931 Lexus GX460s, and 1,064 Lexus SC430s. At the other end sits exactly one 2011 Lexus IS250: one car, one inflator, one owner letter, one dealer visit. Federal recall machinery does not have a minimum size.[1][3]

Zero of these inflators has ruptured. Toyota says so in the filing, and as of November 6 it had received no US field technical reports or warranty claims tied to the condition. Its monitoring program, running since 2016, found no measurable propellant degradation. Toyota recalled anyway, on safe-service-life modeling published by TK Global in November 2021, a model more conservative than the Independent Testing Coalition's October 2019 version. When two models disagree and the conservative one says the clock is up, Toyota picked the conservative one. That is the system working, and it deserves saying plainly, because the dramatic version of this story writes itself and it would be wrong.[1][4]

And the clock keeps running. Toyota's filing notes that same-design inflators installed later "have more safe service life remaining. They continue to be monitored." Every batch of replacement inflators carries its own expiration date. Later batches are still ticking. This recall will recur, on a schedule, for as long as desiccated Takata inflators remain in service. A recall used to be an event. This one is a consumable with a shelf life.[1]

Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not when the letter arrives. Owner letters start December 28, but the lookup works now, and the replacement inflator or airbag assembly is free. That applies even if, especially if, your passenger airbag was already replaced once. Toyota customer service is 1-800-331-4331. If you drive a 2003-2008 Corolla, a 2016-2017 4Runner, a 2005-2006 Tundra, a 2005-2007 Sequoia, a 2017 GX460, a 2002-2010 SC430, or the one 2011 IS250, the two minutes it takes to type 17 characters is the whole job.[2][3]
