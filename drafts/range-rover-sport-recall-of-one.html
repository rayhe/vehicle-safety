Land Rover Filed a Safety Recall for Exactly One SUV

By Rex Driverton
October 7, 2026

Somewhere in America, one person is driving a brand-new 2026 Range Rover Sport built on February 16, 2026, and that person is the entire population of NHTSA recall 26V607000. Not the lead vehicle in the campaign. The campaign. One SUV, one owner, one letter arriving November 20, one free powertrain control module.[1]

vehicles in NHTSA recall 26V607000 — the smallest safety campaign filed by a major automaker

The defect is real, not ceremonial. Bosch, which supplies the powertrain control module, flagged a batch of modules with what JLR's filing delicately calls "anomalous production parameters." Inside at least one module, a circuit board may be deformed or misaligned, and JLR concedes the possible outcomes include the engine stalling without prior warning. That is the kind of failure that strands you in a fast lane with no power steering and rapidly fading brake boost.[2]

Here is where the numbers start to smirk. Bosch's investigation found four vehicles already delivered to customers with suspect modules. The recall covers one of them. The filing offers no explanation for the other three. Either they were checked and cleared, or someone at Solihull did some extremely confident rounding. Federal paperwork does not have a field for "vibes," so the remaining three Range Rover Sports simply do not exist on NHTSA's books.[3]

Mock the sample size all you want. The machinery of a federal safety campaign, quarantine notices, dealer instructions, mailed owner letters, all of it, mobilized for a single VIN. That is either bureaucratic theater or the recall system working exactly as designed, and the honest answer is both.

It gets better. In the same week, Land Rover filed a second recall for the same model line that is the structural inverse: campaign 26V613 covers 873 Range Rover Sport SVs from model years 2024 through 2026 whose rear subframes left the factory with cracks. A forging defect at suppliers Linamar Casting and Gestamp Assembly opened fissures that final machining was supposed to grind away, but routinely did not, and manual inspectors routinely missed them. Some vehicles cannot even be confirmed affected, because the production photos that would prove it were lost to data storage gaps. JLR's own safety committee concluded the cracks could compromise structural integrity in a crash.[4]

Two recalls, one model, one week. One where the entire population fits in a single garage. One where the defect rate is effectively total and the proof is a hole in the archive.

If you own a 2026 Range Rover Sport, do not assume a recall of one means you are safe: Bosch flagged more suspect modules than NHTSA recalled cars. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. If you own a 2024-2026 Sport SV, get the rear subframe inspected; JLR replaces confirmed cracked units free of charge under recall numbers D165 and D166, and owner letters go out by November 20.[5]
