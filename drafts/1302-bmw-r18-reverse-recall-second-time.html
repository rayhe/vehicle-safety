BMW Recalled the R 18 Twice for the Same Fire. The First Fix Was Turning the Feature Off.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

BMW recalled 4,630 R 18 motorcycles this month because a control unit for the reverse gear can overheat and catch fire.[1] If that sentence feels familiar, it should. BMW recalled these same bikes for this same part in 2024. Back then the company had no improved part to install, so the official remedy was to disconnect the unit from the motorcycle's electrical system and send everyone home.

4,630
2021-2024 BMW R 18 motorcycles recalled a second time for the reverse gear control unit, after 5,048 were recalled for the same part in 2024

Read the 2024 paperwork and you will find a promise dressed up as a plan: disconnect now, replace later, once a final remedy exists. Two years passed. Owners kept the hardware and lost the feature, which on a heavyweight cruiser is not a trivial loss. Reverse gear exists on the R 18 because walking a motorcycle this heavy backward out of a parking space is a young person's game, and most R 18 buyers stopped being young people some time ago. For two riding seasons, the feature they paid for sat dead on the bike, disconnected by the manufacturer, while everyone waited for a part that did not yet exist.

Now the part exists, and BMW's own filing for the new recall explains why the waiting period was not as safe as it looked. The 2024 interim remedy was a protective relay, and the new Part 573 report concedes two ways it failed. In several cases, dealers installed the relay incorrectly. In the rest, corrosion already inside the control unit at install time was too advanced for the relay to stop a thermal event anyway.[2] A thermal event in a tropical market in April 2025 happened despite the relay being in place. More followed in hot, humid markets through May 2026, plus one incident in the United States. BMW says it knows of no accidents or injuries tied to the defect. That is the good news, and it is also the entire defense.

Geography told this story before Detroit, or in this case Munich, finished writing it. The first thermal event outside the US surfaced in June 2023. Failure rates ran higher in hot, humid Southeast Asian markets, which got a voluntary recall in February 2024. Only in September 2024 did the recall go worldwide.[5] Moisture is the villain here, and moisture does not respect model years. A seal deteriorates, water gets in, contacts corrode, current leaks where it should not, and a box full of electricity starts making heat it was never designed to make. On a motorcycle, that box sits under a rider who is straddling a fuel tank. Fire risk means something different when there is no door to open and no cabin to exit.

Do the subtraction the filings never do out loud. The 2024 campaign covered 5,048 bikes. The 2026 campaign covers 4,630. That is 418 fewer motorcycles, an 8.3 percent shrinkage, and neither filing explains it. Scrappage, exports, and the tighter "equipped with the optional reverse gear" scoping are all plausible. None is confirmed. What is confirmed is narrower and worse for anyone who assumed this was handled: NHTSA's notice states plainly that motorcycles previously repaired under the 2024 recall will need the new repair completed.[1] A prior dealer visit is not a clearance. It is a prerequisite for a second visit.

What you can actually do: if you own a 2021 through 2024 R 18 with the reverse gear option, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now rather than waiting for the letter, which BMW expects to mail November 27.[1] Watch for BMW's own warning signs from the 2024 owner letter: reverse gear stops working, a smell of overheating electrical components, or smoke. Any of those means stop riding, get away from traffic, and call BMW Motorrad Roadside Assistance at 1-877-680-2176 for a tow. Do not ride it to the dealer to show them the smoke. The remedy is a full replacement of the control unit, free of charge, and BMW customer service is at 1-800-525-7417 if your dealer claims not to know about campaign 26V658.

What this article did not prove: incident counts come from BMW's own field reports, not independent verification, and "thermal event" is BMW's term; the filings do not say how many tropical-market events involved open flame versus overheating or smoke. No US fire, crash, or injury is tied to this defect in any filing, and none is claimed here. The 418-bike population difference is arithmetic from the two campaign totals, not an explained fact. Individual owners may have received replacement units earlier under warranty; the two-year disconnected-feature framing rests on the interim remedy description plus the gap between campaigns.

Fair counter, stated at full strength: a two-year disconnect followed by a free replacement part is the recall system working at the speed of parts availability. BMW caught a moisture-driven failure mode, neutralized it immediately by cutting power to the unit, and shipped a real fix once one existed, with zero US injuries in the meantime. Against that, two facts from BMW's own report refuse to sit down. One: the neutralization only worked when the relay was installed correctly, and sometimes it was not. Two: it only worked when the unit was not already corroded, and sometimes it was. A safety intervention that depends on perfect installation and a clean unit is not a fix. It is a hope with a torque spec.
