The Bracket Holding Your Bronco's Airbag Can Shred It on Deployment. Ford Filed the Opposite Airbag Recall the Same Week.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
August 30, 2027

Let's talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds. In 2,711 model-year 2026 Ford Broncos, the side curtain airbag fires out of the roof rail and meets a metal burr on its own mounting bracket. The burr tears the fabric, and the curtain starts deflating before the crash is over.[1]

2,711
2026 Broncos recalled; the airbag's own bracket can tear it open

Side curtains are not frontal airbags, which is exactly why a tear matters. A frontal bag inflates and deflates in a fraction of a second. A curtain has to stay up. Side impacts and rollovers deliver multiple hits over a longer event, and the curtain also keeps you from being ejected through the side glass. A curtain that deflates early covers the window at the first impact and is gone by the second. Ford's filing says the tear "may cause faster curtain deflation," the filing's polite way of describing an airbag that quits mid-crash.[3]

What should annoy you as much as it annoys me: this is not a design flaw. After a production-layout change, a supplier skipped the deburring step: one skipped step, one bracket, the right-hand side of 2,711 Broncos built between June 27 and July 26, 2026.[2][5] There is no dashboard warning: your truck cannot tell you its airbag has a built-in shredder. Interim letters went out September 15, so the only detection tools you have are that letter and a VIN lookup.[2]

Ford filed two airbag recalls in the same batch, and they fail in opposite directions. Campaign 26V581 covers seven 2026 Explorers whose passenger airbag's active vent may not open, so the bag stays too firm instead of venting on schedule.[4] The Bronco's curtain goes soft too fast; the Explorer's bag will not go soft at all. Same defect category, opposite physics, same filing week. Somewhere in Ford's airbag engineering organization, September was a strange month.

Full strength to the other side. As of August 18, Ford knew of zero warranty claims, field reports, complaints, accidents, or injuries tied to the burr.[3] Ford found this through its own process controls and self-reported: the safety system working as designed. Ford's remedy is a dealer visit that increases the clearance between the bracket and the bag, free of charge, with remedy letters expected around October 30.[4] A torn curtain still deploys; the argument is about deflation rate, not absence. Demonstrated risk is smaller than headline risk, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

What the record does not show: no tear has been observed, so the per-deployment tear probability is unknown. No supplier is named in the public filings, and the skipped-deburring-step account comes from secondary reporting rather than the Part 573 itself.[5] Road Ethos counts this as the 2026 Bronco's ninth recall, a tally I am attributing rather than verifying.[4] The 2,711 figure is the build-window population, not a count of confirmed burred brackets.

Own a 2026 Bronco built between June 27 and July 26? Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls this week instead of waiting for the October 30 letter. No telltale means no warning, which means the lookup is the safety system. Shopping used? Check the VIN before you sign and confirm the clearance fix shows as completed. Airbag recalls you cannot feel, hear, or see on the dash are the ones that demand you go looking.
