# Research — 1156-nhtsa-ncap-deletes-rollover-test

## Proposed angle (1-2 sentences)
NHTSA wants to delete two legacy NCAP tests — the dynamic rollover Fishhook maneuver and side airbag out-of-position checks — effective with model year 2027, while the upgrades those cuts are supposed to fund (THOR dummies, oblique frontal test, crash-avoidance rating) slip to 2029-2033. The comment period closes October 16, 2026: three days from now.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? YES. A federal safety-rating program is deleting tests Congress legislated (TREAD Act 2000 §12 ordered the dynamic rollover test; SAFETEA-LU 2005 put the rollover star on the window sticker and wrote 15-passenger vans into the program). Comment deadline is imminent (Oct 16, 2026), so publication has actionability.
- Novel angle vs. existing coverage? The secondary coverage (theautowire) framed it as policy analysis. The Crash Report angle: quantify what the consumer actually loses — the rollover star becomes a geometry ratio (half track width / CG height), the dynamic maneuver Congress ordered dies, side airbags hitting out-of-position children go from "audited" to "honor system" (there is NO FMVSS covering side-bag OOP force; NCAP was the only mechanism checking), and the replacement dummies (THOR-50M, THOR-05F small-female, WorldSID-50M/05F) keep slipping — the small-female frontal dummy, built to measure what happens to a 108-pound adult, debuts 2031 with its side-impact equivalent in 2033. Meanwhile the crash-avoidance rating Congress ordered in the FAST Act (2015) and again in the IIJA (2021) is now targeted for 2028 — 13 years after the first order.
- Not covered before: queue + stories search for ncap-rollover/fishhook/thor-dummy/ncap-cut = zero hits. PROCEED.

## Original contribution
- The "gap arithmetic": cuts effective MY2027 vs. replacements arriving Q4 2029 / 2031 / 2033 — a 2-to-6-year window in which NCAP ratings shrink while the agency banks the savings. Combined with the 13-year (and counting) delay on the crash-avoidance rating Congress first ordered in 2015, this frames a structural pattern: deletions execute on schedule; additions slip.
- Consumer translation of the Fishhook deletion: post-MY2027, the rollover star rewards one thing — being low and wide. An EV with floor-mounted batteries buys a healthy SSF without any chassis cleverness; roll-rate-sensor stability control and rollover-triggered curtain bags earn no rating credit above the FMVSS 126 floor.
- The 15-passenger van case: Congress wrote them into the dynamic test in 2005 because loaded vans roll; their CG rises with every passenger, but SSF is measured once on an unloaded vehicle. Post-removal, the only dynamic test Congress specifically legislated for this class disappears.
- SAB-OOP: making explicit that there is no FMVSS governing side-airbag force on out-of-position occupants — NCAP was the sole audit of a voluntary commitment, and starting MY2026 manufacturers already self-attest. Deletion moves it to the honor system.

## Counterargument (full strength)
NHTSA's case is genuinely strong on both deletions. The Fishhook hasn't failed a vehicle in 15 years, and when it did, it barely moved ratings (1.6-4.8 pp) — a test that passes everyone tells buyers nothing, and NCAP's value is the gap between the law and the rating. ESC (FMVSS 126, 2008-2011 phase-in, verified by sine-with-dwell — a nastier maneuver than Fishhook) made the dynamic consumer test redundant. On SAB-OOP: ~2 failures/year across two decades, zero tied to a real-world injury, results non-reproducible by design, and therefore legally unusable — a check that can never trigger enforcement arguably misleads consumers more than no check. Freeing lab capacity for the oblique frontal test and THOR dummies is a defensible reallocation; the dummies, not the Fishhook, are the future of the program. The eleven pages of the notice are, on their face, competent regulatory housekeeping.

## Limitations
- All program statistics (tip-up counts, regression deltas, failure rates) are NHTSA's own from the notice; independent replication isn't available in the comment window.
- FARS (our data, 2014-2023) does not isolate "rollover risk under the Fishhook" — no cross-tab of the specific test's predictive power can be run here.
- The roadmap dates are proposed, not final; commenters could change them (comments close Oct 16, 2026).
- No observed real-world harm from the deletions yet — the article argues about rating information value, not body counts.

## Actionable insights (required)
1. File a comment at regulations.gov (docket NHTSA-2026-1651) by October 16, 2026 if you have a view.
2. Shopping a MY2027+ vehicle? Read the rollover star as geometry-only: low and wide wins; ask dealers specifically about roll-rate-sensor stability control and rollover-triggered curtain airbags, because the rating won't reward them.
3. Buying a used 15-passenger van for church/school/shuttle? The dynamic rollover test class is disappearing; check SSF-derived ratings and confirm ESC + roll stability control, and never load roof cargo.
4. Parents of small kids: side airbag out-of-position auditing ends; keep children in the back seat, outboard seating, properly belted/boosted — the 2003-era concern (child leaning against the door when a seatback bag fires) is now self-attested by manufacturers.
