## Scores

**1. General Editor — 8.8/10.** Strong consumer headline with a concrete time hook ("This Month"). Structure: lede → the gap → the numbers → the timeline → honest accounting → actionable close. The 2.2x pull-stat lands early and frames everything. Minor deduction: headline says "Your Car's" while the rule covers only new builds — the body corrects it ("Your current car will never get this alarm"), but a first-glance reader gets a beat of overclaim.

**2. Voice Coach — 8.7/10.** Clara is distinct from the roster: direct, practical, slightly angry on the consumer's behalf ("decline firmly", "do not buy the defeat clip", "congratulations. That means it is working."). No banned phrases, no em dashes, rhythm passes. Minor: "This is federal law now" is colloquial for what is technically a federal standard (FMVSS), not a statute — acceptable in voice, flagged for accuracy review.

**3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.8/10.** No victim-blaming of crash victims; the piece aims its edge at regulators and dealers, not the dead. The defeat-clip warning is pro-safety. The tone toward non-bucklers ("the defiant will keep riding loose") is firm but stops short of cruelty, and the counterargument section grants them a rational-choice reading (short trips, rideshares) before rebutting.

**4. Social Shareability — 8.7/10.** Two strong shareable units: the 2.2x pull-stat and "fifteen years from a congressional instruction to a rear-seat chime." The "child born in 2012 is in high school" line is the quote-tweet payload. Slightly long for skimmers, but the pull-stat and actionable close carry it.

**5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10.** All load-bearing claims verified against primary/near-primary sources: FMVSS 208 amendment (Dec 16, 2024 final rule), front compliance Sept 1 2026 / rear Sept 1 2027, 50 lives / 500+ injuries modeled estimate attributed to NHTSA, 91.6%/81.7% belt-use figures (2022), ~half of 2022 occupant deaths unbelted, MAP-21 (2012) congressional direction, ≤10,000 lbs GVWR scope. The 55%/74% rear-belt effectiveness figures are honestly labeled as "NHTSA figures via secondary reporting" rather than presented as directly verified. The "federal law" colloquialism is the only nit.

**6. Research Rigor — 8.9/10.** Original contributions: the 2.2x inverted-phase-in ratio (computed from NHTSA's own belt-use figures), the 15-year beep timeline (2012→2027), the one-life-a-week reframing of 50 lives/year. Limitations stated in-text: estimates are modeled not measured, data is 2022, fleet turnover means "fully implemented" is a decade out. Strongest counterargument given full strength (Europe's experience, deliberate non-users, $5 defeat clips). Methodology transparent: the 2.2x is labeled "our arithmetic" in the disclaimer. No invented absolutes.

**7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10.** Percentages presented as percentages with the underlying rates visible; the pull-stat pairs the ratio with its components. No chart needed — the numbers are simple enough to carry in prose. The 91.6%/81.7% → 8.4%/18.3% flip is explained rather than assumed.
