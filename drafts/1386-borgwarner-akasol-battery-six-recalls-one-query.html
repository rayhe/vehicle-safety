Six Recalls, One Battery, and a Federal Question With No Answer Yet

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 4, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better. On October 30, 2026, NHTSA opened recall query RQ26005 into BorgWarner Akasol Gen 3 battery packs, and its published summary is exactly two phrases long: "scope and remedy effectiveness" of six recalls.[1] Not a new defect. A federal audit of whether the old fix worked.

6
Recall campaigns covering one battery pack, across five brands

Count them: 25E035 is BorgWarner's own equipment filing. 25V055 is Volvo. 25V185 is Mack. 25V184 is Nova Bus. 25E018 is Cummins. 25V203 is Gillig. Six campaign numbers, one component: the Gen 3 Akasol HV TCC, a high-voltage terminal connector counterpart built between July 16, 2023 and August 8, 2024.[2] A fleet running mixed electrics from those brands must track six notices to follow one defect, because BorgWarner filed an information report under 49 CFR 573.3(f) instead of a recall, which left each vehicle maker to run its own remedy.[2]

Inside the pack, the failure is almost insultingly small. Manufacturing parameters "relative to torque and rework" left hardware that could loosen, detach, and short the pack.[4] A shorted high-voltage battery brings two consequences at once: loss of drive power, and fire risk. BorgWarner's filing names about 204 suspect packs by brand, 138 for Volvo and Mack combined, 21 for Nova Bus, 26 for Gillig, 19 for Cummins.[2] Volvo's own vehicle recall, 25V055, covers 153 VNRE trucks, 142 in the United States and 11 in Canada.[4] Do not add the equipment-report unit count to those pack counts. They measure different things, and adding them would double-count the same hardware.

Now the timeline, because the timeline is the indictment. January 6, 2025: Volvo Bus Europe reports a thermal runaway on an Akasol Gen 3 battery at a body builder in Egypt, a pack not yet installed on any vehicle.[3] January 28: BorgWarner admits the suspect parameters also appeared at its Hazel Park plant in Michigan, after initial analysis had blamed only Darmstadt, Germany.[3] March 20: a new suspect list expands the impact across Mack and Volvo in both the United States and Canada.[3] October 30, 2026: NHTSA opens its query. January 6, 2025 to October 30, 2026 is 662 days from first thermal event to a federal question about whether the fix held, and secondary coverage characterized the query as citing repeated expansions and post-remedy failures, a characterization NHTSA's own summary does not itself make.[6]

Worse: this battery generation had already done this dance once. In 2023, Akasol Gen 3 packs were recalled because over-torqued rivnut inserts on the cell bus bars could snap off and rattle around as debris, shorting the pack; 173 vehicles got replacement batteries whether or not they had completed an earlier Akasol recall.[5] Over-torque in 2023, under-torque and rework in 2025, a remedy-effectiveness query in 2026. Three chapters, one generation, one failure family: torque applied wrong inside a high-voltage box, leaving loose metal where a short can start.

Steel-man the other side, because it deserves steel. About two hundred suspect packs. Zero reported accidents, zero injuries, zero warranty claims in the 2025 defect determinations.[3] Every affected vehicle belongs to a commercial fleet with a maintenance department, and a recall query is routine oversight, not a recall; roughly half of all NHTSA investigations end without one. No family sedan is involved, nobody has been hurt, and the 2023 precedent shows the same supplier replacing whole batteries proactively. All true, and this is also true: these vehicles are buses and trucks, a shorted pack is a fire risk with passengers aboard or a depot full of chargers nearby, and a query about remedy effectiveness exists precisely because a six-campaign patchwork may not have scoped or held. Small populations with catastrophic failure modes are exactly where "did the fix work" is the right question.

What this article does not prove matters too. RQ26005 publishes no complaint, crash, fire, or injury counts in its resume excerpt, so none appear here; inventing them would be fabrication. Nor does the 2023 rivnut recall prove the 2025 fix failed, since the two are distinct mechanisms inside one generation. And RQ26005 covers commercial trucks and buses only, so nothing here generalizes to consumer EVs, whose packs come from different suppliers under different campaigns.

If you run a fleet with Volvo VNRE, Mack LR Electric, Nova Bus LFSe, Gillig Low Floor Electric, or Cummins-powered equipment on Akasol Gen 3 packs from July 2023 to August 2024, check VINs against 25V055, 25V185, 25V184, 25V203, and 25E018, and do not assume a 2025 remedy closed the file while RQ26005 is open. Ask the vehicle maker, not BorgWarner, whether your packs sit inside the query's scope review, since BorgWarner filed an information report rather than a recall, and check any vehicle free at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
