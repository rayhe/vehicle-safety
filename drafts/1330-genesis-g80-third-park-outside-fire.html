Three Strikes, You're Out of the Garage

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 30, 2026

The numbers don't lie, but they do occasionally smirk. On October 9, NHTSA published campaign 26V664, recalling 94,622 Genesis sedans because a damaged front power-seat switch can stick in the "on" position, keep the seat motor running after you walk away, and eventually set the seat, and then the car, on fire.[1] In your garage at 2 AM, with nobody inside.

3
Distinct park-outside fire recalls issued against the Genesis G80 model line: 2021, 2024, and 2026

What makes this one remarkable isn't the mechanism, though a seat switch achieving arson is a strong contender for strangest recall of the year; it's the count. I pulled NHTSA's public recalls API and cross-referenced every campaign filed against the Genesis G80 line, and this is Strike Three, the third separate park-outside fire advisory for the same model family.

Strike One landed in 2021: recall 21V160, an ABS module that could short-circuit and start an engine-compartment fire, park outside until fixed.[2] Strike Two came in February 2024: recall 24V107, water leaking into the starter solenoid, electrical short, fire while parked or driving, park outside until fixed.[3] Now Strike Three, from a switch that slides your seat forward. Three suppliers, three unrelated components, three garage bans in roughly five years: one every two and a half years, like clockwork.

And Strike Three has a feature the first two didn't: there is no fix. NHTSA's remedy field reads, verbatim, "The remedy is currently under development."[1] Owner letters don't go out until December 7. Your 2018 G80 sits in the driveway until then, or you take the loaner Hyundai is offering.

Now the honest math, because a noir detective respects the ledger. Two alleged U.S. fires, zero crashes, zero injuries.[1] That's 2 fires across 94,622 vehicles (about 1 in 47,000, or 0.0021%). Your actual risk is a rounding error; what isn't a rounding error is the timeline: Hyundai first learned of a seat fire on December 31, 2021, recovered the seat from a second fire in late 2023, and in 2024 made an explicit decision to keep monitoring rather than recall. More overheating reports surfaced in Korea that July, and only then, on October 1, 2026, nearly five years after the first fire — did the recall happen.[4] Five years of watching a seat switch burn.

There's a structural point buried in all this, and it's the reason I ran the search in the first place. The Genesis G80 appears zero times in a decade of FARS crash-fatality data. Not because it's the safest car ever built. FARS only counts crashes, and a parked car in a garage isn't a crash. Every fatality statistic this site has ever published is blind to the entire category of parked-car fires. The three defects above never killed anyone in a wreck, so they never show up in the tables. The hazard surface extends past the driveway, and the federal crash database simply doesn't look there.

Fairness demands the counterargument at full strength: the three strikes share nothing but a badge. An ABS module, a starter solenoid, and a seat switch are three different engineering domains from three different supply chains; Hyundai's 2023 ABS fire recall alone swept up 3.3 million vehicles across a dozen models, so the first two strikes are industry-wide gremlins, not a Genesis curse. Most of the 94,622 owners will park outside, get the free fix eventually, and never smell anything but leather. Three garage bans is a memorable statistic. The marginal danger of Strike Three is barely above zero.

That said: if your VIN is on the list, the car sleeps outside tonight. A 1-in-47,000 risk that lands on your house isn't a statistic anymore. It's a claim.

What to do: Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; don't wait for the December 7 letter; VINs have been searchable since October 13. If 26V664 is open on your Genesis or G80, park outside and away from structures until the repair exists. Warning signs: a burning smell from the seat area, or a seat switch that stops working. Call a dealer immediately. Hyundai and Genesis dealers will provide loaner or alternate transportation at no cost, and the eventual repair is free even if you're out of warranty.
