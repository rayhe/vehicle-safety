# Research: 882 — Jeep Grand Cherokee Coil Spring, Third Recall

## Angle
Jeep recalled 328,381 Grand Cherokees because rear coil springs can detach while driving. It is the THIRD recall for the same defect (23V413 in Jun 2023 -> 26V051 in Jan 2026 -> 26V562 in Sep 2026). The second recall happened because dealers botched the first recall's repairs; the third happens because the second still didn't finish the job. Owners who already had their Jeep "fixed" must go back. Nobody has the actual fix yet: only interim letters go out Sept 29.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy: 328K vehicles, physical suspension component separating at speed, third campaign in 39 months. Genuine escalation story, not a routine recall rehash.
- Novel: the "recall of a recall of a recall" chain; Stellantis's own 0.1% defect estimate vs observed 0.12% warranty-claim rate (computed cross-check); ~23% of the original recall population needed re-repair under the second campaign.
- Site coverage: grep "coil spring" in stories/ -> zero hits. Clean.
- Journalist: Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; direct, practical; "what this means for you").

## Key numbers
- 328,381 total (97,477 + 215,433 + 15,471)
- Estimated defect rate 0.1% -> ~328 vehicles expected with an actual mis-seated spring
- Observed: 406 warranty claims / 328,381 = 0.124% — consistent with 0.1% estimate (cross-check)
- Campaign 2 population 80,620 / campaign 1 population ~345,000 = 23.4% of "fixed" vehicles needed re-fixing
- 29 customer assistance records + 406 warranty claims, zero reported accidents/injuries (as of Aug 6, 2026)
- Timeline: Jun 1, 2023 (23V413) -> Jan 22, 2026 (26V051) -> Sep 2026 (26V562/89D) = 39 months, 3 campaigns, 1 defect

## Original contribution
1. Warranty-claim rate (0.12%) vs Stellantis defect estimate (0.1%) cross-check: independent validation that the company's tiny-number estimate is credible.
2. Re-repair math: 23% of vehicles from the first campaign were pulled back in the second. The first "fix" had a ~1-in-4 failure-to-fix rate.
3. Counting campaigns as a safety metric: the number of recalls per defect as a measure of manufacturer follow-through.

## Strongest counterargument
0.1% is 328 cars. No injuries in 39 months. Stellantis found the problem itself, filed voluntarily each time, and owners get free repairs. Three recalls is the system WORKING, not failing — a manufacturer that keeps chasing a residual defect is better than one that doesn't. Also: the second and third campaigns mostly re-inspect vehicles, and only the mis-installed ones get new isolators.

## Limitations
- FARS has no role here; this is a recall mechanics story, not a fatality analysis. Cannot claim injury/fatality outcomes.
- All defect-rate and complaint numbers come from Stellantis's own filings to NHTSA; no independent verification of the 0.1% estimate.
- "Detach while driving" severity: NHTSA consequence language is "road hazard to other vehicles" — the spring leaves the vehicle; we don't have data on how often a detached spring causes a secondary crash.
- Recall completion rates for 23V413/26V051 are not public in these sources; the 23% re-repair figure assumes campaign 2's population is a subset of campaign 1's, which the filings support but with air-suspension exclusions.

## Actionable
- Own a 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee (any trim incl. 4xe)? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable since Sept 9, 2026).
- Already had the spring inspected under a prior recall? You still go back. This is explicit in the filing.
- Interim letter arrives Sept 29-Oct 7; the final-remedy letter comes later. Call the dealer, get on the list, don't wait for the second letter.
