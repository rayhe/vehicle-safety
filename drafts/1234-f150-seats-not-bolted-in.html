Your F-150's Front Seats Might Not Be Bolted In. Ford's Tracking System Says the Repair Was Done. It Wasn't.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 21, 2026

Check your VIN before you drive to work tomorrow. Ford is recalling 2,276 F-150 pickups from the 2025 through 2027 model years because the front seats may not have been properly secured to the vehicle. Read that again slowly: not the seatbelt, the seat, the thing the seatbelt is attached to.[1]

2,276 trucks
2025-2027 F-150s whose front seats may never have been bolted down properly

Every crash test you have ever watched assumes the seat stays put. The airbag deploys at a dummy whose seat is welded to the test sled, and the seatbelt pretensioner yanks against a seat that does not move. NHTSA's filing states the consequence in the flat language of people who have seen what happens when the assumption breaks: an improperly secured seat may not adequately restrain the occupant, increasing the risk of injury during a crash.[1] Your restraint system is only as strong as its least-checked bolt.

Ford's defect report reads like a short story about how quality control dies. On June 23, a warranty report flagged a truck with a missing front seat-to-body bolt; the passenger-side bolt on the same truck was not fully seated. Investigators traced it to one workstation where an autoworker could not secure the seat within the allotted cycle time. The line stopped, as protocol demands, and then a team leader marked the repair complete in the tracking system. Nobody had done the repair.[2]

That is the detail worth sitting with. One missed bolt is a slip; a repair logged as finished when nobody finished it is the system grading its own homework and giving itself an A. Ford's warranty and field reports eventually surfaced 62 VINs with improperly secured seats, and the recall population grew to 2,276 trucks built between August 25, 2025 and September 23, 2026. Somewhere in that window, a record said "fixed" and a truck said otherwise.[2]

And this is not the F-150's first attachment problem this fall. A separate campaign, 26V578, covers 2023-2027 F-150s whose fuel tanks may be improperly secured and could detach. Owner letters for that one went out September 21, and now the seats join the list: the gas tank might fall out and the front seats might not be bolted in, on the same nameplate, weeks apart. America's best-selling truck keeps losing the parts that hold it together.[3]

62 confirmed VINs
Found in warranty and field reports before Ford sized the recall at 2,276

To be fair to Ford, and fairness costs me nothing here: the warranty process caught this. One report in June, an investigation, a recall filed October 6. That is the system working. That population is tiny against the hundreds of thousands of F-150s Ford builds every year, the fix is a visual inspection and a torque wrench, and it is free at any dealer. Most F-150s are fine. Yours is probably fine. "Probably" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, which is why the VIN lookup exists.[4]

Here is what I want you to do, and I want you to do it today. VIN lookups for campaign 26V655 (Ford's number: 26S74) have been live on nhtsa.gov since October 9. Owner letters, though, do not go out until October 26. That is a 17-day gap where you can know something Ford has not mailed you yet. Run both campaigns while you are there, 26V655 for the seats and 26V578 for the fuel tank, because they cover different trucks and a clear result on one says nothing about the other.[3]

If your truck is in the population, the dealer inspects the front seat bolts and tightens or replaces them at no charge. If you have ever felt a clunk or a small shift from the seat base when you brake hard, do not file that under "trucks are like that." On these trucks, it may not have been normal. It may have been the bolt.[5]
