Rivian's Tire Sticker Is Wrong Again. The Fix Is Another Sticker.

Clara Rollover • Consumer Safety Advocate
November 25, 2026

Open the driver's door of your car and look at the door jamb. There is a small white sticker there, the tire placard, and it tells you the correct tire size and the correct cold inflation pressure for your exact vehicle. Rivian just recalled 1,619 R1S, R1T, and R2 vehicles because that sticker may be wrong, not the tires and not the wheels.[1] And this is the second time in 29 months Rivian has recalled vehicles for printing wrong tire information on the same label.[3]

1,619
Rivian R1S, R1T, and R2 vehicles recalled because the tire placard may list the wrong wheel, tire, and spare configuration

Read the filing, because the language is refreshingly blunt. NHTSA campaign 26V761, filed November 19, 2026, covers 2022 through 2027 R1S and R1T vehicles plus the 2027 R2, built between October 18, 2021 and August 25, 2026.[1] Rivian's own number for it is FSAM-1900. The defect, in the filing's words: the tire placard and certification label "may not correctly list the wheel, tire, and spare tire configuration of the vehicle," which means the vehicles fail Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 110, "Tire Selection and Rims."[1] And the risk, again in the filing's words: "The tires may be improperly inflated and/or the wrong size replacement tire may be installed after referencing the incorrect information, increasing the risk of a crash."[1] Reuters reported the recall on November 25.[2]

Now the part that should bother you. Rivian has done this before. In June 2024 the company recalled 666 R1S and R1T vehicles under campaign 24V458 because the maximum vehicle capacity weight printed on the tire placard was incorrect, which could lead owners to overload the vehicle.[3] That recall, internal number FSAM-1491, was fixed by mailing owners a corrected overlay sticker. Two years later, the same regulation, the same label, the same failure mode: wrong tire information, printed on the one sticker every owner is told to trust. First time, 666 vehicles and one wrong field. Second time, 1,619 vehicles, three wrong fields, five model years, and a brand-new model, the R2, swept into the recall before its mass-market rollout has even scaled.

Pause on what the placard actually is, because its boringness is the whole point. That sticker is the single source of truth for your tires. It is where the tire shop looks when it picks your replacement tires. It is where you look when you check pressure. It is also, quietly, the baseline your tire pressure monitoring system is calibrated against: the warning light is designed to fire when pressure falls 25 percent below the placard number.[5] So when the placard number is wrong, the warning is anchored to a wrong number too. A driver doing everything right, checking the door jamb, trusting the dash light, is still wrong, because the safety system trusts the sticker and the sticker is the thing that is broken. NHTSA's own tire research puts the stakes at an estimated 414 deaths, 10,275 injuries, and 78,392 crashes a year from flat tires and blowouts, and tires underinflated by more than 25 percent are about three times more likely to be a critical factor in a crash.[4]

Then there is the spare, which the filing covers too. A wrong spare listing means the one moment an owner is guaranteed to trust the placard, on the shoulder of a highway, in the rain, swapping on a spare, is the moment it matters most. Put the wrong size spare on a truck with all-wheel drive and you are not just limping home. You are driving a vehicle whose handling the manufacturer never validated in that configuration.

And you cannot even check whether your truck is affected. Rivian filed the recall on November 19, but the VINs will not be searchable on NHTSA.gov until January 18, 2027, the same day owner letters are expected to go out.[1] That is a two-month blind window, running straight through winter, in which the only way to verify your placard is to compare it by hand against the tire sidewall and the spare, a check almost nobody does, on a sticker almost nobody reads. Rivian's remedy is a replacement placard, free of charge. You just have to wait two months to find out whether you need one, and the wrong sticker stays on your door jamb until then.

Say the defense at full strength, because it is real. This is 1,619 vehicles against roughly 200,000 Rivians on the road. No crashes, injuries, or complaints are listed in the filing. The defect is a misprinted label, not a broken part, and Rivian found it and reported it itself. A free sticker is about the cheapest recall remedy in existence. All true. But small recalls of cheap defects are exactly how systemic process failures announce themselves, and this is the second announcement in 29 months for the same label under the same regulation. A company that misprints the safety sticker twice has a process problem, not a typo problem. And the two-month VIN blackout turns a small recall into a quiet one, which is the worst kind: the owners who most need to know are the ones least likely to find out.

What to do, concretely. If you own a 2022 through 2027 R1S or R1T, or a 2027 R2, open the driver's door and read the placard, then read the tire sidewall on all four tires and the spare. If the sizes do not match, call Rivian at 1-888-748-4261 and reference FSAM-1900. Inflate to the pressure on the tire manufacturer's guidance for your actual tire size, not the placard, until Rivian replaces it. Do not buy a replacement tire or a spare based on the placard alone. And mark January 18, 2027 on your calendar, because that is the first day NHTSA's VIN lookup will tell you whether your truck is one of the 1,619. Until then, the sticker is a rumor. Verify it yourself.
