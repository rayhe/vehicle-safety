# Research — #1326: Ford's 7.3L "Godzilla" V8, NHTSA defect petition DP26011

**Slug:** 1326-ford-godzilla-idle-patch-quiet-fix
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Working title:** "Ford Already Fixed the Godzilla V8. It Just Didn't Tell Anybody."

## The kill test
- **Newsworthy:** NHTSA ODI opened defect petition DP26011 on October 23, 2026 (petition filed June 13, 2026, ODI no. 11764421). Fresh: multiple outlets reported Oct 26-28, 2026.
- **Novel angle:** The petition's most damning claim isn't the cam/lifter wear itself. It's that Ford allegedly already deployed the remedy — a software calibration raising idle speed to ~1,000 rpm to restore oil pressure — without notifying owners or issuing a recall. A fix that exists but was never announced is a confession written in code.
- **Second angle:** Ford's own dealer network has been replacing cams on these engines since July 2025 under TSB 25-2322 (service kit LC3Z-6250-D), which is a TSB, not a recall — owners only learn about it if they complain about a tick.
- **Surprising:** The Godzilla is marketed as the bulletproof fleet engine (buyers chose it specifically because it wasn't the diesel). One NC fleet owner: 5 of 11 trucks failed, earliest at 45,745 miles.
- **Actionable:** Yes — "if your 7.3L ticks at idle, ask the dealer to follow TSB 25-2322 and keep every repair order; file an NHTSA complaint citing DP26011."
- **VERDICT: PROCEED.**

## Hard facts (verified across sources)
1. NHTSA ODI received a defect petition June 13, 2026; opened defect-petition review DP26011 on October 23, 2026. This is a petition review (grant/deny decision pending), not a formal investigation or recall. (backfirenews.com)
2. Scope: ~54,787 vehicles — 2021-2022 Ford E-350, 2021 F-59, 2021 F-750 with 7.3L Godzilla V8 (autoblog, carscoops). Same engine also in F-Super Duty, F-53 — outside petition scope.
3. Petition claim: oil pressure at low idle ~6 psi fails to lubricate lifter rollers and cam lobes → severe wear → catastrophic failure, sudden loss of motive power at highway speed. (backfirenews, motorbiscuit)
4. Petition claim: Ford released a software calibration patch raising idle to ~1,000 rpm, did not notify owners, issued no recall. (motorbiscuit, topspeed, carbuzz)
5. Ford TSB 25-2322 (July 16, 2025, supersedes 25-2200): symptom = upper-engine ticking/tapping at idle, sometimes rough run, misfire, codes P0301-P0308; cause = excess wear of camshaft and/or valve tappets; fix = service kit LC3Z-6250-D (new cam + all tappets), long block if damage wider. Covers 2021-2025 E-Series, F-650/F-750 7.3L, 2020-2025 F-53/F-59, 2020-2025 Super Duty 7.3L (and 6.8L from 2023). (backfirenews)
6. NC fleet owner complaint (Feb 2024): 11 trucks with 7.3L, five engine failures, 45,745-103,177 miles. (topspeed)
7. NHTSA database: 20 complaints on the 2021 E-350; five describe engine failure; two name the camshaft (one: cam + lifter failure, replacement engine, incident July 31, 2024; other: cam lobe and valve tappet wear). (backfirenews)
8. Agency's first summary: no crashes, fires, injuries, or fatalities attributed. (autoblog, carscoops)
9. 49 CFR 552.8: NHTSA must notify petitioner within 120 days of receipt (June 13 → ~Oct 11). Record dated Oct 23; a denial requires Federal Register notice with reasons within 45 days. (backfirenews)
10. Engine launched 2020; 7.3L Godzilla + 6.8L variant; ~50k-55k vehicles in probe. (wisevoter, hotcars)

## FARS grounding
- Ford E-350 (2014-2023): 776 deaths, 77.6/year, rate 2.51/100M VMT (vs. Ford F-350 pickup rate 0.15). High because vans are commercial work vehicles with high exposure and rollover propensity — context only, not part of the defect.

## What we did NOT prove (limitations)
- The petition is an allegation; NHTSA has not found a defect. DP26011 asks whether the petition should be granted or denied.
- No crash/injury data ties the cam wear to actual road failures; ODI's summary cites a single reported incident.
- The "quiet software patch" is the petitioner's claim, not Ford's admission. Ford has not responded on the public record.
- Petition itself (ODI 11764421) is not posted publicly; all detail comes from NHTSA's summary plus reporting.
- Only 2021-2022 E-350, 2021 F59, 2021 F-750 named; Super Duty 7.3L owners are outside the scope but share the engine.

## Counterargument (at full strength)
One reported incident is a thin reed for a federal probe. The Godzilla has hundreds of thousands of engines in service; a 6 psi idle oil pressure is low but not automatically fatal to cams, and lifter wear can come from oil change intervals, oil grade, or idling habits — all owner-controlled variables a fleet abusing work vans might not control well. Ford's idle-speed calibration update could be routine drivability tuning, not a stealth defect fix, and the TSB is just a repair procedure — the absence of a recall may mean the failure rate is unremarkable. If NHTSA grants the petition and finds the failure rate is, say, 0.2% in commercial duty cycles, Ford's "we'll fix the ones that tick" posture may turn out to be the rational response rather than a cover-up.

## Original contribution
Cross-tabulating the TSB (July 2025) against the petition timeline (June 2026) and the software-patch claim: Ford's dealer channel has been performing cam replacements under a quiet TSB for over a year while the public record holds no recall and (per the petitioner) owners were never told why idle RPM changed. That's a documented two-track remedy: silent for most owners, surgical for those who complain. Worth one sentence of math: 54,787 vehicles in scope; 20 ODI complaints on the 2021 E-350 — a 0.04% observed complaint rate, which is exactly why the fleet owner's 5-of-11 (45%) is either the canary or the outlier.
