## Verified facts
- **Kicks:** redesigned for 2025, initially earned POOR in updated moderate overlap front test. Nissan revised rear seat belts and load limiters on vehicles built after June 2026; retest earned GOOD. 2026–27 Kicks built after June 2026 qualify for Top Safety Pick. Kicks missed Top Safety Pick+ only because its vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention scored Marginal (not required for base Pick).
- **RAV4 (redesigned 2026):** hybrid rated ACCEPTABLE in updated moderate overlap; plug-in hybrid rated MARGINAL, with IIHS rating rear-seat restraints and dummy kinematics POOR. Both earned Good in every other category (small overlap, side). Missed Top Safety Pick entirely.
- **Audi Q3 (redesigned 2026–27):** POOR in updated moderate overlap. Rear lap belt slipped from pelvis to abdomen; moderate risk of rear chest injury as shoulder belt rode toward dummy's neck. Good in all other categories. No award.
- **Chevrolet Trailblazer (2026):** POOR in updated moderate overlap; also fell short on headlights and front crash prevention.
- **Volkswagen Jetta (2027):** GOOD in updated moderate overlap — the only other Good in the round besides the Kicks.
- **Failure mechanism (RAV4 PHEV, Q3):** shoulder belt migrates toward the dummy's neck; lap belt rides up from pelvis onto abdomen, raising abdominal-injury risk. Same failure mode that sank the original Kicks.
- **Test mechanics:** 40 mph moderate overlap front; driver dummy = 50th-percentile adult male; rear dummy = size of 5th-percentile smaller woman, roughly a 12-year-old child, belted behind the driver. IIHS added the rear dummy in 2022 because rear-seat protection had fallen behind front-seat protection; scoring tightened again in 2024.
- **Sales context:** RAV4 was America's top-selling SUV in 2025 with 479,288 units sold, third-best-selling vehicle overall behind only the Ford F-Series and Chevrolet Silverado (CarBuzz, automaker sales data). 2026 RAV4 starting MSRP $31,900 (Toyota.com); 2027 from $32,300.
- **Price context:** Kicks starts at $22,730 (Autoblog, 2025 model) / ~$23,342 new per iSeeCars. Roughly $9,000 cheaper than the cheapest RAV4.
- **Why it matters:** rear seats are where children ride. The test exists because automakers spent two decades armoring the front seats while the back seat got decade-old belt geometry.

## Actionable takeaways (for article)
- Shopping a 2026 RAV4? The hybrid is Acceptable and the PHEV Marginal on rear-seat protection. If your kids ride in back, wait for Toyota to revise the rear belts — Nissan proved the fix is a parts change, not a redesign — or cross-shop the Kicks.
- Buying a used/new Kicks? Only 2026–27 models BUILT AFTER JUNE 2026 carry the fix. Check the build date on the door jamb sticker before signing; pre-June cars carry the Poor rating.
- The award to check is Top Safety Pick, and the test to ask about by name is the UPDATED moderate overlap front test. A Good small-overlap score tells you nothing about the back seat.

## Limitations / counterarguments
- IIHS ratings are lab tests on new vehicles, not real-world fatality outcomes; FARS data will take years to show whether these rear-belt differences move the death count.
- The Kicks' own front crash prevention (vehicle-to-vehicle) is only Marginal — it beat the RAV4 on the test that decides the award, not on every metric.
- Toyota could revise the RAV4's rear belts at any time, as Nissan did; a 2027 running change would make this article's RAV4 criticism stale. Note the build-date caveat.
- Price gap is approximate: Kicks $22,730 is the 2025 base; 2026 pricing may differ slightly. MSRPs exclude destination/fees.
