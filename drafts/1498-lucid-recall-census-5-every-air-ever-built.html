Lucid Recalled 5 Times in 2026. The Biggest One Was Every Air Ever Built.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

Lucid filed five safety recalls in 2026 that touch a 2026-model-year vehicle. Five campaigns, 50,004 campaign-units, and the largest of them covers 27,185 Air sedans. Read that number again. Lucid has delivered roughly 46,000 vehicles in its entire history, and one recall covers 27,185 of them, which is essentially every Air ever built, with Lucid estimating that 100 percent of the population carries the defect. Three fires are already on the record. Before you sign for a used Air, you might want to see this.[1]

27,185
Air sedans in the fire recall 26V540. That is essentially every Air Lucid has ever built, with a 100 percent defect estimate.

Method first, because a census is only as honest as its counting rules. NHTSA’s recalls API returns a 400 when no recall records exist for a model year, and rows when they do. I queried every model the federal vehicle database lists for Lucid in 2026, and the list is exactly two names, Air and Gravity, 14 queries with suffix variants, and two returned rows. Five campaigns survived after keeping only 26V numbers filed in calendar 2026 and deduplicating by campaign number. Two models is the cleanest coverage in the whole twenty-brand series, because there is no third Lucid to miss, and the midsize Cosmos only begins small-volume production at the end of 2026. Both models were recalled. There is no clean sheet in this census.[1]

January brought two camera recalls on the same day, and they are the same failure on two different software stacks. On January 16 Lucid filed 26V017 for the Air and 26V018 for the Gravity, both for a rearview image that may not display when the car shifts into reverse, both failing FMVSS 111. Air population: 10,816 cars with the DreamDrive Pro package, running software 2.8.0 through 2.8.16, where a compute-load spike on waking from sleep could keep the camera image from reaching the screen. Gravity population: 3,900 SUVs running software older than 3.3.20, where the software mishandled power state and camera view signals. Two platforms, two codebases, one blank screen, one day. That is what it costs to build two cars on two software stacks, and it is why the series counts them as two campaigns.[2][3]

Here is the part that should make every Lucid owner check a version number instead of waiting for a letter. Both camera fixes were already out before the filings landed. Gravity remedy 3.3.20 went over the air on December 11, 2025, more than a month before the January filing, and Lucid could see through telematics that 3,462 Gravity owners had installed it while 438 had not. Air remedy 2.8.17 went out shortly after a November stop-sale, and more than 9,000 Airs had it by mid-January. Same pattern in August, bigger stakes. Fire recall 26V540’s remedy, software 2.10.0, went out in July 2026, a month before the filing, and 20,719 owners had installed it while 6,466 had not. An over-the-air fix that is available is not a fix that is installed, and those 6,904 unpatched owners are the actual risk pool. NHTSA paperwork is the only mechanism that reaches them, because the car cannot force an owner to tap accept.[3][6]

August brought the fire, and it is the campaign this census is about. On August 20 Lucid filed 26V540: software logic for the low-voltage exterior lighting circuits, run through an electronic fuse, may provide insufficient overcurrent protection by allowing a circuit to stay energized at excess amperage, so the circuit can overheat. Population: 27,185 Air sedans built between October 11, 2021 and July 27, 2026. Defect estimate: 100 percent. Fires: three. Smoke incidents: four. NHTSA’s subject line reads “Park Outside: Exterior Lighting Circuit May Cause Fire,” and Lucid told owners to keep affected cars away from structures until the remedy software is installed. Parking only, not a stop-driving order. Remedy 2.10.0 lowers the eFuse amperage threshold and disables retry logic that could re-energize a faulted circuit in the same key cycle. Owner letters went out October 16. No other campaign in the twenty-brand series covers a brand’s entire production history with a 100 percent defect estimate and fires already on the record.[6]

March brought the two physical recalls, and both of them are supplier recalls. Lucid designed neither failure. It bought both. On March 26 Lucid filed 26V192 for the Gravity: second-row lap belt anchor brackets with welds shorter than specified and in the wrong location, because seat supplier Camaco changed its manufacturing process “without notice to or approval by Lucid.” Population: 4,476 Gravity SUVs, which is all customer vehicles manufactured before February 14, 2026, effectively every Gravity built to that date, and the first Gravity production figure Lucid has ever disclosed in any filing. NHTSA estimates 97 percent carry the defect. Lucid found it on January 22 during testing for an unrelated issue, issued a stop-sale on January 28, and resumed building with corrected seats on February 14. Trade press reported the stop-sale lasted 29 days. Remedy is an inspection plus a reinforcement bracket with adhesive, or a whole new seat, free. Think about what that defect actually is: a seat belt anchor that may not hold in a crash, because a supplier quietly changed a weld.[4]

Same day, same story, different supplier. Also on March 26 Lucid filed 26V193 for the Air Pure Rear-Wheel Drive: improperly secured half-shaft bolts may let the half-shaft disconnect from the drive unit, causing an unwarned loss of drive power. Population: 3,627 cars, production September 2023 through July 2025. Root cause is an interruption of torque operations during the adhesive curing window for the bolts, plus bolts with damaged adhesive, contaminated half-shafts, and reused bolts with cured adhesive. Half-shafts came from GKN Automotive in Mexico, bolts and brackets from Bossard in Phoenix. Only the Pure RWD is included, because a single rear drive unit means a disengagement is a complete loss of motive power. And this one is a recall of a recall. Lucid’s October 2025 filing covered 225 cars found through a telematics lash-detection algorithm. Then 26 more half-shaft failures showed up on customer cars between October 2025 and February 2026, and the March filing widened the population sixteen-fold. Algorithms found the first 225. Drivers found the rest.[5]

Now the denominator, because 50,004 sounds like a lot until you ask what it is a lot of. Lucid delivered 10,852 vehicles in the first nine months of 2026: 3,093 in the first quarter, 3,953 in the second, 3,806 in the third. Campaign-unit total is 4.6 times the year’s deliveries, and larger than the company’s cumulative deliveries of roughly 46,000. Compare that with Rivian in #1496: three campaigns, 103,526 vehicles, against 19,248 deliveries in a single quarter. Rivian filed fewer campaigns and covered more cars. Lucid filed more campaigns than Tesla’s four, more than Genesis’s three, more than any EV maker in the series, and covered the fewest vehicles, because its entire history is about 46,000 cars. Two more stories belong outside the count and the census names them honestly: 25V855, a December 2025 filing covering 66 Gravity SUVs with mislabeled seat backrest covers that could make the side airbags deploy wrong, and the October 2025 half-shaft filing of 225 cars that 26V193 expanded. Both were filed in 2025 and touch 2026 vehicles, so they sit outside a 2026 census.[1][7]

What to do, concretely, starts with the version number in the car, not the letter in the mail. If you own a 2022 to 2026 Air, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V540 first. It covers essentially every Air ever built, and the remedy is software 2.10.0 or later. If your car is one of the 6,466 that had not installed it at filing, install it now and park outside away from structures until you do. Then check for 26V017, the rearview camera, software 2.8.17 or later, if your Air has the DreamDrive Pro package. If you own a 2024 to 2026 Air Pure Rear-Wheel Drive, check for 26V193, the half-shaft bolts, because a disengagement means a complete, unwarned loss of drive power, and the October 2025 recall covered only 225 cars while the March expansion covers 3,627. If your car was outside the original 225, it is very likely inside the expansion. If you own a 2025 or 2026 Gravity, check for 26V018, software 3.3.20 or later, and 26V192, the seat belt anchor welds. If your Gravity was built before February 14, 2026, it is in the population, and that is effectively every Gravity delivered before March. If you are shopping used, there is no clean-sheet Lucid in this census, so the VIN lookup is the whole story, and every fix is free. Ask the seller for the current software version before you ask about the price.

Limitations, stated plainly, before anyone mistakes this census for a ranking. This is a filing census, not a danger ranking. A campaign counts as one whether it covers 3,627 cars or 27,185, whether the defect estimate is under 1 percent or 100 percent. Campaign-unit totals count a vehicle once per campaign, so the Air’s 41,628 campaign-units are not 41,628 distinct cars: 26V540’s 27,185 is close to the entire Air population, and the distinct-vehicle count is much lower. Delivery figures are Lucid’s own SEC-filed quarterly totals, which Lucid does not break out by model, so the Gravity production figure of 4,476 comes from the 26V192 Part 573 report, not from Lucid’s disclosures. Cumulative deliveries of roughly 46,000 are the sum of Lucid’s reported annual totals through 2025 plus the 10,852 delivered in the first nine months of 2026. The 26 post-recall half-shaft failures and the 29-day stop-sale duration come from trade-press reporting of the Part 573 chronology; the Part 573 itself gives the January 28 stop-sale date and the February 14 clean point.[1][7]

Counterargument, at full strength, because the honest reading deserves a hearing. Lucid had a competent, even exemplary, recall year. Five campaigns sounds like a lot until you look at what they are: three are software, and all three were fixed over the air before the filing, which is the recall system working as designed for a software-defined car. Both physical recalls were caught by Lucid’s own testing or telematics before any crash. Zero crashes or injuries attach to any of the five campaigns. Three fires produced no injuries, the park-outside advisory is precautionary, and 20,719 of 27,185 owners had already installed the fix before the filing. Lucid’s delivery total is small, so any recall looks large against it, and the 4.6x ratio is an artifact of counting five model years of Airs against nine months of deliveries. Gravity’s stop-sale lasted weeks, not months, and production resumed with corrected seats. By any danger ranking, Lucid had a quieter year than Ford, Stellantis, or GM, and the over-the-air pattern is the thing other automakers should copy. All of that is fair, and this article grants it. Counterweight is the shape of the count. Lucid filed more campaigns than any EV maker in the series, and its biggest campaign is a fire recall covering every car it has ever built, with a 100 percent defect estimate. A startup that outsources its seats and its half-shafts inherits its suppliers’ process changes, and the first Lucid learns about a changed weld is when its own compliance test fails. Five recalls, three already fixed by the time they were filed, and the one that matters most is the one 6,466 owners had not installed.
