Ford Recalled 148,663 Mustangs Because Their Ground Wires Can Snap Mid-Drive. The Fix Isn’t Expected Until March.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 6, 2026

Here’s a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: the Mustang that found your defect doesn’t exist yet. Ford engineers picked up two broken ground eyelets on a 2027 Mustang during durability testing — a next-model-year mule, not a car anyone can buy — and followed the thread backward into three model years already on the road. 148,663 Mustangs, 2024 through 2026, are now recalled under Ford campaign 26C40 because their engine-compartment wiring harness ground terminals can fatigue-fracture, killing drive power, headlights, windshield washers, air conditioning, and the engine cooling fan in one stroke.[1][2] The redesigned parts aren’t expected to reach dealers until March 2027.

The failure mechanism reads like a cosmic joke about protection. Ford wrapped the engine wiring in adhesive-lined heat-shrink tubing, a sleeve whose entire job is keeping the wires safe. During manufacturing, adhesive from that tubing spilled onto the metal ground eyelets that anchor the harness to the engine block. The glue hardened, the eyelets couldn’t sit flush, and when they were bolted down over the hardened blob they bent — permanent stress points in exactly the wrong place. Engine vibration did the rest. The sleeve that protects the wires carries the glue that breaks the ground.[4]

Now the arithmetic. One percent of 148,663 is roughly 1,487 cars, so this recall runs at about a 100-to-1 ratio of worry to wiring. Ford knows of eight warranty claims, four in vehicles on the road less than three months, and zero accidents or injuries so far.[3][4] If this is your first Ford recall, savor the novelty: the company has filed 67 campaigns this year and led the industry in recalls for five straight years.[2]

The part that should bother you is the calendar. Owner letters went out in the first week of September. The fix is anticipated for March 2027.[2] Six months of winter driving with a known stall defect and no remedy. Your early-warning system, such as it is: a check-engine light, instrument cluster warnings, an audible chime. On a 2024-2026 Mustang those are no longer generic nagging; they are a possible preview of losing power, lights, and washers at once.[6] And the wipers keep coming up: this is the second Mustang wiper-adjacent recall in a month, after 67,000 cars were recalled for defective wiper motors in August.[3]

What to do: Own a 2024-2026 Mustang? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now rather than waiting for the owner letter.[1] Treat any new check-engine light, cluster warning, or chime as urgent until a dealer rules this out: the defect’s failure mode is sudden loss of motive power, and there is no software workaround while you wait for March. Shopping used? Ask for 26C40 on the service record, and confirm it was completed after the March 2027 parts arrive — an early visit gets you an inspection, not a repair.[5] Do not confuse this with the same-week piston recall 26S61, which affects 237 Mustangs (and 9,764 other Fords) for a different mechanical defect with a different remedy.

What this does not prove: Zero accidents and zero injuries are on the board, so FARS has no body count to contribute. That 1 percent is NHTSA’s estimate from Ford’s filing, not an independent teardown count; the real number could be higher or lower. The 100-to-1 framing is our arithmetic on their estimate, not Ford’s claim. And the 2027-mule discovery is striking but not a scandal: durability testing is supposed to catch things. It caught this one a year late.
