# Research: #1191 — NYC E-Bike Bill Package vs. The Denominator Problem

**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (Data Visualization Editor; methodology beat)
**Kicker:** By The Numbers
**Slug:** 1191-nyc-ebike-23-bills-denominator

## Hook / News peg (Oct 2026)

On Sept 30, 2026, NYC Council Speaker Julie Menin previewed a record **23 bills** ahead of a joint oversight hearing (Transportation + Consumer/Worker Protection + Public Safety committees) on e-bike, e-scooter, and moped safety. Proposals range from enforcement to delivery-app licensing to **phasing out the sale and use of Class III e-bikes** (motor assistance up to 25 mph) over a two-year transition.

## Core data (all sourced)

1. **City data, via NYC Council press release (Sept 30, 2026):**
   - H1 2026: **738 injuries and 13 fatalities** among e-bike riders
   - H1 2025: **601 injuries and 10 fatalities**
   - Injuries +22.8% YoY; fatalities +30% (13 vs 10)
2. **Scale of the denominator (same press release):**
   - ~**680,000 adult New Yorkers** regularly ride a bike at least several times a month
   - ~**65,000 delivery workers** on micromobility devices = ~40% of micromobility traffic
   - Electric Citi Bike alone logged **29M+ trips in 2024** (per CleanTechnica; Citi Bike system data)
3. **NYC 2024 Comptroller report:** e-bikes, e-scooters, and mopeds = ~**4.5% of all pedestrian injuries** 2020-2023
4. **NYC Aug 2026 enforcement announcement (via CleanTechnica):** of **45 e-bike rider deaths 2017-2025, 54% involved devices capable of exceeding 25 mph**; stand-up scooters: 14 deaths, 52% involved devices capable of >20 mph. Devices capable of >25 mph are ALREADY illegal in NYC.
5. **CPSC (federal battery-fire track):**
   - April 2026 staff report: **367 lithium-ion battery fires, 45 fatalities, 2017-2024** across all micromobility products (e-bike fires: 19 deaths / 13 incidents; e-scooter: 15 / 11; hoverboard: 11 / 5)
   - June 24, 2026: CPSC published **proposed mandatory rule** (Federal Register doc 2026-12749): e-bikes to meet UL 2849, e-scooters UL 2272, user-replaceable batteries UL 2271. Comments closed Aug 24, 2026. Not yet final.
   - California requires e-bikes/batteries sold in-state to meet a safety standard starting Jan 1, 2026
6. **Miami (Sept 28, 2026, Fire-Rescue report):** 1,380 micromobility cases logged by first responders; first 8 months of 2026 = 472 cases vs 443 same period 2025 (+6.5%)
7. **Senate pressure:** Gillibrand + Schumer wrote CPSC ~Oct 15, 2026 urging finalization of the battery rule; cited 2026 NYC data (FDNY: 362 fires, 5 deaths this year among micromobility users)

## Novel angle / kill test

Passes. The news peg is fresh (Sept 30 hearing, Oct federal letter) and the angle is a genuine data critique of the legislative framing:
- Headline panic ("injuries up 23%") ignores the denominator: hundreds of thousands of riders, tens of millions of trips.
- More importantly: **the lethal subset is the illegal subset.** 54% of NYC e-bike deaths 2017-2025 involved devices capable of >25 mph, which were already illegal. Class III phase-out targets *legal* 25-mph-capable bikes while the deadliest machines are already-banned throttle monsters. The proposal regulates the denominator, not the numerator.
- Parallel federal track: the battery-fire body count (45 deaths 2017-2024 nationally) is a *product-safety* problem being addressed through UL-standard rulemaking, slow (comments just closed). Crash deaths are an *enforcement-of-existing-law* problem.
- Small-numbers instability: 10 -> 13 deaths is "+30%" that fits in a denominator of 29M+ trips; policy should be per-trip/per-mile, not per-headline.

## Counterarguments (steelmanned)
- Even one rider death matters; per-trip framing can anesthetize legitimate risk concerns.
- Illegal devices are hard to enforce against without registration — the Council's licensing/registration bills may be the practical way to reach the >25mph machines.
- Legal Class III (25 mph assist) genuinely can outrun NYC's 15 mph operating speed limit — there is a case for the phase-out beyond headline-hunting.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
1. If you ride: **speed capability is the killer** — buy a legal Class 1/2 (20 mph) bike, not a 25+ mph throttle machine.
2. **Battery fires kill at night while charging** — CPSC says be present when charging; look for UL 2849 (bike) / UL 2271 (battery) certification.
3. Delivery workers: the DOT safety course + helmet rules already exist — enforcement, not new bans, is the gap.
4. Watch the CPSC final rule — when UL 2849 becomes mandatory, cheap uncertified bikes leave the market.
