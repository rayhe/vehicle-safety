# Research: Hyundai Ulsan Manual-Torque Twin Recalls (26V692 + 26V693)

## Story angle
Two October 2026 recalls from the same Hyundai Ulsan plant in South Korea, filed days apart, trace back to one root cause: a temporary manual torquing process used while wheel-tightening tooling was being modified. The lug nuts on 17 brand-new 2026 Elantras/Venues may be under-torqued, so wheels can detach. A sibling recall (26V692, 24 vehicles) covers front-strut fasteners from the same supplier, Samjin Nut. Hyundai blames manufacturing lapses, not defective parts. The angle: when the robots get reprogrammed, humans tighten the nuts, and wheels fall off.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy: YES. Brand-new 2026 model-year vehicles with wheels that may detach is immediate consumer-safety news. The twin-recall-same-plant-same-supplier pattern gives it a novel angle beyond a routine recall writeup.
- Novel angle on data: YES. The pairing of 26V692 + 26V693 (same plant, same supplier, same tooling-change window) is the original contribution. FARS wheel-separation context supports it.

## Key facts to use
- 17 vehicles: 8 Venue, 4 Elantra, 4 Elantra Hybrid, 1 Elantra N — all 2026 model year, built at Ulsan, South Korea
- 26V692 sibling: 24 vehicles (20 Elantra, 4 Tucson Hybrid), same supplier (Samjin Nut), same Ulsan plant
- Manual torquing used as a stopgap while tooling was modified; ended Sept 16, 2026; Hyundai learned of condition Sept 22
- No incidents, crashes, injuries reported for 26V693; one incident (no crash/injury) for 26V692
- Remedy: inspect + tighten lug nuts; replace hub bearing assembly as necessary; free
- Owner letters by Dec 19, 2026; VINs searchable since Oct 23, 2026

## Actionable insight for readers
If you bought a 2026 Hyundai Elantra or Venue built at Ulsan (check your VIN plate for "U" plant code, or look up the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now), call your dealer before December's letter arrives — Hyundai's own timeline shows the condition was known since Sept 22, and the remedy is a same-day torque check.

## Limitations / counterargument
- Only 17 vehicles affected — this is a factory-process escape, not a fleet-wide defect. The story must not imply all 2026 Elantras are at risk.
- Hyundai reports zero incidents; the risk is potential, not demonstrated. A detached wheel is catastrophic (see site's own 846-wheel-off-loose-lug-projectile for the physics), but none has actually detached here.
- The "manual process" blame narrative comes from Hyundai's own filing — NHTSA's page lists no finding beyond the defect description.
- Transport Canada has a parallel 26V692-style recall (strut bolts) — US-focused story should note the Canadian analog exists.
