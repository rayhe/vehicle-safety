## Key data points (all from DOT HS 813 824)
- 6,228 motorcyclists killed in 2024, down 2.2% from 6,366 in 2023. Injuries rose 5.2% (82,567 → 86,887).
- Age-group changes 2023 → 2024:
  - Under 15: 14 → 30 (+114%)
  - 15–20: 508 → 609 (+20%)
  - 21–24: 613 → 705 (+15%)
  - 25–29: 767 → 706 (−8%); 30–34: 693 → 698 (+1%); 35–39: 581 → 582; 40–44: 569 → 529 (−7%); 45–49: 496 → 426 (−14%); 50–54: 517 → 461 (−11%); 55–59: 508 → 443 (−13%); 60–64: 471 → 400 (−15%); 65+: 626 → 637 (+2%)
- 15–20 speeding involvement in fatal crashes: 56% in 2024 (highest of ANY age group), up from 51% in 2023. Next highest: 21–24 at 52%. All-motorcycle average: 37%.
- Weekday/weekend split for 15–20: +20% both (293→353 weekdays, 215→256 weekends). Weekend share: 46% in 2024.
- 35% of riders in fatal crashes had no valid motorcycle license (vs 14% of passenger-vehicle drivers).
- Average age of rider killed: 40.
- Motorcycles: 0.7% of VMT, 16% of all traffic deaths; 28.00/100M VMT vs 1.05 for passenger car occupants (~27x).
- Helmet law split: in states without universal helmet laws, 48% of motorcyclists killed were unhelmeted vs 11% in universal-law states. Only 17 states + DC + PR have universal laws (2024).

## Original contribution (the decomposition nobody ran)
Young riders (<25) killed: 2023 = 14+508+613 = 1,135. 2024 = 30+609+705 = 1,344. **Change: +209 (+18.4%).**
Riders 25+: 2023 = 6,366−1,135 = 5,231. 2024 = 6,228−1,344 = 4,884. **Change: −347 (−6.6%).**
So the entire national motorcycle death decline (−138 net) is explained by riders 25+ improving while young riders deteriorated. The headline decline masks a youth crisis.

## Limitations
- FARS is fatal-crash only; 6,228 deaths vs ~86,887 estimated injuries (CRSS survey estimates, not counts).
- Under-15 n is tiny (14→30): a 114% swing is statistically fragile, but 15–20 (n=609) and 21–24 (n=705) are robust.
- Speeding determination relies on police reports and citations — likely undercounts.
- "Motorcycles" includes off-road bikes, mopeds, scooters, minibikes, pocket bikes. Some <15 deaths are likely dirt-bike/off-road incidents on public roads.
- NHTSA's own caveat: 2020+ vehicle classification changes affect comparability; starting 2022 motorized bicycles excluded.
- Exposure by age is unknown: we cannot say young riders are dying at higher rates per mile — only that their counts rose while others fell.

## Counterargument (full strength)
The decline among 25+ riders might not be a safety success at all — it could be exposure shifting: older riders may be riding less (aging out, selling bikes) while younger riders are riding more (cheap used bikes, delivery gigs, social media stunting culture). Without age-stratified VMT, "young riders got more dangerous" is unproven; "young riders got more numerous" fits the data equally well. Also, the under-15 count (30) is small enough that a few multi-fatality incidents swing the percentage wildly.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: yes. NHTSA published this July 2026; the under-15 114% jump has not been covered anywhere we can find. Novel angle: the decline-vs-surge decomposition. Distinct from prior site motorcycle stories (scissors, unlicensed, state surges, share, AEB) — none touched the 2024 age-group table.
