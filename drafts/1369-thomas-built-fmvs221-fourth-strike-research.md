# Research: #1369 — Thomas Built fails FMVSS 221 a fourth time (26V702, wheelhouse glue joints)

**Slug:** 1369-thomas-built-fmvs221-fourth-strike
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — paradox/investigation beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** 2026-11-02

## Kill test: PASS

**Proposed angle:** Thomas Built Buses — America's biggest school bus builder — just recalled 13,593 school buses because the structural adhesive bonding the body panels at the interior wheelhouse was applied over improperly prepared surfaces and can't meet the tensile strength requirements of FMVSS 221, the one federal standard written specifically for school bus bodies. The fix is adding mechanical fasteners — the exact technology the adhesive bonding presumably reduced reliance on. And this is the FOURTH time Thomas Built has been caught failing FMVSS 221: 23V333 (2023, 23,590 buses), 26V388 (June 2026, 2 buses), 26V546 (Aug 2026, 65 buses), 26V702 (Oct 2026, 13,593 buses). Total: 37,250 buses recalled for the same school-bus-body standard. Each time, the remedy is the same: drive more fasteners into the joints.

**Challenge:** is this surprising after 1,368 articles? Yes. (1) No one has reported the repeat-offender tally — the four FMVSS-221 recalls are publicly filed but nobody connected them into a series. (2) The glue paradox is real: the joints were structurally bonded with adhesive instead of (or in addition to) mechanical fasteners, the adhesive failed because the surface wasn't prepped, and the repair adds mechanical fasteners back. (3) Timing: the recall was decided Oct 20, announced Nov 2, and owner letters don't go out until Dec 21 — school districts run these buses daily in between.

**Newsworthy?** Yes. 13,593 school buses with noncompliant body joints; filed Oct 22, 2026, DTNA F1044; announced today (Nov 2, 2026 weekly roundup). School buses carry ~25 million children daily (NCES). FMVSS 221 exists so the bus body stays together around the kids in a crash.

**3+ primary sources?** Yes:
1. NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Report 26V702 (DTNA F1044, submitted Oct 22, 2026) — static.nhtsa.gov PDF.
2. NHTSA Part 573 report 26V388 (F1034, June 2026) and 26V546 (F1038, Aug 2026) — same failure category, same builder.
3. NHTSA owner notification letter for 23V333 (FL973, Dec 2023) — "The Recall will take approximately 30 minutes to 1 hour" — plus Justia NHTSA mirror (23,590 buses, rear interior joint).
4. Trucks Parts and Service NHTSA commercial-recall roundup, Nov 2, 2026 (trade press, published today).

## Primary sources (detail)

1. **RCLRPT-26V702-6752.pdf (Oct 22, 2026):** 13,593 buses, 100% estimated with defect. MY2023-2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 (12,039), C2 Jouley (885, electric), HDX2 (487), EFX2 (182). Built Nov 18, 2024 – Sept 2, 2026. "May have improperly bonded joints above the wheel at the interior wheelhouse." Noncompliance: FMVSS 221 S5.1 tensile strength requirements. Chronology: Sept 9, 2026 routine audit identified potential issue "involving preparation of certain body panel areas for structural adhesive bonding"; team investigated through early October; Oct 20, 2026 DTNA decided to recall. Remedy: "Additional mechanical fasteners will be added to the joints." Production corrected Sept 2026 ("surface preparation was improved and additional inspection points were added"). Dealer notice Nov 11, 2026; owner letters Dec 21, 2026. No injuries/deaths/claims known.

2. **23V333 / FL973 (May 2023, letters Dec 19, 2023):** 23,590 MY2021-2024 C2 and MY2021-2023 C2 Jouley. Rear interior joint formed by D-panel overlapping side-sheet at last rafter "manufactured incorrectly, resulting in insufficient joint strength." Fix: "adding additional mechanical fasteners" — 30 min to 1 hr.

3. **26V388 / F1034 (June 2026):** 2 MY2027 HDX2 buses; header material "did not meet the minimum tensile strength" of FMVSS 221; remedy: install additional fasteners.

4. **26V546 / F1038 (Aug 2026):** 65 MY2027 HDX2/EFX2; interior header connector plates missing; noncompliant with FMVSS 210 (seat belt anchorages) AND 221.

5. **Truck Parts & Service, Nov 2, 2026:** weekly commercial-recall roundup covering the new recall; quotes the FMVSS 221 "School Bus Body Joint Strength" noncompliance and 13,593 count.

## Computations

- Repeat-offender tally: 23,590 + 13,593 + 65 + 2 = **37,250 buses** recalled for FMVSS 221 noncompliance across 4 campaigns.
- 26V702 is 100% defect-rate: every one of the 13,593 buses has the bad joints (the population was defined by manufacturing records: Nov 18, 2024 – Sept 2, 2026 production with the adhesive process).
- C2 share: 12,039 / 13,593 = 88.6% are the best-selling C2 model.
- Notification gap: recall decided Oct 20 → owner letters Dec 21 = **62 days** of buses in daily service before districts are told. Dealer notice Nov 11.
- Audit-to-recall: Sept 9 audit → Oct 20 decision = 41 days of investigation.
- Pattern: every one of these four FMVSS-221 recalls is remedied with "additional mechanical fasteners." Three years, four recalls, one repair: more screws.

## Strongest counterargument

The system is arguably working: Thomas Built caught this itself in a routine audit, filed promptly, estimated 100% of the population affected (no hiding behind a small defect rate), and fixed production within weeks (Sept 2026). Zero injuries, deaths, or claims across all four recalls. FMVSS 221 compliance is tested on representative samples, and the remedy — additional fasteners — directly addresses the standard's requirement. A cynic's framing ("fourth strike") could equally be read as four successful detections. Also, the C2 has sold hundreds of thousands of buses since 2004; 37,250 recall mentions over 3+ years is a fraction of the fleet, and the first (2023) recall fixed a design issue that predates the current production.

## Limitations

- No crash data is involved: zero injuries/deaths/claims are reported. This is a compliance story, not a body-count story. Say so.
- The Part 573 report doesn't disclose how far below the S5.1 threshold the joints tested; we know they "may not meet" the tensile requirements, not by how much.
- "Estimated percentage with defect: 100%" is DTNA's estimate, not a per-bus inspection result; it follows from the manufacturing-records population definition.
- FMVSS 221's S5.1 specific force requirement (60% of tensile strength of the weaker material) is not quoted in the recall documents; describe the standard as setting "minimum tensile strength requirements" per the filings, not the specific figure.

## Actionable for readers

- School district fleet managers and bus contractors: run your VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls now — the 26V702 owner letters don't mail until Dec 21, 2026, but the DTNA campaign F1044 is already filed. The fix is free and takes under an hour per bus.
- Parents: ask your district whether its C2, C2 Jouley, HDX2, or EFX2 buses built between Nov 2024 and Sept 2026 have been inspected for the wheelhouse joint recall.
- Used school bus buyers: check FMVSS-221 recall completion for any Thomas Built C2 (2021+) before purchase; there have been four separate joint-strength recalls.
