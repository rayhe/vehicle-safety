## 1. General Editor — 8.8/10
Structure is clean: kicker, lede with the bolded 61% stat, pull stat, five body paragraphs, references, disclaimer with limitations. The lede lands the paradox in two sentences. The Q1 paragraph is the densest passage in the piece (six numbers in three sentences) but the numbers are the story, and each one earns its place. Minor nit: the counterargument paragraph runs long; it is doing honest work, so it stays.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.8/10
Axle's voice holds throughout: numbers first, dry, actuarial, no throat-clearing. "They got more lethal" and "Winter did the damage, and the first quarter tells the whole story in miniature" are the only punches, and both land. No banned phrases, no AI tells, no "The" sentence starters at all. The formulaic "What you can do with this." opener is a site convention at this point, slightly templated but consistent with the series. Rhythm passes on all three metrics.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
Honest about small numbers and stated plainly: nineteen deaths, noisy county comparisons, no causation established, growth as a confounder. No victims named, no crash details sensationalized, no individual blame. The law-firm source chain is disclosed as a secondary presentation of a public database, not dressed up as a TxDOT publication. The counterargument gets full strength rather than a strawman.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.7/10
Headline is a clean share hook: flat crashes, deaths up 61%. The 61% pull stat is the share trigger. County-level framing gives it a local audience in DFW, and the "same metro, opposite directions" table is the kind of comparison that travels. Slightly narrower appeal than a national recall story, which is the only reason this is not higher.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.8/10
All figures verified against the source pages this run (Denton, Tarrant, Collin, statewide CMV). NHTSA Q1 2026 citation (DOT HS 813 833, 7,770 deaths, rate 0.99) verified. No legal claims beyond the data. No invented URLs: Wooley links are verbatim from search results; nsc.org and txdot.gov are parent homepages. One nit: the NSC H1 2026 figure is cited via the site's own #1402 research (verified November 5) rather than re-verified against the NSC release this run; the citation as written does not overclaim the sourcing.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.7/10
Original contribution is real and shown with work: lethality per 1,000 crashes (3.09 to 4.96), fatal-crash share (0.31% to 0.49%), Q1 lethality (6.32 vs 4.25), non-commercial death surge (+74%), three-county divergence table. Limitations are explicit, including the preliminary status of the counts and the exposure confound. Weakness, stated in the article: the primary data reaches the piece through a law firm's analysis of CRIS exports rather than a direct CRIS pull, and the three-county comparison leans on that firm's consistent methodology across its own series.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10
Numbers carry units and denominators throughout; the 61% (deaths) vs 60% (lethality per crash) pair is distinguished in the text so they do not blur. Pull stat plus label frames the core finding. The quarterly breakdown is presented as a table in research and as clean prose in the article. Minor nit: the article never states the three-county combined total (234 to 241) in a way a skimming reader will catch; it is in the fourth paragraph and does its job there.

## Verdict
**Average: 8.80/10. All seven critics at 8.5+. All hard gates pass. SHIP (round 0).**
