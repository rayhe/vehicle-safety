GM Recalled 70,768 Sierra Grilles to Stop Them Flying Off. 21 of the Fixed Trucks Lost Theirs Anyway.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 12, 2026

On October 9, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened Recall Query RQ26003 into the 2022 through 2024 GMC Sierra 1500, after 118 owners reported the truck's upper grille deflector separating at speed.[1] Twenty-one of those trucks had already been repaired under GM's 25V-060 recall, the campaign whose entire purpose was keeping the deflector attached.[1] Some of the liberated panels cracked the windshields of nearby vehicles; others forced drivers into evasive maneuvers.[1] ODI's investigation population is 535,639 trucks. Only 70,768 were recalled.

17.8%
of the 118 RQ26003 complaints are Sierra 1500s that had already received GM's 25V-060 recall repair, so the fix failed on roughly 1 in 6 complainants

Start with the hardware, because the hardware is damning. Eight plastic attachment points hold the deflector in place, and GM's own chronology says those points fracture over time; if all eight let go, a chrome panel departs the vehicle at highway speed.[2] Minth Tennessee International made the tabs. Somebody signed off on a design that asked eight plastic tabs to survive years of vibration, heat cycling, and highway wind load, and then, predictably, the tabs started snapping.

That timeline should embarrass everyone in the room. A GM employee flagged a detachment through the Speak Up for Safety program in January 2024. GM opened a probe in February and closed it in May 2024, finding no defect trend. Claim rates kept climbing, GM reopened the probe in December 2024, counted 1,225 field complaints and four alleged accidents, and recalled in February 2025.[2] "No trend" lasted exactly as long as it took the trend to become undeniable.

Then there is the scope of the original recall, which deserves its own indictment. GM recalled only chrome-grille 2022s, 70,768 trucks, while telling NHTSA the defect rate was 3 percent, roughly 2,100 trucks.[2] Now ODI is looking at 535,639 trucks across three model years, every grille finish. Divide one by the other: 7.57 times the recalled population. Attachment points fracture from load and vibration, not from being chrome, so the original scoping was never engineering. It was accounting.

Fairness demands the strongest case against my alarm, and it is not weak. RQ26003 is a query, not a recall, and plenty of queries die quietly. One hundred eighteen unverified complaints across 535,639 trucks is a 0.022 percent complaint rate, the kind of number that can mean aging plastic tabs across five model years rather than a systemic defect. And the 21 post-remedy trucks may have gotten sloppy dealer repairs rather than a flawed fix; the complaint data cannot tell the two apart. Zero injuries are reported in the investigation documents so far.

Here is what to do, and it matters most for the trucks nobody recalled. If you own a 2022 through 2024 Sierra 1500, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Model years 2023 and 2024 are not recalled yet, but they sit inside the investigation population.[5] Listen for the one warning sign owners report: a rattle from the front grille.[3] If you hear it, get the truck to a dealer before the rattle becomes someone else's windshield.
