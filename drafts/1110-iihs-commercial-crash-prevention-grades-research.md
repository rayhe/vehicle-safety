# Research — #1110: IIHS commercial crash-prevention grades (Oct 8, 2026)

**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage | **Kicker:** Existential Dread
**Slug:** `1110-iihs-commercial-crash-prevention-grades`
**Headline candidate:** "IIHS Finally Graded the Trucks Most Likely to Flatten You. Almost Everyone Failed."

## What happened
On October 8, 2026, IIHS released its first-ever front crash prevention ratings for commercial vehicles: 11 cargo vans and Class 3 pickups (GVWR 10,001-14,000 lbs), rated in vehicle-to-vehicle and pedestrian crash avoidance using the same test protocols and rating scale as passenger vehicles.

## Key findings (primary sources below)
- **Vehicle-to-vehicle:** Only the Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 2500 earned Good. The Chevy BrightDrop 400 was Acceptable. The other 9 of 11 were Marginal or Poor. All four Class 3 pickups (Silverado 3500HD, F-350, Sierra 3500, Ram 3500) failed to earn Good; the Silverado, F-350, and Sierra rated Poor, the Ram 3500 Marginal. Per Detroit Free Press: Express 2500, Transit T250, Savana 2500, ProMaster 2500, Silverado 3500HD, F-350, Sierra 3500 all scored Poor in V2V.
- **Motorcycles were the hardest target.** Several systems did not issue warnings or slow at all in most motorcycle trials. Others slowed but were downgraded for not warning the driver in time. (Autoblog: F-350 avoided the passenger-car target at only 31 mph; in a 43-mph test it did not reduce speed at all.)
- **Pedestrian:** BrightDrop 400 and Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 earned Good; Sprinter Acceptable; F-350 and Ram 3500 Acceptable. Express 2500 and Savana 2500 earned Poor in BOTH tests because they lack AEB entirely (optional forward-collision warning only).
- **Scale of harm:** 6,215 people were killed in 2024 in crashes involving medium/heavy-duty vehicles or light vans = 16% of all US roadway fatalities (IIHS).
- **Regulatory angle:** NHTSA's June 2023 heavy-vehicle AEB proposal estimated the rule would prevent 19,118 crashes, save 155 lives, and prevent 8,814 injuries annually once fully deployed (vehicles >10,000 lbs GVWR). The final rule has not yet been issued; for now the IIHS rubric is voluntary for this class.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy:** Yes — ratings released yesterday, first of their kind, covered by AP-tier outlets.
- **Novel angle:** The heaviest vehicles on the road (the ones that do the most damage to everyone ELSE) are the ones with the worst automatic braking. Physics makes the standard harder for them, but IIHS graded them on the same rubric as a Civic anyway, and only the Germans passed. Meanwhile two models sold new today still skip AEB entirely while NHTSA's mandate sits unfinished.
- **Original contribution:** The asymmetry tabulation: 1 of 11 Good in V2V; 0 of 4 Class 3 pickups Good; 2 of 11 Good in pedestrian. Pairing the voluntary test results with the pending 2023 federal mandate (19,118 crashes / 155 lives) makes the "graded but not governed" gap explicit. Also the motorcycle-detection failure mode as a specific safety hole for riders.
- **Not a duplicate:** #1102 covered the child-dummy "disappearing" finding; #749-equivalents (iihs-first-commercial-test-regulatory-void, iihs-heavy-truck-first-scorecard) covered the June 2026 occupant-protection announcement. This is the October 2026 crash-avoidance ratings round.

## Strongest counterargument
Physics is not malice. Stopping a 14,000-lb loaded pickup from 43 mph in the distance a sedan does it is a harder engineering problem, and IIHS applied the passenger-car rubric unchanged. The F-350 DID avoid the car at 31 mph and reduced speed in other trials; "Poor" is a grade against a standard built for lighter vehicles. The Express/Savana are legacy platforms near end-of-life. And 6,215 deaths involve these vehicles; they are not all CAUSED by them.

## Limitations
- Ratings describe 2025-2027 model configurations tested by IIHS; options and calibrations vary.
- "Involved in" crashes is not "at fault in" crashes.
- The 19,118-crash/155-life figures are NHTSA model estimates for a not-yet-finalized rule covering Classes 3-8, not an observed effect.

## Actionable takeaways
- Fleet buyers: the Sprinter 2500 is the only V2V Good in the class; the BrightDrop 400 and Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 lead on pedestrian avoidance. Check these ratings before speccing vans.
- If you drive/ride a motorcycle: assume the delivery van behind you cannot reliably detect you. IIHS found several systems that did not warn or slow at all in motorcycle trials.
- Express/Savana buyers: the new vans still lack AEB. If you buy one, know that you are buying 1990s-era crash-prevention philosophy with a 2026 VIN.
- Everyone: check VIN recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls; none of these findings are recalls, they are ratings gaps.
