# Research Notes: 1049-ford-78th-recall-record-broken

## Story thesis
Ford's 77th recall of 2026 (Ranger backup camera, 26C42, Sept 28) tied GM's pre-2025 all-time single-year record of 77. Two days later, on Sept 30, Ford filed recall #78 (26C43, contaminated LED headlight chips, 41,748 Expeditions and Super Dutys) and passed it. The record book now reads: Ford 153 (2025), Ford 78 (2026, and counting), GM 77 (2014). The novel data work is the updated pace math and the volume ledger: 78 recalls in 273 days (one every 3.50 days), 14.09M vehicles recalled YTD, now 1.13M past all of 2025's total with a quarter of the year left. The #78 defect itself is a supply-chain electronics story (contaminated LED chip, FMVSS 108 noncompliance, zero reported crashes), and it is the first recall of the 26C4x family to hit the Expedition line.

## Original findings / calculations (show math in article)
1. **Pace:** 78 recalls in the 273 days from Jan 1 to Sept 30, 2026 = 1 recall every 3.50 days (273/78 = 3.50). Full-year projection: 78/273*365 = 104.2 recalls. Monday's #77 (Sept 28) to Wednesday's #78 (Sept 30): 2 days between milestones.
2. **Volume ledger:** 14,048,838 (through 26C42, Sept 28) + 41,748 (26C43, Sept 30) = 14,090,586 vehicles recalled in 2026. That is 1,132,458 more than ALL of 2025 (12,958,128), with 92 days left in the year.
3. **Per-recall mean:** 14,090,586 / 78 = 180,648 vehicles per recall in 2026 vs 84,693 in 2025 (+113%). Fewer fires, bigger explosions, still true.
4. **Record-book update:** All-time single-year recall counts now read 153 (Ford, 2025), 78-and-counting (Ford, 2026), 77 (GM, 2014). The old GM mark Ford tied on Monday now sits third. Ford's own 2025 record (153) remains the ceiling; at the current pace 2026 finishes around 104, nowhere near it.
5. **The #78 defect:** contaminated LED chip in headlight assemblies (supplier contamination, not a burned-out bulb) — loss of DRL, parking, high, or low beams; FMVSS 108 noncompliance. Ford is not aware of any accidents or injuries. A dashboard indicator warns on low/high-beam failure. This is an electronics/supply-chain defect, continuing the pattern #1033 documented (four headlight recalls in 14 months, zero burned-out bulbs).

## Limitations to state
- The "78th" count is this site's running tally of Ford NHTSA filings in 2026, consistent with the #1048 methodology; media snapshots at different dates (July / Sept 9 / ~Sept 24 / Sept 28 / Sept 30) are not perfectly comparable.
- "Vehicles per recall" is an arithmetic mean; the distribution is heavily skewed by a handful of multi-million-vehicle campaigns.
- NHTSA counts recall campaigns; a single campaign can bundle multiple model years. Cross-automaker comparisons assume consistent campaign-splitting practices.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
A recall is the system working: the company found the problem and is fixing it free, and a high count can mean aggressive self-reporting rather than bad engineering. The honest rejoinder: the defects are real, the remedy lag is what converts "system working" into owners driving recalled cars for months, and the per-recall blast radius shows the problems are systemic, not corner cases. Also: nobody was hurt by this one; a contaminated LED chip caught before a crash is the recall system at its best.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE - must include)
1. Own a 2025-2027 Expedition or 2026 Super Duty? Your headlights can partially die with no warning except a dash indicator. You don't have to wait for the Oct 26 letter: call your dealer and schedule the free headlight inspection now.
2. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls quarterly. Ford's pace (1 recall every 3.5 days) means a clean VIN in March can be dirty by September.
3. Before buying used: ask the dealer to confirm all open recalls are COMPLETED, not just identified.
4. If a dealer says the fix isn't available, get it in writing; the 198-day average remedy lag means "remedy under development" can last half a year.
