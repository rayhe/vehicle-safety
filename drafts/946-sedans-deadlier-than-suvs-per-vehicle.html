The Sedan Is America's Deadliest Body Style. The SUV Is Its Safest.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 19, 2026

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: 89,127 people died in sedans between 2014 and 2023. That is 46.6 percent of every occupant death in America, produced by a body style that accounts for just 32.5 percent of the registered fleet.[1] The Accord in your driveway, the Camry in the school pickup line, the Civic your teenager just inherited. Statistically, the most ordinary object in American life is also its deadliest shape.

How much deadlier a sedan is than an SUV, per 1,000 registered vehicles (1.60 vs. 0.68)

Maybe you are thinking this is a mileage artifact. Sedans are commuter cars; of course they rack up exposure. So I ran the per-mile numbers, expecting the gap to collapse, and it got worse. Per 100 million vehicle miles traveled, sedans kill 1.39 people while SUVs kill 0.55. The ratio stretches to 2.5 to 1.[1] Mile for mile, hour for hour, the sedan remains the grim champion, which means exposure is not the explanation and something structural is.

Drunk drivers was my next guess, but the numbers killed that theory too. Driver impairment in fatal sedan crashes runs 20.4 percent, versus 19.5 percent in fatal SUV crashes.[1] The difference is a rounding error wearing a costume. Old beaters? Also no, and this one genuinely surprised me: the median model year of a sedan involved in a fatal crash is 2007, a year newer than the SUV median of 2006. The sedan fleet dying on American roads is not some rusting relic of the nineties. It is the car your neighbor bought certified pre-owned.

What is left is mass, the oldest physics on the road. Below roughly 4,000 pounds of curb weight, every additional 500 pounds buys the occupant a large reduction in death risk; that is the IIHS finding, not mine, and it shows up here with a sledgehammer.[4] Sedans cluster below the line, and SUVs cluster above it. And here is the part that keeps me up at night: the same mass that shields the SUV's occupants is the mass that kills everyone around them. This site has documented the other half of the ledger for years. The SUV is the safest place to sit in a crash and one of the most dangerous things to be crashed into. There is no version of this story where physics picks a hero. There is only a ledger, and everybody pays somewhere on it.

Run the arithmetic to its conclusion and it gets bleak. If sedans died at the SUV per-vehicle rate, 51,077 fewer people would have died over the decade. That is roughly 5,100 a year, every year, a number larger than the annual death toll of several entire vehicle classes.[1] The body count is not driven by exotics. It is driven by the Honda Accord (7,102 deaths), the Honda Civic (6,553), the Toyota Camry (6,328), the Toyota Corolla (4,945).[1] The deadliest cars in America are the ones the reliability forums told you to buy.

What to do with this: if you are choosing between a sedan and a comparably priced small SUV for family duty, the data has a preference, and it is not subtle. Mass protects occupants, so within reason, buy the heavier vehicle with the better IIHS crash ratings, and check any used VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before money changes hands. But do not read this as an excuse to buy a Suburban. Above about 4,000 pounds, your own marginal safety gain nearly flatlines while the danger you pose to pedestrians, cyclists, and everyone in a sedan keeps climbing.[4] The rational purchase is the smallest vehicle that still clears the weight your own survival requires. Nobody at the dealership will tell you that, because the dealership sells mass by the pound.
