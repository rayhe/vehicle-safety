NHTSA Just Closed the File on 807,000 Odysseys. The Airbags That Fire on Their Own Walk Free.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
September 29, 2026

Let’s talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds. That is the entire career of an airbag: the crash sensor feels a deceleration spike, the SRS control unit decides whether the spike is real, and a chemical inflator ignites to fill a nylon bag before your head arrives. Every step depends on the sensor telling the truth. Today, NHTSA closed a file on 806,963 Honda Odysseys whose sensors might not.[1]

Defect petition DP26005, filed May 30 and opened for review in July, alleged that the airbags in 2011 through 2017 Odysseys can deploy while the vehicle is in motion, with no impact, no rollover, and no G-force threshold breach. Thin evidence backed it: 10 complaints to the Office of Defects Investigation. NHTSA reviewed the complaints and other reports, found no clear pattern or linking factor, noted zero crashes and zero severe injuries, and denied the petition, closing the file with no formal probe.[1][2]

10
Complaints that carried the entire petition for 806,963 vehicles

Read the petition’s second allegation before you agree with the denial. It says the diagnostic trouble codes and the supplemental restraint system data conflict: the airbag system records inaccurate information about its own deployments, so sit with that. NHTSA closed the case because it found no pattern in the complaint data, but one of the allegations is that the car’s own black box cannot be trusted to describe what happened. Absence of a pattern is not the same as absence of a defect when the measuring instrument is a suspect.

Consider the timing. In April 2026, Honda recalled 440,830 of the 2018 through 2022 Odysseys because the side curtain airbags could deploy from potholes and speed bumps; the SRS computer misread G-forces as a side impact. Honda had identified the flaw in 2021 and fixed production in 2022, but did not recall the cars already sold until regulators forced the issue.[3] In May 2026, Honda expanded another Odyssey recall for cracking seat weight sensors that could make the front airbags deploy improperly.[3] That is two airbag recalls on the newer generation in two months, followed by today’s denial for the older generation: same badge, same failure family of uncommanded or improper deployment, yet opposite regulatory outcomes within five months.

FARS puts the Odyssey’s record in context. From 2014 through 2023, the Odyssey was involved in 864 deaths across 2,028 fatal crashes, a rate of 0.93 per 100 million vehicle miles. Its drivers are among the soberest in the dataset: 15.4 percent showed any impairment, against 20.0 percent fleet-wide.[4] This is the paradox of the family van: in an Odyssey, the danger is rarely the person holding the wheel. Compare it with its rivals and the picture sharpens: the Toyota Sienna kills at 0.49, the Chrysler Pacifica at 0.19. Its rate is nearly double the Sienna’s and almost five times the Pacifica’s.[4]

Now the other side, at full strength, because the denial has a real case. Ten complaints across 806,963 vehicles over roughly fifteen years is about 1.2 per 100,000, which is deep in noise territory. Every one of those deployments produced no crash and no severe injury, which means the alleged defect, even if real, has so far been a startling nuisance rather than a killer. ODI cannot open a formal investigation on every low-count petition without drowning; the denial is triage, and triage is the system working. You can hold both thoughts: the measurement paradox is real, and the agency still made the right call on the evidence it had.

What I cannot tell you is whether any of those 10 deployments actually happened the way the complainants described, because the diagnostic data is disputed and NHTSA’s full review is not public. I also cannot tie any of the 864 FARS deaths to an airbag deployment; FARS has no field for that, and I will not pretend otherwise. What I can tell you is that an airbag is a small explosive with a hair trigger, and the only thing between you and it is software that Honda has now admitted, twice in two months, can get the answer wrong on newer Odysseys.

If you own a 2011 through 2017 Odyssey: no recall is coming from this petition. If your airbags have ever deployed without a crash, file the complaint at nhtsa.gov anyway. Petitions get reopened when new evidence arrives, and 10 complaints was the entire foundation here; the next 10 could change the math. If you own a 2018 or newer Odyssey, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today and check for the April and May 2026 airbag recalls.[5] And if you are shopping for a used minivan, know the rates: Odyssey 0.93, Sienna 0.49, Pacifica 0.19. For all the marketing about family safety, the safest choice in the segment is not the one with the best commercials.
