# Research: #1083 — "NHTSA Wants Your Ideas on Recalls. It Just Won't Let You Watch."

## News peg
Sept 28, 2026: NHTSA published a Federal Register notice (FR Doc. 2026-19712) for a public meeting titled "Recalls Next: Technological Innovations and Safety Recalls," to be held November 5, 2026, 8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. ET, in the West Building Atrium of DOT headquarters in Washington, D.C. The meeting asks the public how to get more drivers to actually fix recalled vehicles. The notice's own words: "The event will not be live streamed or recorded." Public docket NHTSA-2026-2014 stays open for comments until December 5, 2026. Registration/accommodation deadline: October 28 (NHTSA event page) or October 29 (Federal Register notice) — the two disagree.

## The novel thread (original contribution)
The recall system has a completion problem, and the meeting about fixing it is inaccessible to the people it needs most. Original arithmetic:
- 29.2M vehicles covered by ~900 recalls in 2025 vs. ~282M registered US vehicles: roughly 1 in 10 registered vehicles was recalled in a single year (this site's #997 made the same ratio from the 997/29M figure; the FR notice's "nearly 900 / 29.2M" confirms the order of magnitude from the agency itself).
- Completion arithmetic: prior Crash Report research on a tire recall found a ~12% completion rate. If even a 20% non-completion rate applied to 29.2M recalled vehicles, ~5.8M vehicles with open safety defects are rolling around unrepaired — and the agency's own notice names the reason: letters go to previous owners.
- The comment-window asymmetry: 30-day post-meeting comment window (Nov 5 → Dec 5) is open to everyone, but "the information presented" that the public is asked to comment on is only fully available to people physically in the DOT atrium. The notice does not say slides or summaries will be posted to the docket.
- The procedure asymmetry: companies may file confidential business information under 49 CFR part 512 (redacted public copy + unredacted copy to Chief Counsel); individual commenters are warned everything posts "without change" to regulations.gov, personal info included.
- The stakeholder filter: the groups the notice says NHTSA struggles to reach — used-car owners, people whose addresses changed, lower-completion-rate communities — are exactly the groups least likely to spend a Thursday in a federal atrium. The groups most likely to attend: automakers, dealer groups, suppliers, trade associations.
- Deadline mismatch: FR notice says Oct 29, NHTSA event page says Oct 28 — a one-day discrepancy on the single action item the public can take.

## Arithmetic (original)
- 29.2M / ~282M registered vehicles ≈ 10.4% of the US fleet recalled in 2025.
- 29.2M × 0.20 (illustrative non-completion) ≈ 5.8M vehicles with open recalls still in use.
- 15th consecutive quarterly decline in fatalities (through Q4 2025) coexists with the completion problem: deaths are falling while recall repair rates lag — the two trends are not obviously connected.

## Limitations
- "Nearly 900 recalls / 29.2M vehicles" is NHTSA's own figure from the notice, not independently audited; includes equipment/tire campaigns alongside vehicle campaigns.
- The 12% completion figure came from a single tire-recall case study on this site; vehicle recalls generally complete at higher rates (industry estimates ~70-75% over 18 months for passenger vehicles), so the 5.8M figure is illustrative of the gap, not a measured count.
- Whether slides/summaries will be posted to docket NHTSA-2026-2014 is unstated in the notice — absence of a commitment is not proof of absence of posting.
- The FR notice vs. event page deadline mismatch (Oct 28 vs Oct 29) was reported by The Auto Wire; the current live pages were not re-verified in this research pass.

## Strongest counterargument
Facilitated workshop tables are a legitimate format for honest discussion — streaming can inhibit candor, and the docket is genuinely open to everyone for 30 days after the meeting. NHTSA routinely publishes summaries of public meetings, so the "in-person only" framing may be overstated; the agency may post materials afterward even if the notice doesn't promise it. The real recall-completion problem (stale addresses, used-car churn) is a data-pipeline problem, not a transparency problem, and a meeting format critique is a sideshow next to the OTA/mobile-repair tools actually on the agenda. The Oct 28/29 mismatch is a one-day typo, not a conspiracy.

## Actionable takeaway
1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now — free, shows open recalls whether or not you ever got a letter. Bought used? Register the car with the manufacturer so notices reach you.
2. Want to shape recall policy? File a comment on docket NHTSA-2026-2014 at regulations.gov before December 5 — and leave personal details out of what you submit (NHTSA posts individual comments "without change").
3. Planning to attend Nov 5 in D.C.? Register by October 28 (use the earlier date given the FR notice/event page mismatch).
