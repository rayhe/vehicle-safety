# Research: The HALT Act Body Count — 44,000+ Impaired-Driver Fatal Crashes While NHTSA Studies

## Angle
Congress ordered NHTSA to mandate impaired driving prevention tech in all new cars (IIJA Section 24220, signed Nov 15 2021). Deadline was Nov 15 2024. NHTSA missed it. As of July 2026, they haven't even published a *proposed* rule — they're still in pre-rule stage analyzing comments from a Jan 2024 ANPRM. Meanwhile, the FARS data lets us calculate exactly how many impaired-driver fatal crashes occurred during the delay.

## Self-critique gate
Is this genuinely surprising after 655 articles? YES — this is the first article to compute the specific "delay death toll" of the IIJA Section 24220 mandate. Existing interlock articles cover historical mandate failures, not this specific modern stall. The NHTSA DMS study notice (June 2026) makes it topical. The 44,000+ number is original analysis.

## Key Data Sources

### 1. FARS Toxicology Data (2014-2023)
- Total drivers in fatal crashes: 490,736
- Any impairment: 98,348 (20.0%)
- Alcohol-positive: 74,188 (15.1%)
- Drug-positive: 42,496 (8.7%)
- Annual average impaired drivers in fatal crashes: ~9,835

### 2. IIJA Section 24220 Timeline
- Nov 15, 2021: IIJA signed, 3-year deadline for final rule
- Jul 2023: First report to Congress (no progress on rulemaking)
- Jan 5, 2024: ANPRM published (89 FR 830) — soliciting information, not proposing a rule
- Mar 5, 2024: ANPRM comment period ends (3,000+ comments)
- Nov 15, 2024: STATUTORY DEADLINE PASSES — no rule issued
- Dec 13, 2024: Second report to Congress
- Feb 2026: Third report to Congress — "findings have not changed materially"
- Mar 2026: Carscoops reports NHTSA admits false positive rate "unacceptably high"
- Jun 10, 2026: NHTSA publishes notice requesting OMB approval for a DMS STUDY — still in data-gathering mode

### 3. The Body Count During Delay
From IIJA signing (Nov 2021) to now (Jul 2026) = 4.7 years
FARS annual impaired drivers in fatal crashes: ~9,835
4.7 × 9,835 ≈ 46,225 impaired-driver-involved fatal crashes during delay

IIHS/MADD estimate: technology would save 10,100 lives/year when fully implemented
Even at 50% effectiveness: 5,050 × 4.7 = ~23,735 preventable deaths during delay
Even at 25% effectiveness: 2,525 × 4.7 = ~11,867 preventable deaths

### 4. Why the delay (from NHTSA's own reports)
- No technology currently meets all requirements: passive, accurate, no driver input required
- False positive rate too high — would strand sober drivers
- Breath sensors (DADSS/Senseair): can detect BAC through skin but accuracy at legal limit (0.08) is insufficient
- Camera-based DMS: can detect drowsiness/distraction but not blood alcohol
- Touch-based alcohol detectors: privacy concerns, accuracy varies with skin conditions
- "The detection technology around the legal limit continues to have an error rate that would be unacceptably high" — NHTSA

### 5. FARS Cross-tab: Which vehicles would benefit most?
- Sports cars: 22.5% impairment rate (highest class)
- Sedans: 20.4%
- Pickups: 20.1%
- SUVs: 19.5%
- Vans: 18.1% (lowest — family vehicles)

Top vehicles: Buick Park Avenue 31.7%, Chevy C/K Pickup 28%, Audi A3 27.1%, Chevy Astro Van 27%, Corvette 26.2%

### 6. The irony
NHTSA can mandate a fake noise for quiet EVs (FMVSS 141, finalized 2016, effective 2019) — a rule that affects <5% of the fleet — but can't finalize a rule for impaired driving prevention that affects 20% of all fatal crashes.

## Limitations
- FARS impairment data is from drivers in fatal crashes, not all crashes
- "Any impairment" includes low-level drug positives that wouldn't trigger a DMS
- The 10,100 lives/year figure is from IIHS and assumes full fleet penetration (decades away even if rule passes tomorrow)
- NHTSA's delay may be justified if premature mandates produce bad tech (false positives stranding people)
- ANPRM comment analysis is legitimate regulatory process, not simple foot-dragging
