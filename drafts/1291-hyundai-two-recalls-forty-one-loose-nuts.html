Hyundai Recalled 41 Brand-New Cars Because Somebody Didn't Tighten the Nuts. Twice.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 26, 2026

Forty-one cars. That is the whole recall. Not 41,000. Forty-one. NHTSA campaigns 26V692 and 26V693, both filed in October 2026, cover 41 brand-new 2026 Hyundais whose fasteners were not tightened enough at the factory, and the fix is a torque wrench rather than a redesign, a new part, or a software patch.[1]

41
Brand-new 2026 Hyundais recalled across two campaigns for fasteners not tightened enough

Campaign 26V692 covers 24 cars, 20 Elantras and four Tucson Hybrids, whose front strut assemblies may not have been secured to the front steering knuckles, which means a suspension component could separate while driving.[1] Hyundai knows of one incident tied to that campaign, with no crashes, no fires, and no injuries. Campaign 26V693 covers 17 cars, eight Venues, four Elantras, four Elantra Hybrids, and one Elantra N, whose wheel lug nuts may loosen until a wheel detaches, turning a brand-new car into a road hazard for everyone behind it.[2] Hyundai knows of no incidents at all in that second group.

Either recall alone would be a footnote. Together they are a pattern, because both groups came out of Hyundai's Ulsan plant in South Korea, both used fasteners from the same supplier, Samjin Nut, and Hyundai blames manufacturing lapses in both cases rather than defective parts.[1] Same plant. Same supplier. Same failure mode, once on a steering component and once on a wheel, which are the two places on a car where "loose" stops being an annoyance and starts being a crash. Add a third campaign Autoblog cites, 26V314, covering 172 Ioniq 5 and Ioniq 9 cars with insufficiently tightened rear suspension fasteners built at Hyundai's Georgia plant, and the pattern total reaches 213 cars across two plants in two countries.[1]

Fair objection, stated properly: 41 cars is nothing, nobody got hurt, one incident total, and both recalls were caught before a wheel or a knuckle actually let go on a public road. A reasonable reader could call this the system working exactly as designed, a tiny population with a free fix and letters going out December 19, 2026. That objection is fair on the body count and still misses the point, because the same failure mode showing up in three campaigns at two plants means the next under-torqued batch may not be 41 cars, and a wheel does not fail gently when it finally does fail.

Honestly stated, this article does not prove the root cause beyond Hyundai's own framing: the supplier and plant details come from Hyundai via press coverage of the filings, not from Part 573 documents read directly for this piece, and no fatality data is involved, so no death rate is computed or should be inferred from these numbers. Also note that 213 is a pattern total across three campaigns with different fasteners and different plants, not a single defect population, and it should not be quoted as one.

What you actually do: if you bought a 2026 Elantra, Elantra Hybrid, Elantra N, Venue, or Tucson Hybrid, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for the December 19 letter, because a retorque is free and takes less time than reading this article took.[3] For campaign 26V693, Hyundai's recall number is 310 and its customer service line is 1-855-371-9460. Shopping one of these cars used, check both 26V692 and 26V693, not just one, since the Elantra appears in both campaigns and clearing one does not clear the other.
