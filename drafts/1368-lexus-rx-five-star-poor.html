The Five-Star Lexus RX Earned a Poor on the One Test That Checks the Back Seat

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 2, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. NHTSA hands the volume-selling 2026 Lexus RX a 5-star overall rating, while IIHS scores it Poor on the updated moderate-overlap front test. That is the single crash test that puts a dummy in the back seat. And it is the only reason the RX appears on neither the 2026 Top Safety Pick nor Top Safety Pick+ list.

Poor
IIHS updated moderate-overlap rating for the 2026 Lexus RX, a 5-star NHTSA vehicle

Here is what the five stars actually cover. NHTSA's frontal test seats its dummies in the front row only; side and rollover tests never check what happens to the small adult or twelve-year-old in the rear seat when the front of the car hits something. IIHS's updated test, launched in 2022, fixes exactly that blind spot with a rear-seat dummy, and it grades the things that maim back-seat riders: chest deflection from the belt, submarining under a loose lap belt, head excursion into the front seatback.[1] Small overlap scores Good on both sides and the side test is Good; the car did not fail the test, its rear seatbelts did.

It gets worse: the RX used to pass. When the updated test launched, the RX rated Marginal. Then IIHS tightened the chest-injury index in 2024, and the RX dropped to Poor with zero changes to the vehicle; the bar moved, the car didn't. That is the honest version: Lexus hasn't made this SUV worse. Today's measuring stick is simply better, and now we can see what was always there. Since the 2024 criteria cycle, a Good on this test is required for any IIHS award, so one Poor erases an otherwise strong report card.[2]

Fairness check, because you deserve one. Its real-world back-seat injury record is thin; the fifth-generation body-in-white only dates to 2023, and there is not enough FARS rear-occupant data to confirm the dummy's chest measurements translate into actual injuries.[3] Plenty of luxury competitors also flunked the updated test at launch, and the front seats of this SUV are genuinely among the safest structures you can buy. A Poor on one rear-passenger metric is not the same as an unsafe car. But. Five stars on the window sticker create an expectation of uniform safety that one ungraded back seat quietly breaks, and that is exactly the expectation a fifty-thousand-dollar luxury badge sells you.

So here is the action, plain and simple. When you comparison-shop any SUV, open the IIHS ratings page and look specifically at the updated moderate-overlap line, not the star count. If back-seat passengers matter to you, from kids in boosters to carpools to your own parents, a Poor there is the one result NHTSA's stars will never warn you about. And note the family irony: the smaller, cheaper 2026 Lexus NX made the Top Safety Pick list while the flagship didn't, so shop accordingly.[4]
