# Research — Rivian R2's Second Recall: The Loose Bolt (slug: rivian-r2-loose-bolt-second-recall)
**Article #1087 candidate** | Journalist: Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread Columnist) | Date: 2026-10-07

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Rivian's make-or-break mass-market EV is on recall #2 — and this one is a bolt. A human factory operator caught an under-torqued high-voltage battery fastener that the automated torque station certified as fine, after three months of builds.

## Self-critique gate
- Surprising after 800+ articles? Yes: it's not another recall-size story. The paradox is that the *quality-control robot failed and the human didn't*. Automation trust inverted. Rivian stories on the site (rivian-every-link-broke, rivian-toe-link-investigation-115k) are all about the R1 trucks; the R2 is the brand's first mass-market vehicle and this is its second recall ever.
- Real data with sources? Yes: NHTSA 26V625 with exact build window, part number, torque-station details.
- Verdict: **Proceed.**

## Facts (all sourced, no fabrication)
- NHTSA Recall 26V625 / Rivian FSAM-1888: certain 2027 Rivian R2 vehicles; high voltage battery fastener improperly tightened → sudden loss of drive power; **14 units affected**, build window **May 18 – Aug 27, 2026** (oemdtc.com mirror of NHTSA filing).
- Rivian estimates **100% of the recall population has the defect**; **zero customer complaints or incidents** known; **no warning** before loss of motive power (NHTSA filing, via newestcarsusa).
- Discovered **September 17, 2026** by a factory operator who spotted an under-torqued fastener that had already passed the plant's automated torque station; operator escalated to quality. Automated station reprogrammed to reject under-torque fasteners. NHTSA filing does not explain how the station was mis-programmed (autoevolution).
- Subject part: M6x1.0x24.5 bolt, part number SC00036202-D (autoevolution).
- This is the R2's **second** recall. First (last month / Sep 2026): rearview camera bug, 2,481 R2s built Apr 23–Aug 10, 2026, shared with R1 (~100k total incl. R1s per RivianWave; ~2,481 R2s per autoevolution), fixed **over-the-air**. This one requires a physical service visit (rivianwave, autoblog).
- Rivian told TechCrunch all 14 affected vehicles are already at service centers (via newestcarsusa).
- Owner notification letters mail November 28, 2026; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov same day (NHTSA filing). Rivian customer service 1-888-748-4261.
- Context: R2 is Rivian's crucial affordable EV on the path to profitability; entered production ~Apr 2026 (autoblog).

## Original contribution
1. The inverted quality gate: the automated torque station certified every under-torqued bolt for ~3 months (May 18–Aug 27). The catch came from a human eye on Sep 17, ~3 weeks after the last bad build. No sampling regime would catch a 100% failure rate inside the acceptance window — the station wasn't sampling, it was *certifying*. The recall population = exactly the window the mis-programmed station ran.
2. Contrast math: Recall #1 was 2,481 R2s fixed by OTA push (zero dealer visits); recall #2 is 14 R2s requiring physical service. Ratio of hassle to fleet size runs the wrong way: the smaller recall is the harder fix.

## Limitations
- 14 vehicles, zero incidents, zero complaints. This is a manufacturing-QC story, not a body-count story. FARS data does not apply (vehicle is 3 months old). No fatalities/injuries claimed or implied.
- NHTSA filing does not explain how the torque station was mis-programmed or whether the station was 100%-inspection or sampling-based. State that explicitly.
- Long-term R2 reliability conclusions cannot be drawn from two recalls on a launch vehicle; say so.

## Strongest counterargument
This is arguably the system working: a human caught it before any customer incident, Rivian filed voluntarily, reprogrammed the station, and all 14 vehicles were pulled into service centers. Compared to GM's L87 (499 post-remedy failure complaints, engines dying after the fix), Rivian's response is textbook. The real story may be good QC, not bad QC.

## Actionable takeaway
If you own a 2027 R2 built May–Aug 2026: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls after Nov 28, 2026; loss of drive power comes with no warning. Otherwise: when shopping used early-build EVs, ask whether recalls were software (OTA) or hardware (service visit) — the small ones can be the annoying ones.
