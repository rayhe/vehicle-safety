# Research: #1193 — Ford Super Duty / Expedition Contaminated-LED Headlight Recall

**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate; car-buying guidance; "what this means for you")
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Candidate headline:** "Ford Recalled 41,748 Nearly-New Trucks Because a Semiconductor Factory Contaminated the Headlights"

## The news (primary sources)

**NHTSA campaign 26V606000 / Ford 26C43**, filed Sept 22, 2026:
- 41,748 vehicles: 2025–2027 Ford Expedition (13,298), 2026 F-250 (17,512), F-350 (10,295), F-450 (523), F-550 (120) Super Duty.
- Defect: contaminated LED chips in headlight assemblies. Super Duty trucks can lose daytime running lights, parking lights, low beams, or high beams; Expeditions can lose low or high beams.
- FMVSS 108 noncompliance (49 CFR 571.108 — adequate illumination of the roadway; conspicuity of the vehicle).
- Remedy: dealers inspect and replace headlight assemblies as necessary, free of charge. Owner letters mailed Oct 26, 2026. VINs searchable on nhtsa.gov from Oct 26, 2026.
- Ford customer service: 1-866-436-7332. NHTSA hotline: 1-888-327-4236.
- Warning sign: dashboard indicator illuminates if low/high beams fail.
- Ford estimates 1% of the population actually carries the defect → roughly 417 vehicles expected to have contaminated chips.

**Part 573 chronology (RCLRPT-26V606-9603.pdf):**
- May 21, 2026: warranty claims citing inoperative LED headlamps in 2026 Super Duty brought to Ford's Critical Concern Review Group.
- Root cause: contamination of LED chips, traced to the **Tier 3 chip supplier's manufacturing facility** (Tier 1 lighting supplier: Flex N Gate Inc, Effingham IL).
- July–Aug 2026: affected population scoped via warranty returns + supplier shipping records + traceability at Kentucky Truck Plant.
- As of Sept 10, 2026: 6 warranty claims + 6 field reports on 6 unique VINs; **no accidents or injuries known**.
- Sept 15, 2026: Field Review Committee approved the action.

## Kill test
- Newsworthy? Yes: 41,748 vehicles, most from the 2026 model year, losing headlights while driving. Fresh (filed Sept 22, letters going out Oct 26 — owners being notified NOW).
- Novel after 1,192 articles? Verified: zero prior coverage in the queue (greps for "26v606", "led headlight", "contaminated" returned nothing; #1164 was Explorer headlight/EGR recalls — different campaign).
- Fresh angle: this is a **semiconductor-fab defect on a car part**. The "bulb" is dead — modern headlights are sealed semiconductor assemblies. The contamination happened at a facility Ford doesn't directly contract with (Tier 3), and the only fix is replacing the entire assembly. That plus the math: Ford says 1% defect → 417 trucks are already driving around with time-bomb headlights and their owners may not know until dark.

## Original contribution
- The 1% math: 41,748 × 1% ≈ 417 vehicles expected with contaminated chips. Most will never fail visibly — but the recall inspection is the only way to know.
- Supply-chain depth angle: this is the third-ish supply-chain tier story in the queue (cf. Toyota furnace-skipped knuckles #1163, VW steering bolt #953) — but the first where the defect happened at the *chip-fabrication* level. A dirty fab killed headlights on a $90k truck.
- Comparison: sealed LED assemblies vs. the $8 replaceable bulb era — when the light dies now, the whole unit (hundreds of dollars) gets replaced under warranty, and out of warranty it's the owner's problem.

## Limitations
- Ford's 1% defect estimate is a guess, not measured; real count could be higher or lower.
- The Part 573 report doesn't name the Tier 3 chip supplier.
- No accident/injury data: 6 warranty claims + 6 field reports is thin; underreporting possible since a dimming headlight is easy to miss in daylight.
- FARS data won't show this — the recall is too new and no injuries are attributed.

## Strongest counterargument
- Ford found this fast (warranty claims → field action in ~4 months), flagged it themselves, and a dashboard warning lights up when beams fail. Nobody is hurt. The system worked. This is arguably a recall done right — recall volume ≠ scandal.

## Actionable insights (HARD GATE)
- If you own a 2025–2027 Expedition or 2026 Super Duty: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable from Oct 26, 2026).
- Don't assume a dim headlight is a burned-out bulb — there's no bulb; the sealed assembly needs dealer diagnosis.
- Watch for the headlamp warning indicator on the dash.
- Letters mail Oct 26; inspection and assembly replacement are free.
- Used-car shoppers: these are nearly-new trucks; check recall status before buying.
