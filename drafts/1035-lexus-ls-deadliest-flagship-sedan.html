Sobriety Report

The Lexus LS Is the Deadliest Flagship Sedan in America

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
October 1, 2026

According to the toxicology reports, and there are a lot of them, the champagne of luxury sedans comes with a body count. Lexus built its reputation on cars that never break; its flagship breaks people instead. Lexus’s LS posts 1.44 deaths per 100 million vehicle miles traveled, the worst fatality rate of any flagship sedan in the FARS data. Audi’s A6 manages 0.32; the S-Class sits at 0.40. Put plainly, the reliability king’s flagship kills at four and a half times the rate of the Audi.[1]

4.5x
How much deadlier the Lexus LS is than an Audi A6, per 100 million miles

Now the part I actually get paid to read. In fatal crashes, 21.3 percent of BMW 7 Series drivers tested positive for alcohol. That ties the Corvette for fifth-highest among all 307 models in the dataset.[2] Read that again: a six-figure Bavarian boardroom keeps pace with a fiberglass midlife crisis. The LS runs 18 percent alcohol-positive and 23.3 percent impaired by anything at all. Nearly one in four flagship fatalities had a driver who shouldn’t have been driving.

Blame depreciation: in the model-year data, 93 percent of LS fatal-crash deaths ride in pre-2010 cars.[3] A flagship sedan slides from $75,000 to $8,000, and somewhere around the third owner the driver profile flips completely. Your dentist bought it new; a 22-year-old with two DUIs bought it last. Same sheet metal, different species behind the wheel.

Fair objection, at full volume: maybe the car is fine and the owners are the problem. Depreciated flagships attract younger, poorer, drunker drivers, and the VMT estimates for low-volume luxury cars are squishy enough that 4.5x should be read as “much worse,” not a laboratory measurement. FARS can’t fully separate the machine from the maniac.

So what do you actually do with this? If you’re shopping the depreciated-flagship market, ignore the badge and read the IIHS rating for the exact model year you’re buying.[4] Reputations are earned by cars with 40,000 miles, not 140,000. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, because third owners don’t do recall work.[5] And when the deal looks too good, remember the toxicology: one in four of these crashes had an impaired driver. A flagship is only as sober as its cheapest previous owner.
