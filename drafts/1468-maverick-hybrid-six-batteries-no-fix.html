Six Batteries, Two Fixes, Zero Recalls: The Maverick Hybrid's Dead-Battery Saga

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 11, 2026

On November 7, a Maine owner posted that his 2022 Ford Maverick Hybrid had just received its sixth 12-volt battery. Three of those batteries went in after Ford told its dealers a software fix was on the way. He drives the truck for work, gets 46 mpg in a cold climate, and loves nearly everything about it. What he does not love is a truck that eats a battery every eight months.[1]

1 every 8 months
Battery replacement rate for one 2022 Maverick Hybrid: six 12-volt batteries in about four years, against a normal service life of three to five years per battery.

Ford has known about the drain since October 2024, when its first dealer message promised a software solution in the first quarter of 2025. That fix arrived six weeks late, in May 2025, as an update to the air conditioning control module. Eight months later Ford admitted some trucks still drained to a no-start condition after the update, and by March 2026 it had found a second cause entirely: a brake-pad self-adjustment routine run by the anti-lock brake module, quietly sipping power while the truck sat parked. Until new ABS software was ready, Ford's official interim advice was to set the parking brake every time you turn the truck off, so the routine never runs. ABS software finally landed in April 2026, thirteen months past the first promised date, and Ford reissued the repair again on September 30. Eight dealer messages in twenty-four months, two root causes, three software updates, one parking-brake tip.[1][4]

And then there is the insult. Ford did replace 12-volt batteries for free in one campaign: recall 25V019 covered 272,817 vehicles with defective batteries from a single supplier, and the remedy was a new AGM unit at no charge. Read the recall report and the catch jumps out. It lists 2021 to 2023 Bronco Sports and 2022 to 2023 Mavericks with a gas powertrain. Hybrids are not on it. In February 2026 a 2023 Maverick Hybrid owner complained to NHTSA that his dealer charged him for a battery anyway, writing that the recall mandated no-cost replacements "for these vehicles." It did not. His truck was never in the campaign, whatever the service writer believed.[3]

Timing made it worse. Bumper-to-bumper coverage on a 2022 runs three years or 36,000 miles, and the hybrid's longer eight-year coverage protects the high-voltage battery and the DC/DC converter, not the 12-volt battery. For the Maine owner, coverage ended around spring 2025, just as the first fix arrived. His words: Ford "kicking the can until the 3 years had passed." One 2023 owner paid $1,833 at 45,000 miles. NHTSA opened a defect petition review, DP26007, on August 24, 2026, covering roughly 82,000 Maverick Hybrids, and a November 3 complaint from a 2022 owner cites the newest dealer message and ends with the truck in the shop for over a week while "Ford Motor Company refuses to cover the repairs."[1][2]

The counterargument, at full strength: one owner's six batteries proves nothing about how common the drain is, and the same forum thread shows the spread, with some hybrids running three and a half years on the factory battery and others dying in four months. Causes vary, from module software to telematics hunting for a weak signal, and owners who switch to AGM batteries and adjust settings often stop having problems. A dead 12-volt battery strands you; it does not crash you, and no crash, injury, or death is tied to this drain in any record cited here, which is plausibly why this is a petition and a stack of dealer messages rather than a recall. Ford kept working the problem in writing for two years and handed owners a free workaround. If the drain is mostly an annoyance with a known workaround and a software path, the scandal framing overstates it.

What this does not prove: nobody has published a verified count of how many of the roughly 82,000 hybrids in the petition population actually experience the drain, and whether the Maine owner's truck received the April or September 2026 updates is unknown, so his six batteries may predate the final fix. FARS carries no Maverick fatality data in this site's dataset, so no death-rate comparison is offered, and no per-battery price is asserted beyond the single $1,833 owner report. What the record does establish is narrower and still damning: Ford diagnosed this in writing for two years, fixed it twice, told owners to use the parking brake, and ran a free-battery recall that left the affected trucks out.

If you own a 2022 to 2024 Maverick Hybrid, do this. Before your next battery, get three things on paper: whether the ABS and air conditioning updates from messages SSM 54785 or SSM 55215 are actually on your truck, a printed battery test, and a parasitic draw reading, because a new battery can test fine and still drain. Keep every receipt and quote the message numbers when you call Ford; they give the dealer something specific to look up. Set the parking brake every time you shut the truck off while it gets sorted. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and if a dealer charges you for a battery Ford's own messages acknowledge, file a complaint with NHTSA, because defect petition DP26007 exists only because owners filed.[1][5]
