NHTSA Just Closed the Book on 1.08M Burning Jeeps. The Park-Outside Order Stays.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 2, 2026
☕ 4 min read

On September 29, the federal government closed its investigation into 1.08 million Jeeps that can catch fire while parked: case closed, except the park-outside order is still in force.[1]

That is the whole story, and it is a strange one: NHTSA ended its probe because Stellantis’s recall already covers the defect, then immediately reminded everyone that the defect is still out there, in driveways, and the trucks should sleep outside.

1.08M
Wranglers and Gladiators under a recall that still carries a park-outside order

NHTSA opened its preliminary evaluation in September 2024, after reports of engine-compartment fires in Wranglers and Gladiators with the ignition off, which is to say: parked, cold, and doing nothing.[2] Several of the fires reportedly started near the power steering pump, on the passenger side of the engine bay, in trucks that had been sitting there for hours.

The culprit is the electric-hydraulic power steering pump, which uses an electric motor for hydraulic assist instead of a belt. Stellantis found the pump’s electrical connector could be manufactured outside specification, just enough that it can’t fully seat. A loose connector means electrical resistance; resistance at high current means heat; heat melts the connector and can ignite whatever is nearby. Your Wrangler can burn down your garage without ever leaving it. That sentence is doing a lot of work, and all of it is true.

Before the feds ever opened a file, Stellantis investigated the fires itself, from 2023 into early 2024. Then the company closed its own investigation, citing a low fire rate, and fires kept happening. Stellantis reopened the case in August 2024 and spent more than a year on it (vehicle buybacks, CT scans of wiring connectors, bench testing) before confirming the defect in April 2026 and recalling the trucks that June.[3]

So the file has now been closed twice: once by the automaker, because the fires looked rare enough to dismiss, and once by the government, because the recall covered what it was investigating. The recall covers 2021–2025 Wranglers and Gladiators built between June 24, 2020 and December 2, 2024, nearly 1.08 million trucks in the US. That is 38 percent more vehicles than NHTSA’s original probe covered; the probe drew a box around 781,500 trucks from model years 2021–2023, and the defect turned out to be bigger than the box.

Reuters tied 72 fires and one injury to the defect as of June 2026. If you own one: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or through Stellantis’s Mopar system, because matching the model year alone does not confirm your truck is included.[4] Until the repair is done, park outside, away from structures and other vehicles. Owners pay nothing: dealers inspect the connector and repair or replace the harness or pump, and the remedy has been available since July 2026.

For scale: our FARS data shows the Wrangler at 1,842 crash deaths from 2014 to 2023, roughly 184 a year, which makes 72 fires in 1.3 million trucks, a 0.006% incidence rate, look almost quaint by comparison.[5] That is also the strongest argument for everyone involved: the fire rate was genuinely tiny, the forensic work was genuinely serious, and the probe’s closure is arguably the system working as designed, which is not the same as an acquittal.

NHTSA says it will keep watching the field reports and can act again if new issues surface. The recall stays, the park-outside order stays, and only the investigation is finished: closed the way a detective closes a case where the suspect is still at large.
