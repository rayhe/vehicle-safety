## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Rosenbauer America filed NHTSA campaign 26V640000 on October 2, 2026: certain 2024-2025 Commander and 2024 Warrior fire engines have rim welds that can crack, separate the wheel, and deflate the tire — so the owners are advised **not to drive their vehicles until the remedy is done**. Ten fire engines got a do-not-drive warning. The trucks that exist to respond to emergencies are, themselves, an emergency.

A fire engine that can catch fire, lose a bolt head as a projectile, lose its emergency lights, or lose a wheel is not having a good run.

## Facts (verified 2026-10-14)
1. **26V640000 (NHTSA Recalls API, live query 2026-10-14):** Rosenbauer America recalling certain 2024-2025 Commander and 2024 Warrior fire engines. "The rim weld may crack, causing the wheel to separate and tire to deflate." Consequence: "Wheel and/or tire failure increases the risk of a crash." Remedy: "Owners are advised not to drive their vehicles until the remedy has been completed. Dealers will replace the wheels and tires as necessary, free of charge." Owner letters expected mailed October 19, 2026. Contact: 1-651-462-1000. API record flags **parkIt: true** (do-not-drive). Report received 10/02/2026.
2. **10 vehicles potentially affected;** letters Oct 19; contact 651-462-1000 (Fleet Maintenance, Oct 13, 2026, citing NHTSA).
3. **26V504000 (Aug 2026):** 2024-2025 Commander + 2026 Commercial M2-106/HV507 with Weldon UV800 display; display may stop responding to touch or fail to power on, preventing activation of exterior emergency lights, firefighting and other emergency equipment. Remedy: firmware update + monitor install.
4. **24V604000 (Aug 2024):** 2023-2024 Commander with certain independent front suspension assemblies; lower control arm retention bolts can become brittle and break after tightening. "A broken bolt head can become a projectile."
5. **23V894000 (Dec 2023):** 2020-2022 Avenger, 2017-2024 Commander, 2020-2024 Warrior + commercials; firmware may fail to shut off output pin on short circuit/wiring failure → overloaded circuits → overheat → **fire risk**. A fire engine that can start a fire.
6. Do-not-drive context: Trucks, Parts, Service (2025) documented that NHTSA "Do Not Drive" / "Park Outside" designations are declarative safety statements above the standard recall severity — real danger, not boilerplate. This site's #954 census covered the 20.75M-vehicle do-not-drive universe (Takata era); a 10-truck campaign is the other end of that spectrum.

## Original contribution
Nobody has connected the four Rosenbauer campaigns as a pattern. Three years, four recalls, four different systems (electrical/fire risk, suspension bolts, emergency-light display, wheel rims). The 26V640 do-not-drive is the sharpest irony, but the pattern is the finding: Rosenbauer's fire engines keep getting recalled for the parts a fire engine most needs to work.

## Strongest counterargument
Ten vehicles. Zero reported crashes or injuries cited in the campaign record. The company found the defect, filed with NHTSA, is replacing wheels and tires free, and the letters go out October 19. This is the system working — a do-not-drive on ten trucks is proof the severity scale exists, not proof of a fleet-wide menace. The pattern reading across four campaigns is an inference, not a root-cause finding; 23V894's population (thousands across six model years) dwarfs 26V640's ten.

## Limitations
- The 10-vehicle count comes from Fleet Maintenance's reading of the NHTSA documents (Oct 13); the live API record confirms the campaign and do-not-drive flag but not the count. No incident/complaint counts are cited for this campaign.
- FARS data cannot speak to this story (no fatalities involved; too new).
- Rosenbauer is a fire-apparatus upfitter; fleet managers should check chassis-OEM recalls separately.
- This is a process/institution story, not a FARS data story — flagged openly.
