# Research Notes: Medical Incapacitation Behind the Wheel

## Angle
NHTSA runs billion-dollar enforcement on impaired driving (alcohol/drugs). But medical incapacitation — seizures, cardiac events, blackouts, diabetic reactions — kills ~1,500+ drivers per year and causes up to 12.7% of all crashes. Only 6 states even require physicians to report medically unfit drivers. 34% of state DMV websites don't tell doctors how to report. The crash cause NHTSA doesn't regulate.

## Journalist
Dale Impactor III — Toxicology Desk Chief. This is a different kind of impairment — medical, not chemical. Fits his beat on driver behavior and demographic analysis.

## Primary Sources

### 1. FARS data (Sheth et al., Epilepsia 2001)
- Analyzed FARS 1995-1997
- ~1,513 driver fatalities per year linked to medical conditions
- 83% cardiac/hypertensive disorders
- 0.6% seizures, 1% diabetes
- For comparison: 13,436 alcohol-related fatal crashes/year
- Source: Medscape report on Sheth SG, Krauss G, Epilepsia 2001;42(suppl 7):215-216
- URL: https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/411141

### 2. Lindsey 2011 — "12.7% of crashes had direct medical cause"
- Referenced in Vézina (2013) Crash Rates of Quebec Drivers with Medical Conditions, PMCID: PMC3861823
- Traditional police-reported figure: 1-2% of crashes medical
- Rigorous investigation: 12.7% — 6x higher than reported
- Over-70 drivers: 11.4% of study population but 30%+ of medically-caused crashes
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3861823/

### 3. JAMA Network Open (Tran & Lee, 2024) — State physician reporting requirements
- Only 6 US states mandate physician reporting: CA, DE, NV, NJ, OR, PA
- 4 of 6 only require reporting "lapses of consciousness"
- 34% of state DMV websites lack ANY instructions for physician reporting
- Only 6% (3 states) accept anonymous reports
- Only 7 states keep physician reports confidential without exception
- 74% of states grant legal immunity to reporting physicians (26% don't)
- DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.50495
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10770772/

### 4. Ontario physician warning study (Redelmeier, 2012)
- When physicians report unfit drivers, crashes drop 41% — from 466/year to 273/year
- $200 million in crash costs saved annually
- BUT: 25% increase in depression visits; 30% reduction in return physician visits
- Source: https://medicalxpress.com/news/2012-09-physicians-unfit-drivers.html

### 5. Utah multi-condition crash study (Thiese, 2015)
- 38,000 commercial drivers studied
- 3+ medical conditions: 93 crashes per million miles vs 29/million for all drivers (3.2x risk)
- CMV context but principle generalizes
- Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293661/

### 6. NHTSA DOT HS 813 399 — Drug/alcohol prevalence trauma study
- 56% of seriously/fatally injured road users tested positive for drugs/alcohol
- For comparison: medical incapacitation isn't tested for at all in crash reporting
- URL: https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/65623/dot_65623_DS1.pdf

### 7. NHTSA Countermeasures That Work — Older Drivers
- As of 2019, 6 states require physician reporting for specific medical conditions
- All 50 states + DC permit physicians to report medically at-risk drivers
- Physicians have been held liable for NOT reporting patients who then crash
- URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/book/countermeasures-that-work/older-drivers/countermeasures/legislation-and-licensing/licensing

### 8. Dementia reporting backfire study (Mattke, 2024)
- 4 states mandate dementia reporting: CA, DE, OR, PA
- 14 states require driver self-reporting
- 32 states + DC have no mandates
- Mandatory reporting associated with underdiagnosis — physicians avoid making the diagnosis
- URL: https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-04-laws-requiring-doctors-dementia-diagnosis.html

## Original Contribution
Cross-referencing the Ontario physician warning efficacy data (41% crash reduction) with the JAMA finding that only 6 states mandate reporting and 34% lack even instructions — estimate of preventable crashes/deaths if all 50 states mandated physician reporting.

## Strongest Counterargument
Mandatory physician reporting could destroy the doctor-patient relationship. The Ontario data shows 30% fewer return visits after a physician warning. Patients may avoid diagnosis entirely — the Mattke 2024 study found mandatory dementia reporting is associated with underdiagnosis. The cure could suppress the detection.

## Limitations
- FARS medical-cause data is from 1995-1997 (Sheth). More recent comprehensive data doesn't exist because NHTSA doesn't systematically track medical incapacitation in crashes.
- The 12.7% figure (Lindsey 2011) is one study, cited in a Quebec paper. The traditional 1-2% comes from police reports, which are known to undercount.
- Multi-condition risk data (Thiese) is for commercial drivers, may not directly generalize.
- Ontario crash reduction data is Canadian healthcare context — physician incentives differ in US.
