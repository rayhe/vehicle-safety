# Research: Ford Recalls 148,663 Mustangs for Fracturing Wiring Ground Eyelets; Fix Arrives March 2027 (26C40)

## Angle
On September 1, 2026, Ford filed three recalls with NHTSA. The biggest: 148,663 Mustangs from the 2024-2026 model years whose engine-compartment wiring harness ground eyelet terminals can suffer fatigue fracture, suddenly killing drive power, headlights, windshield washers, AC, and the engine cooling fan. Two details make this more than a parts recall.

First, **the defect was discovered by a car that doesn't exist yet.** Ford's own recall chronology starts with a durability test failure on a 2027 Mustang: two broken ground eyelets on a next-model-year test mule. The future car debugged the past cars. Nothing about the S650 platform changed between the finding and the filing; the 2024-2026 fleet on the road already had the same terminals.

Second, **the failure mechanism is adhesive doing the opposite of its job.** Per Ford's filing (reported by Fox Business from the NHTSA documents), the engine wiring was wrapped in adhesive-lined heat-shrink tubing. Adhesive spilled over onto the metal ground eyelets, hardened, and prevented the eyelets from sitting flush against the engine surface. When the eyelets were bolted down over the hardened adhesive, they bent around it, creating stress points. Normal engine vibration then fatigued those stress points until fracture. The tubing existed to protect the wiring; its glue is what breaks the ground.

Third, **the remedy gap: owners got letters in the first week of September. The redesigned parts arrive in March 2027.** Six months of driving a known stalling defect with no fix. This is the "recall as IOU" pattern The Crash Report has covered before (#748, Ford 741k rollaway ten-month remedy gap) — now wearing a Mustang badge.

## Kill Test
**Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. Filed and in the news September 1-6, 2026 — fresher than anything in the current queue. Novel angles nobody else wrote: (1) the 2027-test-mule discovery — the recall was triggered by a car that isn't for sale; (2) the adhesive-that-breaks-the-ground irony, straight from Ford's own Part 573 mechanism description; (3) the 100:1 recall-to-defect ratio arithmetic (NHTSA's 1% estimate means ~1,487 of 148,663 are actually defective); (4) the March 2027 remedy gap framing. The companion recall 26S61 (piston domes, 10,001 vehicles) is already covered by #809 and is explicitly NOT this story.

## Novel Analysis (original contribution)
1. **The 2027-mule discovery.** Every news writeup treats the durability test as background. It deserves the lede: Ford's next model year caught a defect Ford's production line missed across three model years. The production dates (starting Sept. 7, 2022 — before the 2024 model year's sales launch) mean this design was in the car from day one of the S650 generation.
2. **The 100:1 arithmetic.** NHTSA's 1% estimate on 148,663 vehicles = ~1,487 actually-defective cars. Ford recalls 100 for every 1 that's broken — the scattergun math of modern recall populations, and the reason 147,000 owners are getting a letter for someone else's glue problem. The Kia EV9 story (#804) had the same structure (21,290 recalled, ~1,277 estimated defective, 17:1); this one is 100:1.
3. **The adhesive irony.** The protective tubing's glue is the stress concentrator. This is not supplier sabotage; it's a manufacturing-process interaction defect — two individually-sensible decisions (protective heat-shrink, torqued ground eyelets) combining into a fatigue failure. Same genre as #809's double failure (loose nut + blind tester), different factory, same lesson.
4. **The remedy gap as the real story.** September letters, March parts. The recalled Mustang fleet drives a winter season with no fix. Owners' practical recourse: watch for the MIL/chime/cluster warnings; a check-engine light on a 2024-2026 Mustang is now a possible precursor to losing power, headlights, and washers at once.
5. **The sibling-recall caveat.** 26S61 (piston domes, 10,001 vehicles, incl. 237 Mustangs) is the same-week, same-loss-of-power headline — do not conflate. The Mustang wiring recall's defect is electrical; the piston recall is mechanical. Different populations, different remedies (terminal replacement vs. engine long block).
