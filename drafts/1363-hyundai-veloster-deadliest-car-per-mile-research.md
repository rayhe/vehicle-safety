# Research: The Hyundai Veloster Is the Deadliest Car in America (Per Mile)
Article #1363 | Journalist: Vin Wreckage | Date: 2026-11-02

## Kill test
PASS. Surprising, counterintuitive, data-backed. Not another recall dump: a
FARS-derived ranking result nobody would guess. The fastest-looking stat on the
page turns out to belong to the slowest car on the list.

## Core finding (from fars_output.js, FARS 2014-2023, 337 models with 50+ deaths)
- Hyundai Veloster: **8.54 deaths per 100M VMT** - the highest rate in the entire
  dataset. 598 deaths over 2014-2023 (884 fatal crashes), estimated fleet ~87,500.
- Runner-up context: Chevy Tracker 7.83, Toyota Land Cruiser 6.27, Ford Mustang 6.02.
  The Mustang everyone fears ranks FOURTH; the Veloster beats it by 42%.
- Sports-car class average: 1.95. The Veloster dies at 4.4x the class average.
- Sedans average 1.02, SUVs 0.63. The Veloster is 8.4x the sedan average.
- Honda Accord (3.07) and Nissan Altima (2.88) - the "dangerous sedans" everyone
  cites - look like Volvo wagons next to this.

## The paradox (Vin's angle)
The Veloster is SLOW. Base engine: 147-hp 2.0L four-cylinder. The base model - the
volume seller - has less power than a Camry. Speed cannot be the explanation.
Toxicology (FARS_TOXICOLOGY): Veloster drivers 17.4% any-impairment, 10.2% alcohol,
9.4% drug-positive - thoroughly unremarkable. Not a drunk-driver story either.
Remaining suspects: demographics (young first-time sports-car buyers), driving
behavior the tox panel doesn't catch (speeding without impairment), small-car
physics (short wheelbase, low mass), and the N's 275-hp halo attracting aggressive
drivers into the base model's crash stats.

## Model facts
- Hyundai Veloster, compact three-door hatchback (one door driver side, two
  passenger side), debuted 2011; two generations 2012-2018 and 2019-2022.
- Discontinued after 2022; replaced in Hyundai's lineup by Elantra N and Kona N.
- Engines: 2.0L 147 hp (base, second gen), 1.6T 201 hp (Turbo), 2.0T 275 hp (N).

## Data caveats
- FARS per-model rates are estimates: VMT per model derived from NHTS annual-mile
  assumptions; small fleets amplify uncertainty. Veloster fleet ~87,500 is small
  but 598 deaths is a real count, not noise.
- Rate combines all trims: base 2.0, Turbo, and N are indistinguishable in FARS.
  The halo effect is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Actionable takeaway
Used Velosters are cheap now (discontinued 2022). If you're shopping one for a
teenager because "it's only 147 hp," read the rate again: 8.54. The car shape
matters more than the engine in this data. Check IIHS ratings; the Veloster was
never an IIHS Top Safety Pick.
