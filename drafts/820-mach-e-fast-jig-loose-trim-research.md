# Research: Ford Mach-E Quarter Window Trim Recall (26V487 / 26S56)
Slug: `mach-e-fast-jig-loose-trim` | Number: 820 | Journalist: Mia Crumplezone | Kicker: Investigation

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA Part 573 Safety Recall Report 26V487** (Ford 26S56), submitted Jul 28, 2026 — full defect description, chronology, remedy. https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2026/RCLRPT-26V487-9835.pdf
2. **NHTSA recalls database** — campaign 26V487, 2023-2025 Ford Mustang Mach-E, rear quarter window trim detachment. https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls
3. **NHTSA API record** — Ford campaign data; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from Sep 7, 2026 (per 573 report, Recall Schedule section).
4. Ford customer line 1-866-436-7332; owner letters mailed Sep 10-14, 2026.

## Verified facts
- 86,543 vehicles: 2023-2025 Ford Mustang Mach-E (production Feb 17, 2023 - Oct 8, 2024). Estimated 1% with defect.
- Defect: rear side quarter window glass trim may detach while driving, creating a road hazard for other road users. No FMVSS noncompliance cited (defect, not noncompliance).
- Root cause: supplier Saint-Gobain Mexico introduced a new assembly jig in Feb 2023 that **decreased installation time**; the trim primer no longer had time to cure, producing an insufficient bond.
- Corrective action Sep 28, 2024: a **30-second timer** on the jig blocking trim installation until cure time elapsed.
- 219 warranty reports of loose/detached trim, claims received Jul 31, 2023 - Apr 20, 2026 (US + territories). No accidents or injuries known.
- Jun 3, 2026: Ford gave a trim piece to NHTSA ODI; ODI judged the trim's **sharp edges** a hazard to other road users.
- Jul 21, 2026: Ford Field Review Committee approved field action. Dealer notification Sep 3-7, 2026; interim owner letters Sep 10-14, 2026.
- Warning signs: trim loose/not fully seated; wind noise, squeak, rattle.
- Remedy: dealer inspects and/or replaces trim, free.

## Novel contribution (original analysis)
The timeline is the story. The supplier fixed the root cause on **Sep 28, 2024** (timer on the jig). The field action was approved **Jul 21, 2026** — roughly 22 months after production was fixed, and 3 years after the first warranty claim (Jul 2023). Meanwhile warranty claims kept arriving through Apr 2026. This is the efficiency paradox: a jig built to save seconds on the line created an 86,543-vehicle recall, and the line was fixed nearly two years before the owners were told.
- 219 reports / 86,543 vehicles = 0.25% observed failure rate in warranty data vs Ford's 1% estimate.

## Kill test
Pass. Fresh recall (dealer notices this week), primary source is the actual 573 report, root cause is a genuinely interesting manufacturing-process story nobody else is writing, and the 22-month gap between line fix and field action is an original finding. Distinct from queued #752 (Mach-E every-model-year recall census) and expedition chrome trim story.

## Counterargument
86,543 vehicles is small; zero accidents/injuries; Ford found it via warranty claims and acted. The 22-month gap partly reflects investigation time (CCRG opened May 12, 2026; ODI review Jun 3, 2026). Fair to note.

## Limitations
- 1% defect estimate is Ford's; observed warranty rate is 0.25% (underreporting likely).
- No data on how many trims actually detached at speed vs. were caught loose.
- FARS has no "flying trim" fatality category; cannot quantify risk beyond the report.
