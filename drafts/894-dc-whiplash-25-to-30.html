The Gap

Washington DC Logged Its Safest Traffic Year in Over a Decade. Nine Months Later, Deaths Are Up 88%.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 12, 2026
☕ 3 min read

Twenty-five people died on DC roads in all of 2025. That was the fewest in thirteen years, and the National Safety Council ranked the District's 52 percent drop as the biggest traffic-safety improvement of any state in America.[1] As of September 11, the Metropolitan Police Department's own dashboard counts 30 traffic deaths in 2026, an 88 percent jump over the same stretch last year.[2] The capital blew past its entire record-low year with three and a half months to spare.

30
DC traffic deaths through September 11, 2026, against 25 in all of 2025.

The whiplash is national-record material: in its September 9 analysis of the first half of 2026, the National Safety Council found DC's traffic deaths up 133 percent, the largest increase of any jurisdiction in the country, more than five times the next-worst state.[3] Read that twice: the same city that posted America's biggest decrease in 2025 posted America's biggest increase six months later. Nobody else in the country flipped that hard.

Do the pace math and it gets uglier. Thirty deaths across roughly 254 days works out to an annualized rate near 43, within shouting distance of the 52-death years of 2023 and 2024 that everyone agreed were a crisis. DC is running about 3.6 deaths a month this year against 2.1 a month last year, which means whatever worked in 2025 stopped working.

You can see the other side of the statistics on I-66. On September 6, a Honda Pilot carrying Marlysha and Paul Liddell and their four-year-old daughter Leilani home from Sunday mass rear-ended a stopped commercial truck hauling oxygen tanks in the westbound express lanes near mile marker 64.[4] The Pilot caught fire. All three died at the scene.[5] Virginia State Police say a Toyota 4Runner had been forced out of its lane and struck the express-lane bollards moments earlier; both surviving drivers were charged with reckless driving after prosecutors were consulted. To be clear about the data: that crash happened in Fairfax County, Virginia, so it is not in DC's count. It is in DC's metro, on the road that family drove home from a DC church, on a Labor Day weekend when Virginia troopers wrote 2,800 speeding tickets and still worked 850 crashes.[6]

Now the strongest case against everything above: twenty-five was the fluke, not thirty. DC's own twenty-year table swings between 19 and 54 deaths a year; 2023 and 2024 both hit 52. Fourteen additional deaths in a jurisdiction this small move the percentage eighty-eight points, so part of what you are reading is arithmetic, not apocalypse. And nationally the trend is genuinely good: NHTSA counts sixteen straight quarterly declines, with the first quarter of 2026 posting the second-lowest fatality rate ever recorded.[7] DC is the outlier, which is exactly why it deserves the attention.

Limitations: MPD's counts are preliminary and subject to revision, and some Park Police-jurisdiction deaths are tallied separately. The NSC's 133 percent is an estimate built on a different methodology than NHTSA's; the two agencies did not even agree on 2025's national total (36,640 vs 37,810).[7][1] And none of these sources break down whether DC's increase is speeding, impairment, or pedestrians, so anyone selling you a single cause is guessing.

What you can do: Never stop in a live express lane. The I-66 chain started with two vehicles sitting dead in a travel lane and ended with a fire. If your car dies in one: hazards on immediately, coast toward the edge, get every person out and behind the barrier, then call for help. A stopped car in a 65-mph lane is a roadblock with a steering wheel.
