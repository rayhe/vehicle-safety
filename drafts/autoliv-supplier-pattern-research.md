# Research: Autoliv Supplier Pattern — NHTSA's Invisible Recall Blind Spot

## Thesis
Autoliv, the world's largest automotive safety supplier (44% market share, $10.4B revenue), has been at the center of seat belt recalls across multiple automakers — but NHTSA's automaker-centric recall system makes this pattern invisible. Each recall is filed under the automaker's name (Volvo, Stellantis), never Autoliv's. The supplier was also convicted of price-fixing safety components in 2011.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Autoliv Price-Fixing Conviction (2011)
- DOJ Antitrust Division: Autoliv pled guilty to two felony counts of price-fixing seat belts, airbags, and steering wheels
- Criminal fine: $14.5 million
- Part of broader DOJ auto parts investigation
- Source: DOJ press release, Automotive Fleet reporting
- Cartel involved "meetings in secret" to "allocate supply" and "fix, stabilize and maintain prices"

### 2. Stellantis/Chrysler Seat Belt Recall (Aug 2026) — NHTSA 26V510
- 48,777 vehicles: Dodge Hornet (40,386) + Alfa Romeo Tonale (8,391)
- MY 2023-2025 Hornet, MY 2023-2026 Tonale
- Defect: Rear outboard seat belts twist and fail to retract — occupants not properly restrained
- Supplier: **Autoliv ASP, Inc.** (southeast Michigan)
- Root cause: "design weakness" per Stellantis engineering investigation (began May 2026)
- 484 warranty claims, no injuries reported
- Remedy: Replace rear outboard seat belt retractors, notification Sept 24, 2026
- Source: NHTSA.gov, USA Today, ConsumerAffairs

### 3. Volvo 2.18M Recall (2020)
- 2.18 million vehicles globally (300K+ US)
- Models: S60, V60, XC60, V70, XC70, S80 — produced 2006-2019
- Defect: Steel cable in front seat belts could wear down, reducing restraining capability
- Supplier: Autoliv (both original and replacement parts)
- Largest recall in Volvo's 93-year history
- Fix: Replace steel wire with fixed bracket
- Source: Automotive News, multiple outlets

### 4. Volvo 19K+ Recall (2021)
- ~19,000+ vehicles
- Front seat belt detachment risk
- Supplier: Autoliv (confirmed by CarBuzz reporting)
- Source: CarBuzz, NHTSA

### 5. Autoliv Pretensioner Recall (2016)
- ~270,000 vehicles affected
- Micro gas generator in seatbelt pretensioners could detach and become a projectile
- Parts made April 10 – October 15, 2016
- Source: TorqueNews

### 6. Stellantis Cartel Lawsuit (Dismissed Feb 2025)
- Stellantis sued Autoliv + ZF/TRW for €771M ($807M) in UK Competition Appeal Tribunal
- Alleged cartel overcharges on seat belts, airbags, steering wheels
- Tribunal dismissed: "Stellantis has failed to show that any cartel activity directed against the Stellantis groups resulted in an overcharge"
- Irony: Stellantis now uses the same supplier it sued for overcharging — and that supplier's product just failed
- Source: Reuters via LSE, Financial News

### 7. Autoliv Corporate Data (2024 Annual Report)
- Revenue: $10.4 billion (2024)
- Seat belt products: $3.5 billion (34% of revenue)
- Market share: 44% overall automotive safety
- Employees: 65,000 worldwide
- Claims: "Our products saved approximately 37,000 lives" (2024)
- Source: Autoliv Annual Report

## Original Contribution
NHTSA's recall database is organized by manufacturer (automaker), not by component supplier. When Autoliv supplies defective seat belts to Volvo, the recall appears under "Volvo." When the same supplier's product fails at Stellantis, it appears under "Chrysler/FCA." There is no way to search NHTSA's database by supplier name to see the cross-manufacturer pattern.

This is the same blind spot that delayed the Takata airbag crisis response — it took YEARS to connect the dots across 19 automakers because each filed separate recalls.

Calculation: Autoliv has been the named supplier in seat belt recalls covering **2.5+ million vehicles** across at least three automakers since 2020. Total: 2.18M (Volvo 2020) + 19K (Volvo 2021) + 48.7K (Stellantis 2026) + 270K (2016 pretensioner) = ~2.52M.

## Limitations
- Different failure modes (cable wear, retractor twist, pretensioner projectile) may indicate different root causes at different facilities, not necessarily a single systemic QC problem
- The Volvo recall was 2020 — six years ago. Autoliv may have improved QC since then
- 2.5M vehicles is significant but not Takata-scale (67M+)
- Not all seat belt failures are created equal — none of these recalls involved reported deaths
- The price-fixing conviction was for overcharging, not for quality defects — correlation ≠ causation

## Strongest Counterargument
Autoliv claims its products save 37,000 lives per year. With 44% market share, they make an enormous volume of safety components. Some recall rate is inevitable for any supplier at that scale. Three different defects across six years isn't necessarily a pattern — it might just be the statistical expectation for a company that produces hundreds of millions of components annually. The price-fixing was about pricing, not product quality, and connecting the two is guilt by association.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — even if your car isn't from the automakers named, Autoliv supplies to every major manufacturer
- NHTSA should add supplier-level recall tracking to its public database
- If you own a 2006-2019 Volvo S60/V60/XC60/V70/XC70/S80 or a 2023-2025 Dodge Hornet or 2023-2026 Alfa Romeo Tonale, check whether your seat belt recall has been completed
