Cadillac Filed 7 Recalls for Its 2026 Cars. One Is a Missing Owner's Manual

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

I ran the census on every 2026 Cadillac. Fifty queries against NHTSA's recalls API, eleven canonical models from VPIC, every result deduped by campaign number and filtered to filings from calendar 2026. Seven campaigns touch a 2026-model-year Cadillac. Smallest covers ten vehicles, largest covers 67,380. That spread, 6,738 to 1 inside one brand's single model year, is the story.[1]

67,380
EVs in recall 26V717 whose brake computer can briefly lose braking at low speed, 63,469 of them Cadillacs

Start with the campaign that touches the most Cadillacs, because it is also the strangest. Recall 26V114 exists because a radio failed to download an owner's manual during production, which means nine of eleven 2026 Cadillac models technically violate Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 208, Occupant Protection, for want of a PDF. Every model except the CT4 and XT5 is on the list. Remedy is a dealer radio reset that triggers the download.[2] One campaign in seven is paperwork, and it is the most widespread campaign in the census.

Now the one that matters is recall 26V717, filed October 29, which covers 67,380 Lyriq, Vistiq, Celestiq, and Blazer EV vehicles whose brake system control module can misread wheel movement and briefly lose braking at low speed, but only on performance all-wheel-drive cars, and normal braking returns on the next press.[3] GM estimates 100 percent of the population carries the defect. Cadillac's share is 63,469 vehicles, 94 percent of the recall, including 22,980 from model year 2026 alone. This is the second Lyriq brake recall in two years: 24V589 covered 21,469 all-wheel-drive Lyriqs in August 2024 for an anti-lock system that released brake pressure unexpectedly, and the performance-AWD cars from that recall are folded into this one.[3] Fix is a software update, over the air or at a dealer, and owner letters go out December 14.

Cadillac's two brand-only defects are the ones no other GM brand shares, and both hit the electric flagships. Recall 26V394 covers 14,540 Vistiqs, effectively every Vistiq built to date, because the third-row power seatback may fail to reverse when it meets a person, and GM's own filing says a child could be trapped.[4] GM opened the investigation in March 2026 after Hyundai recalled the Palisade for the same failure mode, then halted Vistiq shipments on June 8 and told dealers to disable the fold feature until a redesigned seat module exists. Recall 26V612 covers 29,347 Optiqs whose key-fob express-up windows fail to stop and reverse in the final 13 millimeters of travel, a blind spot the supplier set by calibrating the anti-pinch range too low; GM tested five cars and four failed.[5] Both fixes are free. One of them does not exist yet.

At the other end of the spread sit the two recalls that make the census look silly and serious at the same time. Recall 26V289 covers 66 trucks and SUVs, including six 2026 Escalades, because a transfer case shipped without its oil pickup tube; GM told those owners not to drive at all.[6] Recall 26V494 covers ten US vehicles, five of them Escalade IQs, because a press machine was misadjusted and a battery module component was left unsecured; dealers replace the entire battery, a job worth roughly $38,000 per car at the Bolt EV benchmark of $190 per kilowatt-hour on a 200 kWh pack.[7] Sixty-six cars get a do-not-drive order. Ten cars get a new battery. Both recalls exist because a supplier skipped a step.

Two models carry a zero, and the zero is the most interesting number in the table. CT4 and XT5 are the only 2026 Cadillacs with no recall filed this year. XT5 is a volume seller, a midsize SUV that moves in numbers the Celestiq will never see, and it filed nothing. Zero is not a safety rating, and it is not a promise about next year, but it is a fact about this year, and it sits in the same table as a brake computer that quits.

For scale, GM as a whole filed 15 campaigns touching a 2026 model year, and the brand splits double-count the shared ones: Chevrolet 13, GMC 11, Cadillac 7, a total of 31 brand-campaign pairs from 15 distinct GM campaigns plus Cadillac's two brand-only defects.[8] One campaign the census excludes, 26V082, covers only 2024 Lyriqs whose rearview camera screen goes gray; the method counts campaigns by the model year they touch, so a 2024-only recall is out of scope even though the car wears the same badge. That is the method's blind spot, and it is the same blind spot that made Acura's count come out as 1 while 470,215 Acuras were recalled in 2026.[8]

Fair objection: seven recalls sounds damning, but the count is a blunt instrument. One of the seven is a radio that forgot a PDF, and two more cover a combined 76 vehicles. Two campaigns that actually matter, the brakes and the seat trap, are a software update and a module swap with no reported injuries between them. That reading is not wrong, but it is also incomplete. Cadillac's two brand-only defects both hit the electric flagships, and the brake recall is the second Lyriq brake recall in two years, which is a pattern the count alone does not show.

What this census does not prove: no FARS cross-tab is meaningful here, because these are 2026 models with no fatal-crash history yet, and the campaign count says nothing about injury rates. Defect percentages are GM's estimates, not independently verified. Campaigns filed after November 14 are not counted, and the excluded sweep only reached back to model year 2023, so a 2026 filing touching only 2022-or-older Cadillacs would be missed, and none was found.

If you own a 2023-2027 Lyriq, 2026-2027 Vistiq, or 2025-2026 Celestiq with performance all-wheel drive, 26V717 is the one that matters: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, take the over-the-air update when it arrives, and know the braking loss is temporary but happens at low speed, exactly where pedestrians are. If you own a 2026-2027 Vistiq, do not let a child sit in the third row while anyone operates the fold switch, and ask your dealer to disable the feature until the redesigned module arrives. If you own a 2025-2027 Optiq, skip the key-fob express-up with anyone's hand near the window. If you own a 2026 Escalade with 24-inch wheels and a dealer-installed accessory brake package, the fix is eight bolts. And if you are shopping, the XT5 and CT4 filed zero recalls this year, which is a fact, not a rating, so check the VIN anyway.
