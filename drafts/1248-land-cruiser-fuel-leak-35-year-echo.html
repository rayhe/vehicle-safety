Toyota Recalled 105,794 Land Cruisers for Leaking Fuel, Just Like It Did in 1991

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
December 12, 2027

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: the vehicle most famous for never breaking is being recalled because a fuel tube near the gas tank can fall apart, letting gasoline leak until the hybrid engine finally shuts itself off. Toyota announced the campaign on October 14 and NHTSA published it on October 21 under campaign 26V673000, covering 105,794 Land Cruiser Hybrids from model years 2024 through 2027.[1]

105,794
Land Cruiser Hybrids recalled for a fuel-tube fire risk, on top of 38,000+ in 1991. Same badge, same defect, 35 years apart.

The failure is embarrassingly primitive: a small retaining clip at the joint where two fuel tubes meet, close to the fuel tank, can lift out of place or go missing. When it does, the connection separates and fuel escapes. Toyota's own investigation found lifted or missing clips in the field but could not determine why the clips move. It recalled the trucks anyway, because a fuel line that can pull apart is dangerous whatever the cause.[3] The toll so far: 13 fuel-leakage reports, one confirmed fire, 23 field reports and 64 warranty claims. The fault lives in a connection you cannot inspect from the driver seat, and there is no driver warning at all, which is exactly why the fix is dealer work.[2][5]

Now the part that should unsettle you. Around 1991, Toyota recalled more than 38,000 Land Cruisers to repair leaking fuel tanks. Same badge. Same failure mode, fuel where it should not be, next to ignition sources that do not care about your overlanding plans. Two campaigns, thirty-five years apart, roughly 144,000 Land Cruisers recalled for fuel leaks.[4] The remedy this time is a supplemental clamp, plus a replacement resin fuel tube for the ones that fail inspection, which means the indestructible legend just needed a hose clamp.

Grant Toyota this, because the strongest case against doom is worth stating at full volume: this is the system working. One fire, thirteen leaks, and the automaker recalled every model year of the current generation before anyone died. A reputation for reliability is not built on never having defects. It is built on bolting on the clamp. Free of charge, by the way, at any dealer, with owner notification letters going out by mid-December.

That December timeline is the only part you should not trust, so do not wait for the letter. If your Land Cruiser Hybrid was built between November 29, 2023 and October 1, 2026, it is on the list, and your move is a VIN lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls today.[5]
