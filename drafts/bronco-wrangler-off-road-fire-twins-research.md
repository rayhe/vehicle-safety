# Research Notes: America's Off-Road Fire Problem

## Angle
Both of America's competing body-on-frame off-road SUV icons — the Jeep Wrangler and Ford Bronco — are under fire recalls at the same time. Combined: ~1.65 million vehicles recalled for fire risk in 2026. Different failure modes, same outcome: your off-road adventure truck might spontaneously combust.

## Sources

### 1. Stellantis Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator Fire Recall (June 9, 2026)
- **NHTSA recall**: 1,076,999 vehicles in US (1.3M globally)
- **Vehicles**: 2021-2025 Jeep Wrangler (787,887) and Gladiator (289,112)
- **Defect**: Electric hydraulic power steering pump wiring may overheat and cause fire — even when ignition is OFF
- **Reports**: At least 72 fires, 1 injury, no fatalities
- **"Park outside"** advisory — do not park in garage or near structures
- NHTSA opened investigation Sept 2024; Stellantis investigated 2023-2024 and CLOSED due to "low rate of fires"
- Previous Wrangler PHEV hybrid fire recalls (at least 3 times). Stellantis discontinued PHEV line end of 2025
- Source: Reuters, USA Today, NHTSA filing

### 2. Ford Bronco Wiring Harness Fire Recall (July 27, 2026)
- **NHTSA recall**: 565,691 Ford Bronco SUVs
- **Defect**: Engine compartment wiring harness could become damaged from movement/abrasion between harness and surrounding engine parts, exposing wires, causing short circuit → fire
- **Reports**: 15 fires between Oct 2024 and June 2026 (7 Bronco Raptors, 8 base models)
- **No injuries**
- Ford also had separate "Do Not Drive" for 4,653 Bronco Sport/Maverick (suspension, June 2026)
- Ford leads industry with 60 recalls in 2026 (vs #2 Chrysler at 24, #3 GM at 19)
- Source: USA Today, NHTSA

### 3. FARS Data — Wrangler
- Deaths: 1,842 (2014-2023)
- Fatality rate: 0.84 per 100M VMT (below national average of ~1.10)
- Fleet: 1,750,000
- Crashes: 3,718
- Crash lethality: 49.5% (each FARS crash has ~50% chance of killing a Wrangler occupant)

### 4. Context: Off-Road SUV Design
- Body-on-frame construction (both)
- Higher center of gravity
- Removable tops/doors (Wrangler and Bronco)
- Off-road electrical routing exposed to elements
- Bronco reintroduced 2021 after 25-year hiatus; Wrangler continuous production since 1987
- Both compete for same buyer: adventure/lifestyle crowd

## Novel Contribution
1. First cross-analysis of simultaneous fire recalls on competing off-road platforms
2. Combined recall population quantification: ~1.65M vehicles
3. Design analysis: what body-on-frame off-road trucks share that makes wiring fires a category problem, not a brand problem
4. FARS-backed Wrangler death context alongside recall data

## Limitations
- Bronco too new for meaningful FARS data (production started 2021)
- Fire-from-recall vs. fire-from-crash are different mechanisms — recalls don't directly affect FARS fatality rates
- "72 fires in 1.08M vehicles" and "15 fires in 565K vehicles" are low absolute rates
- NHTSA fire investigation process is slow — actual defect rate may be higher than reported

## Strongest Counterargument
Both recall rates are low (0.007% for Wrangler, 0.003% for Bronco). Most vehicles will never experience the defect. This may be the recall system working as intended — catching low-probability defects before they scale. The counterargument is that combining them into a "category crisis" overstates the risk.
