Ford Tied the All-Time Recall Record on Monday. It Passed It on Wednesday.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 2, 2026

On Monday, Ford tied the all-time single-year recall record, and on Wednesday, it broke it. Ford's 78th recall of 2026 covers 41,748 Expeditions and Super Duty trucks whose headlight assemblies may contain a contaminated LED chip, which can kill the daytime running lights, parking lights, high beams, or low beams without further notice.[1] The record book has now been reprinted, and it needs a Ford asterisk, and the asterisk needs an asterisk.

78
Ford recalls in 2026 through September 30, one every 3.5 days. GM's 2014 mark of 77, the record Ford tied on Monday, now sits third.

Seventy-eight recalls in the 273 days from January 1 through September 30. Do the division and you get one recall every 3.50 days, weekends included, at a pace that would finish the year around 104.[2] Only two days separated milestone 77 (the Ranger backup camera, September 28) from milestone 78.[3] Some companies measure velocity in vehicles shipped; Ford measures it in filings.

Here is what the record book actually says now: first place, Ford, 153 recalls, 2025; second place, Ford, 78 and counting, 2026; third place, GM, 77, 2014.[2] When Ford tied the old mark on Monday, the honest headline was that it was tying a record it had already shattered a year earlier. Wednesday's filing merely demoted GM's decade-old entry to third and made the top three a Ford group photo with a GM guest.

The defect itself is almost elegant in its specificity. A contaminated LED chip, not a burned-out bulb, not a corroded wire. The affected vehicles are 2025-2027 Expeditions built between June 24, 2025 and July 2, 2026, plus 2026 Super Duty F-250, F-350, F-450, and F-550 trucks, and the failure puts them out of compliance with FMVSS 108, the federal lamp standard.[4] Ford says it knows of no accidents or injuries tied to the issue, and a dashboard indicator is supposed to warn when the low or high beams die.[4] This is the recall system at its best, catching a supplier contamination problem before it catches a driver.

Now the ledger, because the count story undersells the volume story. Add this recall's 41,748 vehicles to the year-to-date tally and 2026 reaches 14,090,586 vehicles recalled. That is 1,132,458 more than all of 2025, the record-setting year, with 92 days still on the calendar.[2] The per-recall mean sits at 180,648 vehicles, against 84,693 in 2025, a 113 percent increase in blast radius per filing: fewer fires than last year, but bigger explosions.

And the paradoxes keep their appointments. Ford remains the industry's recall leader, filing 71 campaigns by September 9 against Chrysler's 33, Toyota's 23, and GM's 22, a lead it has held for five straight years.[5] It also remains the brand that won J.D. Power's 2026 Initial Quality Study, its first mainstream crown since 2010.[6] Initial quality measures the first 90 days of ownership; recalls measure the lifetime of the machine. A company can genuinely lead both rankings, because building a good car and recalling a lot of cars are two different skills, and Ford is practicing both.

The average remedy lag still reads 198 days across a 22-recall subset, a figure Ford disputes.[7] Half a year from filing to fix is the number that converts "found it, fixing it free" into "driving it broken through winter." Nobody was hurt by contaminated LED chips, which is the good version of this story. The bad version is the one where the fix for your recall arrives slower than the next recall.

What to do: if you own a 2025-2027 Expedition or a 2026 Super Duty, do not wait for the October 26 letter.[1] Call your dealer and schedule the free headlight inspection now; a dashboard warning is the only signal you get. Everyone else: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls once a quarter, because a clean check in March can be a recall by September at this pace.[8] Before buying used, make the dealer confirm every open recall is completed, not merely identified, since a VIN lookup shows presence and not remedy status. If the dealer says the fix is not available yet, get it in writing.

Here is what this analysis does not prove: these tallies are this site's running count of Ford NHTSA filings in 2026, consistent with our September 28 accounting, cross-checked against media compilations taken at different snapshot dates, so treat exact counts as directional. One more caution: 14,090,586 divided by 78 is a mean over a skewed distribution, and a handful of mega-campaigns do the heavy lifting. And the strongest counterargument, stated at full strength: a recall means Ford found a problem and fixes it free, and the true quality sin is suppression, not disclosure. GM's ignition-switch era was a low-recall era because GM hid the defect, not because the cars were good.[9] The honest rejoinder is narrower. The defects are real, the remedy lag turns "fixed free" into six months of unrepaired driving, and mega-campaigns signal systemic failure modes, not corner cases, in a system working at a volume nobody designed it for.
