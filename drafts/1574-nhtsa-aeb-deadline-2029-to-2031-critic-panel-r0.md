## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10
Every factual claim traces to a cited source: NHTSA release Nov 24, 2026 [1], Reuters Nov 24, 2026 [2], FMVSS 127 final rule [3], IIHS effectiveness study [4], CarBaseline adoption tracker [5], NHTSA recalls [6]. ## 6. Research Rigor — 8.0/10
Original contribution is real: the delay math (360 x 2 = 720 lives, 24,000 x 2 = 48,000 injuries), the two-model-year fleet framing (~31 million vehicles carrying the weaker bar for a ~12-year road life), the adoption paradox (98% presence vs. performance), and the ten-year runway from the 2021 law to the proposed 2031 compliance date. Limitations are explicit and honest, including the steady-state caveat on the 720 figure and the proposal-not-final status. Strongest counterargument is stated at full strength: NHTSA's own testing says the 2029 bar was not achievable, phantom braking is a real risk, and the pending lawsuit could kill the rule entirely. Deduction, and it is a real one: "a return of roughly 16 to 1" divides NHTSA's lifetime savings estimate ($5.82-7.26 billion) by a single year of compliance cost ($354 million). That is not a return ratio. A valid ROI needs lifetime cost against lifetime savings, and the article does not have lifetime cost. The sentence overstates what the numbers support and has to go. Below the 8.5 bar; revision required.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10
Numbers are layered for skimmers and careful readers alike: pull stat 720, the 360/24,000 annual rates, the 62-mph and 90-mph performance bars, the 98%-to-1% adoption arc, the ~31-million-vehicle two-year fleet. The 720 figure is caveated in the article, the disclaimer, and the research file, which is the right redundancy for a number this quotable. Deduction: the 16-to-1 ratio is the one number in the piece that does not survive its own arithmetic, and it sits in the article's most argumentative paragraph, where a skeptical reader will check it first. Remove the ratio, keep the two figures side by side without dividing them. Passes, conditional on the round-1 fix.
