## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: 105,794 vehicles recalled for a fuel leak/fire risk, announced Oct 14, 2026 with the NHTSA public notice landing **today** (Oct 21, 2026). Fresh data, fresh filing. The angle is novel, not a summary: the defect is a *dual hazard* (stall + fire) from one connector, the investigation ran 27 months before the recall, and it strikes the nameplate whose entire identity is "nothing ever breaks."

## Primary sources (3+)

Fuel tube connection near fuel tank could become disconnected, fuel leaks "until the engine in the hybrid system shuts off." Fire risk in presence of ignition source. Remedy: dealers inspect connection, install supplemental clamp (or replace resin fuel tube + clamp) at no cost. ## Facts
- Every model year of the reintroduced US Land Cruiser (2024, 2025, 2026, 2027) is in the recall. The nameplate returned spring 2024; nothing before 2024 affected.
- The connector joins a resin fuel tube to a metal fuel tube near the tank; connectors can "lift and disconnect."
- Hybrid wrinkle: fuel keeps leaking until the engine in the hybrid system shuts off. So the same defect produces a stall AND a fire. A connector that walks off the job twice.
- Remedy is a clamp. A supplemental clamp on a $55k+ halo SUV.

## Original calculations (the novel contribution)
- **Model-year concentration:** 2025 = 41,955/105,794 = **39.7%** of the recall. Two in five recalled Land Cruisers are 2025s (peak production year).
- **Report-to-recall lag:** first field reports mid-July 2024 to recall Oct 14, 2026 = **~27 months**. 600+ vehicles inspected, vibration/pressure tested, before the call was made.
- **Signal density:** 23 FTR + 64 warranty = 87 US signals over ~27 months = roughly one report every 9 days across the whole investigation.
- **Total nameplate exposure:** every single Land Cruiser sold in the US since the model returned (spring 2024) is covered. Recall-to-fleet ratio ≈ 100% of the modern fleet.
- **Historic echo:** the 1991 fuel-tank-leak recall covered ~38,000 Land Cruisers. This one is ~2.8x that size (105,794/38,000 = 2.78).

## Limitations (dedicated section, not hedging)
- NHTSA Part 573 report not yet posted in the public database as of Oct 15 (checked by The Auto Wire); defect rate estimate, production dates, and causal root-cause detail not yet public.
- Injury/fire counts: Toyota declined to comment when asked Oct 21. Zero confirmed fires is not stated; unknown.
- "Resin fuel tube" supplier and whether the lift-off mechanism involves thermal cycling, vibration, or a tooling tolerance issue is undisclosed.

## Strongest counterargument
Toyota ran a genuinely long investigation: 27 months, 600+ vehicles physically inspected, vibration and pressure testing. That is the opposite of a rush-to-recall coverup; it reads as diligence. The recall-before-any-publicized-fire also suggests the system worked: the recall exists precisely because the evidence crossed a threshold, not because something burned. Also, a clamp is a legitimate engineering fix for a connector walk-off; it is not a Band-Aid. Simplicity of remedy is not evidence of defect severity.

## Actionable takeaways (required gate)
- If you own a 2024-2027 Land Cruiser Hybrid: check your VIN at Toyota.com/recall or nhtsa.gov/recalls NOW. Do not wait for the December letter; notifications go out by mid-December 2026, and the Part 573 report is still not posted.
- The fix is free and takes one dealer visit. There is no reason to wait.
