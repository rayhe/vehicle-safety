# Research: 1250 — Halloween's Real Kill Window Isn't 6 PM. It's 1 AM.

**Journalist:** Dale Impactor III (Toxicology Desk Chief; Sobriety Report kicker)
**Run:** 2026-10-23 05:30 PDT

## The angle

America's Halloween safety ritual is aimed at 6 PM: reflective tape on costumes, flashlights, slow down for trick-or-treaters. But the federal data splits Halloween's danger into two windows, and the drunk-driving body count peaks long after the candy bowls are empty:

- **NHTSA (Oct 19-20, 2026 campaign data):** 176 people died in Halloween-night crashes with drivers at BAC 0.08 or higher, 2019-2024, including 31 pedestrians. At BAC 0.01+, the count is 201.
- **Original calculation:** 176 deaths / 6 Halloween nights = **29.3 per Halloween night** (~12-hour window). 2024 national baseline: 11,904 alcohol-impaired deaths / 365 days / 2 = **~16.3 per 12 hours**. Halloween nights run **~80% above the average half-day** for drunk-driving deaths. (Caveats below.)
- **FLBR Motorsport FARS analysis (Oct 17, 2026):** in Rhode Island (#3 deadliest Halloween state, 2.68 deaths/million), the single deadliest hour was **1:00-1:59 AM** — the bar-close window, not the trick-or-treat window. Nationally NHTSA says fatal alcohol crashes run at 3x the rate at night vs. daytime.
- **JAMA Pediatrics (Staples et al., 1975-2016):** pedestrian fatality risk 43% higher on Halloween evenings; relative risk peaked ~6 PM; children 4-8 at 10x risk. The kid danger is real and early. The drunk-driving danger is real and late. Two separate curves, one night.

## Kill test

- Newsworthy? Yes: NHTSA's 176/31 figures are October 2026 campaign data, never used in the queue. #1096 (Halloween, kid pedestrians + candy-tampering myth) is a different thesis (myth-busting + child risk); this is the adult impaired-driving + hour-window story. Complementary, not duplicative.

- Angle: "The kill window isn't dusk, it's after midnight." Distinct from 1096's "kids on foot + phantom poisoners."

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)

The 80% elevation may be mostly a weekend effect, not a Halloween effect. Halloween nights in 2019-2024 fell on Thu/Wed/Sat/Sun/Mon/Tue — a mix — but weekend nights already run far above the daily average for alcohol fatalities, and the 2019-2024 average includes pandemic years when impaired-driving deaths were elevated nationally (2021-2022). The baseline used (2024's 11,904) is a post-peak year applied to a period that includes peak years, so the "80%" is an approximation, likely rough in the direction of overstatement against 2021-22. Also, the JAMA study's 43% finding is about pedestrians (all causes), and the researchers explicitly did NOT attribute the Halloween pedestrian elevation to alcohol. Conflating the two curves is the exact error this article must avoid: dusk kills kids on foot; after midnight kills everyone on the road. Keep them separate.

## Limitations (honest accounting)

- "Halloween night" is not a published NHTSA hour-window definition; the 12-hour assumption (Oct 31 evening through early Nov 1) is mine and must be labeled as such.
- 2024 baseline applied across 2019-2024: annual alcohol-impaired deaths ranged ~10,100 (2019) to ~13,500 (2021-22); using 2024's figure makes the comparison conservative for early years, aggressive for peak years. State the range.
- The 1-2 AM finding is Rhode Island-specific (FLBR), not national; I pair it with the national 3x night/day rate, but they are not the same statistic.
- FARS is final-file data; campaign figures are preliminary until the annual report file lands.
- BAC>=0.08 captures only tested/reported impairment; actual impaired-involvement is higher (testing gaps).

## Actionable insights (gate requirement)

- If you drive Halloween night: the danger is not avoiding kids at 7 PM, it is sharing the road with impaired drivers after midnight. Get off the road or be off it by midnight.
- Rideshare before the party, not after; the 1-2 AM surge-pricing window coincides with the kill window.
- Pedestrians after dark: be visible; drivers' failure to see you is the mechanism (Safe Kids CMO quote in the FLBR coverage).
- For parents: the 6 PM risk is real (JAMA), but do not let the dusk vigilance fade into late-night driving confidence.
