# Research: ISA Speed Limiter — Every EU Car Has One, America Hasn't Started

## Angle
Every new car sold in Europe since July 2024 comes with Intelligent Speed Assistance — technology that detects the speed limit and nudges drivers to comply. ETSC estimates it will cut road deaths 20%. NYC trialed it on 500 fleet vehicles: 64% reduction in speeding over 2.9M miles. London trial: 62% reduction. NTSB recommended the US adopt it. The US has no rulemaking, no mandate, no timeline. Speeding kills ~12,000 Americans a year — 29% of all traffic deaths. The fix exists. It's in production. America won't install it.

## Primary Sources

1. **NHTSA Speeding & Speed Management** — Speeding was contributing factor in 29% of US traffic fatalities in 2021 (12,330 deaths). Speeding-related fatalities increased 8% from 2020 to 2021. "Speeding-related fatalities have generally decreased over the last decade" but spiked during COVID.
   - Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/book/countermeasures-that-work/speeding-and-speed-management

2. **NHTSA ISA Page** — ETSC estimated ISA will cut road deaths by 20% in EU. US trials showed reductions in speeding among young drivers (Blomberg et al., 2015) and 22% reduction in speeding among Maryland drivers with prior citations (De Leonardis et al., 2014). But "speeding began rebounding within 2 weeks of alerts being discontinued."
   - Source: https://nhtsa.gov/book/countermeasures-that-work/speeding-and-speed-management/countermeasures/other-strategies-1

3. **ETSC: NYC Trial Confirms ISA Effectiveness** — 500 city fleet vehicles, 2.9M miles, 64% reduction in speeding vs control group. NYC expanding to 2,100 vehicles with $2.4M DOT grant. London trial: 62% reduction on 360 vehicles. EU mandated ISA in all new vehicles July 2024.
   - Source: https://etsc.eu/new-york-trial-confirms-isa-effectiveness/

4. **NHTSA FARS 2023 Final Data** — 40,901 total fatalities in 2023 (down 4.3% from 2022). Fatality rate 1.26/100M VMT. 2024 estimate: 39,345 (down 3.8%), rate 1.20/100M VMT.
   - Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/nhtsa-estimates-39345-traffic-fatalities-2024

5. **University of Leeds / HERE Technologies** — Prof. Oliver Carsten: "A 1mph change in the mean speed of traffic on a road is associated with a 5% change in the number of injury accidents." If all UK vehicles complied with speed limits: 29% reduction in injury accidents, 50% reduction in fatal crashes. Modeled ISA over 60 years: minimum 25,000 lives saved (conservative). Called it "the biggest intervention since compulsory wearing of seatbelts."
   - Source: https://www.here.com/learn/blog/intelligent-speed-assistance-benefits

6. **Streetsblog USA** — EU General Vehicle Safety Regulation made ISA mandatory. NTSB recommended US adopt ISA mandate. Automakers resist citing costs and consumer backlash. Drivers can override ISA alerts (passive system, not active limiter).
   - Source: https://usa.streetsblog.org/2024/07/01/three-ways-america-could-import-europes-new-speed-limiter-law

7. **NHTSA 2024 Early Estimates** — Speeding-related fatalities decreased 6% in H1 2024 vs H1 2023. Overall fatality rate dropped to 1.17/100M VMT in H1 2024.
   - Source: https://WWW.NHTSA.GOV/press-releases/nhtsa-releases-2024-early-estimates-decrease-traffic-fatalities

## Key Numbers
- 12,330 speeding-related deaths in US (2021) = 29% of all traffic deaths
- ~11,860 estimated for 2023 at 29% of 40,901
- EU ISA mandate: July 7, 2024 (all new vehicles)
- NYC trial: 64% reduction in speeding, 500 vehicles, 2.9M miles
- London trial: 62% reduction, 360 vehicles
- ETSC projection: 20% reduction in EU road deaths from ISA
- US rulemaking status: NONE. Zero. Not even a notice of proposed rulemaking.
- NTSB recommendation exists but NTSB has no regulatory authority
- 1 mph mean speed reduction = 5% fewer injury crashes (University of Leeds)
- If 20% of US speed deaths prevented: ~2,400 lives/year

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? YES. The technology is mandatory in Europe, proven in US trials, recommended by NTSB. The US has not even started a rulemaking. Every year of delay costs ~2,400 lives. No US outlet has framed this as a concrete gap between EU implementation and US inaction with the trial data.
