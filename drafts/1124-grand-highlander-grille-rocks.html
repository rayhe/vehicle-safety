Toyota Left the Grille Open. Highway Gravel Did the Rest.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 10, 2026

Let's talk about what happens when a rock hits a radiator at 70 miles an hour. Nothing, usually, because somebody put a screen in front of it. On August 20, federal regulators opened defect petition DP26006 into roughly 87,000 2024-2026 Toyota Grand Highlander Hybrids whose grilles have large open apertures and no protective screen between the bumper and the heat exchangers behind it.[1][2] The petition, filed July 20, alleges the failure chain you would expect a grille screen to prevent: rocks and debris fly through the openings during ordinary driving, punch into the cooling stack, coolant leaks out, and the vehicle suddenly loses propulsion.

14
complaints alleging the same rock-through-the-grille failure chain, enough for NHTSA to open a formal petition review

What sits in the line of fire is what matters here. The petition's exposure list covers the main radiator, the A/C condenser, and the hybrid inverter radiator, the last of which cools the power electronics that make a hybrid move.[2] Puncture the inverter radiator and the car does not just overheat. Once the hybrid system faults, a 5,000-pound SUV that was doing highway speed becomes a coasting brick in live traffic, with no warning to the vehicles behind it. That open grille is a styling statement. The missing screen was an engineering decision. Somebody saved a few dollars per vehicle and moved the cost to the driver on the freeway.

0
crashes, injuries, fires, or fatalities tied to these incidents so far, per NHTSA's review

Now the caveats, stated at full volume because this column insists on them. Fourteen complaints across 87,000 vehicles is a 0.016 percent report rate, which is barely above statistical noise. A defect petition is the earliest, weakest rung of NHTSA's process: the agency is only deciding whether the question deserves a technical review under 49 CFR Part 552, not whether a defect exists, and most petitions die at exactly this stage.[3] Rock damage has been classified as a road hazard since before radiators existed. If the petition is denied, that is the system working as designed.

If you drive a 2024-2026 Grand Highlander, do three things. After gravel roads or construction zones, look under the front bumper for coolant smell or wet spots; a green or pink puddle is your early warning. If a coolant-temperature warning appears, pull over and shut down instead of driving to the dealer. And if gravel ever punctures your heat exchangers, file the complaint at nhtsa.gov: ODI's docket only grows when drivers report. Aftermarket grille mesh screens exist as a mitigation; confirm with your dealer that one will not affect warranty or cooling airflow before you bolt anything in front of the radiators.
