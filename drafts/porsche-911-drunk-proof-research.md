# Research: The Porsche 911's Impairment Paradox

## Angle
The Porsche 911 has a 22.8% impairment rate in fatal crashes — essentially identical to the Dodge Challenger (22.5%) and Chevy Camaro (23.0%). But its death rate per 100M VMT is 0.69 — 5x lower than the Camaro (3.44) and 8.7x lower than the Ford Mustang (6.02). 

The original contribution: decomposing the 911's safety into CRASH AVOIDANCE vs CRASH SURVIVABILITY. The 911 has WORSE crash survivability than most peers (0.842 deaths per crash event vs 0.696 for Mustang) but dramatically better crash avoidance (far fewer fatal crashes per mile driven). Its engineering prevents the crash from happening, not from being lethal.

## Primary Sources

### FARS Data (NHTSA, 2014-2023)

**Porsche 911:**
- 48 deaths total, 4.8 per year
- 57 fatal crash involvements
- Fleet: ~87,500 registered
- VMT: ~700M estimated
- Rate: 0.69 per 100M VMT
- Deaths per crash: 0.842
- Impairment: 22.8% any, 19.3% alcohol, 9.4% drug (171 drivers tested)

**Ford Mustang (comparison):**
- 2,739 deaths total, 273.9 per year
- Fleet: ~568,750
- Rate: 6.02 per 100M VMT
- Deaths per crash: 0.696
- Impairment: 21.9% any, 16.4% alcohol, 9.6% drug (4,664 drivers)

**Chevrolet Camaro:**
- 1,204 deaths, rate=3.44
- Deaths per crash: 0.627
- Impairment: 23.0%, alcohol 17.1%, drug 10.2%

**Dodge Challenger:**
- 385 deaths, rate=1.0
- Deaths per crash: 0.522
- Impairment: 22.5%, alcohol 17.5%, drug 9.1%

**Chevrolet Corvette:**
- 320 deaths, rate=1.52
- Deaths per crash: 0.884 (highest in class)
- Impairment: 26.2% (highest in class!), alcohol 21.3%, drug 10.4%

### Key Finding
The 911 and Mustang have nearly identical impairment rates (22.8% vs 21.9%). The 911 has WORSE survivability per crash (0.842 vs 0.696). Yet the 911's death rate is 8.7x lower.

The ONLY explanation: the 911 avoids fatal crashes at a rate that dwarfs American muscle cars. Mid-engine layout (now rear-engine), AWD availability, balanced weight distribution, advanced stability control, and superior braking create a vehicle that compensates for impaired driving through engineering.

### Limitations
- Small sample: 48 deaths, 171 drivers in toxicology. Statistical noise possible.
- Fleet estimate uncertainty: ±15% for low-volume models like the 911.
- Selection bias: 911 drivers skew older and wealthier — may drive fewer night miles, different road conditions.
- FARS captures only fatal crashes — the 911 may have comparable non-fatal crash rates.
- The 0.842 deaths-per-crash ratio doesn't distinguish between occupant deaths and other-party deaths.

### Strongest Counterargument
The 911's lower death rate may have nothing to do with engineering and everything to do with demographics. 911 drivers are older (median 911 buyer: mid-50s), wealthier, and more likely to drive on well-maintained suburban roads. They may drive drunk on different roads, at different times, and with different behavioral patterns than Mustang drivers. This is a classic confounding variable: price as a proxy for driving environment.

Counter-counter: Even accepting demographic confounding, the 911 vs Corvette comparison partially controls for it (similar buyer demographics) and still shows the 911 at 0.69 vs Corvette at 1.52 — a 2.2x gap. And the Corvette's impairment rate is 26.2% vs the 911's 22.8%. So even within the same wealth bracket, engineering matters.
