# Research: The Seasonal Death Tax

## Core Data (2025)
- **2025 full year:** 36,640 fatalities (NHTSA estimate), rate 1.10/100M VMT
- **H1 2025:** 17,140 fatalities (NHTSA estimate), rate 1.06/100M VMT — lowest mid-year rate since 2014
- **H2 2025 (derived):** 36,640 - 17,140 = 19,500 fatalities
- **H2 2025 implied rate:** If VMT split roughly 50/50 (with H2 slightly higher due to summer driving), H2 rate ≈ 1.14/100M VMT
- **15 consecutive quarterly declines** through Q4 2025

## The Novel Calculation: "Seasonal Death Tax"
If H2 2025 had maintained H1's 1.06 rate:
- H2 deaths would have been ~17,140 (same as H1, roughly)
- Full-year total: ~34,280 instead of 36,640
- **Seasonal death tax: ~2,360 lives per year**

This isn't a statistical curiosity. 2,360 is more than the entire annual death toll of several states.

## What Drives the H2 Spike
1. **Summer driving:** More recreational trips, more teen drivers on the road, longer days → more total VMT
2. **Alcohol:** July 4th, Labor Day, holiday weekends — NHTSA's own data shows alcohol-involved crash spikes in summer months
3. **Motorcycles:** Motorcycle season peaks June-September. Motorcycle fatalities have separate seasonal surge.
4. **Speed:** Warmer weather correlates with higher average speeds on rural roads
5. **Tourism:** Unfamiliar drivers on unfamiliar roads

## Historical Pattern (supporting data)
- 2024 final: 39,254 deaths, rate 1.19
- H1 2025: 8.2% decline from H1 2024 (18,680 → 17,140) — biggest H1 drop since 2008
- But full-year 2025 was only 6.7% below 2024 — meaning H2 slowed the improvement

## State-Level Data (H1 2025)
**Biggest decreases:** Connecticut (-32.5%), Delaware (-26.6%), Mississippi (-25.7%)
**Biggest increases:** Hawaii (+47.8%), Kansas (+21.2%), Idaho (+18.6%)

## Counterargument
H1/H2 splits are partly VMT-driven — Americans drive MORE in summer, so some of the H2 death increase is just exposure. The rate accounts for this, but VMT isn't perfectly split 50/50 — H2 typically has 2-3% more VMT. Even adjusting for this, the H2 rate premium persists.

Also: the H1 2025 rate of 1.06 might be artificially low due to weather (harsh winter reducing recreational driving in January-March). The "true" sustainable rate might be higher than 1.06.

## Limitations
- NHTSA H1/H2 estimates are preliminary and subject to revision
- State-level data may shift as final counts come in
- The "seasonal death tax" calculation assumes VMT is roughly evenly split, which slightly overstates H2 excess deaths
- Behavioral factors (alcohol, speed) are inferred from NHTSA subcategory data, not specifically verified against the H1/H2 split in 2025
