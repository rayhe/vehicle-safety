**Working title:** America's Two Best-Selling SUVs Flunked the Back-Seat Test. The Cheapest Car in the Batch Fixed Its Belts and Passed.

## Kill test
PASS. Novel angle: the two best-selling SUVs in America (RAV4 #1, CR-V #2, 883,056 combined 2025 sales) both failed to earn a 2026 IIHS award, and the failure is entirely in the back seat, where children ride. The CR-V scored Poor across its entire current generation (2023-2027). The cheapest vehicle in the same test batch, the Nissan Kicks, fixed the same failure in four months with revised rear belts and passed. Audi and Chevy tried fixes too and still failed. Nobody on the site has run this batch as a roundup.

## Verified facts
- IIHS Oct 22, 2026 batch, six vehicles, updated moderate overlap front test (40 mph, 40% of vehicle width into a deformable barrier; driver dummy = 50th-percentile adult male; rear dummy = 5th-percentile adult female, about the size of a 12-year-old child, belted behind the driver):
  - 2026-27 Nissan Kicks (built after June 2026): Good, Top Safety Pick. Built before June 2026: Poor.
  - 2027 Volkswagen Jetta: Good.
  - 2026 Toyota RAV4 (hybrid): Acceptable.
  - 2026 Toyota RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid: Marginal.
  - 2026-27 Audi Q3: Poor.
  - 2026 Chevrolet Trailblazer: Poor.
- 2026 award bar: Good in small overlap, updated moderate overlap, and side tests, plus Acceptable-or-Good headlights on all trims and Acceptable-or-Good pedestrian front crash prevention (standard). RAV4 and CR-V miss the award on the moderate overlap test alone.
- CR-V detail (test CEF2343, 2024 CR-V LX): structure G, driver measures all G, rear head/neck G, rear chest M, rear restraints and dummy kinematics P. Shoulder belt tension 4.3 kN. Rating applies to every 2023-27 CR-V.
- Failure mode across the batch: shoulder belt rides up toward the rear dummy's neck; lap belt slips off the pelvis onto the soft abdomen (submarining risk). Q3: moderate risk of rear chest injury; rear lap belt moved from pelvis to abdomen. RAV4 PHEV: rear chest Acceptable, restraints/kinematics Poor.
- Fixes attempted: Nissan revised rear seat belts and load limiters (builds after June 2026) — worked, Poor to Good. Audi reworked rear seat belts for builds after May 2026 — still Poor. Chevy upgraded rear belt load limiters for builds after Feb 2026 — still Poor. (Per IIHS release + OctaneDaily/Jalopnik coverage of the same batch.)
- Sales (full-year 2025, KBB/Automotive News): RAV4 479,288 (up 0.9% vs 475,193 in 2024); CR-V 403,768 (up 0.2% vs 402,791). Combined 883,056. Only the Ford F-Series (828,832) and Silverado outsold the RAV4.
- Test history: IIHS added the rear dummy to the moderate overlap test in 2022 and tightened the scoring criteria in February 2024. Rear belts and airbags had lagged front-seat protection for years.
- 12 small SUVs earned 2026 IIHS awards (9 Top Safety Pick+, 3 Top Safety Pick); neither the RAV4 nor the CR-V is among them. The 2026 Forester earned Good in the same test and Top Safety Pick+ (Subaru revised rear seat belts for 2026).

## Original contribution
- The arithmetic nobody ran: 883,056 Americans bought a RAV4 or CR-V in 2025 alone, and neither nameplate's current model can claim a 2026 IIHS award, because of one test, one seat. The CR-V's Poor is not a one-year blemish: it covers the whole 2023-27 generation, so every current-gen CR-V on the road carries it.
- The fix asymmetry: the cheapest vehicle in the batch fixed the failure in one running change; the premium brand (Audi) reworked the same hardware and still failed. The variable is not money, it is whether the belt geometry was actually solved.

## Actionable insights
- Shopping a RAV4 or CR-V: the front seat is fine (all Good). The risk concentrates in the second row, exactly where kids sit. If your kids ride back there, weigh it.
- Shopping a Kicks: check the door-jamb build date. Only builds after June 2026 carry the Good rating and the award.
- Alternatives that passed the same test in the same segment: 2026 Subaru Forester (Good, Top Safety Pick+), 2027 Hyundai Kona, Mazda CX-30, Honda HR-V (all Top Safety Pick+).
- Verify any model at iihs.org/ratings before signing, and check the build-date applicability note, not just the model year.

## Strongest counterargument
The front seat is where the driver sits, and both SUVs score Good in every other crash test, including small overlap and side. A Poor in one IIHS evaluation is not a federal failure: both cars meet every FMVSS standard, and there is no recall and no defect finding. IIHS keeps moving its own bar (new rear dummy in 2022, tighter criteria in 2024), so part of what looks like a failure is a goalpost that moved after these cars were designed. The RAV4, for its part, scored Acceptable, not Poor. Fair. But the counterargument has a limit: the rear dummy is the size of a 12-year-old, the belt failure mode is submarining, and the Kicks proves the fix is a running change, not a redesign.

## Limitations
- One crash scenario (40 mph moderate overlap) is not real-world fatality data; FARS death rates for 2026 models do not exist yet.
- Ratings apply to specific build dates and trims; a 2025 RAV4 is a different car from the redesigned 2026.
- IIHS award criteria are the Institute's own bar, stricter than federal standards; missing an award is not the same as being unsafe in an absolute sense.
- No injury data from real crashes is cited here because none exists yet for these model years.
