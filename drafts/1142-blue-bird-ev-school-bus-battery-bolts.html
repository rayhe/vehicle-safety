Blue Bird Recalled 1,590 Electric School Buses. The Bolts Holding the Battery Might Be Missing.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 12, 2026

Before your kid's next field trip, you might want to see this. Blue Bird just told federal regulators that the bolts holding the high-voltage battery in 1,590 of its electric school buses may be loose or missing, which is a clinical way of saying the most dangerous part of the bus might not be attached to the bus.[1]

1,590
Electric school buses recalled because the battery bolts might be gone

The recall covers Blue Bird Vision and All American electrics from model years 2018 through 2024, plus 2026. NHTSA logged it as campaign 26V641000; Blue Bird calls it R26AK. A shifting battery pack can cut drive power without warning, which on a vehicle full of children is the kind of failure mode that ends careers. Dealers will inspect every mounting bolt, reinstall with thread locker, and replace whatever is damaged or gone, free of charge. Owner letters go out around December 1, which means the buses keep running their routes for another seven weeks first.[1]

This is the third time in nineteen months that Blue Bird has recalled buses because something was not fastened properly. In January, 2,356 Vision and All American buses were recalled for a loose 12-volt cable that could kill brake assist, steering assist, and drive power in one shot.[3] In March of last year, Transport Canada flagged Blue Bird electrics whose hardware inside the high-voltage battery could loosen and detach, risking a short circuit or an overheating pack.[2] Now it is the bolts holding the entire battery. Three recalls, three different components, one factory that keeps shipping loose hardware.

Canada saw the warning first: its March 2025 recall covered loosening hardware inside the battery, and nineteen months later the United States gets a recall for the bolts outside it. Related defects, not the same one, but the failure family is identical. Also unexplained is the gap: model years run 2018 through 2024, then skip straight to 2026, and the federal paperwork does not say what changed in 2025. And the official remedy deserves a slow read: reinstall the bolts with thread locker. The federal fix for a battery that might not be bolted down is bolt glue. It is a legitimate fix, and also a sentence that should never have needed writing.[1][2]

If you sit on a school board, run a transportation department, or just put a kid on a bus every morning, here is the drill. Find out whether your district's electrics are Blue Bird Vision or All American models from 2018 to 2024 or 2026. Run the VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls under campaign 26V641000, Blue Bird R26AK.[4] Do not wait for the December letter; call the servicing dealer now and ask, specifically, whether the high-voltage battery mounting bolts have been inspected. “Has the recall been done” is a yes-or-no question dealers love; “were the battery bolts inspected and thread-locked” is harder to bluff.

What this does not prove: the federal paperwork cites no crashes, injuries, or fires for this defect, which is absence of citation, not proof of a clean record. Fifteen hundred ninety buses is a rounding error against the half-million school buses on American roads. Blue Bird reported this itself and is fixing it free, which is the system working as designed. A fair defender would note these are three separate components, not one repeated mistake; the “loose hardware pattern” is my frame, and frames can overreach. But nineteen months after Canada flagged loosening battery hardware, American districts are learning the bolts outside the pack needed glue, and some patterns earn their frame.
