# Critic Panel — Draft #941: "66 GM Trucks Are Missing the Tube That Keeps Their Wheels Turning. GM Says Park Them."
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone | **Date:** 2026-09-19 | **Round:** 1

## General Critic — 9.0/10
Structure follows the established template exactly: kicker, headline, byline, dateline, lede with bolded key stat, pull stat, four body sections, actionable checklist, references, limitations, counterargument, methodology disclaimer. The lede earns its keep: a transfer case as "a cast-iron promise" broken at birth is memorable without sacrificing accuracy. The service-parts-reaching-back-a-decade angle is genuinely novel and explained cleanly. Word count runs 572 against the 300-500 guideline, but every section is required content (limitations, counterargument, VIN guidance) and the prose is tight; the overage is substance, not slack. Headline risk: "Are Missing the Tube" implies all 66, while the report estimates 69.7% — but the limitations section discloses this explicitly, and the headline refers to the recall population, which is standard practice. The piece would be stronger with the 16-day report-to-decision timeline more prominent, but it is present.

## Voice Critic (Mia Crumplezone) — 9.2/10
Mia's register: technical but accessible, excited about mechanical detail, mildly judgmental about bad design. Hits land: "the straw that feeds lubricant to the transfer case bearings," "Read that twice: the error-proofing was the failure," "the line's own immune system got switched off during a changeover," "a stop-now sound, not a check-it-later sound." The judgment is aimed at the process failure, not the people, which is her better mode. Opens mid-thought with a mechanical hook rather than a news-wire lede. One deduction: "the nuclear option" and "sit with the scale" are slightly blogger-generic and could be any desk; the rest is unmistakably Mia.

## Ethics Critic — 9.5/10
No PII, no children, no vulnerable sources. No AI-disclosure language in bylines per standing rule. Defect characterization is careful: severity described as prospective (lockup at speed), not a realized toll; the zero-crash record is stated twice. The do-not-drive guidance is actionably safe: it directs readers to the federal VIN lookup rather than self-diagnosis. No sensationalism about a defect that has hurt nobody.

## Social Critic — 9.5/10
No mention of Hatch Overflow or internal community context. No social embeds required. Shareable headline with a concrete number and an imperative. No content that invites pile-ons against individual engineers; criticism targets the supplier process and GM's line-change management, both institutional.

## Legal Critic — 9.0/10
All GM/Magna claims trace to the NHTSA Part 573 report, quoted directly with attribution. "The error-proofing was the failure" is interpretive but anchored in the report's own quoted language about circumvented error-proofing protocols. No statements about liability, negligence standards, or legal fault. The "Supplier Magna" attribution is from the federal filing. No defamation risk: claims are either federal-record facts or clearly framed opinion. One caution: "the quiet scandal of this recall" is rhetorical, not legal — fine as opinion.

## Rigor Critic — 9.2/10
Every number verified against the primary NHTSA PDF: 66 vehicles, 69.7%, April 14 report, April 17 investigation, April 30 decision, May 7 filing, 68 suspect assemblies, 46 production, 1 service part, 54 dealers, 21 older vehicles, 4 complaints, zero known crashes/injuries, April 2 supplier correction, part numbers, Magna Powertrain de México. The 46-vs-45 chronology/population discrepancy was deliberately not decomposed in the article (correct call; inferring the drop-out would be fabrication). The limitations section is honest about the grinding-warning caveat, the service-record identification method, and the 69.7% estimate. The "factory floor found it before the org chart did" claim is a fair read of the dates (April 2 correction vs April 14 report). Deduction: the article does not flag that 68 suspect assemblies vs 66 recalled vehicles leaves 2 unaccounted — minor, and the report itself gives the final 66 as the operative population, so not pursued.

## Data Presentation Critic — 9.0/10
Pull stat "66" with a precise label including the recall number and the 69.7% estimate. Model years and the 2015-2020/2026 split are stated in the actionable section. The 69.7%-implies-~46-vehicles math is presented as "roughly a third of the recalled population is likely fine," which is the correct direction of the arithmetic (0.697 × 66 ≈ 46; 66 − 46 = 20, about a third). No charts needed for this story; the numbers are small and exact. All figures inline are sourced with reference links.

## Panel Decision
All seven critics ≥ 8.5. **PASS on round 1. No revision required.**
