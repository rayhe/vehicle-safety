# Research: OTA Recall Fix vs Parts Recall — The Two-Tier Safety Gap

## Angle
Software/OTA recalls fix themselves silently. Mechanical/parts recalls stall because components are imported (44% of OEM parts), tariffed (25%), delayed (9-day delivery), and expensive. The result: a two-tier recall system where your safety depends on whether your defect is in code or in metal. NHTSA's overall 50.3% recall completion rate hides this divergence.

## Self-Critique Gate
**Is this genuinely surprising after 767 articles?** Yes. We've covered unfixed recalls (5 stories) and tariffs (1 story about fleet age). But nobody has drawn the line between OTA-fixable vs parts-requiring recalls as a structural divergence in safety outcomes. This is an original cross-tabulation of two known datasets.

## Primary Sources

### 1. NHTSA 2025 Annual Recalls Report (published March 2026)
- 670 total recall campaigns: 447 mandated, 223 voluntary
- 28 million vehicles recalled
- Software/electronics: 119 campaigns, 8.19 million vehicles
- Powertrain: 87 campaigns, 7.99 million vehicles
- Source: NHTSA annual report, cited in multiple news outlets

### 2. NHTSA 2025 Recall Completion Rates Report
- Overall completion rate: 50.3% as of February 2026
- 14 million recalled vehicles still unrepaired
- "Parts shortages and supply-chain delays" cited as primary cause
- Source: NHTSA report, cited in Easy Lemon / lifestyle.pluralist analysis

### 3. Tariff Impact on Parts
- 44% of OEM repair parts imported (Cox Automotive / PartsTrader)
- 25% tariff on imported auto parts
- $22.4B in added aftermarket parts duties (S&P Global Mobility 2026)
- $30B total added cost to US auto industry (CBT News)
- OEM parts delivery: 9 days in Jan 2026, 8.7 in Feb (PartsTrader Spring 2026)
- Aftermarket: ~3 days (3.1 Jan, 2.9 Mar)
- 4.3% OEM parts inflation post-tariff implementation
- Source: PartsTrader Spring 2026 Collision Industry Trends Report, S&P Global Mobility

### 4. OTA Recall Characteristics
- Tesla pioneered mass OTA recalls — several covering millions of vehicles fixed overnight
- NHTSA has classified OTA software updates as "recalls" since 2021
- Tesla's 2M-vehicle Autopilot recall (Dec 2023) fixed via OTA update
- BMW, Mercedes, Ford, GM all now issuing OTA software recalls
- OTA fix = owner doesn't even need to visit dealer → near-100% completion for connected vehicles
- Contrast: mechanical recall requires scheduling, driving to dealer, parts availability, labor time

### 5. Completion Rate by Vehicle Age (from Stout / NHTSA)
- 0-3 years old: ~90% completion
- 5-10 years: 60%
- 11-15 years: 52%
- 16-20 years: 38%
- Older vehicles disproportionately need physical parts, less likely to be OTA-capable

## Novel Contribution
Cross-tabulating two known facts:
1. Software recalls can be fixed OTA with near-100% completion
2. Parts recalls stall at ~50% because of supply chain + tariffs

The structural insight: The industry is bifurcating into OTA-capable (newer, connected, software-fixable) and parts-dependent (older, disconnected, physically-fixable). Tariffs disproportionately affect the parts-dependent tier — the same vehicles that are older, less safe, and harder to reach.

## Strongest Counterargument
Not all software recalls are OTA. Some require a dealer visit for software flashing. And not all OTA-capable vehicles actually receive updates (connectivity issues, owner opt-outs). The 100% vs 50% gap is real but the boundary between tiers is fuzzy.

## Limitations
- NHTSA doesn't publicly disaggregate completion rates by OTA vs physical repair
- The 50.3% overall rate includes both tiers; we can't precisely split it
- Some "electronics" recalls require physical component replacement, not just software
- Tariff impact on recall parts specifically (vs all auto parts) isn't separately tracked
