Grand Design's ‘Auto-Detect’ Solar Charger Couldn't Detect the One Thing It Was For

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 3, 2026

Sixteen thousand one hundred twenty-eight fifth-wheel trailers are being recalled because of a component whose entire job description is one sentence long: detect whether the system is 12 volts or 24 volts. Every single one of them carries the defect, per NHTSA's 100 percent estimate — a Furrion solar charge controller whose firmware can select the wrong answer. The controller is supposed to pick the voltage. It picks 24 volts on a 12-volt trailer.

16,128
2024–2027 Grand Design Reflection fifth wheels recalled; NHTSA estimates 100% carry the mis-selecting firmware

Let's talk about what happens when you feed 24 volts into a 12-volt power center. In these Reflections, the 7-way bulkhead power center distributes 12-volt power to exactly two things you would really like to keep working on the highway: the trailer brake circuit and the exterior lighting circuit. Exceed the power center's voltage rating and you can damage both, which is how a rooftop solar panel becomes a brake failure and a dead taillight on a 15,000-pound trailer rolling down the interstate behind your truck. Thermal damage to the power center can happen with little or no warning, per the federal filing, which means the failure can cook silently in a compartment you never open and announce itself only when the brakes or lights go quiet.

Grand Design's own chronology reads like a slow-motion discovery. Thermal degradation at the power center was first reported in August 2025. A second report arrived in October 2025. Monitoring was put in place, which is the polite way of saying they watched and waited. A third report landed in March 2026 and opened the safety investigation, lab testing ran from April through August, and on August 7, 2026, testing identified the auto-detect 24-volt selection. Production kept running with the old firmware until September 16, 2026, the clean point, which means units built across three full model years left the factory carrying it, and the defect lived in the wild for thirteen months between first report and recall.

Now the defense, at full strength, because it has one. Three thermal reports in thirteen months across 16,128 trailers is a small observed failure rate, and the 100 percent figure is an estimate of which units carry the capable firmware, not which ones will fail. No fires, injuries, or accidents appear anywhere in the federal filing, and the fire-and-explosion language floating around some secondary writeups is not in NHTSA's consequence text, so it stays out of this article. The fix is free, owner-installable over a Bluetooth app, and permanently removes the 24-volt mode, which makes this one of the cheaper recalls in recent memory to actually complete. If the counterargument is that this is a firmware bug with a firmware fix and a thin incident record, the filing supports it.

But the remedy deserves its own paragraph, because it is quietly remarkable. Grand Design is not asking owners to visit a dealer. Owners install the firmware update themselves through the solar controller manufacturer's mobile app, then submit a screenshot of the updated firmware version along with their VIN, and Grand Design records that screenshot against the VIN for federal recall-completion reporting under 49 CFR 573.7. Your recall is complete when your phone proves it to the manufacturer. Before you tow, the filing instructs, verify the exterior lights and brakes function properly; if either has failed, do not tow the trailer, and contact an authorized dealer. An integrated tow module in the truck may or may not flag a fault, so the pre-tow check is manual, and it is yours.

So. If you own a 2024 to 2027 Reflection fifth wheel, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, install the firmware through the Furrion app, send the screenshot, and then walk around the trailer with the lights on plus a brake test before every tow until you are certain. If you tow anything with an auto-detect charge controller from any manufacturer, this is your reminder that auto-detect is a firmware opinion, not a law of physics: verify the voltage setting yourself after any update. And if you are shopping for a trailer with rooftop solar, ask which controller ships in it and whether its detection logic can be locked to 12 volts. Sunlight is free. The voltage still has to be right.
