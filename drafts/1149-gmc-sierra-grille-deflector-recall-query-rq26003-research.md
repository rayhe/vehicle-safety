# Research — #1149: GMC Sierra Grille Deflectors Flying Off, and the Recall That Was Supposed to Stop Them

## Angle
NHTSA opened Recall Query RQ26003 on Oct 9, 2026 into 2022-2024 GMC Sierra 1500 grille deflectors detaching at speed. 118 complaints; 21 of them are trucks that already received the 25V-060 recall repair. The recall population (70,768, 2022-only, chrome-grille-only) is 7.6x smaller than the investigation population (535,639). GM's first internal investigation (Feb-May 2024) was closed as "no defect trend," then reopened Dec 2024 after claim rates rose.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. Federal recall-query opened 3 days ago; flying debris cracking windshields of nearby vehicles at highway speed; nearly 1 in 5 complainants got the "fix" already. Novel to the site (Mack flying grille #1119 was commercial trucks; Toyota Grand Highlander grille #1124 was gravel ingress).
- Novel contribution: (1) the remedy-failure math 21/118 = 17.8% post-repair complaints; (2) the scope math 535,639 / 70,768 = 7.57x expansion; (3) GM's premature closure of the Feb 2024 probe (closed "no trend" May 23, 2024, reopened Dec 11, 2024 after "rise in claim rates").
- 3+ primary sources: NHTSA Part 573 report RCLRPT-25V060-8914.PDF (static.nhtsa.gov), NHTSA recall acknowledgement RCAK-25V060-5126.pdf, GM Authority Oct 2026 (chronology + RQ26003 details), TFL Truck / CarComplaints corroboration.

## Key facts (all sourced)
- RQ26003 opened Oct 9, 2026 by NHTSA ODI; covers ~535,639 GMC Sierra 1500, model years 2022-2024.
- 118 complaints of upper grille deflector separating while driving. Some report cracked windshields (on other vehicles); nearby drivers taking evasive action. No crashes/injuries/fatalities named in the investigation docs so far.
- 21 of 118 complaints involve trucks already repaired under 25V-060. That is 17.8% of complainants.
- Original recall 25V-060 (Feb 6, 2025; GM N242490120): 70,768 units, 2022 model year only, chrome grille deflectors only, built Nov 9, 2021-Sep 29, 2022. Estimated 3% defect rate (~2,100 trucks).
- Root cause per GM: one or more of eight attachment points fracture over time; if all eight break, the deflector detaches.
- Supplier: Minth Tennessee International (Lewisburg, TN).
- Chronology: Jan 19, 2024 GM employee SUFS report -> investigation opened Feb 27, 2024 -> closed May 23, 2024 as no defect trend -> reopened Dec 11, 2024 on rising claim rates -> 1,225 field complaints identified, 4 alleged accidents -> SFADA recalled Jan 30, 2025.
- Remedy: dealers "reinforce or repair all attachment points"; note: original filing said remedy "in development."
- GM told NHTSA vehicles built after the 2022 model year use parts outside the suspect supplier window; RQ26003 exists precisely because ODI disagrees or wants proof.
- The investigation asks two questions: did the original recall cover all affected vehicles, and does the prescribed repair actually hold.

## Novel contribution (original analysis)
1. Post-remedy failure rate: 21/118 = 17.8% of RQ complainants had the recall repair. The "reinforce attachment points" fix is failing at roughly 1 in 6 complainants.
2. Scope gap: 535,639 investigated vs 70,768 recalled = 7.57x. The chrome-only scoping is suspicious: attachment-point fracture is a structural issue, and RQ covers all grille types across 3 model years.
3. Premature-closure pattern: GM closed its Feb 2024 probe in May 2024 ("no defect trend"), then reopened 7 months later after complaints kept climbing — the trend it said wasn't there.

## Limitations
- Complaints are unverified customer reports; 118 complaints over a 535,639-vehicle population = 0.022% complaint rate. Could reflect noise/aging rather than systemic defect.
- Cannot distinguish from the data whether the 21 post-remedy complaints are fix-design failures or dealer mis-application of the reinforcement.
- No reported crashes, injuries, or fatalities in the RQ docs yet. Hazard is to following vehicles, so incident record may undercount.

## Strongest counterargument
RQ is not a recall; many ODI queries close with no action. 118 unverified complaints across 535,639 trucks is a thin incident record, zero injuries, and dealers may have botched the reinforcement on some of the 21 post-remedy trucks. This could be normal aging of plastic attachment points across 5+ model years, not a defect. Also: the deflector detaching while the truck is moving mostly endangers other drivers, which may explain why the incident record looks quiet even if the hazard is real.

## Actionables
- 2022-2024 Sierra 1500 owners: check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; 2023-2024 trucks are not recalled yet but are in the investigation population.
- Listen for a rattle from the front grille (only warning sign reported); a cracked or loose deflector needs dealer attention.
- If you drive behind a Sierra, keep following distance — the detached deflector becomes an unguided projectile.
- Recall remedy (when available): dealer reinforces all eight attachment points free under N242490120; GM must reimburse prior owner-paid repairs.
