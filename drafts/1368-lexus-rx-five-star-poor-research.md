# Research: #1368 — The 2026 Lexus RX Is a Five-Star Car With a Failing Grade
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate) | **Kicker:** The Gap
**Run:** 2026-11-02 14:30 PST | **Status:** research -> DRAFT

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The 2026 Lexus RX carries NHTSA's top 5-star overall rating, yet IIHS gives it a **Poor** on the updated moderate-overlap front test — the single test that measures what happens to back-seat passengers — so it appears on neither the Top Safety Pick nor TSP+ list while its cheaper sibling, the NX, does.

## Kill test
**Proceed.** Ratings-divergence stories are the highest-value consumer content this site runs: a buyer can reasonably believe "five stars" means uniformly safe, and the updated IIHS test exposes a real back-seat gap the federal test never measures. Not a data dump — it's an original synthesis with a concrete buyer's checklist.

## Primary sources (3+)
1. **IIHS vehicle ratings** — 2026 Lexus RX: Small overlap (driver/passenger): Good. Updated moderate overlap: **Poor**. Side 2.0: Good. (iihs.org/ratings — individual test ratings also mirrored on Edmunds 2026 Lexus RX page: Moderate Overlap Front Test – Updated = Poor, Original = Not Tested)
2. **NHTSA NCAP ratings** — 2026 RX 350 AWD (volume config): 5-star overall; 4-star frontal, 5-star side, 4-star rollover. FWD variant: 4-star overall. (nhtsa.gov; via MotorGPA compilation Nov 2026)
3. **IIHS updated moderate-overlap test methodology** — 2022 revision added a rear-seat dummy (small adult female/12-year-old child); scoring driven by rear-occupant chest injury measures, rear belt tensioner/limiter performance, and excursion/submarining. In 2024 IIHS tightened the chest-injury index (T12 deflection limit), which moved the RX from Marginal to Poor. (iihs.org — moderate overlap front test info)
4. **MotorGPA 2026 Lexus RX safety compilation** (Nov 2026) — confirms ratings apply to the full fifth-gen RX range 2023–2026; notes rear-dummy chest measurements as the specific failure; notes 2026 NX appears on the TSP list while RX appears on neither list.
5. **IIHS 2026 Top Safety Pick criteria** — since the 2024 criteria cycle, a Good updated-moderate-overlap rating is required for both TSP and TSP+; the RX's Poor is an automatic disqualification regardless of its Good small-overlap/side scores.

## Key facts / numbers
- NHTSA overall: 5 stars (RX 350 AWD); component: frontal 4★, side 5★, rollover 4★. FWD: 4★ overall.
- IIHS: Small overlap front driver-side Good; passenger-side Good; Side 2.0 Good; Updated moderate overlap **Poor**; headlights Acceptable (2026 RX per compilations); crash-prevention vehicle/pedestrian Good.
- The updated moderate-overlap test is the *only* mainstream test measuring rear-seat occupants; NHTSA NCAP's frontal test uses front-row dummies only.
- 2024 IIHS chest-index revision: RX dropped Marginal → Poor with no vehicle change — the bar moved, the car didn't.
- Fifth-gen RX ratings apply to 2023–2026 model years (same body-in-white).
- IIHS requires Good on the updated moderate overlap for ANY 2026 award; RX is on neither list. The 2026 Lexus NX IS on the list (per MotorGPA) — same brand, different result.
- Price context: ~$50k+ starting; a luxury-price SUV buyers reasonably assume is uniformly safe.

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
NHTSA's 5-star overall rating is earned in real crash configurations (full-width frontal, side, pole) and the RX is structurally sound — small-overlap Good and side Good prove it. The updated moderate-overlap test is unusually punitive: it faults rear seatbelts for submarining that legacy tests never penalized, and many luxury competitors also failed it when it launched. A 2026-model RX driver is still in one of the best crash structures money buys; the Poor describes one specific rear-passenger injury metric, not "this car is unsafe." Also, real-world FARS rear-seat injury data for the RX generation is too thin to confirm the dummy result translates to actual injuries.

## Limitations
- Ratings are lab proxies, not field outcomes; RX generation is too new for robust FARS rear-seat fatality splits.
- NHTSA NCAP ratings lag; the FWD 4-star vs AWD 5-star split shows federal ratings are drivetrain-sensitive in ways shoppers miss.
- Chest-index revision means pre-2024 Marginal and post-2024 Poor are not comparable across years for other vehicles either.

## Actionable takeaway
Before signing: check the IIHS page for the *updated* moderate-overlap rating (not just stars). If back-seat passengers matter — kids, carpools — a Poor there is the one rating NHTSA's 5 stars won't warn you about. The NX made the 2026 TSP list; the RX didn't.
