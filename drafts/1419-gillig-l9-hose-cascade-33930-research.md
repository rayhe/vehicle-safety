## Story in one line
The Cummins L9 fuel-hose cascade isn't over: Gillig just recalled 1,853 city buses and Autocar 1,339 garbage trucks for the same hose, bringing the known downstream vehicle total to 33,930 — and three of the thirteen buyers Cummins named still haven't filed anything.

- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. Fresh NHTSA filings (Oct 21-23, 2026) from the Nov 2-3 commercial-recall roundups. City transit buses and garbage trucks that can leak diesel and stall. Gillig is the largest transit-bus builder in the US; its Low Floor is the standard American city bus.

## Verified facts (3+ primary sources)
1. **Gillig 26V707 (NHTSA record, via vehiclesafetyrecalls.com / kcnebula, both sourced from the DOT dataset):** report received October 23, 2026. Gillig, LLC recalling certain 2021-2026 Low Floor transit buses. "The remote fuel filter hoses may separate from the fitting, causing a fuel leak and an engine stall." Consequence: "A fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source increases the risk of a fire. An engine stall increases the risk of a crash." Population: **1,853**. Remedy: Cummins will replace the fuel hoses, free of charge. Contacts: Cummins 1-800-286-6467, Gillig 1-800-735-1500. Owner-letter date was not stated in the NHTSA record summaries consulted — do not invent one.
2. **Autocar 26V699 (oemdtc.com NHTSA mirror; Fleet Maintenance Oct 27, 2026):** report received October 21, 2026. Autocar recalling certain 2022-2027 ACX (Xpeditor) vehicles with Cummins L9 diesel engines, part number D563027BX03. Same defect wording. Population: **1,339**. Remedy: Cummins replaces hoses free. Owner letters expected December 17, 2026. Cummins campaign C7311; Autocar ACX-2603. Contacts: Cummins 1-800-286-6467, Autocar 1-888-218-3611.
3. **The supplier recall (from #1046's research, NHTSA 26E066 / Cummins C7311):** September 28, 2026. 39,052 Cummins L9 engines, part D563027BX03, built Dec 7, 2020 – Apr 10, 2026. Defect: remote fuel filter hoses may separate from the fitting (insufficient crimping of the connector). Cummins estimates only **3%** of the engines have the defect. Fifth Cummins recall of the same hose: 21E063 (2021) → 23E055 (2023) → 24E038 (2024) → 25E004 (2025) → 26E066 (2026). The 2026 filing voids the 2023-2025 remedies.

5. **The buyers (Cummins Part 573 buyer list, via morningoverview.com reading of the filing):** 13 buyers named — Big Rig, Battle Motors, Grove Worldwide, TEMSA, Tiffin Motorhomes, Shyft Group, Sutphen Corporation, NOVA Bus, Autocar LLC, New Flyer Industries, Gillig Corporation, DTNA, PACCAR. Ten have filed vehicle recalls. Three — New Flyer Industries, Big Rig, Grove Worldwide — had no vehicle recall in NHTSA's weekly roundups through Nov 3, 2026 (and no coverage on this site beyond the #1046 buyer-list mention).

Remedy: tighten the fasteners free. Different defect, same manufacturer, same failure family: a fastener that wasn't tightened right.

## Original calculations (method stated in the article)
- **Downstream total: 33,930 vehicles.** 26,729 + 2,592 + 1,339 + 1,853 + 453 + 410 + 373 + 177 + 4 + 0 = 33,930. Each campaign counted once; Temsa's zero contributes nothing but is listed so the tally is auditable. Do NOT add the 39,052 engines to the 33,930 vehicles — different units (engines vs. vehicles); the vehicles are a subset of the engines' installed base, not an addition to it.
- **3% arithmetic:** 3% of 39,052 engines = ~1,172 engines Cummins itself estimates actually defective. The other ~37,880 engines get new hoses anyway. At the vehicle level, Paccar also estimated 3% (per Land Line, cited in #1337).
- **Gillig share:** 1,853 / 33,930 = 5.5% of the downstream total; Paccar alone is 78.8%. Gillig is the largest single passenger-vehicle installment (1,853 buses vs. Nova's 453).
- **Timeline:** supplier recall Sep 28 → first vehicle recall Oct 12 (Temsa, 0 units) → Paccar/Autocar Oct 21 → Gillig Oct 23 → Nova Oct 27. Five weeks from supplier filing to the tenth downstream filing, and counting.
- **The recall-of-recalls count:** this hose has now generated 5 Cummins equipment recalls + 10 vehicle recalls = 15 NHTSA campaigns for one hose design.

## Strongest counterargument
The system is working as designed, and the numbers prove it: Cummins found the defect, filed promptly, named every buyer, and every downstream manufacturer filed within five weeks with free remedies. Zero injuries, fires, or crashes are reported in any of these records — the risks are stated as potential outcomes. The 3% defect estimate means roughly 97% of the recalled vehicles were never in danger. Temsa filing a zero-unit recall is diligence, not absurdity. And Gillig's 2024 steering recall is a different defect on a different system; linking it to the hose implies a pattern the record doesn't support — it is, at most, a footnote about fastener discipline, and the article must say so.

## Limitations
- No crash, injury, or fire count tied to this defect appears in any campaign record consulted. That is absence of a published count, not a claim of zero incidents. Say so.
- The 3% estimate is Cummins' estimate, not a measured defect rate; the per-manufacturer defect share is not published for Gillig, Autocar, or the others.
- Gillig's owner-letter date was not found in the sources consulted; do not state one. Autocar's is Dec 17, 2026 (Fleet Maintenance / oemdtc).
- "Still unfiled" for New Flyer / Big Rig / Grove is as of the Nov 2-3 weekly roundups and this site's coverage; a filing could land any day. Frame with the as-of date.
- The 33,930 total counts recalled vehicles, not defective ones; at a 3% defect rate the actually-defective vehicle count would be roughly ~1,000. State both numbers, don't conflate them.
- Temsa's 0-unit recall: the NHTSA record lists 0 potentially affected; the reason (no affected units in the US, or a precautionary filing) is not stated in the sources consulted. Don't speculate beyond noting it.

## Actionable for readers
- Transit agencies and city fleet managers: run Gillig Low Floor VINs (2021-2026) at nhtsa.gov/recalls now — the hose fix is free and Cummins does the work. Same for Autocar ACX (Xpeditor) refuse trucks, 2022-2027.
- Riders: you can't check a bus VIN from the sidewalk, but a diesel smell or a bus that stalls and restarts is the tell — the defect's symptoms are a fuel smell, a leak, or low fuel pressure warnings. Report it to the transit agency, not just the driver.
- Anyone who owns one of the 13 buyers' vehicles with an L9: the Cummins equipment recall (26E066, letters Nov 20, 2026) is the umbrella — check the engine, not just the vehicle badge.
