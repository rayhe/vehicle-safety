## Key facts
- IIHS launched its commercial vehicle assessment program in June 2026. This Oct 8 round is the FIRST front crash prevention ratings for commercial vehicles, covering 11 vehicles: 7 cargo vans + 4 Class 3 pickups (GVWR 10,001–14,000 lbs). Test protocols identical to passenger-vehicle tests. Headlight + rear AEB ratings still to come (this year/next); Class 4–6 phased in later.
- **Rating distribution (original cross-tab from IIHS table + autoblog/freep):** 22 cells (11 vehicles × 2 tests). Good in only 3 cells: Sprinter 2500 (V2V), BrightDrop 400 (ped), Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 (ped). Zero of 4 pickups earns Good in either test. Poor V2V: 8 of 11. So 16 of 22 cells are Marginal or Poor — nearly three-quarters.
- **The disappearing child (novel mechanism):** several vehicles, including the acceptable-rated F-350 and Ram 3500, had a harder time detecting and avoiding the CHILD dummy in daylight than the ADULT at night. IIHS says it may be the child dummy's shorter height vs. the tall front ends: sensors sit near the rearview mirror, and the child dummy was detected at a distance but then "disappeared" as the vehicle got closer. The thing that makes these vehicles lethal to others (height, mass) blinds their own safety systems at close range.
- **Express/Savana (GM's full-size vans):** Poor in BOTH tests — because they ship with NO AEB at all in 2026. Forward collision warning is optional. A 2026 commercial van you can buy new with no automatic braking.
- **Death toll context:** 6,215 people killed in 2024 in crashes involving heavy/medium-duty vehicles or light vans — 16% of all US roadway fatalities (IIHS citing FARS 2024).
- **Motorcycle blind spot:** the motorcycle target was the biggest challenge; several systems didn't warn or slow at all in most motorcycle trials.
- **Regulatory gap:** many government standards for passenger vehicles (airbags, advanced restraints, AEB) don't apply to delivery vans, box trucks, tractor-trailers, larger work pickups (IIHS/Kidd). NHTSA's heavy-vehicle AEB proposal (June 2023 NPRM) cites 155 lives + 8,814 injuries + 19,000 crashes avoided per year — still not finalized.

## Limitations (to state in article)
- IIHS tested unladen (empty) vehicles; loaded behavior differs and braking physics at GVWR is harsher than any passenger-car test.
- Only forward crash prevention rated so far; no headlight, rear-AEB, or crashworthiness ratings yet for these classes.
- Ratings use passenger-vehicle test protocols — speeds/scenarios tuned for cars, not necessarily commercial duty cycles.
- We do not know whether the "disappearing" child-dummy finding generalizes beyond the tested models or was scenario-specific.

## Strongest counterargument (to state at full strength)
Physics is not malice. Stopping a 14,000-lb pickup from 43 mph in the same distance as a 3,500-lb sedan is a harder engineering problem, and IIHS graded them on the passenger-car rubric. The Express/Savana are legacy platforms on their way out. And the child-dummy scenario is an edge case in controlled conditions — real-world near-misses with children in delivery zones are rare events, not daily occurrences. The mandate conversation (#830) also shows AEB has its own failure modes.
