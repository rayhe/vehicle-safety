By The Numbers

Altec Recalled the Same Wrong Bumper Bolts Again. And 1,053 New Trucks Shipped With No Backup Camera.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They did not get better. Altec Industries filed two more recalls in NHTSA's October 27 commercial roundup, covering 1,098 vehicles between them.[1] One is a defect Altec already recalled last year. One is a safety device that was never installed in the first place.

8,021
Altec vehicles across four recalls in two NHTSA weekly roundups about two weeks apart: 6,780 + 143 + 45 + 1,053

Start with the repeat. Recall CSN-3375 covers 45 model-year 2023-2024 Digger Derrick trucks whose front winch bumper may be secured with incorrect bolts.[1] A bumper held by the wrong bolts can detach on the road, and the same bolts can fail suddenly while someone runs the winch. Altec will install the correct bolts, free of charge, with owner letters mailed December 4. Read that defect description twice, because you have read it before. In August 2025, Altec recalled 124 model-year 2024-2025 Aerial Device and Digger Derrick trucks for the identical problem, campaign 25V529, and its Part 573 report named the mismatch precisely: M12 bolts were used where 5/8-inch bolts belonged.[2] A production inspection on July 10, 2025 caught the error, work instructions were updated, and letters went out that October. Fourteen months later, the same defect description is back for a different slice of trucks.

Now the one that should bother fleet managers more. Recall CSN-3376 covers 1,053 model-year 2026 trucks across four product lines, Service Body, Aerial Device, Digger Derrick, and AC Series Crane, that may have been manufactured without a rearview camera installed.[1] Not a camera that fails. Not a camera image that freezes. No camera. Altec will install one or provide a camera kit as necessary, with letters also mailed December 4. A work truck reversing on a job site without a rear camera view is a visibility gap its driver probably never knew existed, since nothing on the dash announces a device that was never fitted.

Count the streak, because the streak is the story. Two weeks earlier, this site covered Altec's CSN-3374, a platform-access step that could separate after an improper weld, affecting 6,780 vehicles, plus CSN-3370, boom stow wiring, affecting 143.[4] Add the new pair and Altec has filed four recalls totaling 8,021 vehicles in about two weeks, with recall numbers running in sequence: 3370, 3374, 3375, 3376. Every one of these four is a factory-floor completion failure, a weld done wrong, wiring done wrong, bolts done wrong, a camera never fitted. None is a part that wore out. Wear is forgivable. Shipping a truck without its camera is a checklist that never got checked.

What you can actually do: if your fleet runs Altec equipment, check every VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now rather than waiting for the December 4 letters.[5] For the bumper recall, watch for a bumper that sags or shifts, which the 2025 filing flagged as the visible tell when bolt holes are not sized for the bolts fitted.[3] For the camera recall, walk the yard and look: a 2026 Altec truck with no camera at the rear is not a trim option, it is a recall. Altec customer service is at 877-462-5832 for both campaigns, and both remedies are free.

What this article did not prove: no filing explains why the wrong-bolt bumper defect reappears for 2023-2024 trucks after the 2025 recall covered 2024-2025 trucks. Whether the first sweep missed an earlier model-year slice or the same production error recurred is an open question, and this article does not answer it. No crashes, injuries, or fires are tied to CSN-3375 or CSN-3376 in the roundup, and none are claimed here. One more precision point: the camera recall is framed as a visibility gap, not as a federal violation, because the NHTSA notice alleges no FMVSS noncompliance and heavy work trucks fall outside the light-vehicle rear-visibility mandate. NHTSA campaign numbers for CSN-3375 and CSN-3376 were not stated in the roundup, so this article cites Altec's numbers only and invents no others.

Fair counter, stated at full strength: Altec found both problems itself, reported both, and is fixing both free of charge, with no reported injuries attached to either. Self-reporting is exactly what the recall system is supposed to incentivize, and punishing it would produce fewer recalls, not safer trucks. Against that, two facts from Altec's own paperwork refuse to sit down. One: a defect with a named bolt-size mismatch, a dated production inspection, and updated work instructions still showed up again fourteen months later. Two: a camera that was supposed to be on 1,053 trucks was on none of them, which means the miss survived every inspection between the assembly line and delivery. Self-reporting deserves credit. A fourth recall in two weeks deserves a question about what changed on the factory floor, and nobody at Altec has answered that one yet.
