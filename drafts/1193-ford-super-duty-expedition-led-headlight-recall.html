Ford Recalled 41,748 Nearly-New Trucks. The Headlights Died Because a Chip Factory Got Dirty.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 18, 2027

Before you check whether your 2026 Super Duty's headlights are still under warranty, check whether they're about to quit. Ford is recalling 41,748 Expeditions and Super Duty trucks because contaminated LED chips can kill the headlights while you drive, and these are not aging fleet beaters. The oldest trucks in the recall rolled off the line about fourteen months ago.[1]

417
Estimated recalled vehicles actually carrying the contaminated chips (1% of 41,748)

The failure is not a burned-out bulb, because there is no bulb. Modern headlight assemblies are sealed units built around semiconductor chips, and when one of those chips is contaminated at the factory, the only repair is replacing the entire assembly. Ford traced the contamination to the Tier 3 chip supplier's manufacturing facility, which is a polite way of saying the factory Ford doesn't buy from directly. The Tier 1 lighting supplier is Flex N Gate in Effingham, Illinois. The dirty chips came from somewhere further down the chain.[1]

Credit where it is due: Ford caught this one fast. Warranty claims about dead headlights on 2026 Super Dutys reached Ford's Critical Concern Review Group on May 21, 2026; by September, engineers had matched the failures to supplier shipping records and traceability data from the Kentucky Truck Plant; and on September 15 the Field Review Committee approved a recall; four months, claim to action. Six warranty claims and six field reports, six unique VINs, no accidents, no injuries. If you count only the official record, this is the safety system working exactly the way it is supposed to.[1]

Still, the warning gap should bother you. A dashboard indicator lights up when the beams fail, which is perfectly useful at noon. At midnight on a two-lane highway, a headlight warning is a decoration. Ford estimates 1% of the recalled population carries the contaminated chips, which works out to roughly 417 trucks and SUVs driving around with headlights that can die without warning. Owners cannot tell which group they are in until a dealer looks.[1]

The deeper problem is what this recall says about the modern headlight. A replaceable bulb cost eight dollars and five minutes in a parking lot. A sealed LED assembly costs hundreds of dollars, requires dealer service, and fails for reasons no mechanic can see, because the defect happened in a semiconductor fab months before the truck existed. Vehicle lighting has been computer-chipped into something the owner can neither inspect nor fix. When the recall warranty expires, the owner inherits the bill.

Owner notification letters go out October 26, 2026, and VINs become searchable on NHTSA's recall lookup the same day, with inspection and any assembly replacement free of charge. If you bought a 2025–2027 Expedition or a 2026 Super Duty new, run the VIN before the letter arrives.[2] If you are shopping these trucks used a few years from now, remember: a dimming LED headlight on a used Expedition is not a bulb swap. It's a sealed assembly, and out of warranty it comes out of your pocket.
