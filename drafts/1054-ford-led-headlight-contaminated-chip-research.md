# Research — #1054: Ford 26C43 LED headlight recall (contaminated chip)

**News peg:** Ford recall 26C43, filed with NHTSA late September 2026; reported by USA Today (Sept 30, 2026) and Fox Business / IndexBox (Oct 2, 2026).

## Facts

- **41,748 vehicles** potentially affected.
- Models: **2025–2027 Ford Expedition** (built June 24, 2025 – July 2, 2026), **2026 Ford F-250 / F-350 / F-450 / F-550 Super Duty**. F-150 not included.
- Defect: headlight assemblies **"may contain a contaminated LED chip"**, which can cause loss of daytime running lights, parking lights, high beams, or low beams.
- Noncompliance with **FMVSS 108** (Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment).
- Official warning tell: "A dashboard indicator light will illuminate for customers experiencing inoperative low beam and/or high beam headlamps."
- Remedy: dealers inspect and replace headlight assemblies if needed, free. Owner notification letters expected Oct 26, 2026. Ford customer line 1-866-436-7332.
- Ford is **not aware of any reports of accident or injury** related to this issue.

## Novel angle (kill test: PASS)

After 1,053 articles, "another recall" needs a reason to exist. Two here:

1. **The contamination angle.** This is not a design flaw — it's a component-level manufacturing contamination. One dirty LED chip batch can knock out the lights on a $90,000 truck. LEDs were sold as lifetime components (no bulb changes, ever). A microscopic impurity reintroduces headlight failure as a year-one event, and unlike a burnt bulb, there is no field fix: the whole sealed assembly gets swapped.
2. **The indicator paradox.** The most reliable lamp on the truck is the one on the dashboard. Ford's own safety report says the tell for dead headlights is a glowing icon *inside* the cab — your headlights fail, and the car congratulates itself for noticing.

## Original contribution

- **Batch-concentration analysis:** Expedition production spans June 24, 2025–July 2, 2026 (over a year of builds); the Super Duty population dates to August 4, 2026 (single-day production). Same contaminated component crossing two nameplates and two timelines = supplier-batch problem, not a line problem. (For the Super Duty, "produced on August 4th, 2026" per Ford Authority; treat as the identified affected build date.)
- **Failure-economics contrast:** sealed LED assemblies are not serviceable like bulbs; the recall remedy is full assembly replacement. One contaminated chip → entire headlight unit in the trash.

## Strongest counterargument

This is precautionary, not proven carnage: Ford reports **zero accidents or injuries**; the contamination is batch-level, so most of the 41,748 inspected vehicles may have clean chips and need nothing; the dashboard warning works as designed; FMVSS 108 is a noncompliance finding about equipment standards, not a field-failure count.
