Dodge Recalled the Durango's Spoiler Twice, It Kept Falling Off, and the Third Recall Has No Fix Yet

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 5, 2026

A roof spoiler is a strip of molded plastic that weighs a few pounds and does almost nothing, and on 74,578 Dodge Durangos it has now been recalled three times for coming off at speed and landing in traffic. Campaign 26V528, filed August 13, 2026, covers 2021-2023 Durangos whose spoilers sit too high against the roof, scrape every time the liftgate cycles, and eventually detach.[1] Cars already repaired under the first two recalls have to come back for a third repair, and when Stellantis filed this one, the remedy was still under development.

218,434
Recall notices issued for one spoiler on one SUV line, across three campaigns

21V842 came first in October 2021, covering 4,837 Durangos, then 23V115 in February 2023, covering 139,019. Both were supposed to end it. In March 2026, Stellantis opened an investigation because field data showed spoilers detaching on cars already through the remedy, and by July 29 it had logged 161 records, 8 customer assistance cases, 116 warranty claims, and 37 other service reports, all filed after a fix existed.[2] That count is what forced recall number three. Twice was not enough. Add the campaigns together: 218,434 notices for a single part, over four years and nine months, the same part number on every one.

Understand what failed, because it was not the part. Stellantis says this is an assembly issue, not a defective component: some spoilers were installed over-flush, so the liftgate scraped them on every cycle until the mounting gave out. Same spoiler, same supplier, same installation, three times. FCA estimates under 1% actually carry the defect, roughly 597 vehicles, but the population was defined by build date, so about 74,000 owners get inspected to find them.[3] You can hear it coming: a scraping sound when the liftgate cycles, or a rattle at speed, means the spoiler is loosening. Do not keep driving it.

Fair objection, and it deserves its full weight. This is a cosmetic trim piece on a three-year-old SUV: zero known crashes or injuries, a defect rate under 1%, and a manufacturer that caught the pattern itself and recalled the cars voluntarily, twice, then a third time when the data said the fix had not held. That is the recall system working exactly as designed, and a spoiler is not an airbag, a brake, or a fuel tank, so three recalls for a part that has hurt nobody is evidence of diligence, not negligence. That argument collapses on one fact, which is the remedy. Two months after interim letters went out on September 10, no final fix has been published, and owners are driving with a known detachment risk and a letter that says, in effect, we know and we will write again. A recall without a repair is a notification system, not a safety system, and every mile between the letter and the fix is a mile with a loose part hanging over somebody else's windshield.

So check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for campaign 26V528, Stellantis number 61D, and do not assume an earlier spoiler repair means you are done, because cars fixed under 21V842 or 23V115 need the new repair too.[4] If your liftgate scrapes or your roof rattles, call the dealer now; Chrysler customer service is 1-800-853-1403. If you are behind a Durango and its roof spoiler is fluttering, drop back. It is a part that detaches, and it detaches at speed. And if your dealer says no fix is available yet, that matches the filing, so keep the interim notice and check the VIN lookup monthly until the remedy notice arrives.

Limitations: No crash or injury count is published for this defect; Stellantis reports none known as of July 29, 2026, and none is claimed here. The under-1% defect estimate is Stellantis's filed estimate, not a measured rate, and the roughly 597-vehicle figure is arithmetic on that estimate. The remedy description differs across sources: Stellantis's filing says under development, while the NHTSA summary describes inspection, replacement or repair, and liftgate realignment with a second owner notice to follow; remedy status is stated as of November 5, 2026, and readers should check the VIN lookup rather than assume a fix is available. This is a defect story, not a fatality-rate story; FARS cannot quantify the risk of a detached spoiler, and no fatality figure is offered. Trim-level exposure is not published, so no trim is singled out as more affected than another.
