# #1056 Research: Toyota Highlander/Kluger Second-Row Seatback Recliner Recall

## News peg
October 2, 2026 (this week): Toyota Australia EXPANDED its March 2026 Kluger seatback recall from 34,377 to 46,467 vehicles, extending the build range by 18 months (now Mar 31, 2021 to Jun 10, 2025). Five years after the supplier's design change, Toyota is still finding vehicles it affects. (CarExpert, TorqueCafe, DriveAgent, all Oct 2, 2026.)

## The defect
- Vehicles: 2021-2024 Toyota Highlander and Highlander Hybrid (US); Kluger 4x2/AWD/Hybrid GX, GXL, Grande (Australia).
- Mechanism: second-row seat recliner assemblies use a locking spring and a return spring. During a supplier design change in 2021, "a failure to consider the force balance between the locking spring and the return spring" (Australian recall notice, via CarExpert) combined with recliner return-spring orientation and outer clearance between the recliner guide and ratchet means the recliner teeth do not fully engage during seatback adjustment.
- Consequence: the seatback does not remain in a locked position. It looks and feels normal but may collapse forward in a crash, failing to restrain the occupant. Toyota's wording: "may not properly restrain occupants, increasing the risk of injury in the event of a crash at higher speeds."
- Discovery: October 2023, routine factory inspection at the plant that builds Highlanders: a seatback skipped its first locking position during adjustment. (AboutLawsuits, citing NHTSA safety report.)
- Field data: 12 of 343 second-row seats Toyota tested in its field survey failed to stay locked (3.5%). 10 field reports, 18 warranty claims. No crashes or injuries reported as of the March 2026 filing.

## Numbers
- US: NHTSA campaign 26V128000 (Toyota 26TB06 / 26TA06), announced March 5, 2026. 550,007 vehicles.
- Canada: ~40,000 additional (MotorBiscuit).
- Australia: 46,467 Klugers (post-Oct-2 expansion; +12,090 vs March).
- Global same-design-change census: 550,007 + 40,000 + 46,467 = 636,474 vehicles.
- Original calculation: 12/343 = 3.497% observed failure in Toyota's survey. Applied to the 550,007-vehicle US population: ~19,250 vehicles may have at least one second-row seatback that will not stay locked. Caveat: the surveyed 343 were a suspect-enriched sample, not a random fleet sample, so the true population rate is probably lower; treat 19k as an upper-bound estimate.

## Timeline
2021 (supplier design change) -> Oct 2023 (factory inspection catch) -> Feb 27, 2026 (Toyota decides on recall) -> Mar 5, 2026 (US announcement, letters by Apr-May 2026) -> Mar 2026 (Australia original recall, 34,377) -> Oct 2, 2026 (Australia expansion to 46,467, build range to Jun 2025).

## Kill test
Pass. A silent, invisible latch defect in 550k+ of America's best-selling three-row SUVs; the driver's check (adjust the seatback, it stays put) cannot detect it; and the campaign is STILL expanding five years after the supplier change. Novel vs queue: no Highlander/26V128000/recliner coverage in 800+ stories.

## Original contributions
1. The 3.5% -> ~19,250 extrapolation (flagged as upper bound).
2. The three-market census (636,474 vehicles from one supplier design change).
3. The invisibility framing: restraint chain fails at the latch, the one component the driver trusts without verification.

## Strongest counterargument
No crashes or injuries tied to the defect; it only matters in a crash; the survey sample was suspect-enriched so 3.5% likely overstates the true rate; the US remedy has been available free since spring 2026, so many of the 550,007 may already be fixed.

## Limitations
- Australian expansion data does not imply the US build range widens too; separate campaigns, separate production lines.
- No public data on US completion rates for 26V128000 as of this writing; unknown how many of the 550,007 are fixed.
- The 12/343 figure comes from Toyota's own investigation summary via secondary reporting (AboutLawsuits), not the raw NHTSA filing I could verify line-by-line.

## Actionable insight
2021-2024 Highlander/Highlander Hybrid owners: check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (campaign 26V128000); remedy is free return-spring replacement at any Toyota dealer. Physical test: after adjusting a second-row seatback, push it forward firmly; if it skips a lock position or feels soft, get it inspected. Used-Highlander buyers: verify recall completion by VIN before purchase.
