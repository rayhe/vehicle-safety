Ford's Godzilla V8 Was Sold as Unkillable. Now a Secret Idle Patch Is Under Federal Investigation.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

Ford sold the 7.3-liter Godzilla V8 as the answer to a question every fleet manager asks: what engine will simply not quit? Buyers picked it for ambulances, transit vans, delivery chassis, and medium-duty trucks because a big pushrod gas V8 has fewer ways to strand you than a diesel. On October 26, 2026, federal investigators opened a defect petition into that engine, and the allegation reads like a confession Ford never meant to file.

NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened petition DP26011 after a June 2026 filing, covering an estimated 54,787 vehicles: 2021 and 2022 E-350 vans, 2021 F-59 stripped chassis, and 2021 F-750 medium-duty trucks.[1] Petition claims low oil pressure at idle, about 6 psi, lets lifter rollers and camshaft lobes grind each other down until the engine fails outright, possibly at highway speed with a loaded commercial vehicle behind it.[2]

54,787
Vehicles under defect-petition review, not failed engines: 2021-2022 Ford E-350, 2021 F-59, 2021 F-750 with the 7.3-liter Godzilla V8

Fleet complaints give the petition its teeth. A North Carolina operator reported 5 of 11 trucks with the 7.3 failed, earliest at 45,745 miles and latest at 103,177 miles.[3] Montachusett Regional Transit Authority in Fitchburg, Massachusetts, reported 22 failures, some engines replaced twice, after a failure at 78,172 miles chewed up camshaft lobes and valve tappets.[4]

Most telling is what Ford allegedly did before any of this reached NHTSA. Petition claims Ford pushed a software update that raised idle speed to roughly 1,000 rpm, lifting oil pressure, without telling owners the patch existed or why it mattered.[2] Ford had already run customer satisfaction program 24B27 for low idle oil pressure on 2021-2022 models.[4] A customer satisfaction program is not a recall, and it carries no federal safety finding.

What you can actually do: if you run one of these vehicles, ask your dealer whether CSP 24B27 was applied and get a hot-idle oil-pressure reading at full operating temperature. Ticking or tapping at idle is the symptom owners report first. Do not wait for a highway-speed failure to take it seriously, and file an NHTSA complaint if a dealer dismisses valvetrain noise, because petition reviews run on complaint volume.

What this article did not prove: NHTSA has found no defect yet, and its opening summary attributes no crashes, fires, injuries, or deaths to this failure.[2] Population figure 54,787 counts vehicles under review, not failed engines. Ford's knowledge and the secret-patch claim are petition allegations, not findings.
