## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Volkswagen filed a FOURTH US battery-safety recall for the ID.4 on October 6, 2026 (NHTSA 26V630, 22,524 vehicles, MY 2023-2026): charging past 80% can trigger thermal propagation, the root cause is still unknown, and this time there is no remedy at all — just "charge it less." The recall net also widened: it explicitly covers vehicles "not included in previous battery recall campaigns," meaning cars VW had cleared are being pulled back in, and the model-year range now reaches 2026.

## Limitations (to state in article)
- Root cause unknown: VW and SK Battery America have not determined it. We cannot assess the true per-vehicle fire probability.
- "Certain models" in the affected range: electrek/VW did not publish a precise VIN-range split; owners must check VINs.
- The 22 claims are US-only and count claims, not confirmed fires — the mapping from claim to thermal event is not published.
- We do not know whether the 80% cap fully mitigates the risk or merely reduces it; VW has not said.
- This story predates the fix; the article's remedy status reflects October 2026.

## Strongest counterargument (to state at full strength)
22 claims over two and a half years against tens of thousands of cars is a low incidence rate, and the 80% cap is precautionary, not proof of imminent danger. VW found this through its own ongoing field study — the system working as intended, proactively widening the net before more incidents. EV battery fires remain far rarer per vehicle than gasoline-car fires. And the class action (#866) alleges concealment, but the October recall shows VW is still actively expanding the campaign, not hiding it.

## Actionable insights (required)
- If you own a 2023-2026 ID.4: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls NOW; set your charge limit to 80% in the car's charging menu; expect a risk letter by ~Nov 27, 2026 and a second letter when a remedy exists; VW customer service 1-800-893-5298 (recall 93EX).
- If shopping used: 2023-2026 ID.4s carry four open/closed battery campaigns — verify all four are complete (25V836, 26V028, 26V030, 26V630) before buying; ask the seller for recall-completion paperwork.
- General EV note: an 80% daily charge cap is normal battery hygiene anyway; what is NOT normal is a manufacturer mandating it because it cannot find the fire's cause.
