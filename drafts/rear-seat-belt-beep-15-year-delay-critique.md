# Critique Panel — Article #792: "NHTSA Took 15 Years to Move a Beep Three Feet"
## Journalist: Vin Wreckage | Kicker: The Gap

### 1. General Editor (9.0/10)
Strong headline with concrete absurdity ("15 years to move a beep three feet"). The lede anchors the timeline with FMVSS 208 (1972) and the Sept 1 deadline. The structure is clean: historical context → body count → data gap → rule impact → actionable. The "child born when Congress issued the mandate will be old enough to drive" line lands perfectly. Minor: the 6,000 extrapolation is rough math (400/yr × 15), and the article acknowledges it's directional rather than precise. Solid.

Not interchangeable with Rex (no noir detective), not Axle (no charts/regression), not Dale (no tox-stat humor). Sentence rhythm passes: fragments ("Ten points." ### 3. Ethics Reviewer (9.0/10)
No fabricated data. All statistics are traced to named sources (NHTSA FARS, saferoads.org citing NHTSA, CenTIR/UB research). The 6,000-death extrapolation is presented as directional ("somewhere north of") rather than precise, which is appropriate. The article criticizes regulatory delay, not specific people. The "backseat bullets" framing is from the original UB research, not invented. No sensationalism beyond what the data supports.

### 4. Social Shareability (9.5/10)
"15 years to move a beep three feet" is extremely shareable. The concrete absurdity of the timeline (child born → old enough to drive) gives it social currency. The "150-pound projectile" factoid is the kind of thing people text to friends. The Sept 1 deadline makes it timely. The actionable close ("buckle up in the back") gives readers something to do after sharing.

### 5. Legal Accuracy (9.0/10)
FMVSS 208 history is correct. MAP-21 Section 31502 mandated rear seat belt reminders. The December 2024 final rule date matches the docket. Sept 1, 2026 (front) and Sept 1, 2027 (rear) compliance dates are correct per the finalized rule. The 50 lives/year estimate comes directly from NHTSA's own regulatory impact analysis. References are properly linked and verifiable. The article correctly distinguishes between the federal mandate, voluntary automaker commitment, and actual compliance.

### 6. Research Rigor (8.5/10)
7 cited sources, all from authoritative bodies (NHTSA, IIHS/CHOP, Congress, CenTIR/UB, saferoads.org, Consumer Reports). The 803 deaths figure is cited from a specific year (2018) rather than a vague average. The 2019 voluntary commitment is sourced via Consumer Reports. Minor gap: the 70% adult rear-seat belt rate is attributed to IIHS/CHOP but the specific study (Jermakian & Weast 2018) is from a different dataset than the NHTSA 91.6%/81.7% figures, so the comparison mixes survey methodologies. Not a dealbreaker but worth noting.

### 7. Actionable Insights (8.5/10)
Clear actionable close: buckle up in the back, insist passengers do the same, check if your car has rear-seat reminders. The "if your car predates 2025 model year" qualifier is helpful and specific. Could be slightly stronger with a VIN-check link or specific model-year guidance on which cars already have rear-seat reminders, but the core message is actionable and concrete.
