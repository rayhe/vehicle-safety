Buick Filed 2 Recalls for Its 2026 Cars. One Covers a Single Car

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 15, 2026

I ran the census on every 2026 Buick. Twenty queries against NHTSA's recalls API, four canonical models from VPIC, every result deduped by campaign number and filtered to filings from calendar 2026. Two campaigns touch a 2026-model-year Buick. That is the lowest count of any GM brand, and the smaller of the two touches exactly one car.[1]

1
2026 Buicks in recall 26V539, a 306,511-vehicle rearview camera recall: a single Encore GX built on December 2, 2024

Start with the campaign that touches the most Buicks, because it is also the least mechanical. Recall 26V114 exists because a radio failed to download an owner's manual during production, which means the 2026 Enclave and Envision technically violate Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 208, Occupant Protection, for want of a PDF. Two of Buick's four models are on the list, alongside more than 40 GM models in total. Remedy is a dealer radio reset that triggers the download, and owner letters went out April 16.[2] Half of Buick's entire recall year is paperwork, and it is the most widespread campaign in the census.

Now the one with actual hardware. Recall 26V539, filed August 20, covers 306,511 Trax, Trailblazer, Envista, and Encore GX vehicles whose rearview camera screen can distort or go blank, because a plasma-cleaning treatment used to prepare the camera housing weakened the adhesive bond holding the unit together. GM estimates 10 percent of the population carries the defect. Buick's share is 72,175 vehicles, 23.6 percent of the recall, and nearly all of them are 2024 and 2025 model years.[3] Here is where the census turns into a joke with a punchline. Exactly one 2026 Buick is in the recall, a single Encore GX built on December 2, 2024, the only day of 2026-model-year production in the suspect window. Seven Trailblazers share the distinction. Every other vehicle in the recall is a 2024 or 2025.

GM's own chronology makes the camera recall stranger than the count. Investigators opened a case on May 20, 2025, then closed it on September 4, 2025, having found no defect trend, while continuing to monitor field data at six-month intervals. Monitoring caught what the first investigation missed: claim rates rose, a second investigation opened March 25, 2026, and GM counted 33,365 field complaints between July 3, 2024 and May 30, 2026 that were potentially related to the condition. Recall decision came August 13, 2026, fifteen months after the first investigation opened.[3] No accidents or injuries are known. Supplier is Wuxi Sharp Optoelectronic Technology Co. of China, and the fix is a physical camera swap, not software, which means every one of those 306,511 owners needs a dealer visit if their camera fails inspection.

One model carries a zero, and the zero is the most interesting number in the table. Envista is Buick's cheapest and newest nameplate, the one most likely to be built to a price, and it filed nothing this year. Its platform twin, the Chevrolet Trax, anchors the biggest campaign in the recall with 159,821 vehicles, while the Envista sits at zero. Zero is not a safety rating, and it is not a promise about next year, but it is a fact about this year, and it sits in the same table as a camera that goes blind in reverse.

For scale, GM as a whole filed 15 campaigns touching a 2026 model year, and the brand splits double-count the shared ones: Chevrolet 13, GMC 11, Cadillac 7, Buick 2, a total of 31 brand-campaign pairs from 15 distinct GM campaigns plus Cadillac's two brand-only defects.[4] Buick is the only GM brand with zero brand-only defects in 2026. Both of its campaigns are GM-wide, which means Buick's entire recall year is borrowed trouble. No campaign filed in 2026 touches only older Buicks either, so the excluded count is zero, and the census is complete on its own terms.

Fair objection: two recalls sounds like a clean bill of health, but the count is a blunt instrument, and here it flatters Buick in both directions. A brand with four models cannot rack up campaigns the way Chevrolet can with a dozen nameplates, so the 2-count is partly an artifact of a tiny lineup. Meanwhile the camera recall that barely touches a 2026 Buick still touches 72,175 Buicks of earlier model years, and the 33,365 complaints behind it make it one of GM's largest recalls of 2026 by volume. That reading is not wrong, but it is also incomplete. Buick is the only GM brand with no brand-only defect this year, and the Envista, the cheapest car in the lineup, filed zero while its platform twin anchors the biggest recall of the year. That split deserves the headline, not the count alone.

What this census does not prove: no FARS cross-tab is meaningful here, because these are 2026 models with no fatal-crash history yet, and the campaign count says nothing about injury rates. Defect percentages are GM's estimates, not independently verified. Campaigns filed after November 15 are not counted, and the excluded sweep only reached back to model year 2023, so a 2026 filing touching only 2022-or-older Buicks would be missed, and none was found. Complaint counts are GM's "potentially related" figures, an upper bound rather than a confirmed defect rate.

If you own a 2024-2025 Envista or 2024-2026 Encore GX with the standard rearview camera, 26V539 is the one that matters: shift into reverse and watch the screen, and if it flickers, distorts, or goes blank, the dealer inspects and replaces the camera free. Surround Vision trims are not affected. If you own a 2026 Enclave or Envision, 26V114 is the only recall touching your car this year, and it is a radio reset that downloads the owner's manual, so ask the dealer to confirm the manual is actually on the car. If you own a 2026 Envista, your car has zero recalls filed in 2026, which is a fact, not a rating, so check the VIN anyway. And if you are shopping, a 2026 Encore GX built after December 2, 2024 is outside the camera recall entirely, and the build date is on the label in the driver's door jamb.
