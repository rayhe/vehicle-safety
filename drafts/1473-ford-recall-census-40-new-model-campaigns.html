Ford Said the Recall Deluge Was Old Cars and Software. Its Newest Vehicles Just Hit 40 Recalls.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 11, 2026

Back in September, Ford's explanation for the recall avalanche sounded almost reasonable. Spokesman Mike Levine told the Detroit Free Press campaigns were down 38 percent, driven by old cars and proactive testing. Our census that week put a number on “current production”: 25 federal campaigns touching 2026-model-year Fords, against 5 for Toyota and 2 for Honda. I ran the same census again today, same method, same NHTSA database, and Ford's newest vehicles are now at 40.

40
federal recall campaigns touching 2026-model-year Ford vehicles, filed Jan 1–Nov 11, 2026, against 7 for Toyota and 2 for Honda

Forty campaigns in ten months, on cars mostly short of their first birthday. Fifteen more campaigns landed while the company told reporters the problem was shrinking; Toyota added two, Honda none.

Spread the 40 across the lineup and it stops looking like one bad part. Explorer leads with 12 campaigns, Bronco has 9, F-150 has 8, Ranger 7, and both Super Duties carry 5 or 6 each, spread across twelve different Ford models with at least one campaign. September 9 alone produced six in one day: rear axle, stabilizer rod, fuel tank, seat-belt buckles, curtain air bags, frontal air bags. That is not a testing program finding things early; it is a production system finding things late, all at once.

Then there is the software defense: back in July, Ford spokeswoman Sharyn Ghacham said more than 80 percent of the year's recalls “are resolved through convenient, software-only updates.” Run the new-model cut and the number is zero. Not one of the 40 campaigns carries NHTSA's over-the-air flag. Instead the list reads like a parts catalog with a grudge: axles, stabilizer rods, fuel tanks, seat-belt buckles, air bags, wiper motors, tailgate cables. Biggest in the set is the Bronco's engine-compartment wiring: 565,691 trucks, fire risk, roughly 1 percent estimated defective. A software update does not re-route a wiring harness.

In fairness, the other side of the ledger is not nothing. Campaign counts are not severity scores: a 23-vehicle torque check and a 565,691-vehicle fire risk each count once. Ford recalls aggressively and early, and its 2025 NHTSA consent order puts it under independent oversight that rewards over-reporting. Toyota's 7 and Honda's 2 could partly reflect different reporting thresholds rather than different quality. But the breadth answers most of that: twelve models, every system from visibility to fuel, an Explorer with a dozen campaigns before its first scheduled service. One bad supplier does not do that, and neither does one bad quarter.

If you own a 2026 Ford, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls tonight. Explorer owners most of all: twelve campaigns, and several remedies, including the Bronco wiring fix, arrived months after the owner letters. If you are shopping, the Explorer's dozen is the most useful number in this piece. First-year redesigns with that many federal campaigns are a reason to buy year two, or to negotiate like you already know.
