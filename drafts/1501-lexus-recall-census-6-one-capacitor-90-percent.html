The Gap

Toyota Recalled Its Cars 15 Times. Lexus Recalled 6. One Missing Capacitor Was 90% of It.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 14, 2026

Lexus filed six recall campaigns for its 2026 cars this year, five of them footnotes. One of them covers 144,200 cars, 90.2 percent of every Lexus recalled in 2026, and the defect is a capacitor that isn't there. Not a failed part or a worn part, but a part somebody forgot to put in a USB charger, which means the backup camera can go black the moment you shift into reverse.[1]

90.2%
Share of all 159,849 recalled Lexus vehicles covered by the single backup-camera campaign, 26V162

Start with the count, because the count is the whole method. I ran the corrected-vocabulary census this site built for Toyota and Honda across all eleven of Lexus's canonical 2026 models, 44 queries against the NHTSA recalls API, keeping only campaigns filed in calendar 2026. Six campaigns came back, touching six models, while five nameplates, including the ES and LS sedans, carry zero.[2]

Let's talk about what happens in the first eight seconds, because that's the window. You start the car, you cycle the ignition from off to on within about eight seconds, and the voltage dips while the engine cranks. In most cars that dip is nothing, a flicker the electronics ride through. In 144,200 Lexuses, the in-cabin USB charger has no capacitor to smooth it out, so the voltage drop interrupts the backup camera's boot sequence and the screen goes black when you shift into reverse. Toyota's own filing says every one of the 144,200 contains the condition, a 100 percent defect estimate, which is the kind of number a manufacturer only writes when the engineering is settled and the argument is over. Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111 requires a rearview image. A blank screen is a failed standard, not a glitch.[3]

And the fix is software. Or a camera, if the software doesn't take. Dealer visit, free of charge, owner letters mailed in May. Which is fine, except it means the most expensive cars Toyota sells were recalled in six figures because of a component that costs cents and does nothing except sit there absorbing a voltage dip. Somebody specced a charger without it, somebody approved it, and 144,200 dashboards shipped with the gap. That's the engineering story here, and it is not complicated: a cheap part, missing, with an expensive consequence.

Now the TX, because the TX is where the census gets interesting. Toyota's newest three-row appears in four of the six campaigns, more than any other Lexus nameplate, and it appears in every kind of defect the brand filed this year. The software defect lives in the camera recall. Paperwork, in the load-capacity label recall, where six TX350s got stickers with the wrong weight on them. Structure, in the rear axle recall, where forged-aluminum axle carriers from Bharat Forge in Sanford, North Carolina, were made with insufficient material strength and can break, taking vehicle stability with them. Steering, in the knuckle recall, where forged-aluminum front steering knuckles shipped without their aging-furnace heat treatment and can crack and break.[4][5]

Two details in those last two campaigns are worth your attention, because they cut in opposite directions. In the axle recall, Toyota recalled 5,408 SUVs to find roughly 270 with bad parts, a 5 percent defect estimate, which means 95 percent of the owners who got the letter have perfectly good axle carriers and a free inspection. In the knuckle recall, the supplier's own quality audit caught the skipped furnace step after an eight-day production window, June 18 to 26, and Toyota traced the bad batch down to 617 individual vehicles, 364 of them Lexuses, with zero warranty claims and zero field reports. One recall is a haystack with a needle inside. The other is a supply chain safety net that worked exactly as designed, catching a steering-loss defect before a single knuckle broke. Same brand, same year, same forged aluminum, and very different stories.

Then there's the RZ, which is the third verse of a song this site has already heard twice. A Denso battery ECU memory fault shuts down the drive system at speed, loss of drive power, 100 percent defect estimate, dealer software update. Toyota's bZ got it in June, the C-HR in September, and the RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid in October. Now the RZ, 4,739 cars, same supplier, same failure mode, same fix: three brands, one part, one outcome. When the same control-unit fault shows up in the fourth new electric car in five months, the question stops being whether the part is bad and starts being how many more nameplates share it.[6]

For completeness: the LX600 got a transmission recall, 4,374 cars, a solenoid failure that breaks communication between the transmission ECU and the engine ECU, with over-revving and a possible fluid leak as the consequences. Zero field reports, zero warranty claims, dealer software update. And the GX550 dominates the label recall in a way that deserves a raised eyebrow: 4,709 of the 8,230 cars in that campaign are GX550s, a sticker with the wrong accessory weight on it, found during an internal audit in August 2025. Fifty-seven percent of a labeling recall is one model. Somebody's accessory-weight spreadsheet had a GX550-shaped hole in it.[7]

So where does Lexus sit? Toyota filed 15 campaigns for its 2026 cars; Lexus filed 6. But campaign counts rank manufacturers, and vehicle counts rank defects, and those two tables tell different stories. Lexus's 183,820 campaign-units across the six filings are dominated by a single campaign, the camera recall at 144,200, and the concentration is the story: one defect, nine-tenths of the brand's recalled cars. Strip out the four campaigns Lexus shares with Toyota's lineup, and the brand's own record is two filings: a transmission software fix with zero claims, and a backup camera that goes dark because of a missing capacitor. One of those is a footnote. The other is 90 percent of the brand's recalled cars.

One series-standard check, for the record: none of the six campaigns is flagged as an over-the-air update in the NHTSA API, the same zero the series has found for Ford, GM, Toyota, and Honda. Unlike GM's census, where the flag turned out to be wrong, the remedy text here agrees with it: all three software fixes are dealer visits, not downloads. The flag and the filings tell the same story this time.

In fairness, the case for Lexus is stronger than the 6-count suggests, and the strongest version of it goes like this. Four of the six campaigns are shared Toyota defects, so the brand's independent record is genuinely small: two filings, one with zero claims, one with a narrow trigger. The TX's four appearances are partly a platform artifact, since the axle and knuckle campaigns are Grand Highlander defects that happen to include the TX, not TX defects. And the camera failure only triggers when you cycle the ignition off and on within about eight seconds, a narrow window, with a free software fix already in dealers' hands. A reader could reasonably conclude that Lexus built a quiet year and one loud capacitor.

What the data does not prove: campaign counts are not severity scores, and the 144,200 number counts every vehicle containing the condition, not every vehicle that will ever see a blank screen. Unit counts are the manufacturers' filed estimates for all model years in each campaign, not 2026-only counts; the 159,849 Lexus total spans model years 2022 through 2026. Two 2025-filed campaigns that also touch 2026 Lexus vehicles are excluded by this census's method: 25V744, the predecessor backup-camera recall from October 2025, and 25V595, an RX instrument-panel recall. Including them would make the count 8, not 6. And the NHTSA API's vocabulary gaps remain the central risk in this method: a campaign hiding under an untested model spelling would be missed, and the direction of any remaining bias is undercount. The 2026 model year is still in progress, and late filings will move every number in this article.

If you own a 2023-2026 RX350, a 2022-2025 NX250 or NX350, or a 2024-2026 TX350, the camera recall is a free dealer software update or camera replacement, and the fix is in dealers' hands now. If the screen ever goes blank in reverse, don't guess what's behind you. If you own a 2026 RZ, the battery ECU fix is a free dealer software update for a defect the filing estimates at 100 percent; don't wait for the letter. If you own a 2026 TX built April 10 to 27, the axle inspection is free, and only about 5 percent actually have the bad carrier, but the consequence is a sudden loss of stability. If you own a 2026 RX or TX built June 18 to 26, the knuckle inspection is free, and a cracked knuckle means loss of steering. If you own a 2025-2026 LX600, the transmission software update is free. And if you're shopping a 2026 Lexus, shop by model, not by badge: the TX carries the count, the ES, IS, LC, UX, and LS carry zero, and any 2026 Lexus VIN should also be checked against the two 2025-filed campaigns this census excludes.
