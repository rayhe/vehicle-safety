# Research Notes — Article #756: mass-shooting-road-death-spillover

## Angle
A JAMA Psychiatry study (Patel et al., July 2026) found that fatal traffic crashes spike 14.3% nationwide the day after major mass shootings. The extra road deaths across the 10 deadliest shootings (2008–2023) total ~75% of the shooting victims themselves. These secondary deaths are invisible — filed in FARS as ordinary traffic fatalities, never counted as part of the shooting's toll.

## Primary Sources
1. **Patel VR et al.** "The Broader Impact of Mass Shootings on Traffic Fatalities," *JAMA Psychiatry* (2026). DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.1912
   - Quasi-experimental analysis of 10 deadliest US mass shootings, 2008–2023
   - Compared traffic fatalities day-after-shooting vs 10-day pre-shooting baseline
   - 8 of 10 events showed increased fatal traffic crashes
   - Average increase: 14.3% (~20 additional deaths per event day)
   - Cumulative extra deaths ≈ 75% of shooting victims
   - Effect was NATIONWIDE — not just near shooting sites
   - Google Trends search interest for shootings peaked on same day as crash spikes
   - Effects consistent across age, gender, urban/rural, weather
   - Mechanism hypothesis: media-induced emotional distraction

2. **NHTSA FARS** 2014–2023: baseline fatal crash data, ~36,000–43,000 annual deaths

3. **NHTSA Q1 2026 estimates**: 7,770 deaths, rate 0.99/100M VMT (lowest Q1 since 2014)

## Novel Contribution
Nobody cross-references FARS daily fatality counts with mass shooting dates. This JAMA Psychiatry study did — and found that the official toll of mass shootings systematically undercounts casualties. The secondary road deaths are invisible because they're filed as traffic fatalities, not gun violence.

## Strongest Counterargument
Only 10 events studied. Correlation ≠ causation. Other emotional shocks (celebrity deaths, natural disasters, elections) might produce similar spikes, meaning the effect isn't unique to shootings but reflects any mass emotional disturbance.

## Limitations
- Only the 10 deadliest, highest-media-coverage shootings — smaller events may not trigger the same effect
- FARS data lag means post-2023 events aren't captured
- Mechanism not definitively established — could be emotional distraction, phone-checking for news, reduced police presence during shooting response, or a combination
- Study design (quasi-experimental, no true control) limits causal inference
- 10 data points is a small sample for generalization

## Actionable Insights
1. After a mass shooting makes national news: avoid non-essential driving for 24 hours
2. At minimum: no phone, no news radio while driving
3. If emotionally overwhelmed, pull over — grief is impairment
4. Transit is safer than driving while emotionally distracted
5. Traffic safety advocates should track this correlation systematically
