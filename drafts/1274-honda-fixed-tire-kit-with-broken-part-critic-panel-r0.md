## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10

Structure is textbook Crash Report: hook lede, pull stat, mechanism, numbers, trust argument, limitations, action close. The "You did everything right" opening is the strongest lede device in the piece and it pays off in the final line. Deduction: length. Body runs ~760 words against the guide's 300-500; the limitations and counterargument paragraphs are load-bearing, but the numbers paragraph could lose a sentence. Not a ship-blocker: the extra length is argument, not filler.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.5/10

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

No victims to exploit (zero injuries reported, stated plainly, not buried). No sensational inflation: the article says explicitly that this is small by recall standards and that the system caught it. The anger is aimed at a process failure, not at frightened owners. No self-congratulation. The kit-use safety tip is genuinely useful rather than performative.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 9.0/10

Headline is the share: "The Fix Was the Broken Part" is a complete argument in eight words. Lede doubles as a social card ("You did everything right... It was not"). The 4,804 pull stat is concrete and quotable. The one-line closer ("You already trusted the fix once") is built for quote-posting. Strongest share asset in the recent queue.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.5/10

Campaign numbers (26V663, 26V366, Honda PPW), dates (filed Oct 8, ack Oct 13, VINs Oct 17, letters Nov 16), and the mechanism all trace to the NHTSA record or Honda's filing, with trade reporting used only for supplier attribution (TEK, July 3 discovery, change-point controls) and the Canada count. Deduction: the "118,000 in Canada" figure comes from trade reporting of the original recall, not from a 26V663 document, and the article does not flag that sourcing distinction inline. Minor; the claim is peripheral and the source is cited.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Original contribution is real: the re-recall rate (<0.5% of the original population), the ~48 expected defective units at Honda's 1% estimate, the 97-day discovery-to-filing gap, and the valve-deletion remedy analysis are all computed in the piece with inputs shown. Limitations are a dedicated, honest section, not hedging. The counterargument (small, well-handled, system worked) is stated at full strength and partially conceded before the rebuttal. The one unverifiable: TEK's internal controls are Honda's characterization, and the article says so.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10

Pull stat (4,804) is the right number, labeled with its meaning. The 2,745/2,058/1 split is given in prose where a reader can follow it; the 1% → ~48 derivation is shown, not asserted. Deduction: a two-row comparison (original recall vs re-recall) would land the scale contrast faster than prose. Not required; the numbers are all present and checkable.
