## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Of every mass-market vehicle in the FARS fatal-crash database, the Chevrolet Corvette — America's flagship performance car — has the highest alcohol-positive rate among its drivers: 21.3%, ranked #1 of 168 nameplates with 500+ fatal-crash drivers.

## Kill test
After 1,006 articles, impairment-by-model is well-trodden (Dale Impactor III's beat), and drug angles were done (#868 druggiest drivers/Buick, #883 van drugs-beat-alcohol, #928 safest cars druggest drivers). But **alcohol-specific #1 ranking** has never been done, and no article has named the Corvette. Novel contribution: a full nameplate-by-nameplate alcohol-positive ranking from FARS toxicology data — a cross-tab nobody ran — revealing that the fastest American car has the drunkest drivers. News pegs: Feb 28, 2026 Newport Beach DUI Corvette pedestrian killing (Steven Alcaraz, 21; driver Jesus Alvarez fled, arrested for DUI), and the Henry Ruggs Corvette case (0.161 BAC, 156 mph, Tina Tintor killed, 3-10 years). ## Primary sources

Corvette: 1,147 drivers, 244 alcohol-positive (21.3%), 119 drug-positive (10.4%), 300 any impairment (26.2%). ## Key numbers (methodology: alc-positive = BAC > 0, NOT necessarily ≥ 0.08)
- National fatal-crash-driver baseline: 490,736 drivers; 15.1% alcohol-positive, 8.7% drug-positive, 20.0% any impairment
- Corvette: 1,147 drivers; **21.3% alcohol-positive (#1 of 168)**; 10.4% drug-positive; **26.2% any impairment** (more than 1 in 4)
- Corvette vs. siblings: Camaro 17.1% alc, Challenger 17.5%, Mustang 16.4% — Corvette is 25%+ drunker than its muscle-car cousins
- Runner-ups: Cadillac CTS 20.6%, Infiniti G37 19.6%, Hyundai Tiburon 20.8%, Toyota Celica 19.7%, Porsche 911 19.3%, Mitsubishi Eclipse 19.4%
- Sports-car class overall: 17.1% alc (highest of any body class); Corvette still beats the class average by 25%
- Sober-end of sports cars: Hyundai Veloster 10.2%, Acura RSX 13.8%, Miata 14.7% — half the Corvette's rate

## Original contribution
The nameplate alcohol ranking itself (computed 2026-09-28 from fars_output.js). Secondary finding: within sports cars there's no clean price→sobriety gradient (cheap Tiburon is #4 drunkest; cheap Veloster is the soberest) — the Corvette's position isn't explained by price alone.

## Limitations
- FARS captures only FATAL crashes; this is impairment among drivers who died or killed, not among all Corvette drivers.
- Alcohol-positive = BAC > 0; some of these drivers were below the 0.08 legal limit. The data does not separate "had a beer" from "three sheets to the wind."
- Toxicology testing is not universal — testing rates vary by state, year, and whether the driver survived; 1,147 Corvette drivers is a solid sample but selection effects remain.
- Estimated ±: FARS 2014–2023 window; the C8 generation (2020+) is mid-engine and only partly in this window.

## Strongest counterargument
A Corvette is disproportionately likely to be involved in a fatal crash in the first place (speed, power-to-weight), and alcohol-positive toxicology is a correlate of the nighttime/high-speed driving that makes Corvette crashes fatal — so the 21.3% may partly measure "the kind of crash that kills you" rather than "the kind of driver who owns a Corvette." Also possible: higher testing rates for exotic/high-profile crashes. The ranking is real, but the causal story (car attracts drinkers vs. speed makes crashes fatal) can't be separated from this data.

## Actionable insight
Shopping for a used performance car? The Corvette's toxicology record says these cars concentrate risk-taking drivers — budget for a performance driving school, not just tires. Riding with a Corvette owner at 11 PM? Offer to drive. And the eternal one: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — it won't fix the driver, but it's the part you can control.
