# Research: #1199 — The Drugged Body Count (Dale Impactor III)

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Apply the rate-vs-volume lens to drugged driving: the "druggiest vehicles in America" rate leaderboard is dominated by dead beaters (Oldsmobile Alero 18.4%), but the actual body count of drug-positive fatal-crash drivers lives in the mainstream fleet. The Chevrolet Silverado alone put 2,060 drug-positive drivers in fatal crashes — more than 7x the COMBINED drug-positive drivers of the entire top-10 drug-rate leaderboard (292). The druggiest car in America is a work truck.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. It directly complicates this site's own prior toxicology framing (#805 beater effect, #1065 impairment U-curve, both rate-led) with a volume cross-tab nobody ran: drugPct × drivers per model across all 307 FARS nameplates. The finding inverts the reader's mental model — the drugged-driving crisis isn't fringe Oldsmobiles, it's 26.3% of all drug-positive dead drivers concentrated in the 10 best-selling mainstream models.
- **Novel angle on data?** Yes. Decomposition: ratePct leaders vs. drugN leaders have near-zero overlap. The Alero's 18.4% crown represents 26 actual drivers; the Silverado's "unremarkable" 8.7% represents 2,060.
- **Verdict: PROCEED.**

## Original findings (computed from fars_output.js FARS_TOXICOLOGY, 490,736 drivers, 307 models)
- Fleet totals: 490,736 drivers with toxicology; 42,493 drug-positive (8.7%); 74,175 alcohol-positive (15.1%).
- **Top 10 by drug-positive DRIVER COUNT:** Silverado 2,060 (8.7%); F-150 1,696 (8.0%); Camry 1,160 (8.4%); Accord 1,146 (8.3%); Civic 1,114 (9.0%); Altima 917 (9.0%); Sierra 857 (9.2%); Corolla 813 (7.9%); RAM 706 (8.0%); Malibu 693 (9.4%).
- **Top 10 by drug-positive RATE combined:** 292 drivers (Alero 26, Park Avenue 43, BMW M5 17, Verano 52, Five Hundred 28, Grand Vitara 20, NV200 27, Rodeo 18, GLK 28, Commander 33). Silverado alone is **7.06x** that combined total.
- The top 10 volume models account for **26.3% of all drug-positive fatal drivers** in the dataset.
- Alcohol side: Silverado 3,717 alcohol-positive dead drivers (15.7%), F-150 3,052 — the same volume logic applies, but the rate story there is already told.
- Key inversion stat: drug-rate leaders average ~229 drivers per model; volume leaders average ~14,500. The rate leaderboard is a small-sample theater.

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
Rate matters for personal risk: if you're choosing between two $3,000 beaters, the Alero's 18.4% says something real about the cohort behind that wheel, even at n=141. Volume is driven almost entirely by sales — the Silverado is the drugged body-count champ partly because America buys 600,000 of them a year. A body-count metric can make anything look guilty if enough of it exists. Also, "drug-positive" is not "drug-impaired": FARS records detectable drugs, and the GAO itself says available crash drug-involvement data is unreliable for impairment inference (see sources). The honest read: rates identify risky cohorts; counts identify where the actual casualties concentrate. Both lists are true. The mistake is only ever publishing one.

## Limitations
- FARS fatal crashes only (~40k deaths/yr vs ~6.7M crashes). Non-fatal drugged driving invisible.
- Drug-positive ≠ impaired. THC detectable days/weeks; NHTSA/GAO explicitly warn against impairment inference (GAO: "lack of a clear link between impairment and drug concentrations... exacerbates challenges").
- Testing is inconsistent across states (~60% average, some below 10%); testing bias could skew both rate and volume.
- Volume counts are not rate-normalized: best-sellers dominate by construction. Silverado's 2,060 must be read as population-level harm, not per-driver risk.
- n=141 for the Alero: its 18.4% has wide confidence intervals.

## Methodology
1. Parsed FARS_TOXICOLOGY (307 models; fields drivers, drugPct, alcPct, anyPct) from fars_output.js (generated by fars_process.py from NHTSA FARS bulk CSV, 2014-2023).
2. drugN = drivers × drugPct/100 per model; ranked by drugN and by drugPct; computed top-10 sums and share of fleet total (42,493).
3. Top-10-by-rate list restricted to drivers ≥ 40 (matches #1065 threshold).

## Actionable takeaways
- The drugged-driving threat is not a beater-car subculture — 1 in 4 drug-positive fatal drivers died behind the wheel of a top-10 mainstream model. The cars around you on any commute are the drugged body count.
- For regulators: rate-led shaming of fringe vehicles misses where intervention (roadside drug screening, employer policies for fleet/work-truck drivers) would actually move the body count.
- For buyers: Silverado/F-150 drivers have the lowest drug-positive RATE among trucks — the vehicle isn't the risk factor; ubiquity is. Check your VIN for recalls and drive like the statistics say: someone around you is high.
- What to do: support roadside drug-screening R&D (no validated roadside device exists, per GAO); if you're impaired by anything — prescription, OTC, weed — don't drive. The morgue data doesn't care what kind of high it was.
