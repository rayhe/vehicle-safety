# Research — #1187: GAO-26-107954 on driver visibility (9,200 pedestrians/cyclists killed 2024)

**Slug:** `1187-gao-visibility-report-blind-spots`
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — vehicle design analysis, safety tech beat; judgmental about bad vehicle design)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Dateline target:** October 16, 2026

## Angle
The federal government's own watchdog did the math the regulator hasn't: GAO-26-107954 (July 2026) found ~9,200 pedestrians and cyclists killed on US roads in 2024, up about 65% since 2010, and found that heavy-duty trucks had *at least 12 times the odds* of turning (vs. going straight) in fatal pedestrian crashes compared with cars. GAO's recommendation: DOT should (1) determine a method to measure driver visibility, and (2) fully analyze the risks. DOT agreed with the first. It declined the second. The EU has adopted driver visibility standards; London requires direct-vision safety equipment on commercial trucks. The US has nothing binding — a regulatory gap with a body count attached.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Nonpartisan congressional watchdog report, July 2026, with two formal recommendations to DOT and a recorded partial non-concurrence. 9,200 dead in one year with a 65% decade-plus climb is a genuine news event.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. The Crash Report has covered IIHS blind-zone ratings (#1093/#1102/#1140/#1144, all work-truck test stories) but has never covered GAO-26-107954 or the DOT partial non-concurrence. The angle here is accountability, not test scores: the regulator was asked to fully analyze the risk and said no.
- **Data-grounded?** Yes. Numbers are from the GAO report itself (highlights page + full PDF), a primary source.

## Primary sources
1. **GAO-26-107954 Highlights (primary):** "PEDESTRIAN AND CYCLIST SAFETY: DOT Should Take Steps to Address Risks Related to Limited Driver Visibility," July 2026. https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-107954-highlights.pdf
   - ~9,200 pedestrians and cyclists killed on US roadways in 2024, up ~65% since 2010.
   - Heavy-duty trucks (e.g., semitrucks) had at least 12x the odds of turning (vs. going straight) in fatal pedestrian crashes compared with cars.
   - EU has adopted driver visibility standards; London developed a direct-vision standard requiring some commercial truck operators to install additional safety equipment.
   - DOT "recognized risks... but has not fully analyzed those risks or developed responses," in part because it has not determined a method to measure driver visibility.
   - Recommendations: (1) determine a method to measure driver visibility; (2) fully analyze risks limited driver visibility poses to pedestrians/cyclists and develop responses. **DOT concurred with (1) and did NOT concur with (2).**
2. **GAO-26-107954 full report (primary):** https://files.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-107954.pdf — 18 studies reviewed; larger vehicles, certain design features, and turning maneuvers associated with more limited visibility and greater fatality risk.
3. **HSToday (secondary, corroboration):** "GAO: DOT Needs Better Data on Vehicle Blind Spots Linked to Pedestrian and Cyclist Deaths," July 2026. https://www.hstoday.us/industry/latest-from-gao/gao-dot-needs-better-data-on-vehicle-blind-spots-linked-to-pedestrian-and-cyclist-deaths/
4. **Streetsblog USA (background):** June 2024, on Rep. Raskin's request that launched the GAO probe: https://usa.streetsblog.org/2024/06/17/congress-launches-probe-into-why-massive-cars-kill-so-many-pedestrians-and-cyclists

## Secondary/context
- IIHS commercial-vehicle crash-avoidance ratings (Oct 8, 2026): first US ratings of work trucks/vans; no binding regulation behind them.
- GAO reviewed studies from 2015–2025 and DOT pedestrian fatality data; interviewed 5 auto and 5 truck manufacturers (which GAO says have developed side bicyclist alerts, pedestrian AEB, and do internal visibility benchmarking — i.e., the industry can measure what the regulator can't).

## Original contribution (not just synthesis)
- The 12x odds ratio translated for readers: among fatal pedestrian crashes, heavy trucks were dramatically more likely to have been mid-turn than cars — the classic "I never saw them" crash geometry (right hook, cab-over blind zone), quantified by the government's own analysts.
- The concurrence ledger: DOT agreed to figure out how to *measure* blind zones but declined to *fully analyze* the risk. That half-concurrence is the story's spine — measurement without analysis is a data-collection exercise, not a safety program.
- The regulatory-gap timeline: EU adopted direct-vision requirements (EU General Safety Regulation) and London runs a working Direct Vision Standard with permits; the US has voluntary IIHS ratings (Oct 2026) and nothing binding.
- Math: 9,200/yr = ~25 pedestrians or cyclists killed per day in 2024.

## Limitations
- GAO used turning maneuvers as a *proxy* for visibility-limited crashes because blind-zone size data don't exist — the odds ratio is suggestive, not proof that blindness caused the crashes. Turning trucks are also bigger, heavier, and harder to stop.
- The 12x figure is "at least" 12x odds for heavy-duty trucks vs. cars for turning-vs-going-straight in fatal pedestrian crashes; it's a within-crash-type comparison, not an absolute crash-risk multiplier.
- DOT's non-concurrence with recommendation 2 is documented; GAO "continues to believe DOT should fully implement both." DOT's stated reasoning (in the full report) should be acknowledged, not caricatured.
- FARS/DOT fatality data cover deaths only; injury patterns may differ.

## Strongest counterargument
Turning as a proxy is noisy: semitrucks log far more urban turning miles (deliveries, right turns) than passenger cars, so higher turn-involvement in fatal crashes could reflect exposure patterns, not just visibility. And pedestrian AEB, which GAO notes manufacturers already deploy, is arguably the cheaper fix than redesigning every cab — regulation of design is slow; regulation of tech (like FMVSS 127 for light vehicles) may be faster. Also: 2025 GHSA data showed pedestrian deaths *falling* for the third straight year (6,732), so the 2024 GAO snapshot sits at the top of a curve that may already be bending down.

## Actionable insights
- If you drive anything tall: the front and right-side blind zones are the kill zones. Slow right turns, check twice before pulling away from a stop, and assume pedestrians can't be seen within ~10 feet of the front bumper on many pickups (per IIHS blind-zone measurements).
- If you're a fleet buyer: London's Direct Vision Standard shows what a procurement policy looks like — require direct-vision ratings or camera/monitor retrofits (Class V/VI mirrors, sensors) before buying heavy trucks.
- If you shop passenger vehicles: check the IIHS commercial-vehicle crash-prevention ratings for vans/pickups and pedestrian AEB fitment before buying; DOT's five-star program still doesn't include pedestrian safety tests as of this report.
