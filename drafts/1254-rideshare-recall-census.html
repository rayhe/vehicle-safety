Your Uber Has a 1-in-4 Chance of an Unfixed Recall. In New York, It’s 2 in 5.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
December 18, 2027

The numbers don’t lie, but they do occasionally smirk. CARFAX ran 437,583 taxi and rideshare VINs across 16 major American cities against federal recall records, and 26.67 percent came back with at least one unfixed recall. That beats the roughly one-in-five open-recall rate for the general driving population. Your Uber is statistically worse than your neighbor’s beater.[1][2]

26.67%
of taxi and rideshare vehicles in 16 cities carry at least one unfixed recall

New York medallion cabs top the list at 38.6 percent, then Seattle at 37.5 and Memphis at 30.2. Then the floor drops: Los Angeles 11, Boston 9, Baltimore 4. A Manhattan medallion cab is 4.3 times likelier to carry an open recall than a Boston rideshare car, and the affected NYC cabs average 1.38 open recalls apiece, so plenty carry more than one.[1][3]

Smirk explained: the most-inspected vehicles on the road are the least repaired, and the reason is arithmetic, not apathy. For a retail customer, a recall means a phone call and a two-week wait. For a driver whose vehicle is the paycheck, the same appointment is a direct income hit. In New York it turns structural: a driver can operate only the single vehicle registered to them, and transferring the permit to a replacement takes six to eight weeks, wiping out roughly 15 percent of annual revenue. The car keeps earning while the recall keeps waiting.[2]

A word on what the data does not prove. CARFAX sells recall lookups for a living, so read the marketing accordingly, and the VIN set came from officials and open-data portals with uneven city coverage. An open recall is not the same thing as a dangerous car: as Elena Sierra of the Transportation Alliance put it, recalls range from software updates and labeling corrections to significant mechanical issues. This study counts defects, not crashes. It measures exposure, not bodies.[3]

None of that rescues the number. Fleets that prioritize recalls manage it: Voyager Global Mobility, one of the country’s largest rideshare rental fleets, fixes every safety recall regardless of downtime. Independent drivers absorb the gap, since the economics are a hostage situation for them, alongside dealers a thirty-year industry veteran says are largely not built for commercial customers. Passengers get no recall check in the booking flow, on any platform.[2]

What you do depends on which seat you are in. If you drive for hire: the VIN check at nhtsa.gov/recalls is free, takes thirty seconds, and repairs are free at the dealer. Your passengers would rather not ride in the 26.67 percent. If you ride for hire in New York: the house odds are 38.6 percent, and nobody in the app is checking for you. If you run a fleet: find a dealer that is actually fleet-minded. All that volume, sitting right there, unfixed, on a lift nobody scheduled.[4]
