## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Wabash National's 2027 van trailers have now been recalled three times in five months, and each recall is more kinetic than the last: a missing label, then loose underride-guard bolts, and now the wrong wheel-bearing grease, which can seize a wheel end solid and lock a wheel on a moving trailer. The story is not any one recall (all small, none with injuries); it is the pattern, same product line, same model year, three factory slips in a row.

## Original contribution (this article's novel findings)
1. **The tally:** 920 + 533 + 475 = **1,928** model-year 2027 Wabash van trailers recalled across three campaigns in five months (June → October 2026). Same product line, same model year, three federal filings.
2. **The escalation ladder:** each recall is more kinetic than the last. A missing label confuses a following driver. Loose guard bolts weaken the underride protection that keeps a car from sliding under a trailer. A seized wheel end locks a wheel on a moving trailer. The severity curve points the wrong way.
3. **The letter gap, quantified:** 26V683 was reported Oct 16; letters go out Nov 25. That is 40 days of affected trailers hauling freight before owners are told by mail. Same pattern as the Thomas Built piece (62 days): the federal process moves at mail speed while the trailers move at highway speed.
4. **The "most" problem:** Land Line reports most of the 475 are expected to carry the defect. That is a very different shape from a 2% or 10% recall: the recall population here is close to the defect population. (Exact percentage unverified; see Limitations.)

## Strongest counterargument (at full strength)
Three small recalls with zero reported incidents or injuries can just as honestly be read as a manufacturer catching small problems early and filing promptly. Wabash self-reported all three; the remedies are free and simple (a label, a torque wrench, a grease swap); and the label recall is a paperwork noncompliance, not a part that breaks. Trailer recall populations are small by nature, and 1,928 trailers is a rounding error against national trailer production. If the system is supposed to surface defects before people get hurt, three filings with no injuries is arguably the system working, not failing. The article must carry this reading, not just the indictment.

## Limitations (must state in article)
- The Part 573 report for 26V683 was not independently read; the "most of the population defective" characterization comes from Land Line's reporting, and the exact estimated defect percentage is unconfirmed. Do not state a percentage.
- Production windows and VIN ranges for the 26V683 population are not in the ack letter and are not claimed here.
- 26V391/26V506 details come from NHTSA ack letters only (not their Part 573 reports); incident/injury data for those two is "none stated," not "confirmed zero."
- The three recalls may cover overlapping or distinct VIN sets; the 1,928 tally counts recalled units across campaigns, not necessarily 1,928 distinct trailers. State it as a campaign tally.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own or operate a 2027 Wabash van trailer: check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now; do not wait for the Nov 25 letter. Wabash customer service: 1-765-771-5404.
- A wheel end running hot, dragging, or noisy on a 2027 Wabash van is a park-it-and-call symptom until the grease is verified.
- Buying a used 2027 Wabash van: ask for recall-completion records for all three campaigns (26V391, 26V506, 26V683), not just the latest one.
