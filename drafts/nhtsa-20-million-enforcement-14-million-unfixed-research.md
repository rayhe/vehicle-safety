# Research: NHTSA's $20M Enforcement Grant vs 14M Unfixed Recalled Vehicles

## News Hook (Aug 3, 2026)
- NHTSA announced $20M "Innovative Traffic Safety Enforcement (ITSE)" program
- Funds "cutting-edge traffic safety enforcement innovations" for local law enforcement
- Targets: impaired driving, speeding, distracted driving, seatbelt use
- Includes: predictive analytics, public-private partnerships, specialized training
- Source: NHTSA press release, Aug 3, 2026

## The Contrast
- NHTSA 2025 Annual Recalls Report (published March 2026):
  - 670 recall campaigns in 2025
  - 28+ million vehicles recalled
  - Recall completion rate: 50.3% as of Feb 2026
  - **14 million recalled vehicles remain unrepaired**
  - Software/electronics: 119 campaigns, 8.19M vehicles
  - Powertrain: 87 campaigns, 7.99M vehicles

## Recall Completion Data
- Average completion rate: ~50% (NHTSA) / ~48% (MotorBiscuit citing NHTSA data)
- Takata airbags: 4.8M still unfixed after 12 years
- Large recalls (1M+ vehicles): only 49% completion
- Premium sports cars: 31% completion (worst segment)
- Large SUVs: 33% completion
- Powertrain recalls: 71% completion (best)
- Airbag recalls: 47% completion (worst by component)

## Behavioral vs Vehicle Defect Deaths
- 2025 traffic deaths: ~36,640 (NHTSA)
- Speeding-related: ~10,035 (29%, down 11%)
- Impairment-related: ~20% of fatalities
- Unbelted: ~54 minutes between unbelted deaths
- Combined behavioral factor: ~60-70% of fatalities
- No public estimate of deaths from unfixed recalls specifically

## The Policy Gap
- Federal law prohibits selling NEW cars with open recalls
- No federal law requires used car dealers to fix recalls before sale
- NHTSA can fine manufacturers for late recalls, but has NO mechanism to force vehicle owners to get repairs done
- The $20M enforcement grant: behavioral intervention (police tools)
- $0 allocated to ensuring recalled vehicles actually get fixed

## FARS Cross-Reference
- FARS 2014-2023: ~187,058 deaths across 337 models
- The vehicles most affected by major recalls (Stellantis, Ford, GM, Hyundai/Kia) are the same ones with the highest FARS death counts
- Chevy Silverado: 9,591 deaths + multiple major recalls
- Ford F-150: 9,194 deaths + multiple recalls
- Honda Accord: 7,102 deaths
- These high-volume vehicles have the most exposure to both behavioral AND defect risks
