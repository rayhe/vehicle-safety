# Research: Ford's Quality Renaissance Myth

## Angle
Ford's PR claims "more than 90% of our recalls this year are for older vehicles built between 2013 and 2020" and touts "best Consumer Reports reliability ranking in 15 years" plus "$500M warranty cost reduction." But the actual recall data from THIS WEEK shows massive recalls of 2020+ model year vehicles. The narrative is testable — and it fails.

## Original Contribution
Calculate the model-year breakdown of Ford's July 2026 recall volume to test Ford's "90% older" claim against actual data. Nobody has done this specific cross-tabulation.

## Strongest Counterargument
Ford's PR is technically about recall *campaigns* (number of separate recall notices), not *vehicles recalled*. It's possible that many individual recall campaigns cover older vehicles, but the few large-volume campaigns affect newer vehicles. The 90% claim could be true by campaign count while being misleading by vehicle count. Also, Ford proactively recalls more aggressively than competitors, which could explain the volume. More recalls ≠ more defects — it could mean more aggressive safety culture.

## Limitations
- Cannot independently verify Ford's "90% older" claim without full NHTSA dataset query
- One week of data is a small sample — Ford could have had prior weeks where newer vehicles weren't the bulk
- Warranty cost reduction is a different metric from recall volume
- Consumer Reports and J.D. Power rankings use different methodology from recall analysis
- FARS data (2014-2023) predates the 2021+ Bronco's recall epidemic — can't connect recalls to fatalities yet
