The Level Said Green. The Crash Dummy Failed Anyway.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 3, 2026

A pendulum the size of a thumbnail told 35,951 parents their baby's car seat was reclined correctly. Federal testers strapped a 12-month-old dummy into that exact recline and watched its chest and head scores come back over the limit. Evenflo's recall of its Revolve180 LiteMax NXT infant seats, filed as NHTSA campaign 26C005, turns on a defect with no broken parts, no warning light, and no symptom a parent could spot: a red/green level indicator whose printed mark was never calibrated to the seat's actual crash performance.[1]

35,951
Infant seats recalled because the level indicator certified an installation the test bench fails

Inside the base sits a pendulum mechanism that swings with gravity, plus a printed pad showing red or green to a caregiver leaning over the back seat. Green means the recline is right, but on seats built before April 1, 2025, green at recline position 1 meant the baby sat more upright than the design intended. More upright sounds trivial until you run the test: with the seat rear-facing on its detachable base and lower anchors, the dummy's chest acceleration and head injury scores exceeded what FMVSS 213b allows. Evenflo's own filing states the consequence plainly, that a dynamic test in recline position 1 may yield chest g's and/or HIC scores over the regulatory requirement. Its warning section is shorter still: a caregiver might notice the child looks more upright than expected. Otherwise, the filing says, the condition is not apparent.[1][2]

NHTSA's Office of Vehicle Safety Compliance found it first. On May 26, 2026, the office told Evenflo that its compliance test on a Revolve180 LiteMax NXT, model CS160412519, had exceeded the limits. Evenflo answered on July 3 that its investigation was ongoing but the recline position looked potentially relevant, particularly in light of a change to the recline indicator in March 2025. By July 30, the company had decided a potential noncompliance existed in seats made before that change. Production had already moved on: around March 20, 2025, Evenflo changed the indicator so recline position 1 would no longer read as proper installation on a test bench, with the change fully in place by March 31. Every seat built before that line is in the recall, and every seat built after it is not.[1]

Count the recall two ways and you get two different recalls. August coverage, working from NHTSA's record, put the figure at 22,981 seats. October's Part 573 filing lists 35,951, a gap of 12,970 seats, or 56.4 percent more than parents read about in August. Both numbers describe campaign 26C005. Only the later one is the filed population, and it carries a 100 percent defect estimate, meaning Evenflo considers every recalled indicator mis-calibrated, not a fraction of a batch. Meanwhile the calendar did what calendars do. Interim owner letters went out from September 1, and the September 18 owner notice projected a remedy kit, a new bubble level indicator to cover and replace the original pendulum indicator, for sometime in November 2026, or shortly thereafter. November is here, yet Evenflo's recall page still describes the kit as being finalized, with seats usable in the meantime under interim guidance.[1][2][3][5]

Most of these seats did not arrive in a car-seat box. Travel-system variants account for 22,349 of the 35,951 units, or 62.2 percent, with the single largest variant, a Shyft Intuiti system in Black Shale, contributing 4,426 on its own. A buyer who thinks of the purchase as a stroller system may never register the seat, and Evenflo mails remedy kits to registered owners or owners it can otherwise reach. Unregistered owners, secondhand buyers, and families who received the seat as a hand-me-down sit at the back of that line. Timing sharpens the point. Seats built between December 2024 and March 2025 had already been in use for fourteen to seventeen months when the May 2026 test notice arrived, and a November kit lands nineteen to twenty-three months after manufacture. Infant seats are typically outgrown around twelve months, so a meaningful share of these seats may already be handed down, resold, or sitting in a garage before the fix exists.[1]

Evenflo's rotating seats had a rough summer. A sibling recall, campaign 26C004, pulled 59,661 REO by Revolve360 convertible seats after their headrests shifted position in crash tests. Two rotating-seat recalls in one summer, two different mechanisms, one shared lesson: moving parts add failure modes that fixed seats never offer. None of that makes 26C005 a repeat of 26C004, since the seat, the defect, and the fix are all different. It does make the pattern harder to dismiss as bad luck.[6]

What this analysis does not prove: the filing publishes no actual chest-g or HIC numbers from the May test, only that they exceeded requirements, so this article claims no margin of failure. August's 22,981 and October's 35,951 both appear in the record, and the documents reviewed do not explain the difference, so this article reports both figures with their dates and asserts no mechanism for the change. No injuries have been reported in connection with this defect, which is not the same as proof that no child was hurt, only that none were reported. Finally, the November kit timing is Evenflo's September projection, not a guarantee, and one aggregator's claim that kits are already in the mail is not corroborated by Evenflo's own page, so this article does not repeat it.

Strongest counterargument. This is a bench-test failure with a sticker-grade fix: no injuries, no crashes, a government dummy on a test rig, and a seat that still protects a child installed at the correct angle. Evenflo had already changed the indicator in March 2025, before the May test, which suggests the company was fixing the problem before the government found it. All fair, and all beside the point that matters most, because the defect punishes exactly the careful parent: whoever reads the indicator, sees green, and trusts it is whoever installs the seat wrong. A safety device that certifies its own failure is worse than no indicator at all. Doubt makes a parent double-check, while green makes a parent stop checking.

Check the label before anything else: if you own a Revolve180 LiteMax NXT made before April 1, 2025, find the model number and manufacture date on the base label, in the center of the base on the LockStrong belt-tensioning panel, and match both against Evenflo's recall list. Register the seat at registration.evenflo.com/evenflo/us/carseats, even if you bought it secondhand or as part of a travel system, because registration is how the kit finds you. Until the kit arrives, use Evenflo's interim guidance: install the seat without the rotating base, or use the rotating base at a slightly adjusted recline after calling ParentLink at 1-800-233-5921 for the specific setting, and do not return the seat to the store. If you already paid to fix this condition, reimbursement covers out-of-pocket costs from December 1, 2024 through August 17, 2026, through ParentLink.[2][3]
