## Verified facts (primary sources)

### The fire (The Auto Wire, Oct 15 2026, citing FWFD / Star-Telegram / Dallas Express)
- Friday Oct 9, 2026, ~5:00 PM dispatch to Frank Kent Cadillac, 3500 block of West Loop 820, Fort Worth.
- Crews found one SUV engulfed, fire moving into adjacent SUVs; then determined the vehicles were electric.
- Last unit back in service 10:30 AM Saturday, Oct 10: **17 hours 33 minutes** on scene.
- **6 electric SUVs destroyed, at least 2 others damaged.** No injuries.
- Cause under investigation; FWFD has **not** said which Cadillac models burned. Nothing released by FD or dealership mentions a recall.
- FWFD social post: EVs "burn much hotter than an ordinary vehicle fire"; sealed battery packs "deliberately built not to let water in" - the same seal keeps a fire hose from reaching burning cells.
- Fire blankets used to hold the fire while other vehicles were moved; crews rotated through the night.
- PIO Craig Trojacek: "Outdated tactics on some of these new fires may be the difference between life and death."
- Value at sticker: Cadillac EV lineup runs $49,995 (Optiq) to $127,405 (Escalade IQ); six destroyed = $299,970-$764,430 before options (models unknown, so range only).

### GM recall 26V494 (NHTSA Part 573, RCLRPT-26V494-6339.pdf; filed Jul 30 2026, updated Sep 10 2026)
- Population: **11 vehicles**: 2x 2026 Cadillac Escalade IQ, 3x 2026 Escalade IQL, 4x 2026 Chevrolet Silverado EV, 1x 2026 GMC Sierra EV, 1x 2022 GMC Hummer EV pickup.
- Estimated defect percentage: **0.8%**.
- Defect: high-voltage battery internal module component (sense line assembly fingers) not fully secured; can move and cause internal damage; increased fire risk.
- Cause: **a press machine at GM's Factory Zero misadjusted during scheduled downtime.**
- Chronology: May 17, 2026 - two modules with unsecured sense line fingers spotted at Ramos plant; May 19 containment; May 20 - GM quality manager files via Speak Up for Safety (SUFS) after suspect modules may not be fully contained; June 5 investigation opened; July 23 SFADA approves recall; Sept 8 corrected aftersales info adds one more vehicle (11 total US).
- GM told NHTSA it was **not aware of any field incidents** at filing.
- Remedy: dealer replaces the high-voltage battery. Consumer advisories: **DO NOT DRIVE, PARK OUTSIDE.**
- GM internal campaign number N262562600. Cadillac customer service 1-800-333-4223.
- Related: 25V483 (July 2025) covered 53 Lyriq/Vistiq with loose battery mounting bolts.

### The 50-foot rule (GM emergency response guide + NTSB SR-20/01, adopted Nov 13, 2020)
- GM's emergency response guide for the Escalade IQ: use large amounts of water; ABC dry chemical extinguishers will not put out a battery fire; **expect reignition**; after the fire, store the vehicle ~15 m (50 ft) from other vehicles or otherwise separated.
- NTSB SR-20/01 ("Safety Risks to Emergency Responders from Lithium-Ion Battery Fires in Electric Vehicles"): coined the problem of **stranded energy** - charge left in a damaged battery after flames are out, which can shock responders and restart the fire, with no way to measure it on scene. Documented a Mountain View, CA crash where the battery **reignited at the impound lot five days later** - a lot that also lacked the 50 feet of clearance the manufacturer recommended. Found most automakers' guides lacked vehicle-specific firefighting detail; recommended modeling guides on ISO 17840.
- Therefore: 17.5 hours on scene is not a slow crew; it is the NTSB's documented reality of battery fires. "Knocking down the flames is one job; standing by until a pack stops heating up is another."

### Explicitly NOT claimed
- No established link between 26V494 and the Fort Worth fire. The Auto Wire's own reporting is careful: "Five recalled Cadillacs spread across the country, an unknown mix of models in Fort Worth, and no cause from investigators: on the record as it stands, tying this fire to the recall is a guess."
- Fire origin unknown; may have started in one vehicle and spread, or started elsewhere.

## Supporting angle notes
- Photographic manufacturing records: GM narrowed the suspect window so tightly that only 14 vehicles were candidates and 11 got recalled. The recall is a masterclass in traceability. The Fort Worth fire is a masterclass in what traceability cannot solve: one EV goes up on a dense lot, and density does the rest.
- Dealer lots are the densest parking of EVs in America, and the guidance effectively cannot be followed there. Fire codes regulate gasoline storage at dealers; nothing equivalent exists for EV parking density.
- Escalade IQ battery: 205 kWh pack (newsbharat360). Not essential.

## Actionable takeaways (HARD GATE: must be in article)
- Own a 2026 Escalade IQ/IQL, Silverado EV, Sierra EV, or 2022 Hummer EV pickup: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Recall 26V494 (GM N262562600); fix is a free full battery replacement. Do not sell/trade with an open recall (federal law bars dealers from selling new vehicles with open recalls).
- If you are shopping for an EV from a dealer lot: the recall lookup is free and takes 30 seconds.
- Anyone parking EVs in a garage or fleet lot: the "park outside" advisory class of recall is the one that burns houses. This recall carried it.
- Firefighters/dealers: GM's own guide says ABC dry chemical extinguishers will not put out a battery fire; fire blankets + water + separation are the playbook.
