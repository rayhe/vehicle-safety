# Research Notes: Tire Recall Completion Rate — 12.3%

## Core Angle
NHTSA's 2025 Annual Recalls Report reveals tire recall completion rates at 12.3% (2024), compared to 73.4% for vehicles. That means 87.7% of recalled tires — tires with known, documented defects — are still on the road. The system that works for cars (VIN + DMV registration = NHTSA can find the owner) doesn't exist for tires.

## Kill Test
**Is this genuinely newsworthy?** YES. 
- 6x gap between vehicle and tire recall completion
- Structural reason nobody discusses: no tire-owner registry
- TREAD Act (2000) required tire registration but most consumers never do it
- Direct body count implication: tire failures (tread separation, blowouts) are catastrophic at highway speed
- Novel contribution: cross-referencing completion rate data with the registration gap

## Primary Sources

### 1. NHTSA 2025 Annual Recalls Report
- URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/2026-03/2025-annual-recalls-report.pdf
- Key data:
  - Tire recall completion rate 2024: 12.3%
  - Vehicle recall completion rate 2024: 73.4%
  - Child seat: 57.3%
  - Equipment: 25.4%
  - Tire completion rate history: 8.6% (2021) → 5.3% (2022) → 12.3% (2023) → 12.3% (2024)
  - Note: "vehicles generally see high registration participation with State DMVs. Thus, it is easier to obtain accurate owner information for motor vehicles compared to other vehicle equipment."

### 2. NHTSA Recall Completion Rate Report Update (January 2025)
- URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/2025-01/recall-completion-rates-report-update-01172025.pdf
- Vehicle completion rates by age: new cars ~90%, 8+ years <50%
- Takata effect on overall numbers

### 3. TREAD Act of 2000 (Public Law 106-414)
- Enacted November 1, 2000 after Firestone/Ford Explorer crisis
- Required tire identification numbers (TINs) and registration
- BUT: registration is voluntary — most consumers don't do it
- Dealers are supposed to register at point of sale but compliance is poor
- 271 deaths + 800+ injuries from Firestone tread separations led to the Act

### 4. NHTSA Tire Safety Data
- ~738 tire-related fatalities per year (FARS)
- Tire failure contributes to ~11,000 crashes per year
- Tread separation at highway speed = near-certain rollover for SUVs/trucks

### 5. Beacon Journal Investigation (2024)
- URL: https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/investigations/2024/08/14/cars-trucks-recalls-dangerous-defects-automakers-government/72887500007/
- "In the past 10 years, more than 300 million vehicles have been recalled"
- Vehicle recall completion drops with age: <5yr: 83%, 5-10yr: 60%, 11-15yr: 52%, 16-20yr: 38%

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing NHTSA completion rate data with the tire registration gap to show a structural failure in the recall system that has persisted for 26 years since TREAD Act.

## Limitations
- NHTSA tire completion data may include tires already worn out/discarded
- Some "unrepaired" tires may have been replaced by consumers for other reasons
- Registration compliance data is sparse

## Strongest Counterargument
Tire lifecycle is shorter than vehicles — many recalled tires may have been worn out and discarded before the recall reached them. But NHTSA doesn't track this, so we can't verify it, which is itself the problem.
