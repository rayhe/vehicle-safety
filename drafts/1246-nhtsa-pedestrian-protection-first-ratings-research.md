## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: NHTSA released its FIRST-EVER pedestrian-protection test results for MY2027 vehicles on September 25, 2026 (Friday). For the first time, the federal 5-star program rates what a car does TO a pedestrian — not just what it does for its occupants. 102 models from 10 manufacturers met the new criteria. This is the first consumer-facing data of its kind in the US. Novel angle: the list is PASS-ONLY (no failures published, no scores, no rankings), the passing bar is 60% while Europe demands 70%, and the brands whose full-size pickups kill the most pedestrians (GM, Stellantis) appear nowhere on it.

## Facts
- First federal pedestrian-protection assessment results ever published in the US: released Friday, September 25, 2026.
- 102 MY2027 models from 10 manufacturers met the criteria. Passing brands: Subaru, Toyota, Lexus, Infiniti, Nissan, Mitsubishi, Mazda, Acura, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, Tesla, Ford.
- Tesla: 1 model (Model 3). Ford: 1 model (gas-powered Explorer).
- GM (Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, Cadillac), Stellantis (Ram, Jeep, Chrysler, Dodge), VW, BMW, Mercedes: no model on the published list.
- Passing score: 21.6/36 = 60% — a D-minus by American classroom standards, and below Euro NCAP's 70%.
- The credit is a checkmark, not a star rating, and it expires: valid for MY2026–2027 only; MY2028 brings the new NCAP rating system.
- Trim caveat: not every trim of a listed model meets the criteria.
- This rating measures CRASHWORTHINESS (what happens on impact), not crash avoidance — pedestrian AEB is evaluated separately under the ADAS program (10 MY2027 models selected Oct 15).

## Original calculations / novel contributions
1. **The 60% bar:** 21.600/36.000 = exactly 60%. Euro NCAP's threshold is 70%. NHTSA's inaugural pedestrian bar is 10 percentage points — nearly one-seventh — lower than the European standard. In a US school, 60% is a failing grade.
2. **Pass-only opacity:** NHTSA published 102 passing models with no scores, no rankings, and no published failures. A consumer cannot tell whether a pass is 21.6 or 36.0 — a 67% score gap — or which models were tested and failed.
3. **The pickup paradox:** per GHSA, SUVs and pickups account for >50% of pedestrian fatalities. Not one full-size pickup from GM, Ford, or Ram appears on the list; Ford's sole entry is a unibody crossover. The vehicles deadliest to pedestrians are the category least represented in the inaugural results.
4. **Two-year window:** the checkmark applies only to MY2026–2027; the new NCAP rating system begins MY2028. This inaugural round is effectively a pilot, and its consumer-facing shelf life is two model years.

## Limitations (dedicated section, not hedging)
- Inaugural round only; the test program is brand new and its real-world predictive power is unproven. Europe's experience is suggestive, not proof the US numbers will translate.
- Only models NHTSA selected were assessed; absence from the list may reflect testing scope, not failure. NHTSA published passers, not failures.
- Tests use standardized impactors at fixed lab speeds — they model a limited set of strike geometries, not every real-world scenario (e.g., turning vehicles, multi-impact events).
- The rating covers crashworthiness only: a car can pass the hood-impact test while its pedestrian automatic emergency braking fails to stop — avoidance is rated in a separate program.
- The 60% threshold is a policy judgment, not a physical constant; NHTSA itself notes the two-model-year checkmark window was set partly to avoid setting the bar too high for manufacturers.
- Sources on the exact release date and counts are trade-press reports of the NHTSA release; the NHTSA press release page itself carries the results.

## Strongest counterargument
The industry's case, at full strength: John Bozzella (Auto Innovators) argues NHTSA rates new safety tech too slowly to inform buyers — a binary checkmark on a two-year pilot, published 21 months after the final rule, doesn't fix that. And engineers could reasonably argue the test incentivizes softer front ends with real trade-offs in occupant protection, weight, and aerodynamics; plus the biggest life-saving lever is never hitting the pedestrian at all, which this rating doesn't measure. The counterpoint to the counterpoint: Euro NCAP's longer-running pedestrian program coincided with European automakers redesigning hoods and bumpers without abandoning occupant safety — the trade-off argument has been tested in the real world for years.

## Actionable insight
If you're shopping for a 2027 model: check nhtsa.gov/ratings for the pedestrian-protection checkmark — and check YOUR exact trim, because NHTSA says not all trims of a listed model qualify. A 5-star occupant rating says nothing about what the car does to a person outside it. If your current car predates MY2027, no version of this rating exists for it; the bluntest predictor remains front-end geometry — GHSA's finding that SUVs and pickups cause over half of pedestrian deaths is worth knowing when you parallel-park next to a crosswalk. And if you drive: the tests model a survivable strike, not an avoidable one — the AEB/PAEB rating (separate, 10 MY2027 models) is the one that measures whether the car brakes before the test ever matters.
