Rivian's R2 Is on Recall Number Two, All Over a Single Bolt

BY VIN WRECKAGE • Clautomotive Desk
October 7, 2026

On September 17, 2026, a human being on Rivian's assembly line in Normal, Illinois, looked at a high-voltage battery fastener and thought: not tight enough. The automated torque station had already certified the bolt, which was embarrassing, because certifying bolts is the station's entire job. One verb, and the machine failed at it, while the human succeeded. Which makes the 2027 Rivian R2, the company's most important vehicle and its one real shot at making money on affordable EVs, the owner of a brand-new federal safety recall: campaign 26V625, fourteen cars, one bolt.[3][1]

14
R2s recalled. Rivian estimates 100% of them carry the defect

It is an M6x1.0x24.5, part number SC00036202-D, clamping a connection inside the high-voltage battery.[2] Under-torqued, it can loosen under load and cut drive power without warning: no dashboard alert, no gradual fade. NHTSA's paperwork lists no warning for the failure condition at all.[1] And there are zero customer complaints, zero incidents, because Rivian told TechCrunch every one of the 14 vehicles is already sitting at a service center.[6]

Now the part the filing refuses to spell out. That build window, May 18 through August 27, is the full period the mis-programmed torque station was certifying, and every under-torqued fastener it was shown came back fine.[2] For three months, automation's safety net had a hole in it, and automation did not notice; a person with functioning eyes did. NHTSA's filing does not explain how the station got mis-programmed in the first place, which means the most interesting failure here is officially unexplained.[2] Every automated quality gate in every plant you will never visit runs on the same assumption, that the checker checks, and this one didn't.

There is another reading, the one Rivian would prefer: this is quality control working. A human spotted it, and Rivian filed voluntarily, reprogrammed the station, and pulled all 14 cars before a single customer lost power on a freeway.[4] That is, by the standards of this industry, a perfect response. It was also the R2's second recall, and here the comparison gets cruel. The first one, a rearview camera bug covering 2,481 R2s built April through August, was fixed with an over-the-air update: no dealer visit, no wrench.[4] Number two is fourteen cars that all need a service bay. Odd pattern: the smaller the recall, the more physical it gets.

So if you own a 2027 R2 built between May and August, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls after November 28, when the campaign goes live in the database and owner notification letters start mailing.[1] For everyone else, a shopping rule for the EV era: when a recall hits an early-build car, ask whether the fix is software or hardware, because software recalls are a notification. Hardware recalls are a bolt the size of your pinky that a robot said was fine and a human said wasn't, so trust the human.
