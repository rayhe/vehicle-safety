The Recall That Recalled the Recall Just Got Recalled

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 11, 2026

Before you sign that lease, you might want to see this. Stellantis is recalling 328,381 Jeep Grand Cherokees because the rear coil springs can detach while the vehicle is moving.[1] Same springs. Third recall. This new campaign replaces the two earlier ones, including one whose entire reason for existing was that dealers botched the fix the first time around.[2] Run the numbers and NHTSA has now issued 754,001 recall notices for a single component on what is essentially a single fleet of Jeeps.

754,001
Cumulative recall notices issued across three campaigns for one rear coil spring

June 2023. Recall 23V413: roughly 345,000 Grand Cherokees, rear coil springs installed wrong at the factory, spring could walk out of its seat and detach.[2] February 2026. Recall 26V051: 80,620 vehicles, 29,139 standard Grand Cherokees and 51,481 three-row L models, because the dealer remedy for 23V413 had itself been performed wrong, with some springs installed incorrectly or left in an incomplete repair state.[2] Jeep’s own filing counted 20 customer assistance records and 284 warranty claims tied to the botched repairs.[2] September 2026. Campaign 89D (NHTSA 26V562): 328,381 vehicles, the 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee, the 2022-2023 4xe, and the 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee L.[1][4] The remedy this time is new lower spring isolators, and every Jeep already repaired under either previous campaign has to come back.[1][5] Three years. Three recalls. One spring. Nobody at Stellantis will say it out loud, so I will: the recall system had to recall the recall’s recall.

In fairness, the filings report zero accidents and zero injuries across all three campaigns,[1] so this is a story about luck, not a body count, and FARS cannot attribute a single fatality to this defect. It is also, irritatingly, the system working: NHTSA caught a bad dealer remedy through warranty data and forced a do-over instead of burying it. But note who bears the risk when a spring lets go at 70 mph: the driver behind the Jeep, who never signed anything. “Potentially affected” is not “actually defective,” and the true failure rate is unknown, which is exactly why the third campaign covers nearly the entire original fleet instead of just the 80,620 from round two. FARS only captures fatal crashes, so a defect that throws debris onto a following car can hide in the statistics until somebody gets unlucky.

If you own one of these Jeeps, even one that was already “fixed” twice, go back. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls; VINs became searchable on September 9, 2026, and interim notification letters mail September 29.[5] Air-suspension models are excluded from the campaign. The repair is free, the dealer replaces the lower isolators, and FCA customer service is at 1-800-853-1403.[1] Third time had better be the charm. There is no fourth act left in this spring’s story.
