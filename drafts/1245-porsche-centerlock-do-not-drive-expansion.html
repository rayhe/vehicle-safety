Porsche Recalled the Wheels in 2024. It Forgot 548 of Them.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 22, 2026

Two years ago, Porsche told the federal government it had found every car with a defective center-lock wheel bolt. Case closed. Then in May of this year, and again in August, center locks fractured in the field. Nobody was hurt. The case was never closed. On October 16, Porsche filed NHTSA campaign 26V682: 548 more 2024 911 and 718 models recalled because their wheels might fall off, all under a Do Not Drive order.[1]

548
Additional 2024 Porsches recalled — the ones the 2024 recall missed

Follow the paper trail. Recall 24V809, filed October 8, 2024, covered 2,251 cars from the same model year for the same defect: center-lock wheel fastening components manufactured out of specification, capable of fracturing, with the wheel separating from the hub at speed. That recall carried a Do Not Drive warning too. The problem was not the remedy. The problem was the roll call. Porsche's original method for identifying which vehicles got the bad parts simply missed some of them.[2]

Finding the missing cars took two years and two field fractures. Porsche went back to its supplier, matched supplier data against production dates and build records, and discovered that components from the same out-of-specification material batch had gone into 548 vehicles the first investigation never flagged. Every one is a 2024 model: 168 Carrera GTS coupes, 94 Turbo S, 73 Cayman GT4 RS, 70 Spyder RS, 50 GT3, 41 GT3 RS, 39 911 S/T, and 13 Turbo.[3]

These are not commuter cars. Each affected model carries a base price well into six figures, and center-lock wheels exist for one reason: motorsport aesthetics and fast pit stops. A single fractured bolt concentrates the entire wheel's clamping force on one point of failure. When it lets go, per Porsche's own reporting, you may feel a vibration in the steering wheel first. After that comes the part where a wheel leaves a 640-horsepower car at highway speed.[2]

Now the part Porsche doesn't advertise: the calendar. The recall was filed October 16. Dealers were notified October 20, and VINs became searchable on NHTSA's site the same day. But owner notification letters aren't expected until December 15, 2026. That's roughly two months during which the owners of some of the most expensive cars Porsche sells are told to keep them parked, waiting on a remedy notice that arrives like a Christmas card.[3]

What this means for you

If you own a 2024 718 Cayman GT4 RS, 718 Spyder RS, 911 Turbo, 911 Turbo S, 911 GT3, 911 GT3 RS, 911 S/T, or 911 Carrera GTS with center-lock wheels: stop driving it and check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now. The VIN search has been live since October 20. Call Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243. Dealers will inspect the center-lock fastening system and replace any out-of-specification parts free of charge. If you feel any unusual vibration through the steering wheel, do not drive the car another mile. Porsche is notifying owners by phone, email, and the MyPorsche app over the next one to two weeks, but given the December 15 remedy date, plan on your car sitting until then.

Two years. Two field fractures. Five hundred forty-eight cars found by doing the traceability homework the first time should have done. The bolts were bad in 2024. The counting was bad too.
