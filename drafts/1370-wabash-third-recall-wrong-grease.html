Wabash's Brand-New Trailers Have Been Recalled Three Times. The Third One Locks a Wheel.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
November 2, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better. Wabash National has recalled its 2027 van trailers three times in five months, and the newest filing covers 475 trailers whose wheel bearings were packed with the wrong grease. Wrong grease in a wheel end does not cause a rattle or a warning light. It causes the bearing to seize, the wheel end to lock, and a loaded trailer to stop behaving like a trailer. Federal filings put it plainly: wheel lock-up can cost a driver handling and control, which is the polite way of saying everyone nearby is now part of the problem.[1]

1,928
Model-year 2027 Wabash van trailers recalled across three campaigns in five months (June to October 2026)

June brought the first one, a recall of 920 trailers because bumper tube bolts were improperly tightened, which weakened the rear impact guard that keeps a car from sliding under a trailer in a rear-end crash. August brought the second: 533 trailers whose external ABS malfunction light was missing its label, a paperwork violation of FMVSS 121 that confuses the driver behind you. October brought the third, and it is the one that actually stops a wheel. Add them up and you get 1,928 trailers across three campaigns, same product line, same model year, five months.[4][5]

Rank the three by what they do to a highway and the curve points the wrong way. A missing label might confuse a following driver. Loose guard bolts weaken the last piece of steel between a sedan and the back of a trailer. A seized wheel end locks a wheel outright, and Land Line reports most of the 475 trailers in the newest recall are expected to carry the defect, which makes this less of a lottery ticket and more of a batch problem, a figure that matters because a recall population this close to the defect population leaves almost no room for the usual comfort that only a fraction of the recalled units are actually affected. Wabash reported the defect on October 16, while owner letters do not go out until November 25. Forty days of affected trailers hauling freight while the notification travels by mail.[2]

Fairness requires the other reading, and it is a strong one. Nobody has been hurt. Land Line reports no incidents or injuries tied to the grease recall, and the earlier two filings state no casualty claims either. Wabash found these problems itself, filed all three, and will fix all three free: a label in the mail, a torque wrench on the guard bolts, a grease swap at the dealer. Three small recalls with zero injuries can be read as quality control working early rather than failing late, and a reader who lands there is not wrong. What that reading cannot explain is the direction of travel. Each recall reaches further into the physics of a crash than the one before it.

Owners and fleet managers running 2027 Wabash vans should run every unit through nhtsa.gov/recalls today, not when the November 25 letter arrives. Campaign 26V683 is the grease recall; 26V391 is the guard bolts; 26V506 is the label. A wheel end running hot, dragging, or noisy on one of these trailers is a park-it-and-call symptom until the grease is verified, and Wabash customer service takes the call at 1-765-771-5404. Buying a used 2027 Wabash van? Ask for completion records on all three campaigns, not just the latest. One recall is a bad day at the plant. Three, in five months, on one model year, from one manufacturer whose trailers haul freight behind every sedan on the interstate, is a pattern worth pricing into the next purchase.[1][3]
