## Key verified facts
- **The streak:** last deathless day on Texas roads was **November 7, 2000**. As of November 16, 2026, that is **9,505 days** — 26 years and 9 days — without one day in which nobody died in a Texas traffic crash.
- **The body count:** more than **95,000 people** killed on Texas roads since Nov 7, 2000 (TxDOT). 95,000 / 9,505 days = **9.99 deaths per day, every day, for 26 years**. Call it ten a day.
- **The best year:** 2025 recorded **3,763 Texas traffic deaths, down 9.6% from 4,162 in 2024** (TxDOT). "Down nearly 10%" in TxDOT's words. It is the lowest annual total in the streak's recent history and the sharpest single-year improvement Texas has posted in the post-pandemic decline.
- **Texas's share:** 3,763 / 36,640 US deaths in 2025 = **10.3% of all American road deaths**, from a state with roughly 9% of the US population.
- **The stall, already here:** NHTSA's H1 2026 projection puts Texas at **1,851 deaths, down 0.3% from 1,856 in H1 2025** — statistically flat. The 10% annual improvement evaporated within six months. Texas's H1 2026 fatality rate is **1.19 per 100M VMT vs 1.02 nationally — 16.7% higher per mile**.
- **The biggest killer, by count:** Texas had the **largest state death total in H1 2026** (1,851), ahead of California (1,825) and Florida (1,439).
- **Where the deaths concentrate:** Harris County alone killed **517 people in 2025 — 13.7% of the state total** — and leads Texas in DWI fatalities (KPRC/TxDOT). Rural Texas holds 22% of crashes but 45% of deaths; 27 of 254 counties recorded zero deaths (#1439).
- **What Texas is spending:** **$16 billion** in safety improvements through the state's 10-year plan — cable barriers, rumble strips, roundabouts (TxDOT: roundabouts "can reduce fatalities at intersections by 90%"), AI automatic incident detection.
- **What Texas is doing this month:** DPS troopers are distributing safe-driving tips during traffic stops for two weeks from Nov 7 — buckle up, avoid distractions, drive safe speeds, get a sober ride, move over or slow down.
- **Side stat (secondary source, use with attribution):** 11.87% of Texas drivers were uninsured as of September 2025 (Texas DMV, TexasSure page, via Argento release).

## Original contribution (Vin's math and synthesis)
1. **Ten a day, every day, for 26 years.** The streak arithmetic is the story: 95,000+ deaths over 9,505 days is 9.99 per day. Texas has not merely had a bad run — it has killed the equivalent of a small town's worth of people every single year (3,763 in 2025 alone is a town of ~3,800, erased annually) without one calendar day off.
2. **The improvement and the stall are the same data, six months apart.** 2025's -9.6% was Texas's best year of the streak. H1 2026's -0.3% is the stall arriving on schedule — the same deceleration the national numbers show (Q1 -5.0%, Q2 -0.2% in DOT HS 813 875; #1494 covered the national version). Texas's best year and its flat half-year are not contradictory; they are the before and after of a trend running out of road.
3. **The rate gap, not the count gap.** Texas kills the most people in absolute terms (1,851 in H1 2026) partly because it is big. The honest indictment is the rate: 1.19 vs 1.02 nationally, 16.7% more death per mile driven. If Texas drove at the national rate in H1 2026, roughly 260 fewer Texans would have died (1,851 x (1 - 1.02/1.19) = ~265). Label the arithmetic plainly: it assumes the rate gap is fully closable, which it is not.
4. **The county inversion inside the streak.** Harris County holds 20.1% of Texas crashes but 13.6% of its deaths; rural Texas holds 22% of crashes and 45% of deaths (#1439). The streak is not a Houston problem or a Dallas problem. It is a rural-roads problem wearing a big-city death toll: the counties that crash the least kill the most per crash.
5. **The campaign's own math.** DPS tips and a hashtag are TxDOT's ask for the next two weeks; $16 billion is its ask for the next ten years. The streak will end on a day when the daily death count hits zero — a Poisson event with a mean of ~10 deaths/day has a probability of roughly e^-10, about 1 in 22,000. Even at the improved 2025 pace (~10.3/day), a deathless day is a statistical near-impossibility without a structural break, not a behavioral nudge. Say so: the streak ends when the system changes, not when the hashtag trends.

## Strongest counterargument
The streak is a denominator artifact dressed as a curse. Texas is the second-largest state by population and land area, with 80,000 miles of state-maintained road and some of the highest speed limits in the country (85 mph on some rural highways). With ~10 deaths a day as the mean, the chance of any given day being deathless is effectively zero — the streak is what the math guarantees, not evidence of exceptional Texan recklessness. The 2025 decline (-9.6%) beat the national decline (-6.7%), the $16 billion investment is real money aimed at the actual killers (rural high-speed roads, intersections), and Harris County's 517 deaths are 13.7% of the state total from a county holding ~17% of the state's population — Houston is, by this measure, under-represented in deaths. A fair reading: Texas is improving faster than the country, from a worse starting point, and the streak is the wrong metric for a state this large. The article must grant all of this — then answer with the rate: 1.19 vs 1.02 is not a denominator artifact, it is 17% more death per mile, and it has not closed.

## Limitations
- TxDOT's 3,763 (Nov 5 announcement) and the CRIS extraction used by #1439 (3,814) disagree by 51 deaths — different extractions/vintages of the same crash database. Use 3,763 (TxDOT's own announced figure) and disclose the 3,814 CRIS figure. A separate law-firm release description cites 3,809 for 2025 — a third figure, likely a different vintage; do not use it as the headline, note the disagreement exists.
- NHTSA H1 2026 numbers are statistical projections, not final FARS counts; the 2025 FARS Annual Reporting File is due later this year and will revise both the 2025 and H1 2026 figures.
- The "1 in 22,000" deathless-day probability assumes deaths follow a Poisson process with a constant daily mean — a simplification. Real daily counts cluster (holidays, weather), so the true probability of a zero day is somewhat higher than the Poisson estimate, but still far below 1%.
- The "~265 fewer deaths" counterfactual assumes the entire Texas-US rate gap is closable; rural road mix, speed limits, and uninsured-driver rates make part of the gap structural.
- The 11.87% uninsured-driver figure comes via a law-firm release citing the Texas DMV TexasSure page, not from TxDOT or NHTSA directly; treat as secondary.
- Harris County's "leads the state in DWI fatalities" is TxDOT via KPRC, not a published table; no exact DWI-fatality count for Harris County was available in the sources checked.

## Actionable insights (hard gate)
- If you drive in Texas: the streak is not an abstraction — it is ten deaths a day, and the two levers that move your personal odds are the same ones TxDOT lists: buckle up and never drive impaired. Harris County leads the state in DWI fatalities; if you are drinking in Houston, the sober ride is not a courtesy, it is the statistic.
- If you drive rural Texas: the roads that kill are the fast ones. #1439's speed ladder: 75-mph roads kill 21.50 per 1,000 crashes vs 3.19 on 30-mph streets. Rural roads posted 70+ mph kill 27.08 per 1,000 crashes — 9.6x urban streets at 35 mph or less. Slow down on the two-lane highway; that is where the streak lives.
- If you are buying a car in Texas: check the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before signing — Texas's 2025 improvement does not extend to unrepaired recalls, and the state's rural crash lethality means a crash here is more likely to kill than the same crash in a city.
- The streak ends on a day nobody dies. That day will not arrive by hashtag. It arrives when the daily mean falls far enough that zero becomes plausible — which means the $16 billion in cable barriers, rumble strips, and roundabouts matters more than the two weeks of DPS tip cards.
