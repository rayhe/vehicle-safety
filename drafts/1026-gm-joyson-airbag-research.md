# Research: #1026 — GM's Third Roof-Rail Airbag Inflator Recall This Year (26V611)

**Date of run:** 2026-09-30 (morning PDT)
**Assigned journalist:** Dale Impactor III
**Article number:** 1026

## Story

General Motors has issued its THIRD recall this year for rupturing roof-rail airbag (RRAB) inflators — this time NHTSA campaign 26V611, covering 1,559 Chevrolet Silverado 1500/Silverado HD and GMC Sierra 1500/Sierra HD trucks from model years 2016 and 2017 (Crew Cab only). Two earlier recalls of the same defect family were addressed under 26V166 and its expansion 26V325 earlier in 2026. This batch — traced by GM to supplier production lot B116B — was missed by the first two recalls.

## The defect (verified)

- Suspect inflators may have TWO manufacturing defects that combine: a small crack in the inflator canister introduced during production, plus a small amount of water left inside after the washing process. Together they allow stress-corrosion cracking to weaken the pressurized canister over time.
- Failure mode: the inflator end cap separates or the sidewall ruptures. Compressed gas escapes rapidly and propels the end cap or other inflator fragments into the cabin. Failure can occur spontaneously — "shortly after the truck began moving" in the one known incident, NOT in a crash.
- Supplier: Joyson Safety Systems, Hermoso, Mexico facility.
- The trigger incident: GM became aware in late July 2026 after a customer reported a left-side airbag rupture in a 2016 Chevrolet Silverado 1500. Inspection found a split inflator sidewall, damage to the headliner and roof. Occupants escaped with ringing in their ears; no other injuries reported.
- Owner notification letters: expected mailed November 9, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA's site since September 24, 2026.
- GM recall number: N262569920. Chevrolet customer service: 1-800-222-1020. GMC: 1-800-462-8782.
- Fix: dealers replace both left- and right-side roof-rail airbag modules free of charge, with parts manufactured outside the suspect production window.

## The Takata-successor angle (verified from primary/corporate sources)

- Joyson Safety Systems is the company born from Takata's bankruptcy: Key Safety Systems (KSS, owned by Ningbo Joyson Electronics of China) acquired substantially all of Takata's global assets out of cross-border bankruptcy in April 2018 for ~$1.6 billion, then rebranded as Joyson Safety Systems, headquartered in Auburn Hills, Michigan. Takata's recall liabilities were explicitly excluded from the deal. (PAG press release, April 11, 2018; Automotive World, June 2026.)
- The rupturing-inflatormetal-fragment failure mode is the SAME one that destroyed Takata: an inflator that turns into a fragmentation device inside the cabin.
- A 2021 proposed class action (88-page complaint) alleged GM and FCA knew Joyson's inflators — made in the SAME Mexico facility as earlier Takata airbags — were contaminated by moisture during manufacturing and could explode spontaneously, spraying metal shrapnel. Named the 2015-2016 Silverado/Sierra roof-rail airbags and Ram side-curtain airbags. The suit argued automakers "knew or should have known" because Joyson is Takata's successor-in-interest. (classaction.org, 2021.)
- In other words: a 2021 lawsuit described this exact defect mechanism in these exact trucks, and five years later GM is on its third recall for it.
