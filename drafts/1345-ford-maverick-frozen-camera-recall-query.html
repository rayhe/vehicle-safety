Ford Fixed the Maverick's Frozen Backup Camera With a Software Update, But Five Owners Say the Update Didn't.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 31, 2026

Your backup camera shows a clear driveway, nobody behind you, so you hit the gas. What the screen never told you: the picture stopped updating three seconds ago, and there is a person where the empty driveway used to be. On October 23, NHTSA opened recall query RQ26004 to ask whether Ford's fix for exactly that failure actually worked, after five Maverick owners reported their cameras still freezing after the fix.[1]

5
Post-fix frozen-camera complaints filed with NHTSA, enough to trigger a federal recall query covering 273,301 trucks

Let's talk about what happens inside the first frozen frame. The Maverick's backup video runs through the Connected Touch Radio, the infotainment module that renders the camera feed on screen. A memory leak in that software starves the image pipeline, and when the pipeline dies, the frame buffer just keeps displaying the last frame it ever received. This is the engineering detail that makes the defect genuinely scary: a dead display goes dark, which at least warns you, but a frozen display shows a confident, plausible, completely wrong picture. A blank screen says "I don't know." A frozen screen says "all clear" while the world behind you moves.[2]

Ford's original recall, 24V684, covered 144,516 Mavericks in September 2024 and blamed that memory leak. But the remedy, a software update to version G2.2 for the Audio Control Module, didn't reach dealers until March 2025, six months later. Now five owners have told NHTSA the freeze survived the update, and the agency's investigators verified several complete failures. Because this query evaluates the effectiveness of the earlier recall, the population is bigger than the original: 273,301 trucks, every 2022 through 2024 Maverick and Maverick Hybrid on the list.[1]

Ford, to its credit, says it cannot reproduce the failure in G2.2. Take that claim seriously, because there is a gaping hole in the five complaints: the update was a dealer service action, not an over-the-air push. Any owner who never brought the truck in, and any dealer who fumbled the paperwork, leaves a truck running the old software with the recall "closed" on paper. Five complaints against 273,000 trucks is not a pattern; it is a question mark, and zero crashes appear in the query file. The honest reading is that this could be a failed remedy, or it could be five trucks that never got the remedy.[3]

What this article did not prove: whether any of the five trucks had confirmed G2.2 installs, whether the memory leak has a second trigger the patch misses, or whether NHTSA will escalate to a full Engineering Analysis. What you should do is unambiguous: if you own a 2022-2024 Maverick, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and look for 24V684, then check that your service paperwork actually shows the ACM software update was performed, not just that the recall was "opened" or "scheduled." If your camera ever freezes, stutters, or shows a frame that looks suspiciously still, file a complaint at NHTSA's Report a Safety Problem page: the five VOQs that opened this query prove the system listens. And in general, treat the backup camera as one input, not the verdict. FMVSS 111 made the camera mandatory, but it never made the camera infallible. Your mirrors still work, and unlike the software, they cannot freeze.[4]
