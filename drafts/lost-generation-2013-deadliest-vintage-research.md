# Research: The Lost Generation — 2013 Model Year Is the Deadliest Vintage on American Roads

## Journalist: Clara Rollover
## Kicker: The Gap
## Slug: lost-generation-2013-deadliest-vintage

## News Hook
Ford VP Josh Levine (July 27, 2026) admitted Ford "still has challenges with vehicles designed in the 2013 to 2020 time frame." But FARS model year data reveals this isn't just a Ford problem — it's America's problem.

## Original FARS Finding
Cross-tabulating FARS model year deaths across ALL 323 tracked vehicles:

| Model Year | Total Deaths | Vehicle Age (in 2025) |
|------------|-------------|----------------------|
| MY 2010    | 6,200       | 15 years             |
| MY 2011    | 5,965       | 14 years             |
| MY 2012    | 6,569       | 13 years             |
| MY 2013    | 6,903       | 12 years             |  ← PEAK
| MY 2014    | 6,668       | 11 years             |
| MY 2015    | 6,655       | 10 years             |

The 2013 model year cohort has 6,903 deaths — the highest of any recent vintage still on the road.

## Why 2013?
- **Pre-AEB era:** The voluntary AEB commitment wasn't signed until 2016. Standard AEB didn't arrive until 2018+ on most models.
- **Pre-IIHS side crash update:** IIHS didn't update the side crash test (heavier barrier) until 2021.
- **No rear-seat occupant test:** IIHS didn't add rear-seat dummy to moderate overlap test until 2024.
- **ESC had just become mandatory** (Sep 2011 for MY 2012) — 2013 was the FIRST full year, and compliance ≠ optimization.
- **Fleet size sweet spot:** 2013 vehicles sold in massive volumes (post-recession rebound) and are now 12 years old — old enough to lack tech, young enough to still be daily drivers.

## The Safety Tech Timeline
- 2008-2011: ESC phase-in
- 2012: ESC mandatory (FMVSS 126)
- 2014: Rear backup cameras proposed (FMVSS 111)
- 2016: AEB voluntary commitment signed (20 OEMs)
- 2018: Backup cameras mandatory; AEB becoming standard on many models
- 2022-2023: IIHS adds rear-seat dummy, updates pedestrian AEB test
- 2029: AEB mandate (FMVSS 127) takes effect

A 2013 model year vehicle has ESC (first year mandatory) and nothing else from this list.

## The Consumer Angle (Clara Rollover's Beat)
If you're driving a 2012-2015 vehicle:
- Check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — these vintages have the highest unfixed recall rates
- Your vehicle almost certainly lacks AEB, lane departure warning, blind spot monitoring
- The safety gap between a 2013 and a 2023 model of the same vehicle is the largest generational gap in automotive history
- IIHS publishes per-model crashworthiness — look up your exact year

## Counterargument (Required)
The decline in deaths for newer model years could partly reflect EXPOSURE — newer vehicles have accumulated fewer total miles over the FARS 10-year window. A 2022 model year vehicle was only on the road for 1-2 years of the FARS 2014-2023 dataset. So the 2013 peak may partly reflect the fact that 2013 vehicles were on the road for the ENTIRE 10-year span.

However: this makes the consumer argument STRONGER, not weaker. The problem isn't that 2013 vehicles are unusually dangerous — it's that they're dangerously old and still everywhere.

## Limitations
- FARS model year death counts are cumulative over the 10-year FARS window (2014-2023), not annual snapshots.
- Fleet size estimates use industry sales data, not actual registration counts.
- VMT estimates introduce ±15% uncertainty for individual model years.
- Can't separate occupant vs. non-occupant deaths by model year.
