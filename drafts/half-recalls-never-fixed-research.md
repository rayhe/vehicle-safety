# Research: Half of All Recalled Vehicles Never Get Fixed

## Angle
The recall system is a consent-based honor system. NHTSA issues the recall. Manufacturers mail the letters. Then nothing happens for roughly half of all vehicles. By February 2026, the completion rate for 2025 recall campaigns stood at 50.3%, leaving an estimated 14 million recalled vehicles unrepaired and on public roads. Tariffs are making replacement parts 5-6% more expensive. Average vehicle age is 11.4 years — squarely in the completion-rate dead zone.

## Self-Critique Gate
**Proposed angle:** The recall completion rate crisis — 14 million unrepaired vehicles on the road, and the system has no enforcement mechanism.
**Challenge:** Is this genuinely surprising? The recall completion problem is known. But the SPECIFIC data — 50.3%, 14 million vehicles, the age-based dropoff curve (83% for new → 44% for 5-10yr → ~29% for 10yr+, with average age at 11.4), combined with 2026 tariff pressure making parts MORE expensive — creates a novel compounding argument nobody has assembled in one place.
**Verdict:** Proceed. The compounding data is original analysis.

## Kill Test
- Is this newsworthy? YES — 14 million recalled vehicles on public roads is a safety crisis hiding in plain sight
- Novel angle? YES — connecting age-based completion decay to average vehicle age, showing most vehicles fall in the worst completion tier. Adding tariff pressure as an accelerant.

## Key Data Points

### NHTSA 2025 Annual Recalls Report (published March 2026)
- 670 total recall campaigns: 447 mandated, 223 voluntary
- Covered more than 28 million vehicles
- Software/electronics: 119 campaigns affecting 8.19 million vehicles
- Powertrain: 87 campaigns affecting 7.99 million vehicles

### Completion Rate Data
- By February 2026: 50.3% completion rate
- Estimated 14 million recalled vehicles still unrepaired
- Explicitly attributed to parts shortages and supply-chain delays

### Age-Based Completion Dropoff (NSC / NHTSA Report to Congress)
- Newer vehicles (<4 years): 83% average completion
- Vehicles 5-10 years old: 44% completion
- Vehicles 10+ years: drops another ~15% (to ~29%)
- Average vehicle age in U.S.: 11.4 years
- This means THE MAJORITY of recalled vehicles are in the tier with worst completion rates

### Tariff Impact (S&P Global Mobility 2026)
- $22.4 billion in added aftermarket parts duties
- 47% of U.S. parts imports originate in Mexico
- 5-6% revenue impact on repair operations
- Making recall parts more expensive to source

### Premium Sports Cars: 31% completion (worst segment)
### Large/Work Vans: 86% completion (best segment)

## Novel Cross-Tabulation
Average vehicle age (11.4 years) × age-based completion curve = most recalled vehicles are in the 10+ year tier where only ~29% ever get fixed. That's not a recall system — it's a notification system that produces paperwork.
