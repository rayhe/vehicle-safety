**Working headline:** "Thanksgiving Morning Is the Fastest Three Hours on American Roads"

## Angle
Thanksgiving has a reputation as the deadliest stretch of the driving year. The federal numbers say otherwise: about 110 deaths a day over the holiday vs 107 a day across all of 2024. What actually changes is the mix, and Allstate's fresh Thanksgiving Driver Report (published Nov 10, 2026, from 44M+ Drivewise trips) found the sharpest behavioral spike in the window everyone assumes is safest: Thanksgiving morning, 6–9 AM, when the share of miles driven at 80 mph or faster rose nearly 200% — roughly triple the normal rate — and stayed more than 100% above normal through lunch. About 1 in 5 Thanksgiving Day trips stacked multiple risky behaviors, and over 90% of those paired speeding with phone handling. The counterintuitive core: empty roads don't calm drivers down. They speed them up.

## Original contributions (must include in article)
- A. The empty-road paradox, stated as arithmetic: Allstate's metric is a SHARE of miles, so emptier roads mechanically raise the high-speed share — the same empty highway that feels safer is the one where the speed share triples. The safety advice people actually follow (leave early, beat traffic) pushes them into the fastest window of the year.
- B. The per-day toll comparison: ~110 deaths/day over Thanksgiving vs 107/day annual average (2024). Thanksgiving is not a body-count outlier; it is a behavior outlier. The holiday's danger is compositional (alcohol share 50% on the day itself vs 30% baseline; speed share 3x in the morning), not volumetric.
- C. The stacking stat as a ratio: 1 in 5 trips multi-risk, and 90%+ of those are speed + phone — so roughly 18% of all Thanksgiving Day trips in the dataset combined speeding with phone handling. (1/5 × 90% ≈ 18%.)

## Strongest counterargument (must state at full strength)
Allstate's data measures behavior, not crashes. Drivewise users are Allstate customers who opted into tracking — younger, phone-carrying, discount-seeking drivers, not a representative sample of American drivers. The 80+ mph share is a share of miles, so part of the "tripling" is mechanical: fewer cars on the road means fewer reasons to slow down, and a mostly empty interstate at 6 AM will show a high speed share even if absolute crash risk is low. NHTSA's own numbers show the holiday is barely deadlier per day than average. It is entirely possible Thanksgiving morning's fast, empty highways are mostly harmless — the speed is real, the danger is inferred, and the report never links the behavior to a single crash. The strongest read of the data is narrower than the headline: people drive fast when the road is empty, which is true every Sunday at dawn, not just Thanksgiving.

## Limitations (must state explicitly)
- Drivewise sample is self-selected Allstate customers; not representative of all drivers. Allstate itself flags this.
- Behavior data, not outcome data: no crash linkage in the report. The speed spike is documented; the death toll from it is not.
- "Nearly 200%" is a share-of-miles change, not an absolute count of speeders; empty roads inflate the share mechanically.
- NHTSA holiday fatality figures stop at 2024 and the 2024 file is an early release subject to revision.
- NSC's 428 is an estimate with a 372–487 confidence interval, not a count.
- The 110/day vs 107/day comparison uses the annual average because NHTSA holiday tables do not break out an ordinary November week (OctaneDaily's own caveat).

## Actionable takeaways
- If you're driving Thanksgiving morning: set cruise control before the highway empties out. The 6–9 AM window is when the speed share triples — the empty road is the trap, not the traffic.
- Phone down before you move. Roughly 18% of Thanksgiving Day trips in Allstate's data combined speeding with phone handling; the two behaviors travel together.
- Wednesday night is the other spike: trips up 40%+ after 9 PM, and half of Thanksgiving Day 2024 deaths involved a drunk driver. If you drink, don't drive; if you drive, don't drink. The belt stat is blunt: 45% of Thanksgiving weekend 2024 occupant deaths were unbelted.
- Before you go: check tire pressure cold (it falls with temperature), check your battery (Monday after Thanksgiving was Allstate's second-busiest roadside day of 2025), and check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
