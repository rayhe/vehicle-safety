# Research: 1341 — IIHS's First Crash-Avoidance Ratings for Work Trucks: Nine of Eleven Flunk Vehicle-to-Vehicle

## Angle
On October 8, 2026, IIHS published its first-ever front crash prevention ratings for cargo vans and Class 3 pickups, and the American work truck bombed its own test. Of 11 vehicles, only the German Mercedes Sprinter 2500 earned Good in vehicle-to-vehicle; the Chevrolet Silverado 3500HD, Ford F-350, and GMC Sierra 3500 all rated Poor at avoiding a passenger car. In one 43-mph trial the F-350 didn't reduce speed at all. The cruelest detail: the motorcycle target was the biggest challenge — several systems "did not issue warnings or slow at all" — and a child dummy "disappeared" from sensors as the tall trucks closed in. This is the sequel to the June 2026 checklist launch (covered in #... the June scorecard piece); that was paperwork, this is performance, and the performance is worse. The novel Mia Crumplezone hook: the vehicles that kill 16% of everyone who dies on American roads are the ones least able to avoid killing them, because the safety standards that would force AEB simply don't apply to them.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? Yes. "IIHS tested work trucks" is a wire story. This story is different: the first performance ratings (not the June checklist) for a class exempt from the mandates that made passenger-car AEB ubiquitous; zero American pickups earned a single Good; the Express/Savana duo ships with NO automatic emergency braking in 2026; and the detection failure maps precisely onto the most vulnerable road users (motorcyclists, children). No prior site coverage of the Oct 8 crash-avoidance ratings — the June piece covered the driver-protection checklist launch only, and "no-airbag-required" (June 17) covered the regulatory exemption as policy, not as test results. VERDICT: PROCEED.

## Key facts
- V2V ratings: Sprinter 2500 Good; BrightDrop 400 Acceptable; Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 + Ram 3500 Marginal; Silverado 3500HD, Sierra 3500, F-350, Express 2500, Savana 2500, Transit T250, ProMaster 2500 Poor.
- Pedestrian ratings: BrightDrop 400 + RCV-Delivery 500 Good; Sprinter 2500, F-350, Ram 3500 Acceptable; Silverado 3500HD, Sierra 3500, Transit T250, ProMaster 2500 Marginal; Express 2500, Savana 2500 Poor.
- Express/Savana (still on sale, model years 2025-27): no AEB at all; optional forward collision warning only.
- Test protocol: FCW + AEB at 31/37/43 mph vs passenger car and motorcycle targets; FCW vs semitrailer; pedestrian tests day/night, adult and child dummies.
- Commercial vehicle assessment program launched June 2026 (driver protection checklist); crash avoidance is the second wave; headlights + rear AEB ratings coming 2026-27; Class 4-6 phased in later.

## Novel calculations
- **The 16% anchor:** 6,215 / 0.16 = ~38,844 implied total U.S. roadway deaths in 2024, consistent with NHTSA's published preliminary FARS estimate (~39,345). The IIHS share claim checks out.
- **The American-pickup shutout:** 4 Class 3 pickups tested, all from Detroit's Big Three (plus Ram) — zero Good ratings in either test. The only V2V Good is German (Sprinter); the two pedestrian Goods are purpose-built electric delivery vans (BrightDrop 400, Rivian RCV-Delivery 500).
- **Nine of eleven substandard in V2V:** 7 poor + 2 marginal = 82% of the tested fleet below acceptable at not hitting the car in front of them.
- **The kinetic mismatch:** IIHS's own words — front crash prevention "especially important for these larger, heavier vans and trucks because of the danger they pose to other road users" — combined with the exemption math: the deadliest vehicles to be near are the least required to avoid you.

## Counterarguments
- These are professional drivers in fleet service; track-test protocols built for passenger cars may penalize tall geometries and different braking dynamics unfairly.
- Tests run unloaded; a loaded work truck with a trailer brakes differently, and AEB tuning trades off against false-positive braking that has real costs at fleet scale.
- Small sample (11 vehicles); protocol performance is not field crash rate — no exposure denominator was published.
- The industry responded to the June checklist (seatbelt reminders improved); the crash-avoidance results give manufacturers a target, which is how IIHS ratings historically work.
- HLDI is now curating commercial insurance loss data — real-world rate comparisons are coming.

## Limitations
- IIHS measured controlled-track protocol performance, not real-world crash outcomes; no crashes-per-mile denominator.
- Vehicles tested unloaded or as incomplete builds; fleet duty cycles differ.
- The motorcycle-detection finding describes the tested scenarios; it does not establish field fatality causation.
- Cannot verify whether the Express/Savana's optional FCW would change its rating if equipped.
