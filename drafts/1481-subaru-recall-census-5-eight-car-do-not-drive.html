Subaru Recalled Its New Cars 5 Times. Eight Cars Got a Do-Not-Drive Order.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

Same method as the Ford, Stellantis, GM, Toyota, Hyundai, Honda, Kia, and Nissan censuses before it: every canonical model name in NHTSA's database, every hybrid and plug-in variant, every spelling the API might hide a campaign under. 44 queries. Ten models. Subaru recalled its 2026 models 5 times, the second-quietest count in the series, and the five campaigns cover the entire absurdity range of the modern recall system in a single company. Forester 3, Crosstrek Hybrid 2, Forester Hybrid 2, Ascent 1, Crosstrek 1, Solterra 1. Outback, Impreza, Legacy, WRX, BRZ, Trailseeker, Uncharted: zero. Five campaigns, five different components, and not one of them fixed over the air.[1]

5
Recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching Subaru's 2026-model-year vehicles. The smallest covers 8 cars and the largest covers 541,237.

Start with the eight cars, because a recall that small tells you something a recall of half a million never will. Campaign 26V685, filed October 19, covers four 2026 Crosstreks and four 2026 Foresters, all built in August, and every one of them got a do-not-drive order. A castle nut on the left-front lower control arm ball joint may never have been tightened to spec. If it lets go, the ball joint separates and the driver loses control. Subaru traced the problem to a supplier, TOA (USA) of Mooresville, Indiana, whose workers had apparently placed finished control arm assemblies in the finished-goods area before the final nut-tightening operation was completed. One field report, dated September 15, of a ball joint that had already separated. Eight cars, identified by VIN, and Subaru is calling those owners directly. Letters go out December 16, which is nearly two months after the filing, and that gap is the one thing about this recall worth complaining about. Eight people were told their cars might lose a wheel, and the mail takes two months.[2]

Now the other end of the scale. Campaign 26V436, filed July 8, covers 541,237 vehicles because the certification label on the door jamb prints the wrong gross axle weight rating. Ascent owners take the biggest share, 384,407 of them across model years 2019 through 2026, plus 77,504 Forester Hybrids, 49,946 Foresters, and 29,380 Crosstrek Hybrids. Nobody's axle snapped, and no part failed anywhere in the count. A driver who loads the truck to the rating printed on the label could overload the rear axle without knowing it, which is a real risk wrapped in the most boring possible failure. Remedy is a corrected overlay label, mailed to owners, with instructions to stick it on themselves. Interim letters went out in late August, and remedy letters arrive in January 2027. NHTSA flagged the error and Subaru recalculated and agreed, which is the polite way of saying the regulator caught a mistake Subaru had been printing on half a million cars for eight years. Nissan did the same thing this year on 168,149 Armadas and QX80s, campaign 26V455, so the wrong-sticker club now has two members.[3][4]

Between the two extremes sit three campaigns that are, frankly, the kind of thing that makes you check your own driveway. Campaign 26V106, filed February 24, covers 69,153 Crosstrek Hybrids and Forester Hybrids whose fuel filler cap gasket cannot seal when a near-full tank heats up in the sun, so fuel leaks out of the filler neck. Subaru estimates 100 percent of them have the defect. Interim advice is park outside, keep the tank half full, and wait for a ten-minute gasket swap. Campaign 26V346, filed May 28, covers 69,663 Foresters whose power moonroof glass may have been improperly bonded to its sliding frame, because supplier Webasto applied the primer wrong, and the panel can detach while driving. Subaru estimates about 2.9 percent actually have the defect, roughly 2,000 cars, and the fix is an inspection and a replacement assembly if needed. Campaign 26V393, filed June 18, covers 4,757 Solterras whose battery control unit software can shut down the electric drive system at speed. Steering and brakes keep working, but power does not. Toyota filed that one, because the Solterra shares Toyota's e-TNGA platform with the bZ and the Lexus RZ, which means the same campaign sits in Toyota's count of 15 and in Subaru's count of 5, and both counts are right.[5][6][7]

Fairness requires saying what 5 gets right. No engine failures, no transmission failures, no airbag failures, and no driver-assistance failures. Outback, WRX, BRZ, Impreza, and Legacy have zero campaigns, and the two brand-new EVs, Trailseeker and Uncharted, have zero in their first year. Subaru caught the ball joint from a single field report and recalled eight cars instead of waiting for a body count. That is how the system is supposed to work. But the counterweight is the sticker: a company that prints the wrong weight rating on the certification label of its largest vehicle, across eight model years, and only fixes it after the regulator flags it, has a quality process problem, not a typo problem. And the count flatters Subaru in one more way: the 2026 Forester actually has four campaigns touching it, not three. Campaign 25V889, a rear hatch support bracket that can detach and drop the tailgate on someone, covers 5,603 Foresters and was filed December 18, 2025, so it falls outside a census that counts campaigns filed in 2026. Both numbers are true: a VIN lookup will show four, while this census counts three filed this year. Read the filing date before you argue about the total.[1][8]

What to do with this: if you own a 2026 Forester or Forester Hybrid, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and book the moonroof inspection first, because a glass panel coming off at highway speed is a hazard to everyone behind you, then check for the label overlay and the ball joint. If you own a 2026 Crosstrek Hybrid, get the fuel-cap gasket replaced, keep the tank at half until then, and park outside. If you own a 2026 Solterra, the battery control unit software update is free at the dealer, and it is not over the air, so you have to go in. If you own a 2019-2026 Ascent, you are probably in the label recall, so compare the gross axle weight rating on your door jamb against the owner's manual and do not overload the rear axle until the corrected sticker arrives in January. If you own one of the eight, Subaru already knows who you are, so do not drive it.
