## Key numbers (all from sources 1-3 unless noted)
- Telematics dataset: 95,000+ drivers, 500 million miles, 135.1 million driver behavior events, 245 fleets, first half of 2026.
- Survey: 740 fleet, broker, and insurance professionals.
- Construction fleets: 254 severe speeding events per 100,000 miles (highest median rate among industries). Transportation & warehousing: 48 per 100,000 miles. Ratio: 254/48 = 5.29x.
- Construction fleets monitor roughly 92% of their vehicles; 86% cited improving safety as a reason for using telematics.
- Speeding represented 73% of all recorded driving events in the dataset.
- Severe speeding defined as traveling at least 21 mph over the posted speed limit or faster than 85 mph.
- Timing: 41% of severe speeding events occurred 6 a.m.–noon; 23% afternoon; 19% evening; 17% overnight. (41% of events in 25% of the day = 1.64x the uniform rate.)
- Telematics adoption saturated: 90% of fleets use it, but only 5% adopted within the past year.
- 53% of fleets say interpreting and acting on the data is now a leading challenge.
- 43% of insurers say value creation is a top challenge — nearly tripled from 16% last year.
- 84% of insurers and brokers now consider distracted driving a major threat to commercial auto profitability, up roughly 10 percentage points in one year — matching litigation and "nuclear verdicts" as the top threat.
- AI as interpretation layer: 60% of fleets and 94% of insurers report some AI use, including analyzing driver risk data and reviewing footage.
- Quote: Kristy Kennah, Head of Insurance at SambaSafety — "Understanding risk isn't just about having more data. It's having the right information to reveal the story behind it."

## Novel contributions (original to this article)
1. **The surveillance-obedience gap:** 92% monitoring + 86% "for safety" + 5.3x worse speeding = the all-seeing eye changed nothing. The gap is action, not data.
2. **Morning concentration math:** 41% of severe speeding in the 6am–noon window (25% of the day) = 1.64x overrepresentation. The deadliest window is the morning commute, not the midnight run.
3. **The AI confession:** 60%/94% AI adoption framed as the industry's admission that humans can't act on their own surveillance data — so they hired software to read it for them.
4. **Distracted driving = nuclear verdicts:** the report ranks driver distraction alongside nine-figure litigation as the top commercial-auto threat — a parity nobody states outright.

## Limitations
- Industry-funded research: SambaSafety sells driver-risk management software; findings align with their product story (telematics good, but you need help interpreting it). Numbers are descriptive, not peer-reviewed.
- "Severe speeding events" are sensor triggers, not crashes — 254/100k miles is an event rate, not a crash rate. The crash linkage is the industry's inference.
- Morning concentration: SambaSafety itself notes greater driving exposure in the morning may contribute; the 1.64x figure is not exposure-adjusted.
- Median rates by industry hide within-industry variance; construction fleets include diverse operations (pickups, dump trucks, heavy equipment haulers).
- Distraction-concern figures (84%) are survey opinions about profitability, not measured crash attribution.

## Actionable insights
- The 6am–noon window concentrates severe speeding from heavy commercial vehicles: leave extra following distance around work trucks and dump trucks in the morning, and don't linger in their blind spots. The industry's own sensors say this is when the fleet is fastest.
- Construction trucks are the statistical outliers: when a work truck tailgates you on the highway, the data says it's not your imagination. Give them room; your car loses the physics argument against 80,000 pounds at 21-over.
- If you're ever in a crash with a commercial vehicle: its telematics almost certainly exists. Ask your insurer or attorney to request preservation of the telematics record immediately — it logs speed, location, and behavior events second by second.
- The same monitoring tech that fleets can't act on can pay you: usage-based insurance programs (telematics discounts) reward the drivers who actually do slow down. If you drive clean, you're leaving money on the table by not enrolling.
