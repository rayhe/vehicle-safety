Two Recalls, One Week, Same Falling Water Tank. The RV Industry Has a Dropping Problem.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 7, 2026

Last week NHTSA published two recalls, numbered one digit apart, for the same defect: the fresh water tank can fall off your travel trailer at highway speed.[1] One covers the 2022-2024 Starcraft Super Lite Maxx. The other covers the 2022-2024 Highland Ridge Range Lite Air. A full 40-gallon fresh water tank weighs over 330 pounds, which makes it the heaviest thing your trailer is allowed to lose without anyone calling it cargo.

4
Documented falling-water-tank recall cycles across the RV industry since 2013

Starcraft's campaign number is 9902669; Highland Ridge's is 9904669, and both list the same customer service line, 1-800-283-8267.[2] The numbers don't lie, but they do occasionally smirk: these are two brands of Jayco, which Thor Industries bought in 2016, filing sister recalls from what is plainly one quality pipeline.[3] The dealership sells you brand diversity while the underbelly is one design.

This is not the industry's first dropped tank; it is the fourth. Monaco RV recalled 4,009 vehicles in 2013 after a single NHTSA complaint described a tank "falling through the underbelly"; the remedy was a cross brace between the mounting brackets.[4] Transport Canada flagged Forest River Viking and Clipper trailers in 2024 for tanks that "could fall from the frame."[5] Then Forest River recalled 1,868 XLR Toy Haulers in late 2025 because mounting brackets "may be improperly located or secured," and the toll across all of it reads the same every time: same tank, same road, same hazard.[6] Three corporate families. Thirteen years.

The 2025 filing contains the detail that ruins the whole genre. Forest River said the tank could detach when full. Water weighs 8.34 pounds per gallon, so a 40-gallon tank goes from empty dead weight to a 330-pound pendulum exactly when the owner does the responsible thing and fills up before the trip, which means the failure mode punishes preparation. Engineers call that a load case; everyone else calls it a trap.

Jayco should have replaced every tank strap on both model lines the day the first fastener failed, because the pattern was already written in three other companies' recall files. Instead the industry keeps discovering, one brand at a time, that hanging hundreds of pounds of water under a trailer with fasteners rated for the brochure weight is a plan with an expiration date.

A note on what this did not prove: the 2026 filings do not report any crashes or injuries, and neither did Monaco's in 2013. Populations for the two new recalls were not published at press time, so this is a failure-mode story, not a body count. The tank capacities used above are illustrative math, not the spec sheets of the Super Lite Maxx or Range Lite Air. And some of these failures may be maintenance as much as design: owners overload tanks, skip inspections, and tow washboard roads that would rattle fillings loose.

Consider the other side at full strength: the US ships hundreds of thousands of RVs a year, so a few thousand recalled tanks across 13 years is a small absolute rate, and every one of these recalls was a manufacturer self-reporting under federal rules before anyone got hurt, which is the system working. The counter to the counter is the recurrence itself: when four separate recall cycles at three companies describe the identical failure, the common denominator is not bad luck at one factory. It is an industry that keeps engineering the same weak joint.

What to do about it: if you own a 2022-2024 Super Lite Maxx or Range Lite Air, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now; the letter can wait, the tank cannot. Until the brackets are inspected, tow with the fresh tank empty or near-empty. Shopping used? Crawl underneath and look at the tank straps and brackets. Rust, cracked welds, or missing hardware are walk-away signals, because the next recall in this genre is already being written and it is just waiting for a fastener.
