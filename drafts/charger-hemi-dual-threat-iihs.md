# Research Notes: Dodge Charger HEMI — IIHS Dual Death Rate Leader

## Angle
The IIHS published its latest real-world death rate study on July 30, 2026, covering 2023-model vehicles and equivalent earlier models using 2021-2024 fatality data. For only the second time ever, IIHS published "other-driver death rates" alongside traditional driver death rates.

The Dodge Charger HEMI 2WD appears on BOTH lists — and no other vehicle does with numbers this high:
- **Driver death rate: 127** per million registered vehicle years (4th highest overall)
- **Other-driver death rate: 161** per million registered vehicle years (2nd highest overall, behind only Ram 3500 at 204)
- **Combined: 288** — the highest total carnage rate of any vehicle in the IIHS study

## Why This Is Novel
- The Ram 3500's 204 other-driver rate already got coverage (our article Aug 5). But its driver rate is near zero — the Ram protects its own.
- The Kia Rio's 170 driver rate is the highest. But its other-driver rate is ~55 (average). It kills itself.
- The Charger HEMI kills everyone on both sides of the crash. There is no safe seat in this collision.
- It is a SEDAN. Not a 7,000-lb truck with legitimate hauling reasons. A 4,500-lb muscle sedan with a 5.7L or 6.4L V8.

The Charger HEMI's driver rate (127) exceeds its own class average (104) by 22%. Its other-driver rate (161) exceeds the class average (107) by 50%.

## Original Contribution
No existing article combines the driver AND other-driver death rates to identify the single most dangerous vehicle to EVERYONE on the road. The Ram 3500 story focused on the externality ratio. This story focuses on the dual-threat anomaly — a vehicle that kills both sides of every crash at extreme rates.

## Cross-reference with FARS
From our FARS data:
- Dodge Charger: 715 deaths, 1,536 crashes → 46.5% kill rate per crash (occupant side)
- But 2.15 crashes-per-death ratio = 53.5% of its fatal crashes kill someone else
- Impairment: 22.7% (above fleet average of ~19%)
- Annual deaths: 71.5

The Charger's FARS data independently validates the IIHS findings: it kills frequently, it kills both sides, and a quarter of its drivers are impaired.

## Limitations
- IIHS rates are adjusted for age and sex but NOT for driving behavior, speed, or annual mileage
- The Charger HEMI attracts aggressive driving — but IIHS President David Harkey says that's the point: the death rate reflects how a vehicle is used in the real world, not just its crashworthiness
- The Charger has been discontinued (last ICE model year 2023). This is a eulogy for a vehicle that killed without discrimination.
- Small sample sizes in some IIHS cells — death rates for individual models can be volatile

## Strongest Counterargument
The Charger HEMI's dual-threat status reflects its driver profile more than its engineering. A 5.7L or 6.4L V8 muscle sedan attracts a specific demographic — young, male, speed-oriented — who crash at higher speeds and in more dangerous scenarios. The vehicle's structure isn't uniquely deficient; it's the human inside it. This is true and worth stating. But it's also the point: NHTSA and IIHS don't issue separate death rates for "if driven responsibly." The death rate IS the vehicle's death rate, because the vehicle IS how it's driven.
