Rain Can Kill the Power Steering on 265,512 Ram ProMaster Vans, and There Is No Fix Yet

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 12, 2026

Five model years of Ram ProMaster work vans have a steering defect that announces itself exactly never. Water enters the electric power steering wiring harness, degrades the wiring, and the assist dies mid-drive with no dashboard warning.[1] Chrysler's recall 92D covers 265,512 vans: the 2022 through 2026 Ram 1500, 2500, and 3500 ProMaster, plus the 2024 to 2026 Ram 3500 Electric. Interim notification letters go out November 17, while the final remedy, per Chrysler's own filing, does not exist yet.[2]

265,512
ProMaster vans recalled; the interim letter promises a remedy that has not been developed

Start with the engineering, because the engineering is where the embarrassment lives. Electric power steering is a fully electronic system, and the whole thing depends on a wiring harness connector that apparently cannot survive weather. Rain. Road splash. The stuff that touches every vehicle ever made. Once the water is inside, the assist fails without the one courtesy every other critical failure gives you, a warning light, which means the first symptom is the steering wheel suddenly fighting you in traffic.[3] Stellantis knows of exactly one linked accident, no injuries, which is fortunate and almost certainly incomplete.

The part that bothers me most is the timeline, and it fits in three words: five model years of vans carrying this connector. Chrysler built this connector into every ProMaster from 2022 through 2026, and the brand-new electric ProMaster, the one on dealer lots right now, carries the same defect, so this is not a legacy problem being cleaned up, it is a current-production problem being papered over. The breakdown is 44,391 Ram 1500s, 147,204 2500s, 72,043 3500s, and 1,874 EVs.[4] Half a decade of vans, and nobody sealed the connector.

Fairness requires the strongest case against my own alarm, so here it is: losing power assist is not losing steering. The column and rack stay mechanically connected, so at highway speed the van steers fine, just heavy. The danger concentrates at parking-lot speeds, where muscling a roughly 4,500-pound van without assist can exceed what some drivers can produce in a panic moment, and even there most drivers will manage, sweatily, to pull over.[1] One known crash in a population of 265,512 is a genuinely thin incident record. This is a design defect with an unknown failure rate, not a proven body count.

What I cannot tell you, because nobody can yet: how many of those 265,512 vans will actually fail, how often assist dies at speed versus at a crawl, or when the real remedy arrives. Chrysler will inspect and replace the harness and the steering gear free of charge once a remedy exists, and until then the official guidance is a letter. In the same week Chrysler also recalled 16,385 2026 Ram 1500s whose tire pressure sensors may fail to warn of low pressure at all, recall 51D, letters October 27.[2] October is apparently sensor failure month in Auburn Hills.

The actionable part is short, and it is this: if you own a 2022 through 2026 ProMaster, or you run a fleet of them, which describes roughly every plumbing, electrical, and Amazon-adjacent contractor in America, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now and do not wait for the November letter.[5] Fleet managers should brief drivers directly: the assist can vanish without any warning, and the driver who expects a light or a chime before the fight starts will discover the fight already started.
