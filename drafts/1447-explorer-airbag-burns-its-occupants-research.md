# Research — #1447: The Airbag That Burns the People It Saves

**Slug:** `1447-explorer-airbag-burns-its-occupants`
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — design analysis beat)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** November 9, 2026

## Angle
The device that is supposed to keep you alive in a side impact is giving people third-degree burns. NHTSA opened Preliminary Evaluation PE26008 on November 6, 2026, into an estimated 1,236,155 Ford Explorers (2021–2026) after seat-mounted side airbags left drivers and front passengers with second- and third-degree burns requiring medical treatment. One officer needed surgery and a skin graft after a UPS truck hit his Police Interceptor. Three of the eight complaints say the bag's exhaust vent points inward, at the occupant, instead of outward toward the door.

- **Novel angle:** Yes. Nobody on the site has covered an airbag that injures by design-adjacent failure: the bag deploys correctly and still burns you. ## Verified facts (3+ primary/secondary sources)

1. **NHTSA Preliminary Evaluation PE26008, opened November 6, 2026** (via Torque News, Nov 9, 2026, which links the ODI resume; corroborated by Detroit Free Press, Autoblog, Reuters, Morningstar/Dow Jones, all Nov 9, 2026). Population: an estimated 1,236,155 Ford Explorer SUVs, model years 2021–2026. Alleged defect, in NHTSA's one sentence: "Deployment of the seat-mounted side airbag may result in serious burn injuries to front seat occupants." Basis: 8 Vehicle Owner Questionnaires + 4 Early Warning Reporting "Death and Injury" reports. Excluding duplicate reports from Ford, NHTSA counts 11 separate incidents, 10 injuries, 0 deaths. Injuries typically diagnosed as second- or third-degree burns requiring medical treatment. Purpose: "to evaluate the scope, frequency, and potential safety-related consequences of the alleged defect." NHTSA aims to finish a PE within eight months. No recall exists for this condition. Ford had not commented publicly as of the morning of November 9.
2. **The vent pattern (Torque News, Nov 9, 2026, reading the eight VOQs):** Three of the eight complaints describe the same arrangement: the bag's exhaust port facing inward, toward the person in the seat, instead of toward the door. A fourth names the exhaust port as the burn source without stating direction. Four of eight point to a vent or exhaust port. NHTSA's summary describes burns and makes no finding on vent direction.
   - Complaint 11602427 (July 2024, Dec 2023 crash, 2023 Explorer ST): "Side seat airbag has exhaust port facing inwards, instead of outward, towards the door of the vehicle, that caused the hot chemical from the airbag to push out all the gas at the left side of my body, melting my clothes and causing chemical burns to my side."
   - Complaint 11766049 (filed Sept 22, 2026; Aug 20, 2026 crash, 2026 Police Interceptor): "The airbag vent faces towards the driver when it deploys. The air escaping the vent burned through the shirt of the officer driving the vehicle and also caused a mixture of 2nd and 3rd degree burns to the hip and side abdomen region."
   - Complaint 11766053 (filed Sept 22, 2026; Sept 2, 2026 crash, 2024 Police Interceptor): vented gas toward the driver; heat melted components on his police utility belt; complainant wrote that without the belt "there is a high probability that the driver would have suffered burns similar to one of our other officers."
   - Complaint 11647596 (Feb 20, 2025 crash, 2022 Police Interceptor hit by a UPS truck): "THE OFFICER'S VEST, SWEATER, UNDERSHIRT, T-SHIRT AND THE OFFICER'S RIGHT SIDE SUFFERED THERMAL BURNS FROM THE EXHAUST PORT. THE OFFICER SUFFERED 3RD DEGREE BURNS AND HAD TO HAVE SURGERY AND SKIN GRAFT."
3. **Police skew (Torque News, Nov 9, 2026):** Three of the eight complaints decode (via NHTSA's VIN decoder) as Explorer Police models with the 3.3-liter engine. The other five decode as three XLTs, one ST, one Timberline (2021–2023). Two of the three police complaints describe duty gear in the path of the gas: a vest in one, a utility belt in the other.
4. **Not all eight agree on mechanism (Torque News):** Complaint 11559767 (passenger, 2023 XLT, T-bone Dec 2023): only curtain airbags inflated to the passenger's knowledge, yet something burned through three layers and left first- and second-degree burns ribs to hip. Complaint 11739351 (May 2026 crash, 2023 Explorer, 40 mph): blames the front passenger-side curtain airbag for a second-degree chemical burn through two layers. Complaint 11685505 (2022 Timberline driver): side airbag burst open and left unreacted propellant on the skin; burn grew to fist-size and ate through skin over following days. Complaint 11668481 (Sept 2024 crash, 2021 XLT): "The airbag burnt a hole in a cotton shirt and then left 2nd and 3 degree burns on my side." NHTSA grouped all eight under the seat-mounted side airbag. Sorting out which bag did what is part of the investigation.
5. **Earlier Explorer airbag recalls fixed fasteners, not burns (Torque News):** 20V497 (Aug 2020, 2020 Explorer/Aviator): side airbag or seat back module attachments may not have been properly tightened. 21V652 (Aug 2021, 2021 Explorer/Aviator): side airbag attachment weld studs could detach during deployment, failing FMVSS 214. Sept 2026: seven 2026 Explorers recalled because the active vent on the passenger frontal airbag may not open as intended (dashboard bag, not the seat bag). None of the Explorer recall summaries NHTSA lists for 2020–2026 mentions burns.
6. **Ford recall context (Autoblog, Nov 9, 2026):** Ford issued 153 recalls covering almost 13 million vehicles in 2025, breaking GM's 2014 record of 77. In 2026, 85 recalls published as of Nov 9, covering over 14 million Ford vehicles. The Explorer was the most-recalled 2026-model-year passenger vehicle in America at 14 campaigns (Crash Report #1430, NHTSA API pull Nov 7–8, 2026).

## Original contribution: original math + FARS cross-check

All calculations from figures above and repo `fars_output.js` (FARS_BY_MODEL, 2014–2023):

- **Incident rate:** 11 incidents / 1,236,155 vehicles = **0.89 incidents per 100,000 vehicles** in the probe population.
- **Burn conversion:** 10 of 11 incidents (90.9%) produced second- or third-degree burns requiring medical treatment. When this bag burns someone, it burns them badly.
- **Vent share:** 3 of 8 complaints (37.5%) describe the vent facing inward; 4 of 8 (50%) point to a vent or exhaust port at all.
- **Police share:** 3 of 8 complaints (37.5%) are Police Interceptors.
- **FARS Explorer baseline:** Ford Explorer, 2014–2023: 3,797 deaths in 6,626 fatal crashes, fatality rate 1.54 deaths per 100M VMT. Ranked **#9 of 337 models by total deaths**. Rate vs. SUV median (0.35): **4.4x**. Rate vs. Toyota RAV4 (0.19): **8.1x**. Rate vs. Honda CR-V (0.53): 2.9x. Lethality 0.573 deaths per fatal crash vs. fleet-wide 0.569: essentially average, so the Explorer's problem is frequency, not per-crash deadliness.
- **The pairing:** the SUV that most often ends up in a fatal crash badly enough to need its side airbag is the same SUV whose side airbag is now under federal investigation for burning the occupant it just protected. The safety device and the danger share a nameplate.

## Limitations
- PE26008 is a preliminary evaluation, not a defect finding and not a recall. NHTSA has made no finding on vent direction or on any mechanism.
- Complaint narratives are self-reported; two complainants blamed curtain airbags, one described a torn bag with unreacted propellant. The mechanism is unsettled by design: that is what the PE exists to settle.
- Eight complaints across 1.2 million vehicles is a small numerator. The incident rate (0.89 per 100k) is a floor, not a true rate: burns in crashes are underreported, and NHTSA counts only reports it received.
- FARS 2014–2023 blends Explorer generations (including pre-2021 models outside the probe) and predates the probe population; the 4.4x rate comparison is a nameplate-level signal, not proof about 2021–2026 Explorers specifically.
- Airbags vent hot gas by design. Some burn risk is intrinsic to the technology; the open question is whether this vent's direction or output makes it worse than the industry norm, which NHTSA has not yet answered.

## Strongest counterargument
Eight complaints in 1.2 million vehicles is 0.00065% of the population, as Autoblog computed. Every one of these bags deployed in a real crash, doing the job it was built for, and nobody died. The burns, however severe, arrived in crashes that could have killed the occupants without the bag. NHTSA itself grouped complaints that disagree about which bag burned whom, which reads less like a single defect and more like the known, ugly edge of a technology that inflates with a small explosion inches from your ribs. If the PE closes with no defect finding, this story is a footnote about eight unlucky people, not a scandal. That is the honest read, and it is also why the vent-direction pattern matters: three independent complaints describing the same inward-facing exhaust is the difference between "airbags sometimes burn people" and "this airbag vents at people."

## Actionable insights
- If you own a 2021–2026 Explorer (or drive a Police Interceptor): there is no recall and no repair to schedule. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls anyway; investigation information appears with the vehicle search, and the Explorer already carries 14 other 2026-model-year campaigns.
- If a side airbag deployed and burned you: file a complaint at NHTSA.gov or call the Vehicle Safety Hotline at 888-327-4236. NHTSA says there is no minimum complaint count to open an investigation, and staff may contact complainants for receipts or photos. Photos of the deployed bag, the seat, and the damaged clothing are the evidence a complaint can carry.
- Fleet managers running Police Interceptors: ask your officers whether any deployment left burns, and log it. Two of the three police complaints only surfaced because duty gear (vest, belt) took the heat or the department filed for its people.
