Porsche Recalled Zero 2026 Cars. Its Wheels Were Still Falling Off.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 13, 2026

Porsche recalled zero 2026 cars this year. Not few. Zero. Fifty-nine queries against NHTSA's recalls API, five model lines, twenty-two trim spellings, and not one campaign filed in 2026 touches a 2026 Porsche. Meanwhile, in October, Porsche told 548 owners not to drive their cars because a wheel bolt could fracture and let the wheel come off at speed.[3] Both facts are true. That is the whole story, and it is a story about what a recall count actually counts.

0
Safety recalls filed in 2026 covering a 2026-model-year Porsche, the second zero in the fifteen-brand census

Start with the method, because a zero is only interesting if you can prove it is real and not a search failure. NHTSA's own vehicle database lists five Porsche models for 2026: 911, Cayenne, Macan, Panamera, Taycan. No 718. Production ended, eight cars sold in the third quarter, and the line is gone from the 2026 list entirely.[9] I queried every canonical model name, plus hybrid and electric suffix variants and twenty-two trim spellings, for model year 2026, fifty-nine queries in all. Fifty came back HTTP 400, nine came back 200, and none returned a campaign filed in 2026.[1]

Here is where a zero gets slippery, so pay attention to the error codes. NHTSA's API returns 400 when no recall records exist at all for a model-year, and 200 with rows when records exist. I know this because I ran the same spellings against model year 2025 as controls. "911" for 2025 returns 200 with two real campaigns, "Cayenne" with two, and "Taycan" with four. Seven of ten controls returned 200.[1] Same words, different year, different answer. When "911" returns 400 for 2026, it means the database holds no recall record for any 2026 911, not that my spelling was wrong. Cayenne and Panamera returned 200 for 2026, and both rows on file were filed in 2025. That is a clean zero with receipts, not an absence of evidence dressed up as a finding.

One more control closes the loop. Query a 2024 911 and the API returns four campaigns, including 26V682, the center-lock expansion filed October 16, 2026. The system can see the wheel recall. It just does not touch a 2026 car.[1][3]

Now the table, because a zero means nothing without company. Fifteen brands counted by the same method, same window, same rules: Ford leads with 41 campaigns, and Stellantis follows with 27. GM, Toyota, and Hyundai sit at 15 each. Mercedes filed 8, Nissan 7, BMW 7, Subaru 5, Honda 4, Kia 4, Volkswagen 4, Audi 1. Mazda and Porsche bring up the rear with zero apiece. Fifteen brands, 153 campaigns, an average of 10.2. Porsche is one forty-first of Ford and a seventh of its Volkswagen Group sibling. On paper, Porsche had the quietest real year in the series alongside Mazda.[1]

On paper is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Mazda's zero came with almost no 2026 cars to recall. Porsche's zero comes with 49,038 cars delivered in the United States in nine months, including 11,778 911s, the best-selling sports car in America by a wide margin.[9] A company can sell fifty thousand cars and recall none of the new ones while its owners spend the year dealing with defects that happened to file on the wrong side of a date line. Count the defects that missed the window and the quiet year gets loud fast.

First, the wheels: campaign 26V682, filed October 16, 2026, covers 548 model-year 2024 cars: 911 Turbo S, Turbo, S/T, GT3 RS, GT3, Carrera GTS, 718 Cayman GT4 RS, and 718 Spyder RS. A center-lock wheel bolt may fracture, and a fractured bolt means a wheel can detach, and a detached wheel at speed means loss of control, which is the polite way NHTSA says the car stops being a car. Porsche attached a do-not-drive advisory, the strongest language in the recall system.[3] Two more field fractures, in May and August 2026, with no injuries, reopened an investigation Porsche thought it had closed in October 2024, when the original campaign 24V809 covered 1,851 cars. Supplier records showed unrecorded parts transfers inside the supplier's own facilities, which means defective bolts moved off the books and the original recall boundary was drawn from incomplete paperwork. Matching delivery records to build dates found the extra 548 cars, built June 7 to July 19, 2024. Owner letters go out December 15, and VINs have been searchable since October 20. If you own one of these cars, do not drive it until a dealer inspects the center-lock system. Call Porsche at 1-800-767-7243 and cite campaign ATA1.[3]

Second, the camera. Campaign 25V896, filed December 19, 2025, covers 173,538 cars: 2019 to 2025 Cayennes, 2020 to 2025 911s and Taycans, 2024 to 2025 Panameras. A software error may prevent the rearview camera image from displaying when the car goes into reverse, which fails Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111.[4] It is the largest Porsche recall in recent years by population, and it was filed thirteen days before the census window opened. Twelve days. That is the entire distance between "Porsche recalled zero cars" and "Porsche recalled 173,538 cars." Letters went out in February 2026. Fix is a software update, free. Your 2025 911 was recalled this year in every sense that matters to you as an owner. It was not recalled in any sense that matters to the count.

Third and fourth, the two that actually touch 2026 cars. Campaign 25V634, filed September 22, 2025, covers 2024 to 2026 Cayenne and Panamera models plus the 2025 Panamera E-Hybrid: high-pressure fuel pump screw connections may not have been tightened to specification, which can cause a fuel leak. NHTSA's dealer bulletin lists 107 affected vehicles, a small surgical campaign, and the remedy has been available since October 17, 2025.[5] Campaign 25V415, filed July 15, 2025, covers 2025 to 2026 Panamera models, the 2025 Panamera E-Hybrid, and the 2025 Taycan: a front suspension strut retaining ring can loosen.[6] Both were filed in 2025. Both touch cars sitting in driveways right now with 2026 on the window sticker. Neither counts.

And then there is the one that is not a recall at all. Workshop campaign WTF7, documented June 2, 2026, addresses a battery management system software defect on certain 2025 to 2026 911 models, including GT3 variants: unintended software resets in the battery management system cause intermittent electrical-system warnings. Porsche classified it as a workshop campaign, not a federally mandated safety recall.[8] A 2026 911 defect, handled in 2026, invisible to every recall census by design. Recall counts measure what manufacturers file as recalls. Manufacturers decide what to file as a recall.

Sales put the zero in its proper frame. Porsche delivered 49,038 cars in the US through September, down 14.1 percent. The 911 grew 32.8 percent to 11,778 units while the rest of the lineup sagged. Taycan sold 537 cars in the third quarter, down 65.4 percent. The 718 sold eight. Eight cars in a quarter, against 1,719 a year earlier, because the line stopped taking American orders last year and gas Macan production ended in July.[9] Porsche is becoming a one-sports-car company with an SUV business attached, and the sports car is the one whose wheels were falling off in October. That is not a causal claim. It is a juxtaposition, and juxtapositions are what this desk does.

What to do depends on which Porsche is in your driveway. If you own a 2026 model of any kind, you are in zero counted recalls, and that is real as far as it goes. Check your VIN anyway, because 25V634 and 25V415 both touch 2026 Cayennes and Panameras, and both were filed before the window opened. If you own a 2024 GT3, GT3 RS, Turbo, Turbo S, S/T, Carrera GTS, Cayman GT4 RS, or Spyder RS, check whether you are in 26V682 and do not drive until a dealer clears the center-lock system. If you own a 2019 to 2025 Cayenne, a 2020 to 2025 911 or Taycan, or a 2024 to 2025 Panamera, you are probably in 25V896, and your backup camera may not display. If you own a 2025 to 2026 911 GTS or GT3, ask your dealer about WTF7, because a recall search will not find it. None of this advice changes because the census says zero. The census counts filings, and your car does not care about filings.

Limitations, stated plainly: this census counts campaigns filed January 1 to November 13, 2026, covering 2026-model-year vehicles, so a campaign filed after November 13 would not appear. The API's 400 semantics are inferred from controls, not documented by NHTSA, and the press cross-checks found no 2026-filed Porsche safety recall touching a 2026 car, which bounds that risk. The "718 cayman" and "718 boxster" spellings return 400 for both 2025 and 2026, so any 718 finding rests on the VPIC list (no 2026 718 exists) rather than a clean API query. The 25V896 population is 173,538 in NHTSA's acknowledgment letter and 172,107 in the Lemon Law Group Partners tracker; this article uses the NHTSA figure. The 25V634 population of 107 comes from the dealer bulletin's VIN-list count. The 49,038 sales figure covers all model years, so it is a denominator for scale, not a population. WTF7 is a workshop campaign, not a safety recall, and its population was not stated in the public summary.

Counterargument, at full strength: zero is good news. Porsche's 2026 cars genuinely had no safety recall filed in 2026, and the API proves it three ways. The do-not-drive expansion touched 2024 cars. The camera recall touched 2025 and older. The fuel pump and strut recalls were filed in 2025. A skeptic will say the honest headline is that Porsche built a clean year, 49,038 cars sold and none recalled, and this article manufactures a story out of filing dates. That reading is mostly fair, and this article says so. The counterweight is that the defects that mattered most to Porsche owners in 2026 are not small in the ways that matter: a wheel bolt that can fracture and let a wheel detach, a backup camera that can go blank on 173,538 cars, a fuel leak on the SUVs Porsche is selling now, and a battery management defect on the 2026 911 GTS that Porsche handled outside the recall system entirely. A recall count of zero is good news for Porsche. A wheel coming off at speed is not good news for anyone who owns the car, and the count is the wrong place to look for it.
