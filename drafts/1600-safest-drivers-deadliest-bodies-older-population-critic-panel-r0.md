The counterargument and limitations paragraphs are the most honest stretches. Weaknesses: the headline overstates (see Rigor); the body at 1,015 words is more than double the guide's 300-500, though in line with recent 900-1,600-word practice; the merged lede sentence ("That much is grim but familiar, and what follows is not the part anyone expected, because by every behavioral measure...") is a run-on that shows the rhythm fix's seams. No structural fix required beyond the headline.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 8.6/10
Citations are primary NHTSA documents read directly this run (DOT HS 813 839 full text, DOT HS 813 791, DOT HS 813 829 via prior verified research). Every number pulled from the fact sheet reconciles: 8,019 deaths (20.4% of 39,254), 54.4M licensed older drivers, 15.60 involvement rate, 10% vs 23% BAC, 8% speeding, 70% vs 46% restraint, 15% vs 10% left-side impact, 33% vs 24% intersections, 52% rural, 8,626 deaths involving older drivers, 1,535 pedestrian deaths (+49%), 237 pedalcyclist deaths (+130%). The article's own arithmetic reconciles: 8,019/6,237 = +28.6% (~29%), 8,019/39,254 = 20.4%. Deductions: the headline claims "Record Rates" but the 65+ fatality rate per 100,000 (13.11) is flat against 2015 (13.09) and below its 2021 peak (~13.9) in the fact sheet's own Figure 1; the count is a decade high, the rate is not. Ref-3 (IIHS Fatality Facts older-people detail page) was cited from the secondary summary, not fetched directly this run.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.4/10
Novel contribution is real and checkable: (1) the per-driver vs per-population paradox stated side by side (lowest involvement rate 15.60, higher per-capita death rate 13.11 vs 11.13); (2) the left-turn signature quantified (15% vs 10% left-side impact, 33% vs 24% intersections); (3) the 85+ cliff (17.31 rate, +9.3% in one year); (4) the sex gap persisting into old age (23.48 vs 8.44); (5) the pedestrian/cyclist blind spot (+49% / +160%). Limitations are stated at full strength (ARF not Final File, restraint-use-known caveat, state small denominators, per-mile vs per-driver distinction) and the counterargument (exposure flatters older drivers; per-mile rates rise from 70-74) is given honestly and answered on its own terms. Deductions, both fixable: the headline's "Record Rates" is inaccurate as written (see Legal); the pull-label's "two to three-and-a-half times higher" range for young drivers is not precisely verifiable from the fact sheet's Figure 4 as extracted this run (the young-group totals are ~2.2-2.5x, the 3.5x figure matches the top male subgroup, and the garbled figure text does not let the mapping be assigned with confidence). Both are one-line fixes, not research failures.

The prose is clear enough that this is a polish note, not a gate failure.
