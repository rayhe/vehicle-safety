Ford's Trucks Had a Race Condition. 4.3 Million Trailers Lost the Race.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
August 15, 2026
4,380,609
Ford trucks and SUVs recalled because a software race condition can silence the trailer control module at startup, killing stop lamps, turn signals, and trailer brakes.

In computer science, a race condition is what happens when two processes compete for the same resource and the loser fails silently. In an app, that crashes your spreadsheet. In a Ford F-150 pulling a 10,000-pound boat down I-95 at dusk, it turns your trailer invisible.

NHTSA recall 26V104000, filed February 20, 2026, covers 4,380,609 Ford and Lincoln vehicles because the Integrated Trailer Module can lose its argument with the CAN bus during the startup sequence.[1] Ford's own safety recall report describes the root cause as a "software vulnerability" creating a "race condition" between the ITRM and the CAN bus Standby Control bit. Every module on the bus tries to talk when you turn the key; if the trailer module's voice gets stepped on, it goes quiet, and quiet means no stop lamps, no turn signals, and on vehicles with the high-series module, no trailer brakes at all.[2]

The affected list reads like a census of American driveways: 2021 through 2026 F-150s account for more than 2.3 million units alone, followed by over 1.1 million Super Duty trucks, plus Rangers, Expeditions, Navigators, Mavericks, and E-Transits.[1] Ford estimates roughly 1% of these vehicles actually exhibit the defect, which still puts the exposed population north of 43,000 trucks that might silently drop their trailer's entire lighting and braking system the next time someone turns the ignition.

Ford knew about 407 incidents before filing the recall, and despite that volume reported zero crashes and zero injuries across the entire population.[3] The company discussed the issue with NHTSA during a monthly meeting in December 2025, reopened its investigation in January at the agency's urging, and issued the recall in February. That timeline matters because Ford has been operating under a 2024 NHTSA consent order that installed an independent monitor and required accelerated safety disclosures, and the Center for Auto Safety's executive director Michael Brooks has noted that Ford's recall volume "started creeping up to significant levels right about the time the consent order was in the works," making Ford the U.S. recall leader for three consecutive years.[4] The consent order is doing exactly what it was designed to do: forcing problems into daylight faster than the old system ever managed.

FARS data from 2014 through 2023 logs 26,529 deaths in pickup trucks across the four major nameplates, the highest body count of any vehicle class, though FARS does not isolate towing-related fatalities as a distinct crash type, so the intersection between this specific defect and fatal outcomes remains unmeasured.[5] What is measured: a software bug that can erase your trailer from the perception of every driver behind you. Ford's fix is a free over-the-air update or dealer visit, with owner notification letters mailing since March 17, and if you own any of these trucks and tow anything, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you hitch up.
