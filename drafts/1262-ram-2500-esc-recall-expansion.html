Ram's Stability Control Can Vanish Without Warning. This Is the Second Recall for the Same Defect.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
December 26, 2027

Talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds of a slide, and you are talking about electronic stability control. Sensors read steering angle and yaw, a computer decides the truck is going somewhere the driver did not point it, and brakes bite one wheel to drag it back in line. All of it happens before most drivers finish reaching for the wheel. On certain 2026 Ram 2500 pickups, that whole chain can go dark with no warning at all. NHTSA recall 26V-651, filed October 6, 2026, covers 8,329 trucks whose steering column control modules can cause a loss of electronic stability control, putting them out of compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 126.[1]

0
Warnings before the failure, per the NHTSA record as reported: the ESC light comes on only after the module resets

That zero is the detail that should stop a Ram owner mid-scroll. Most safety defects announce themselves, a light, a noise, a shudder, something to drive to the dealer about. According to the recall record as reported, this one does not. Once the steering column control module resets, the ESC light illuminates in the cluster. Before that reset, nothing. A driver towing through rain on a crowned two-lane could be driving a truck with no stability control and no reason to suspect it.

And this is not the first time Ram has recalled this defect. NHTSA campaign 26V-244, reported April 16, 2026 under FCA recall 36D, covered 6,605 model-year 2026 Ram 2500 pickups for the same steering column module problem, with owner letters mailed May 7, 2026.[2] October's campaign, FCA recall 90D, explicitly expands that earlier recall. Add them together and the population reaches 14,934 trucks, with the second recall larger than the first by 1,724 trucks. A fix that needed expanding within six months is a fix whose scope was wrong the first time, and owners of trucks just outside the April population spent the summer driving on the assumption that they were clear.

14,934
Trucks across both campaigns for the same defect: 6,605 in April (26V-244) plus 8,329 in October (26V-651)

Why this system matters more than most recall subjects comes straight from NHTSA's own research. In the study behind the FMVSS 126 mandate, ESC reduced fatal run-off-road crashes by 36% for passenger cars and 70% for light trucks and vans.[3] A 2025 NHTSA update found a vehicle with ESC is 51.6% less likely on average to be in a single-vehicle, first-event rollover crash than an otherwise identical vehicle without it.[4] ESC saved an estimated 2,202 lives from 2008 to 2010 alone, while the mandate was still phasing in.[5] Stability control is not a convenience feature that makes a truck feel planted. It is, by NHTSA's numbers, the most consequential crash-avoidance system after seat belts, and on these trucks it can be switched off by a module that also handles turn signals and steering-wheel buttons.[6]

Fairness requires the other number, too. TopSpeed reports the filing puts the estimated defect rate at 0.1% and the affected build window at September 13 to November 15, 2025.[7] At 0.1%, roughly 8 of the 8,329 trucks would actually carry a defective module. That sounds reassuring until you ask how an owner is supposed to know whether theirs is one of the eight. They cannot, which is exactly why the recall covers the whole window. No crash, injury, or death count is attached to this defect in the sources found, and this article invents none. Risk here is not a body count. It is a mandated system disappearing silently from a heavy-duty truck built to tow, in the exact driving situations where a slide turns into a rollover.

What to actually do with this

If you own a 2026 Ram 2500, do not wait for the November 24 letter. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, where these VINs have been searchable since October 13, 2026, and call FCA at 1-800-853-1403 with recall 90D if yours is listed. If your ESC light comes on, treat the truck as ESC-less until a dealer replaces the module free of charge: slow down on wet or crowned roads, give a trailer more following distance, and skip the maneuver you would only attempt with stability control underneath you. Shopping a used 2026 Ram 2500? Get the 90D, and the earlier 36D, remedy confirmed in writing before you sign, not just a clean inspection report.
