The Bulletproof Truck Leaks Fuel: 106,000 Land Cruisers Recalled Over Separating Fuel Tube

By Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 29, 2027

Toyota is recalling 106,000 Land Cruisers because a fuel tube near the gas tank can separate, leak gasoline, and turn a $60,000 reliability icon into a fire hazard. The company has received 13 reports of fuel leakage and one report of an actual fire.[1]

Resin, the tube is resin. On the truck that people buy because nothing on it ever breaks.

Understand what changed underneath the badge. The Land Cruiser that returned for the 2024 model year is not the V8 bruiser your uncle drove: it downsized to a 2.4-liter turbocharged four-cylinder paired with an electric motor, makes 326 horsepower and 465 pound-feet of torque, and rates 23 mpg combined against the old V8's 14.[1] That smaller, lighter, more electronics-heavy package is where a resin fuel tube lives, and it is where one came apart.

106,000
2024–2027 Land Cruisers recalled for a separating fuel tube; 13 leak reports and 1 fire on file

This has happened before, on this badge. Agency records show Toyota recalled more than 38,000 Land Cruisers built around 1991 to repair leaking fuel tanks.[1] Thirty-five years later, the same nameplate carries a fuel-containment recall again, which should embarrass everyone who approved the materials spec on the new platform.

Here is what the fatal-crash data says about the old truck, because the new one postdates the numbers. Across 2014–2023, the Land Cruiser logged 343 deaths at a rate of 6.27 per 100 million miles of travel, and its drivers were among the soberest in the dataset: just 8.9 percent showed any impairment.[2] The deaths skew old, with model-year peaks from 2001 through 2008, long before the redesigned 2024 truck existed.[2]

Fairness requires saying what this recall is not. One fire in 106,000 trucks is a rare event, and Toyota identified the problem, filed the recall, and will fix it free by installing a supplemental clamp and replacing the tube where needed.[1] The system is working. But the incident rate was never what sells these trucks. What sells them is the idea that Toyota thought of everything, and a separating fuel tube is that idea failing in public.

Two caveats travel with this story. FARS captures fatal crashes, not fires, and its window ends before the 2024 redesign, so the death data describes the old truck, not this one. And the 13 leak reports are allegations filed with NHTSA, not adjudicated defects. If you own a 2024–2027 Land Cruiser: run your 17-digit VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, watch for the owner letter later this month, and let the dealer install the clamp for free.[3] If you are shopping a used one, a VIN check costs nothing, and this is exactly the kind of defect that shows up on it.
