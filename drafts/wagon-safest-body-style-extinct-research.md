# Research: Wagons Are the Safest Body Style in America. America Killed Them Anyway.

## Primary Sources

### IIHS Driver Death Rate Study — July 30, 2026
- URL: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/driver-death-rates-remain-high-for-small-cars-models-with-powerful-engines
- Uses 2021-2024 FARS fatalities for 2023 model year vehicles
- Includes "other-driver death rate" for only the second time ever

**Key class-level data (deaths per million registration years):**

| Vehicle class | Driver death rate | Other-driver death rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Midsize wagons** | **27** | **27** |
| Small wagons | 25 | 35 |
| Midsize cars | 67 | 65 |
| Small cars | 79 | 56 |
| Large cars | 104 | 107 |
| Minicars | 158 | 55 |
| Small SUVs | 38 | 46 |
| Midsize SUVs | 24 | 46 |
| Large SUVs | 26 | 67 |
| Very large SUVs | 20 | 70 |
| Small pickups | 31 | 55 |
| Large pickups | 29 | 67 |
| Very large pickups | 24 | 110 |
| All sizes & classes | 37 | 53 |

**Critical insight:** Midsize wagons (27/27) are the ONLY class where both rates are below the fleet average (37/53) AND the two rates are approximately equal. Every other class is either:
- Safe for its driver but deadly to others (SUVs, pickups)  
- Deadly to its driver but gentle to others (small cars, minicars)
- Deadly in every direction (large cars)

**The wagon is the unicorn: safe for everyone.**

## Original Contribution
Nobody has combined the IIHS July 30 class-level death rate data to show that wagons are uniquely balanced — the only body style that scores well on BOTH the driver death rate and the other-driver death rate. Every other class makes a tradeoff: SUVs protect occupants but kill others; small cars sacrifice their drivers but spare others. Wagons don't trade. They just don't kill.

The novel cross-tabulation: computing the "aggression ratio" (other-driver death rate / driver death rate) for each class:
- Very large pickups: 110/24 = 4.58x
- Large SUVs: 67/26 = 2.58x
- Large pickups: 67/29 = 2.31x
- Midsize SUVs: 46/24 = 1.92x
- Large cars: 107/104 = 1.03x (equal opportunity killer)
- **Midsize wagons: 27/27 = 1.00x** (perfectly balanced)
- Small cars: 56/79 = 0.71x (self-sacrificing)
- Minicars: 55/158 = 0.35x (extremely self-sacrificing)

## Strongest Counterargument
Wagons attract a specific buyer: typically older, affluent, safety-conscious drivers who log moderate highway miles. The death rate advantage may reflect driver demographics more than vehicle design. IIHS acknowledges this: "Differences in driver behavior most likely also explain why death rates are lower for ordinary cars than for sports cars and muscle cars and lower still for wagons." The wagon's numbers could be a selection effect, not a safety effect.

BUT: midsize wagons share platforms with midsize sedans. The Outback rides on the same Global Platform as the Legacy. Same safety tech, same crash structure, radically different death rates (27 vs 67 for the class). Driver selection can't explain a 60% gap on the same hardware.

## Limitations
- IIHS wagon sample is small (few models on sale = fewer registration years)
- "Wagon" vs "SUV" classification is increasingly blurry (Outback classified as wagon by IIHS but SUV in some databases)
- IIHS rates use registration years, not VMT — wagons driven fewer miles would look safer
- Can't isolate vehicle design from driver behavior from fleet composition
