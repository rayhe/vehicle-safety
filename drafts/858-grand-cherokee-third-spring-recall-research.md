# Research: #858 — The Recall of the Recall of the Recall

Journalist: Clara Rollover | Kicker: Investigation | Date: 2026-09-09

## Angle (1-2 sentences)

Jeep just recalled 328,381 Grand Cherokees for a rear coil spring defect — the THIRD federal recall for the same springs (23V413 → 26V051 → 26V562/89D). This campaign openly admits the prior two campaigns' repairs were insufficient: inspection alone is no longer enough, and every vehicle gets both rear isolators replaced.

## Kill test

- Newsworthy: YES. Dealer notification went out TODAY, Sept 9, 2026; VINs become searchable on nhtsa.gov today; interim owner letters start Sept 29. Announced Sept 6-8, 2026 (Autoblog, USA Today, TFLcar).
- Novel: YES. Nobody has done the three-campaign cascade math: ~754K cumulative recall actions on one spring family, a 39-month path from first recall to actual remedy, and a remedy-escalation ladder (inspect → re-inspect the repair → replace everything). #857 was a cross-manufacturer loose-bolt census; this is the single-defect version, and the consumer angle is different (owners who already "got fixed" TWICE have to come back again).
- Clara-appropriate: direct consumer guidance — check your VIN today, and if you were remedied under the earlier campaigns, that repair is now void. Proceed.

## Timeline (the cascade)

| Campaign | When | Population | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23V413 / FCA 64A | June 2023 | 345,000+ | Inspect springs, reposition if needed |
| 26V051 / FCA 20D | January 2026 | 80,620 | Re-inspect — the 2023 repairs themselves failed. 20 customer assistance records + 284 warranty claims; springs detaching AFTER the first repair |
| 26V562 / FCA 89D | September 2026 (today) | 328,381 | Replace BOTH lower rear coil-spring isolators on EVERY vehicle. "Inspection alone is not enough." Replaces both prior campaigns |

Per-vehicle breakdown (26V562): 97,477 two-row Grand Cherokee (built May 17, 2021–May 31, 2023), 215,433 Grand Cherokee L (built Dec 5, 2020–May 31, 2023), 15,471 4xe PHEV (built Aug 4, 2021–May 31, 2023). Air-suspension vehicles excluded; 2024+ model years unaffected.

## Original computations (the novel contribution)

1. **~754,000 cumulative recall actions** (345K + 80.6K + 328.4K) across three campaigns for one spring assembly. The populations overlap heavily — the 328,381 are mostly re-recalls — which is the point: the same owners keep being recalled.
2. **39 months** from first recall (June 2023) to superseding remedy (Sept 2026).
3. **Remedy-escalation ladder:** inspect → re-inspect the repair → replace everything. Each campaign is an admission the previous one's verification failed. The June 2023 remedy (inspect and reposition) was trusted; by January 2026 FCA admitted repaired vehicles were detaching springs; by September 2026 FCA gave up on inspection entirely.
4. **What a detached coil spring actually does:** it becomes an unguided projectile for other road users. The hazard isn't just the owner's car — it's everyone behind it.

## Thesis

A recall is a promise that a defect gets fixed. When the same spring needs three recalls over 39 months — and the third one voids the first two — the promise itself is the story. Owners who dutifully brought their Jeeps in twice have to bring them in a third time, and this time FCA isn't bothering to inspect: it replaces the parts on sight.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)

Zero crashes and zero injuries are tied to this defect across all three campaigns. FCA proactively expanded the remedy at each step rather than hiding anything — the cascade is arguably the recall system WORKING: complaints surfaced, the company investigated, the remedy got stricter. A spring detaching is primarily a road-hazard problem for other drivers, not a loss-of-control event, so the actual danger per vehicle is low. And air-suspension Jeeps plus all 2024+ models were never affected — most of the 2021–2023 fleet's exposure window is already behind it.

## Limitations

- FARS data cannot isolate spring-detachment incidents; no deaths are attributed to this defect.
- The three campaign populations overlap heavily; ~754K is cumulative actions, NOT unique vehicles.
- The final remedy's availability date is unannounced — interim letters (Sept 29) notify owners of the risk before dealers have the parts fix ready.
- Warranty-claim counts (284) under 26V051 don't cleanly separate pre- and post-repair detachments in the public reporting.

## Actionable insights

- Own a 2021–2023 Grand Cherokee, L, or 4xe with standard coil springs? **Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting today (Sept 9).**
- Already "fixed" under the June 2023 or January 2026 recalls? **That fix is void.** The new campaign replaces both; you go back a third time.
- Expect two letters: an interim risk notice starting Sept 29 and a second letter when the parts remedy is actually available. Don't ignore the first one.
