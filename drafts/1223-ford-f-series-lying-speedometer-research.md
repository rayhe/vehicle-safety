# Research Brief — #1223: Ford's F-Series Speedometers Lie (26V-653)

**News peg:** Ford recall announced October 20, 2026 (today). NHTSA campaign 26V-653 / Ford 26C46.
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (investigations, recalls).
**Slug:** `1223-ford-f-series-lying-speedometer`
**Working title:** "Your Speedometer Is Lying to You. Ford Recalled 102,159 F-Series to Prove It."

## Core facts (verified)

- **Population:** 102,159 vehicles. 2025–2026 F-250/F-350/F-450/F-550/F-600 Super Duty (built Dec 14, 2024 – Apr 17, 2026); 2026 F-150 (built Aug 22, 2025 – May 12, 2026); 2026 F-750 SD (built Nov 21, 2025).
- **Defect:** Analog cluster's tachometer and speedometer may become stuck or move erratically, providing inaccurate readings to the driver. Fails FMVSS No. 101, "Controls and Displays."
- **Remedy:** Dealers replace the instrument cluster modules, free of charge. Interim owner letters mailed Oct 26, 2026; remedy letters once parts available, anticipated December 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov since Oct 7, 2026 (report received Oct 6).
- **Ford is not aware of any accidents or injuries** related to this condition (Ford Authority, Oct 20).
- **Best detail in the filing (USA Today, Oct 20):** "Customers with a redundant digital speedometer function available in the cluster may notice a difference between the 2 speeds displayed." The dashboard argues with itself.
- **Ford recall-year context:** Per #1049 (Rex Driverton, Oct 2 run), Ford had filed 78 recalls in 2026 by Sept 30, passing GM's 2014 single-year record of 77. 26V-653 is roughly Ford's 80th recall of the year. 14,090,586 vehicles recalled YTD as of Sept 30.
- **F-Series context:** Best-selling vehicle line in America for 40+ years; America's best-selling truck. (F-150 + Super Duty.)

## Kill test

- Genuinely newsworthy? YES — breaking today, 102K of America's best-selling vehicle, safety-critical instrument.
- Novel angle? YES — (a) the dashboard argues with itself: analog needle vs redundant digital readout showing two different speeds simultaneously; (b) the enforcement paradox: a lying speedometer makes legal compliance unknowable — you can be ticketed for speeding while driving legally; (c) FMVSS 101 exists precisely so drivers can obey speed limits; this recall is a compliance-with-the-law defect, not a comfort defect; (d) fits Ford's record recall year (~recall #80 of 2026, per #1049's 78-through-Sept-30 census).

## Actionable insights (for article)

1. Check VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — searchable since Oct 7, 2026.
2. If your truck has both analog and digital speed readouts: cross-check them now. A persistent disagreement is the tell.
3. Interim letter arrives ~Oct 26, 2026; remedy letters ~December 2026. The fix is a free instrument-cluster module replacement.
4. Driving a recalled truck before the fix: lean on the digital readout, but know NHTSA doesn't certify which of the two is right.

## Limitations

- Ford reports no accidents/injuries; the crash risk is theoretical per the filing (erroneous speed reading → crash risk).
- Root cause (stepper motor? solder? firmware?) not disclosed in the Part 573 summary available to press. Do not speculate on mechanism.
- Exact per-model production volumes for the build windows not verified; avoid precise affected-rate claims without data.
