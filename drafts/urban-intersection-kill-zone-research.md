# Research: Urban Intersection Kill Zone

## Angle
The 2024 FARS Annual Report File (released July 2026) reveals that 72% of all intersection fatalities in the United States occur in urban areas. Intersections account for 37% of urban traffic deaths — more than any other crash type except roadway departure. Meanwhile, rural roads kill people primarily through roadway departure (63% of rural fatalities), not intersections (20%). America has two distinct traffic death problems that require completely different solutions, and the one killing more people (urban intersections) gets less attention.

## Kill Test
- **Genuinely surprising?** Yes. The "dangerous road" mental image is a dark rural highway, but urban intersections kill 8,518 people/year — more than all rural roadway departure deaths combined (wait, rural roadway departure = 10,034... so no, but 8,518 is still massive).
- **Novel angle after 718 articles?** Only 1 existing intersection article (left-turn geometry). No urban intersection data piece.
- **Data-backed?** NHTSA FARS 2024 ARF, just published July 2026.

## Primary Sources (3+)

### Source 1: NHTSA FARS 2024 Rural/Urban Traffic Fatalities (DOT HS 813 828, July 2026)
- 39,254 total traffic fatalities in 2024
- Rural: 16,006 (41%), Urban: 22,905 (58%), Unknown: 343 (1%)
- **Intersection fatalities:** 11,764 total — 3,152 rural (27%), 8,518 urban (72%), 94 unknown (1%)
- **Roadway departure fatalities:** 18,348 total — 10,034 rural (55%), 8,166 urban (45%)
- Rural = roadway departure problem (63% of rural deaths)
- Urban = intersection problem (37% of urban deaths) + night problem (59% at night)
- Urban fatalities up 30% since 2015 (17,573 → 22,905)
- Urban fatality rate up 25% in a decade (0.81 → 1.01 per 100M VMT)
- URL: https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813828

## Key Data Points for Article

1. **The split:** 72% of intersection deaths are urban. But only 58% of ALL deaths are urban. Intersections are disproportionately an urban problem.
2. **The volume:** 8,518 people died at urban intersections in 2024. That's 23 people per day. More than one per hour.
3. **The comparison:** Rural deaths are dominated by roadway departure (63%). Fix: guardrails, rumble strips, better road geometry. Urban deaths are dominated by intersections (37%) and night crashes (59%). Fix: signal timing, protected lefts, roundabouts, AEB, pedestrian infrastructure. Two completely different engineering problems.
4. **The trend:** Urban fatalities up 30% in a decade. The urban fatality rate climbed 25% from 0.81 to 1.01 per 100M VMT (2015-2024). Cities got denser, more congested, and more dangerous.
5. **The spending mismatch:** Federal highway safety spending has historically favored rural infrastructure (guardrails, rumble strips, wider shoulders) because rural death RATES were higher. But the urban body count now dwarfs rural by 43% (22,905 vs 16,006).
