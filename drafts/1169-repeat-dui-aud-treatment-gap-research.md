# Research — #1169: The Treatment Gap Behind Repeat Drunk Driving

## Angle
Repeat drunk drivers are not primarily a punishment problem. They are an untreated-disease
problem. A new analysis of federal data finds up to 84% of repeat DUI offenders meet DSM-5
criteria for alcohol use disorder, while SAMHSA's own survey data shows roughly 85% of people
who need substance-use treatment never receive it. We built an enforcement machine that arrests
the symptom and a treatment system that ignores the disease.

## Primary sources
1. NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts 2024: Alcohol-Impaired Driving (FARS 2024 ARF),
   rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/91903 — 11,904 alcohol-impaired fatalities (30% of 39,254
   total traffic deaths); 68% (8,097) in crashes with a driver at BAC 0.15+; role split:
   impaired drivers 61%, their passengers 12%, other-vehicle occupants 16%,
   nonoccupants 11%.
2. NHTSA, State Alcohol-Impaired-Driving Estimates 2024, rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92320 —
   testing-rate caveats (8% Mississippi to 86% Montana known BAC results); national
   alcohol-impaired fatality rate 0.36 per 100M VMT.
3. SAMHSA, 2024 NSDUH Key Indicators, samhsa.gov — 27.913 million people aged 12+
   met DSM-5 criteria for past-year alcohol use disorder.
4. SAMHSA, 2025 NSDUH release, samhsa.gov/blog — among people who needed substance-use
   treatment in the past year, ~16% received it; ~85% did not. Only 2.6% of people
   with past-year AUD received FDA-approved alcohol-use-disorder medication (MAUD).
5. NHTSA, Report to Congress: Advanced Impaired Driving Prevention Technology (Feb 2026),
   nhtsa.gov/document/report-congress-advanced-impaired-driving-prevention-technology —
   comprehensive economic cost ≈ $14.1M per traffic fatality (2024 dollars).

## Secondary / attributed sources
6. Siegfried & Jensen analysis of federal traffic, health, and enforcement data (Oct 2026),

8. Becker's Behavioral Health summary of 2024 NSDUH release, beckersbehavioralhealth.com —

## Original calculations (the novel contribution)
- Societal cost of 2024 alcohol-impaired deaths: 11,904 × $14.1M = **$167.8 billion**
  (inputs: NHTSA FARS fatality count × NHTSA comprehensive cost per fatality, both 2024).
- Untreated AUD population: 27.9M × ~85% untreated ≈ **23.7 million untreated** people
  (NSDUH 2024 prevalence × NSDUH 2025 treatment-receipt gap).
- The 8,097 deaths at BAC .15+ come from the severe end of that untreated population.
- Cost contrast: a year of generic naltrexone (an FDA-approved AUD medication) runs
  roughly $500–$1,500; one prevented fatality is worth $14.1M in comprehensive costs.
  The treatment-to-tragedy price ratio is on the order of 10,000-to-1.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)
Treatment is not a silver bullet, and mandating it is not obviously the fix. Many repeat
offenders already cycle through court-ordered programs with poor completion rates.
Ignition interlocks cut reoffense dramatically while installed (the incapacitation story
#1084 told). Utah's .05 law suggests deterrence moves even heavy drinkers (#826). The
84% figure comes from a plaintiff law firm with a financial interest in the
disease-framing. And nothing in the data proves the counterfactual: that treating these
drivers would have prevented those 11,904 deaths. AUD treatment works best on the
willing; coerced treatment outcomes are weaker. The honest position: interlocks plus
treatment, not treatment instead of consequences.

## Limitations
- FARS "alcohol-impaired" means BAC ≥ .08 in any driver or rider, estimated by NHTSA when
  test results are unknown; it does not prove alcohol caused the crash.
- The 84% repeat-offender AUD figure is from a non-peer-reviewed law-firm analysis;
  the underlying federal datasets are sound but the synthesis is theirs.
- NSDUH is self-reported survey data; AUD prevalence is likely undercounted (stigma).
- MAUD 2.6% figure is 2025 NSDUH data; death data is 2024 FARS (year mismatch noted).
- $14.1M comprehensive cost per fatality includes quality-of-life valuations, not just
  cash outlays; do not present it as budget spending.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- If someone you know has repeat DUIs, the data says the odds are ~5 in 6 that this is
  a diagnosable medical condition, not a willpower deficit. SAMHSA's treatment locator
  (findtreatment.gov) and the ask: has a court or clinician ever done an AUD assessment?
- FDA-approved AUD medications exist (naltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram) and reach
  only 2.6% of the AUD population. Readers on these meds' behalf: ask a doctor.
- Policy: states that mandate interlocks for all convicted offenders (#1084) should pair
  the interlock with AUD screening and treatment referral, the way NHTSA's behavioral
  research recommends. Incapacitation plus treatment beats either alone.
- For everyone else: 68% of alcohol deaths involve BAC .15+, nearly double the limit.
  The .08 debate is about the margin; the bodies are at the extreme.
