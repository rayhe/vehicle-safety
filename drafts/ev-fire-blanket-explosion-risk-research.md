# Research: EV Fire Blanket Explosion Risk

## Angle
The safety equipment marketed as the solution to EV garage fires — fire blankets — can create an explosion hazard. UL Research Institutes burned 18 vehicles in controlled experiments and found that flammable gases accumulate under deployed fire blankets and can deflagrate. Nothing halts thermal runaway once it starts. Water is the best tool. And the buried finding: EV fires and ICE fires are virtually identical in growth rate, peak fire size, and duration.

## Primary Sources (3+)

### Source 1: UL Research Institutes / Fire Safety Research Institute
- **Report:** "Full-Scale Electric Vehicle Fire Experiments and Recommendations for Fire Incident Response" (August 2026)
- **Press release:** https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ul-research-institutes-releases-new-evidence-based-report-examining-electric-vehicle-fires-including-tactical-considerations-for-firefighters-302842674.html
- **Key findings:**
  - 18 full-scale vehicle burns (9 free-burn, 9 suppression tests)
  - EV and ICEV fires are similar in fire growth rate, peak fire size, and fire duration
  - EVs released more total energy on average (attributed to greater vehicle mass)
  - Fire blankets: effective for controlling flaming BUT explosion hazard when flammable gases accumulate beneath the blanket
  - Fire blankets: deflagration risk in confined spaces (garages)
  - Fire blankets should NOT be repositioned once deployed
  - Fire blankets are NOT a replacement for water-based suppression
  - No suppression technique halted thermal runaway once started
  - Added suppression agents no more effective than plain water
  - Full PPE/SCBA required throughout, including overhaul — elevated metals, particulate fluoride in smoke, runoff water, turnout gear
  - Six-minute delay before suppression replicates standard North American fire response time
  - "EV Fire Tactical Decision Aid" developed as step-by-step tool

### Source 2: EV vs ICE Fire Rate Data (AutoInsuranceEZ / Swedish MSB / NFPA)
- BEV: ~25 fires per 100,000 vehicles sold
- ICE: ~1,530 fires per 100,000 vehicles sold  
- Hybrid: ~3,475 fires per 100,000 vehicles sold
- Source: Compiled from US Bureau of Transportation Statistics, NTSB, Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB)
- Sweden 2022: 23 fires among ~611,000 EVs (0.004%) vs 3,400 fires among ~4.4M gasoline/diesel (0.08%)
- Poland 2020-2025: 51,142 vehicle fires total — 50,833 ICE, 222 hybrid, 87 EV
- NFPA: "no current evidence to suggest EVs are more likely to be involved in a fire than ICE vehicles"

### Source 3: MOTOR.com Analysis (July 2026)
- URL: https://www.motor.com/2026/07/ev-fires-vs-ice-fires-myth-vs-reality/
- Explains why hybrids lead statistics: carry failure modes of BOTH architectures, aging fuel systems + aging battery packs
- EV battery fires: not more frequent but more aggressive — thermal runaway releases stored electrochemical energy over sustained period
- Gasoline fires: rapid combustion of volatile liquid, peak heat release within minutes
- Battery fires: long burn periods, latent heat areas, reignition risk

### Source 4: NHTSA fire/defect data
- Multiple recalls involving fire risk in 2026: Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator 1.08M (power steering wiring), Ford Bronco (engine wiring harness), Stellantis 700K worldwide
- Fire is consistently top recall category

- Fire blankets can trap flammable gases → deflagration (explosion)

- Water is the most effective and only recommended suppression

- EV fleet is much younger than ICE fleet overall
- Fire blanket deflagration risk depends on confined space conditions
