# Research: GHSA 2025 Pedestrian Deaths, Third Straight Decline

## Slug
1374-ghsa-pedestrian-2025-third-decline

## Kill test
PASS, with a caveat stated honestly. The headline number (6,732 projected pedestrian
deaths in 2025, down 7%) is good news, but good news alone is not a story for this
site. The story is the composition underneath it: deaths are falling while the
conditions that produce them have barely moved. SUVs and pickups still account for
more than half of pedestrian fatalities. More than 1,100 people died on freeways
and interstates, roads not designed for walking. Nearly one in four pedestrian
deaths in 2024 was a hit-and-run, and the distracted-driver share rose from 15.6%
(2019) to 21.7% (2024). A third straight decline that still sits 5% above the
pre-pandemic level is progress with a ceiling, and the ceiling has a shape.
No prior Crash Report article covers the GHSA 2025 preliminary pedestrian data
(filename and content grep checked 2026-11-03; nearest neighbors are #1299 on the
2025 overall total with the cyclist exception, and #1184 on hit-and-run flee rates).

## Key facts (all traced)
- GHSA projects 6,732 pedestrians killed in the U.S. in 2025, down 7% (505 fewer)
  from 2024, the third straight annual decrease and the largest annual reduction
  since the decline began. Rate fell below 2.0 deaths per 100,000 population for
  the first time since before the pandemic. Still 5% above the 2019 level.
- NHTSA early estimate for ALL 2025 traffic deaths: 36,640, down 6.7% from 39,254
  in 2024; fatality rate 1.10 per 100M VMT (second lowest on record, behind 1.08
  in 2014); Q4 2025 was the 15th consecutive quarterly decline since Q2 2022.
  Pedestrian fatalities fell about 8% in 2025 per NHTSA's July 2026 update.
- GHSA's analysis of 2024 FARS data: SUVs and pickups account for more than half
  of pedestrian fatalities. More than 1,100 people (15% of pedestrian deaths)
  died on freeways and interstates. Pedestrians crossing expressways produced
  relatively few crashes (4,170) but the highest fatality rate (56.7%).
- Hit-and-run: drivers who fled killed 1,669 pedestrians in 2024, down 4% from
  2023, but hit-and-runs were 23.8% of all pedestrian fatalities in 2024, up in
  share terms.
- Distraction: 21.7% of 2024 pedestrian fatalities involved a distracted driver,
  up from 15.6% in 2019.
- Economic toll of 2024 pedestrian fatalities: $9.4 billion (productivity,
  medical, legal, congestion, emergency services).
- Caveats: 2025 figures are projections from preliminary state data (Oklahoma
  had a reporting issue and is excluded from some state comparisons); final FARS
  Annual Report File for 2025 arrives early 2027 and will revise counts slightly.

## Angle
Axle McScatter, data roundup: run the decline three ways (count, rate, share)
and show why each one tells a different story. Count is down, rate is below 2.0
for the first time since the pandemic, but the vehicle mix, the freeway deaths,
and the hit-and-run share have not improved at the same pace. Actionable close:
what actually moves pedestrian risk (crossing design, speed, vehicle front-end
height when shopping, walking routes that avoid freeway-adjacent crossings).
