# Research: 1254-rideshare-recall-census

## Angle
The car you summon for a ride has a roughly 1-in-4 chance of driving with an unfixed
federal safety recall — worse than the national average. CARFAX ran ~438,000 taxi and
rideshare VINs across 16 U.S. cities (data reported Oct 16, 2026): 26.67% had at least
one open recall, vs ~20% of vehicles nationally. NYC medallion cabs are worst at 38.6%.
The paradox: the most-regulated, most-inspected vehicles on the road carry the most
unfixed defects, because for a driver, the recall fix costs them income.

## Kill test
Newsworthy? Yes — passenger-facing risk stat nobody has computed for this site, fresh
Oct 16 2026 CARFAX study, city-by-city spread (NYC 38.6% vs Baltimore 4%), and a real
economic mechanism (downtime = lost income; NYC permit transfers take 6-8 weeks).
Novel vs site archive? No queue entry on rideshare recalls. PASS.

## Primary sources (3+)
1. ClaimsJournal, "Report: 1 in 4 Taxis, Rideshare Vehicles Have Unfixed Recalls,"
   2026-10-16. 438,000 VINs from 16 cities; NYC medallion 38.6%, Seattle 37.5%,
   Memphis 30.2%, LA 11%, Boston 9%, Baltimore 4%. VINs from state/city officials via
   public-records requests and open-data portals; checked via Vehicle Research Search
   Service (CARFAX x Alliance for Automotive Innovation joint venture).
   https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/10/16/340664.htm
2. Auto Remarketing commentary, John Possumato (DriveItAway Holdings CEO), ~Oct 2026.
   437,583 vehicles, 26.67% unfixed vs ~20% national. NYC 39% vs Boston 9%. Drivers
   lose income on shop downtime; NYC drivers can't swap to a loaner (permit locked to
   one vehicle; 6-8 weeks to transfer a permit = 15% of annual revenue). Quote from
   Jeremy Moskowitz, EVP, Voyager Global Mobility (one of largest U.S. rideshare
   rental fleets): recalls prioritized but hard in many markets.
   https://www.autoremarketing.com/ar/retail/commentary-the-rideshare-recall-problem-why-some-dealers-are-fleet-minded-and-others-arent/
3. CARFAX press release (via PRNewswire, carried by emphas.is/lifestyle). ~438,000
   VINs; NYC medallion nearly 2-in-5 (38.6%), Seattle 37.5%; LA 11%, Boston 9%
   beat the ~20% national average. Quote: Paul Nadjarian, CARFAX Chief Product
   Officer: "If you're paying for a ride, you should be able to count on the vehicle
   being safe, too, but our data shows that isn't always the case." Counterpoint:
   Elena Sierra, Executive Director of the Transportation Alliance: "It's important
   to recognize that not all recalls present the same level of safety risk" — ranges
   from software updates and labeling corrections to mechanical issues.

## Numbers to use
- 26.67% open-recall rate, taxi/rideshare, 16 cities (~437,583-438,000 VINs)
- ~20% national average open-recall rate
- NYC medallion cabs: 38.6% (~10,769 NYC VINs, avg 1.38 open recalls per affected vehicle)
- Seattle: 37.5%; Memphis: 30.2%; LA: 11%; Boston: 9%; Baltimore: 4%
- NYC permit transfer if vehicle is out of service: 6-8 weeks (~15% of annual revenue)

## Limitations (for article)
- CARFAX is a commercial vendor with a product (carfax.com/recall) adjacent to the
  finding; methodology was not peer-reviewed. VINs came from officials/open-data,
  but coverage varies by city program.
- An open recall is not the same as an unsafe vehicle: severity ranges from
  software updates and label corrections to mechanical defects (Sierra point).
- Study covers registered taxi/rideshare fleets in 16 major cities, not the whole
  U.S. gig fleet; rural/regional rideshare unknown.
- The study reports presence of open recalls, not crash outcomes in these vehicles.

## Strongest counterargument
Most open recalls are minor — software, labels — and a taxi with an open label recall
is not a death trap. NYC's 38.6% reflects an older, harder-used medallion fleet, not
necessarily 38.6% of rides being dangerous. The economically trapped driver story is
real, but fleets like Voyager do prioritize safety recalls, so the worst cases skew
toward independent owner-drivers.

## Methodology note
Rates are CARFAX's: affected VINs / checked VINs. National ~20% is CARFAX's stated
benchmark, not independently verified here. NYC sub-sample: 10,769 VINs.

## Actionable takeaways
- Rideshare/taxi drivers: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — free, 30 seconds;
  repairs are free at the dealer; ask the dealer for same-day recall scheduling.
- Passengers: on long rides or airport runs, note the plate and run it later; in
  NYC, nearly 2 in 5 medallion cabs carry an open recall — the platform won't check
  for you.
- Fleet managers: route recall work to fleet-minded dealers that take commercial
  service appointments (Possumato's point: volume, not margin, makes this profitable).
