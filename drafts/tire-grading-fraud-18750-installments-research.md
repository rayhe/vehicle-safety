# Research: Tire Grading Fraud — $18,750 Installments

## Article #757
**Slug:** `tire-grading-fraud-18750-installments`
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread)
**Kicker:** Existential Dread

## Thesis
P.T. Elangperdana Tyre Industry, an Indonesian tire manufacturer, was penalized by NHTSA for providing tires with inaccurate UTQG (Uniform Tire Quality Grading Standards) safety grades. The total fine: $425,000, paid in 16 installments of $18,750 each — less than the cost of a new Camry. While the company was still making those payments, NHTSA testing found their Vizzoni Dreamliner A/T tires had sidewall separation violating FMVSS 139. The company blamed NHTSA's storage of the test samples.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: NHTSA Civil Penalty Settlements Page
- URL: https://WWW.NHTSA.GOV/laws-regulations/civil-penalty-settlements
- Company: P.T. Elangperdana Tyre Industry
- Violation: Providing tires with inaccurate grading information (49 U.S.C. § 30117(a); 49 C.F.R. § 575.104(d))
- Agreement date: 12/20/2024
- Total penalty: $425,000
- Payment structure: 16 installments of $18,750 each (total of $300,000 in installments — suggests $125,000 may have been paid separately or suspended)
- Payments documented from October 2025 through March 2026 (at minimum)

### Source 2: NHTSA Recall 26T008 (P.T. Elangperdana)
- Product: Vizzoni Dreamliner A/T tires, size LT265/75R16
- Quantity: 5,125 tires
- Production dates: January 29, 2019 through October 14, 2025 (6+ years)
- Issue: Sidewall separation identified by NHTSA compliance testing
- FMVSS: Noncompliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 139
- Company response: Internal testing "has not revealed this issue"; suggested "a slight, undetected fluctuation" or "improper storage of those sampled tires" by NHTSA
- No consumer complaints reported
- Remedy: Still being finalized

### Source 3: NTSB Tire Safety Data (SA-044, 2016)
- 596 people died in tire-related crashes in 2014
- ~19,000 people injured annually in tire-related crashes
- 69% of vehicles had at least one underinflated tire (2014 RMA survey)

### Source 4: NSC / NHTSA Estimates
- 78,000 collisions per year due to unsafe tires
- 738 tire-related deaths in 2017
- 1 in 4 passenger vehicles has at least one underinflated tire

### Source 5: Safety Research Symposium
- Researcher Randy Whitfield showed FARS data indicates tire-related deaths have NOT decreased since 1995
- NHTSA's own claims of tire safety improvements are based on disputed methodology
- NHTSA acknowledged the FARS data issues but continued citing incorrect figures

## Novel Contribution
Cross-referencing NHTSA's penalty settlement database with its recall database reveals the same company (P.T. Elangperdana) was simultaneously:
1. Paying installment penalties for faking tire safety grades
2. Selling tires with structural defects (sidewall separation)

The UTQG grading system — the ONLY federally mandated consumer-facing tire quality metric — can be gamed for a penalty of $18,750/month. This is the only article to connect these two enforcement actions against the same manufacturer.

## Strongest Counterargument
The $425,000 penalty may be proportional to the company's U.S. revenue (likely small — they're an Indonesian manufacturer selling budget tires). The penalty may have genuinely deterred the grading fraud. And the sidewall recall was initiated proactively after NHTSA testing, not after injuries or deaths.

## Limitations
- We don't know Elangperdana's U.S. market share or sales volume
- The UTQG grading inaccuracy could have been minor (overstatement by one grade) or major
- No consumer injuries or complaints were reported for either the grading issue or the sidewall recall
- We don't know if the same tires that had inaccurate grades are the ones with sidewall separation
- Tire-related fatality data (596-738/year) is from 2014-2017; more recent figures may differ

## Actionable Insights
1. Check your tires' UTQG ratings at nhtsa.gov/equipment/tires/tire-ratings-search
2. Check tire recalls using the DOT code on your tire sidewall at nhtsa.gov/recalls
3. Very few consumers register tires with manufacturers — without registration, you won't get recall notices
4. Budget tires from unfamiliar brands may have grading accuracy issues
5. Inspect tires for sidewall bulging, cracking, or separation — these are signs of structural failure
