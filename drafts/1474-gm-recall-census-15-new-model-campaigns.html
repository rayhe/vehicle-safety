GM Recalled the Same Steering Nut Twice in Four Months. It Still Beats Ford 15 to 40.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 11, 2026

Yesterday I ran Ford's new-model recall census: 40 campaigns. Today I ran the same census on General Motors, same database, same method, same ten months. GM's brand-new vehicles carry 15, Ford's lead is not close, and GM should enjoy the win, because a third of its 15 belongs to one vehicle that cannot keep a steering nut tight.

15
federal recall campaigns touching 2026-model-year GM vehicles, filed Jan 1–Nov 11, 2026; Ford 40, Toyota 7, Honda 2

Fifteen campaigns across roughly fifty GM nameplates, from Corvette to Hummer EV. Ford needed fourteen models to reach 40; GM needed four brands to reach 15. Strip out the one campaign touching nearly every GM vehicle, a radio that failed to download the owner's manual, and the real-defect count falls to 14, with eleven models dropping to zero. GM's headline number is flattered by paperwork in a way Ford's simply is not.

The Chevrolet Express and GMC Savana vans carry four campaigns each, more than any other 2026 GM vehicle, and three of the four are steering. June brought 26V399, a steering-gear nut that may not have been properly tightened. October brought 26V718, the same nut again, because vehicles not properly inspected under 26V399 need re-inspection, four months and two federal campaigns later, for one nut. A recall of a recall should never happen twice on the same part.

Elsewhere the list has real teeth: campaign 26V289 covers a missing transfer-case component that can lock a truck's wheels without warning; NHTSA's filing traces it to 68 suspect assemblies, 46 in 2026 vehicles, found after one engineer filed a Speak Up For Safety report when a Silverado 1500 locked up in a parking lot. Campaign 26V717 is a brake-control-module software defect on the Lyriq, Celestiq, Blazer EV, and Vistiq that can cost a driver braking. Campaign 26V496 covers Silverado EV and Sierra EV steel wheels that may crack at the vent holes until the disc separates. None of the 15 carries NHTSA's over-the-air flag, the same zero-for-40 result Ford posted yesterday, so the software-fix story both companies tell does not survive contact with their newest cars.

Campaign counts are not severity scores, and GM's 15 flatters it twice over. The manual recall pads nearly every model by one, with no mechanical danger behind it. A low count can mean clean manufacturing or quiet reporting, and this census cannot tell which. Ford's 40 includes aggressive early filing under its November 2024 NHTSA consent order, which rewards over-reporting. But breadth answers most of that: GM's 15 spread across vans, full-size SUVs, heavy-duty pickups, EVs, and Cadillacs, touching steering, wheels, seats, brakes, batteries, and fuel. That spread is a system-wide pattern, not a single bad part.

If you own a 2026 Express or Savana, check whether your van got both steering-nut inspections; the second campaign exists because the first one missed vans. Silverado EV and Sierra EV owners should ask about the wheel inspection at the next service. Everyone else with a 2026 GM vehicle: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls tonight. Fifteen campaigns is a good number next to Ford's 40, and a bad number if one of them is yours.
