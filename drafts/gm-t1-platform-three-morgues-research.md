# Research: GM T1 Platform — Same Bones, Three Morgues

## Angle
Three vehicles built on GM's T1 platform (Tahoe, Suburban, Escalade) share the same body-on-frame architecture, powertrains, and crash structures — yet their FARS fatality rates span a 3.9x range. The Tahoe kills at 2.49 per 100M VMT. Stretch it 13 inches and you get the Suburban at 1.36. Put leather on the Tahoe's identical frame and call it an Escalade: 0.64. Same bones, three very different body counts.

## Kill Test: Is This Genuinely Newsworthy?
YES. This isn't just another vehicle comparison. It isolates the two variables that traditional vehicle safety ratings ignore:
1. **Wheelbase** (Suburban's advantage: longer = more rollover-resistant)
2. **Buyer demographics** (Escalade's advantage: wealthy buyers drive differently, on different roads, at different times)

The crash/death ratios are IDENTICAL (Tahoe 1.96, Suburban 1.94, Escalade 2.14) — meaning once in a fatal crash, all three protect occupants equally. The difference is entirely in how often they END UP in fatal crashes.

## FARS Data (2014-2023)

### Chevrolet Tahoe
- Rate: 2.49 per 100M VMT
- Deaths: 2,592 | Crashes: 5,078
- Fleet: 831,250 | VMT: 10,391M
- Crash/Death: 1.96
- Impairment: 20.6% (alcohol 15.7%)
- 88% of deaths from pre-2010 model years
- Peak MY: 2003 (311 deaths)
- Wheelbase: 116 in (GMT800/900), 120.9 in (T1)

### Chevrolet Suburban
- Rate: 1.36 per 100M VMT
- Deaths: 593 | Crashes: 1,151
- Fleet: 350,000 | VMT: 4,375M
- Crash/Death: 1.94
- Impairment: 20.3% (alcohol 16.2%)
- 82% of deaths from pre-2010 model years
- Peak MY: 2002 (72 deaths)
- Wheelbase: 130 in (GMT800/900), 134.1 in (T1)
- +14 in longer than Tahoe = 45% lower death rate

### GMC Yukon (Tahoe rebadge)
- Rate: 2.55 per 100M VMT
- Deaths: 1,114 | Crashes: 2,339
- Fleet: 350,000 | VMT: 4,375M
- Crash/Death: 2.10
- Impairment: 21.4% (alcohol 16.5%)
- 83% of deaths from pre-2010 model years
- CONFIRMS: Yukon ≈ Tahoe rate (2.55 vs 2.49) — badge doesn't change rate

### Cadillac Escalade (Tahoe + luxury trim)
- Rate: 0.64 per 100M VMT
- Deaths: 211 | Crashes: 452
- Fleet: 262,500 | VMT: 3,281M
- Crash/Death: 2.14
- Impairment: 22.0% (alcohol 15.9%) — HIGHEST impairment in the family!
- Peak MY: 2007 (30 deaths)
- Same wheelbase as Tahoe (120.9 in current)
- Rate is 3.9x LOWER than Tahoe on identical platform

## Original Contribution
Cross-tabulation of fatality rates across three vehicles sharing a single platform, controlling for:
- Engineering → crash/death ratios identical (~2.0), proving survivability is equivalent
- Impairment → similar or higher in Escalade (22.0%), ruling out sobriety
- Fleet age → all dominated by pre-2010 deaths
- Wheelbase → partially explains Suburban (14 in longer)
- Demographics → the remaining variable explaining the Escalade gap

## Key Numbers
- 2.49 → 1.36 → 0.64: the rate descent from Tahoe → Suburban → Escalade
- 3.9x: gap between Tahoe and Escalade on same platform
- ~2.0: crash/death ratio for ALL three (survivability is the same)
- 22.0%: Escalade impairment rate (highest of three — the rich drive drunk more but die less)
- 13+ inches: wheelbase difference between Tahoe and Suburban

## Sources
1. NHTSA FARS 2014-2023 (fatality data, crash counts, toxicology)
2. GM T1 platform specifications (public: same 5.3L/6.2L V8, same 4WD system, same frame)
3. NHTSA FARS query tool (cdan.dot.gov)

## Limitations
- VMT is estimated from fleet size × assumed annual miles. Escalade owners may drive fewer annual miles (urban, weekend use), which would overstate VMT and deflate the rate. However, even a 50% VMT overestimate would only bring the Escalade rate to ~1.28 — still below the Suburban.
- Fleet size estimates may not capture police/fleet Tahoes accurately, which would understate Tahoe VMT and inflate its rate.
- FARS doesn't distinguish standard vs. extended wheelbase Escalade (ESV), which matches the Suburban's dimensions.

## Strongest Counterargument
"The Escalade's low rate is just a VMT estimation artifact — luxury vehicles are driven fewer miles."
Rebuttal: Even doubling the assumed Escalade rate still leaves it below the Suburban. And the Suburban-Tahoe gap (same buyer demographics, different wheelbase) can't be explained by VMT at all.
