# Research Notes: Ford = Half of All US Vehicle Recalls in 2026

## Angle
Ford has recalled 13,518,991 vehicles through July 28, 2026 — more than every other automaker combined. Ford holds ~12.6% US market share but accounts for 50.6% of all US recall volume. This "recall intensity ratio" of 4.0x is the story.

## Key Data Points

### Ford 2026 Recall Volume (source: Carscoops / NHTSA)
- 13,518,991 vehicles recalled across 61 campaigns (through July 28, 2026)
- US industry total: 26,740,225 vehicles across 226 campaigns
- Ford's share of recalled vehicles: 50.6%
- Ford's share of campaigns: 27.0% (61/226)
- Average Ford recall size: 221,622 vehicles/campaign
- Average industry recall: 118,319 vehicles/campaign
- Average non-Ford recall: 80,129 vehicles/campaign (13,221,234 / 165)
- Ford's average recall is 2.77x larger than non-Ford average

### Ford Market Share (sources: Cox Automotive, Ford Q2 2026 report, Experian)
- 2025 full year: 13.2% annual share (Ford's best year this decade)
- H1 2026: ~12.6% (Cox Automotive estimate)
- Q2 2026: 549,200 units sold (10.3% YoY decline)
- F-Series: #1 truck, 49 consecutive years
- Ford estimated to sell ~1.9-2.0M vehicles in 2026

### Novel Calculations
- Recall intensity ratio: 50.6% / 12.6% = 4.0x (Ford recalls 4x its market weight)
- Ford recalled ~6.7x its annual US sales volume through July alone
- Non-Ford automakers combined: 13,221,234 recalls / ~87% market share = recall intensity ratio of ~0.57x (industry is 0.57x, Ford is 4.0x — a 7x gap)

### FARS Cross-Reference (2014-2023, my data)
- Ford: 34,954 deaths across 29 models = 18.3% of tracked FARS deaths
- On ~12.6% market share, Ford's FARS death share / market share = 1.45x
- Compare: Chevrolet 35,905 deaths (18.8%), Toyota 22,019 (11.5%)
- Ford's F-150 alone: 9,194 deaths (rate 1.04 per 100M VMT)

### Kill Test: Is This Genuinely Newsworthy?
YES. The 50.6% threshold is a genuinely shocking stat: one company accounting for more than half of all US vehicle recalls. The "recall intensity ratio" (4.0x market weight) is a novel calculation. Cross-referencing with FARS death share (1.45x) creates a genuine paradox: Ford recalls at 4x its weight but its death rate is only 1.45x its weight. Are the recalls working? Or are they chasing the wrong defects?

### Strongest Counterargument
Ford's high recall volume partly reflects its older fleet age and the fact that recalls span 8 model years of production. A manufacturer with a younger average fleet (like Tesla or Hyundai) would mechanically have fewer recall-eligible vehicles. Ford also sells disproportionately more trucks and commercial vehicles, which have longer lifespans and accumulate more recall campaigns per vehicle. Volume ≠ negligence; it can mean proactive detection.

### Limitations
- FARS data covers 2014-2023, not 2026. Can't directly link 2026 recalls to current fatality rates.
- Recall volume doesn't distinguish severity: a software OTA fix and a "do not drive" order count equally.
- Market share is for new vehicle sales; installed fleet share differs (Ford's fleet share is likely higher than 12.6% given fleet longevity).
- The 13.5M number counts vehicles, not unique VINs — a single vehicle could appear in multiple recalls.
