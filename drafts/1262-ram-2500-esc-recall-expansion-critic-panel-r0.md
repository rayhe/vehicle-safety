## 1. General Editor — 9.0
The zero-warning hook lands in the lede and the second-recall twist gives the story its spine: a fix whose scope was wrong the first time. Structure follows the template (lede, pull stats, ESC research, fairness paragraph, action). The "eight trucks" arithmetic makes the reassuring-sounding 0.1% figure work against itself. Minor: the towing framing is asserted rather than sourced to a crash, but it is framed as the driving situation at risk, not as an event that happened.

## 2. Voice Coach — 8.5
Mia's voice is distinct and sustained: mechanism-first, millisecond-level, judgmental about bad engineering ("a module that also handles turn signals"). It cannot be swapped with Dale or Rex without rewriting. Loses half a point because the opener ("Talk about what happens...") awkwardly paraphrases her own catchphrase instead of using it; the sentiment is right, the phrasing is a half-step off. Not worth a revision round on its own.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0
No invented victims, no crash count claimed where none exists, and the disclaimer states that plainly. The 0.1% estimate is attributed to TopSpeed's reading of the filing rather than presented as a confirmed failure rate. Owners are told what to do, not frightened into a dealer visit with an exaggerated body count.

## 4. Social Shareability — 8.5
"Second recall for the same defect" plus "no warning" is a two-beat share hook, and Ram 2500 owners are a large, identifiable audience who will forward this to each other. Loses half a point because ESC is abstract compared with a wheel falling off; the article has to teach the system before the danger lands, and some scrollers will not wait for the second paragraph.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0
FMVSS 126 noncompliance, campaign numbers (26V-651 / 26V-244), FCA numbers (90D / 36D), dates, counts, and remedy are all attributed to the NHTSA record as reported by multiple outlets, with no claim that Ram broke the law beyond the noncompliance finding itself. No defamation risk: the article says the scope was wrong, which the expansion itself demonstrates, not that anyone acted in bad faith.

## 6. Research Rigor — 8.5
Campaign arithmetic checks out (8,329 + 6,605 = 14,934; expansion larger by 1,724) and the ESC effectiveness figures trace to NHTSA's 2007 study and 2025 update, both cited. Loses half a point because the campaign details rest on secondary summaries of the NHTSA record (USA Today, KBB, TopSpeed, trade roundups) rather than a directly opened Part 573 filing; the convergence across independent outlets mitigates this, but it is a real sourcing limitation and is disclosed in the research file.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.0
Two pull stats carry the argument (0 warnings, 14,934 trucks) with precise labels, and every other number in the body (51.6%, 36%/70%, 2,202 lives, 0.1%, Sept 13-Nov 15 window) is sourced in the references. No chart needed; the numbers are few and each earns its place.
