# Research: MG 3 Seat Broke Loose — Four Stars Anyway

## Article #774
**Slug:** mg3-seat-broke-four-stars-anyway
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover
**Kicker:** The Gap

## Angle
Euro NCAP crash-tested the MG 3 in September 2025. During the frontal offset test, the driver's seat latch failed — the seat twisted mid-impact, worsening leg injuries and causing the dummy's head to bottom out the airbag and strike the steering wheel. This had never happened before in 29 years of Euro NCAP testing (since 1997, hundreds of vehicles tested). Despite this unprecedented component failure, the MG 3 still earned four stars because Euro NCAP's scoring system has no mechanism to override or deduct points for a critical component failure. Euro NCAP's own programme director recommended consumers "consider other cars."

The systemic angle: The star rating — the single most influential safety metric consumers use — structurally cannot fail a car for a catastrophic component failure. The system rates; it cannot warn.

## Counterargument
MG responded quickly — recall launched Nov 2025, 12,000+ fixed by Apr 2026, expects 90% by Nov 2026. Euro NCAP verified the design fix eliminates risk. Cars from Aug 2025 onward already had the fix. No real-world incidents reported. The system's inability to dock stars doesn't mean it failed to communicate — Euro NCAP issued an unprecedented public warning.

## Limitations
- MG 3 not sold in the US — direct consumer impact is European/Australian/South African markets
- Euro NCAP's 2026 protocol changes may already address this gap, but the new protocols' actual scoring override mechanism hasn't been publicly detailed
- One incident in 29 years could be genuinely anomalous rather than systemic, though the Global NCAP Nissan Almera precedent suggests otherwise
- Recall completion rate (2/3 by April 2026) is actually above industry average for that timeframe
