## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 8.7/10
The moral reasoning is handled straight: it refuses both easy stories (reckless riders, innocent riders) and gives the licensing/speeding/alcohol triad its full paragraph before showing the compound. The left-turn paragraph explicitly assigns fault to the turning driver ("still not the rider's fault"), which keeps the piece from sliding into rider-blaming. No victim-blaming of the dead, no policy prescription dressed as data; the helmet-law split is presented as a natural experiment, not a sermon. One watch item: "the dead were disproportionately unlicensed, speeding, and drunk" is a population statement that a careless reader could take as a claim about any individual rider; the limitations section carries the correlation caveat, which keeps it honest. No issues requiring changes.

## 4. Social Shareability — 8.6/10
