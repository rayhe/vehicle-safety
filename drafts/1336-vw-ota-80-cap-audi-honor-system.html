Volkswagen Cut 20% of Your Battery Over the Internet. Audi Is Asking You to Do It Yourself.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
March 10, 2028

Some night this week, thousands of Volkswagen ID.4s quietly lost a fifth of their range while parked in their owners' driveways. Volkswagen pushed an over-the-air update that caps the battery at 80 percent, and owners had about a week to opt out. Meanwhile, 9,048 Audi Q4 e-tron owners got a very different deal: set the limit yourself, in the car or the app, whenever you get around to it.[1]

31,572
VW and Audi EVs told not to charge past 80% — enforced two completely different ways

The defect is identical. In certain charging profiles above 80 percent state of charge, a high-voltage battery fault can trigger thermal propagation, which is the industry's polite term for a battery fire. Neither company knows the root cause. Both estimate roughly 0.1 percent of the recalled vehicles actually carry the defect, and neither can tell you whether yours is one of them.[2] Volkswagen counts 22 incident claims between January 2024 and August 2026. Audi says it knows of no Q4 e-tron fires from this issue at all.[1]

So the safety instruction is the same, but the enforcement could not be more different. Volkswagen reached into your car over the internet and derated it for you, then mailed stickers reminding you not to undo it. Audi trusts you to remember. One approach treats owners like endpoints to be managed. The other treats them like adults who read their mail. Both share one awkward feature: there is no actual fix. No remedy exists. ID.4 owners get letters by November 27; Q4 owners may not hear from Audi until December 18. Until then, the official guidance is to keep charging less and wait.[1]

Consider what Volkswagen just demonstrated: your carmaker can remotely cut your car's capability by 20 percent, and it treated a one-week opt-out window as consent. Audi's honor system has the opposite flaw: every owner who never sets the limit keeps charging to 100 percent with the same defect in the pack.

What this means for you

If you own a 2023–2026 ID.4: check whether the OTA cap landed on your car (the charge screen tops out at 80). If you opted out, or if the update never arrived, set the limit yourself in the charging menu. If you own a 2024–2025 Q4 e-tron or Q4 Sportback e-tron: set the 80 percent limit in the car or the myAudi app now, and do not wait for the December letter. Either way, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and confirm whether campaigns 26V630 or 26V698 apply to your car.[3]

This is the ID.4's fourth battery-safety recall in the United States, and the fourth time the interim answer has been some version of "charge it less." A software cap you didn't ask for beats a fire nobody predicted. But when the same defect gets a remote lock on one brand and a polite request on the other, both companies owe owners the same answer: when does the actual fix arrive?
