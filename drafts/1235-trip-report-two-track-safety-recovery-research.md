## Original contribution
1. **Two-track decomposition:** TRIP shows aggregate deaths down (4th straight year) while vulnerable-road-user deaths are up 8% over the same 2020-2025 window. Therefore the entire improvement in the national fatality count came from vehicle occupants — the people inside cars got safer while the people outside them got killed more often. Nobody has stated this decomposition against the TRIP report.
2. **Per-death cost math:** GHSA's H1-2025 figure ($40B+ for 3,024 pedestrian deaths) → ≈$13.2 million of societal harm per pedestrian death. And $1.75T/year ÷ 365 ≈ $4.79 billion of societal harm per day. These are original calculations with shown methodology.
3. **Vehicle-form-factor link:** GHSA's ">half of pedestrian deaths involve SUVs and pickups" cross-read with TRIP's vulnerable-user deterioration — connects the pedestrian death curve to fleet weight mix (consistent with this site's body-on-frame coverage).

## Data tension to report honestly
- GHSA preliminary says pedestrian deaths FELL 7% to 6,732 in 2025 (third straight decrease), while TRIP's 2020-2025 window shows pedestrians UP 5% (6,236 → 6,529) and the combined vulnerable category up 8%. Both can be true: the 2020 baseline was a pandemic-depressed year for pedestrian exposure, and the recent trend within the category is improving (per GHSA preliminary state data), while the five-year arc is worse. Also the two totals differ (6,529 vs 6,732) — preliminary state-reported data vs. finalized/estimated national figures; different vintages. This is the strongest counterargument to the "two-track" framing and must appear at full strength.

## Limitations
- 2025 final NHTSA FARS numbers are not out; TRIP leans on estimates and preliminary state data
- Cost methodology applies NHTSA's 2023 report (2019-based unit costs) to 2025 dollars — inflation and medical-cost drift introduce uncertainty
- The 2020 baseline was anomalous (pandemic driving patterns); choosing it as the window start flatters the "occupant improvement" story and steepens the "vulnerable worsening" story
- Pedestrian preliminary numbers (6,732) may be revised when final FARS lands

## Actionable takeaways
- The pedestrian safety levers with evidence: lower speeds (71% of MA pedestrian deaths on 25-35 mph roads), sidewalks, lighting (75% at night), sober driving, enforcement
- If you walk: freeways/interstates are death traps by design (15% of pedestrian deaths); hit-and-run is 1 in 4 — get off the road, be visible at night
- If you buy: vehicle choice has externalities — SUVs/pickups are over half of pedestrian deaths; front-end geometry matters
- Check VIN recalls, but the biggest 2025 lever isn't a recall — it's speed and separation
