An Alignment Vendor Touched 490 Buses. Forest River Says Every Single One Has the Defect.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 3, 2026

Most recalls start with a broken part. This one starts with a service appointment. Forest River Bus sent 490 brand-new transit buses to a third-party vendor for front-end alignments before shipping, and the buses came back with steering hardware that was either torqued wrong or missing entirely, so Forest River recalled all of them under NHTSA campaign 26V705, filed October 23, 2026.[1] Alignment is supposed to make a vehicle track straighter. On these buses it did the opposite.

100%
Forest River's estimated share of the 490 recalled buses that actually carry the defect, against the 1 to 3 percent estimates typical of large recalls

To understand why a bolt matters this much, you need about thirty seconds of suspension geometry. Caster is the tilt of the steering axis, and positive caster is what makes a wheel want to roll straight and snap back to center after a turn. A caster bushing and its bolt hold that angle in place at the front end. Loosen the bolt or leave the hardware out, and the wheel is no longer held at its designed angle, which is exactly why Forest River's own warning symptom reads like a driver complaint rather than an engineering memo: steering control and the front end of the vehicle may shake severely.[1] Shake is the tell. Loss of control is what comes after it.

Run the numbers and the shape of the recall gets stranger. Forest River lists 27 vehicle lines across its Starcraft, Glaval, Champion, Elkhart Coach, Eldorado, and StarTrans brands, every one of them traced through production records to the same alignment vendor, and the single largest line, the 2027 Starcraft Allstar, accounts for 160 of the 490 buses, or 32.7 percent on its own. A defect that lives in a factory usually lives in one model. A defect that lives in a vendor's shop lives wherever the vendor's trucks went, which is how one bad torque spec spreads across six brands and two model years without any of those brands sharing a single part number.

Then there is the calendar, which deserves credit for once. Forest River's compliance office learned of the matter on October 16, 2026, opened a research item on October 19, and had a recall vote from its corporate committee that same day, with the federal filing landing October 23, seven days from flag to filing and three days from flag to vote.[1] Compare that with recalls that spend months in committee while complaints pile up, and this one looks like the process working as designed, fast, boring, and before anyone got hurt, at least as far as the filing shows, since its chronology starts with an internal compliance flag rather than a crash report and names no crashes or injuries.

What should a fleet operator or driver do with this? If you run Starcraft, Glaval, Champion, Elkhart Coach, Eldorado, or StarTrans buses from model years 2026 or 2027, check every VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and call Forest River at 1-800-348-7440 with recall number 05-2110, because owner letters are not expected until December 21, 2026, and the fix is an inspection, not a waiting game.[1][3] Dealers will check whether the front caster bushing and bolt are present and properly torqued, install the hardware if it is missing, and torque it to spec, all free. Drivers get one simpler instruction: severe front-end shake means pull over and stop driving the bus, since that shake is the filing's stated warning that the steering geometry is already compromised.

What this analysis does not prove: a 100% estimate is Forest River's estimate, not a measurement taken from all 490 buses, and the filing does not say how many buses were actually inspected before the recall decision, so the true count of loose versus missing hardware is still unknown. "Vendor error" is the filing's stated cause, but the filing does not name the vendor, publish the torque specification that was missed, or explain how the error survived whatever quality check should have caught buses leaving an alignment shop without their hardware. No crash, injury, or complaint count is published in the filing, which means "no crashes cited" is not the same as "no crashes happened."

Strongest counterargument. Four hundred ninety buses is a small recall, the defect was caught by the manufacturer's own compliance office in days rather than by a body count over years, and a torque error at one vendor says little about Forest River's buses generally, since the same alignment work happens thousands of times a day across the industry without incident. It is also possible that most of the 490 buses would never have shown a symptom, with bolts loose enough to fail an inspection but tight enough to hold for years. All of that is true, and it still misses the point: a bus does not get to have a maybe-loose steering bolt, because the vehicle behind that bolt carries passengers who did not choose the alignment vendor and cannot check his work.
