# Research: Honda Built the Safest Subcompact in America. Then It Killed It.

## Primary Sources

### FARS Data (2014-2023)
- Honda Fit: 290 deaths, 0.72/100M VMT, fleet 350,000
- Subcompact comparison:
  - Chevy Sonic: 494 deaths, 1.40/100M VMT (94% higher than Fit)
  - Chevy Spark: 517 deaths, 1.28/100M VMT (78% higher)
  - Kia Rio: 268 deaths, 1.07/100M VMT (49% higher)
  - Ford Fiesta: 513 deaths, 1.02/100M VMT (42% higher)
  - Nissan Versa: 722 deaths, 0.90/100M VMT (25% higher)
  - Hyundai Accent: 360 deaths, 0.80/100M VMT (11% higher)
  - Toyota Yaris: 190 deaths, 0.76/100M VMT (6% higher)

### Impairment (FARS Toxicology)
- Honda Fit: 19.1% any impairment, 13.9% alcohol, 7.9% drug (832 drivers)
- Average subcompact impairment is ~20-22%
- Fit drivers are LESS impaired than average — part of the safety story

### IIHS Ratings (historical)
- 2015-2020 Fit earned IIHS Top Safety Pick
- Good in frontal overlap, side, roof strength
- 2020 was final US model year

### Honda's decision
- Honda discontinued the Fit in the US after 2020 (MY 2020 last produced)
- Continued selling in Japan, Europe as Jazz
- Replaced effectively by the HR-V (a subcompact SUV)
- HR-V FARS: 142 deaths, 0.13/100M VMT — MUCH safer, but $5K+ more expensive

### Key paradox
- The Fit's rate (0.72) beats vehicles MUCH larger and more expensive:
  - Honda Civic: 2.25 (3x worse)
  - Toyota Corolla: 1.85 (2.6x worse)
  - Nissan Sentra: 2.13 (3x worse)
  - Honda Accord: 3.07 (4.3x worse!)
- A $17K subcompact outperformed $25K-30K sedans

### Model year breakdown (US-relevant 2007+)
- 2007-2009 (1st US gen): 83 deaths
- 2010-2014 (2nd gen): 54 deaths  
- 2015-2020 (3rd gen): 66 deaths
- Deaths declining per gen despite growing fleet = improving safety

### The gap
- The Fit is gone. Its replacement (HR-V) costs $5-8K more.
- For buyers who can ONLY afford a $12-16K used car, the Fit was the safest option
- Now they choose from: Versa (0.90 rate), Accent (0.80), Spark (1.28), Sonic (1.40)
- Every alternative is less safe per mile

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? 
YES — this is the first systematic ranking of every US subcompact by FARS death rate showing the Fit at the top. The paradox that a $17K car beat $30K sedans in real-world safety is counterintuitive. The discontinuation creates an equity angle: the safest affordable car is no longer available.
