## Angle (1-2 sentences)
Last night (Oct 31, 2026) was a Saturday Halloween, the first since 2020. Saturday is already the deadliest day of the week for alcohol-impaired crash deaths; Halloween night is the most impairment-dense night on the calendar. They collided, and we can estimate the toll before FARS catches up.

## Verified facts (with sources)

1. **176 dead, Halloween night, BAC >= 0.08, 2019-2024.** Quoted directly from NHTSA data in Gov. Hochul's Oct 2026 press release. Corroborated by Live Insurance News (Oct 28, 2026) and DriveTeam. Sibling figure: 201 killed at BAC >= 0.01. Of the 176: 31 pedestrians, 144 drivers/passengers. (sources: einpresswire/ny-gov, liveinsurancenews, driveteam)

2. **54% of people aged 21-34 killed on Halloween night 2019-2024 were in drunk-driving crashes.** Same Hochul/NHTSA release. The kill window has an age bracket. (source: ny-gov press release)

3. **Saturday = 22%+ of annual alcohol-impaired fatal crash deaths**, the highest share of any day. Fair share would be 1/7 = 14.3%. Saturday multiplier ~= 1.54x. Single secondary source (Live Insurance News, citing NHTSA) — flag as such in the article. (source: liveinsurancenews, attributed to NHTSA)

4. **Half of Halloween night crash fatalities in 2023 were alcohol-related — and 2023 was a Tuesday.** AAA figure, via Live Insurance News. This is the killer line: if Tuesday Halloween hits 50%, what does Saturday do? (source: AAA via liveinsurancenews)

5. **Kentucky: 9.1 fatal crashes per million residents on Oct 31** over the last four years on record, 134.57% above the national average. Analysis by Florida attorney Andrew Pickett from NHTSA data (Streetsblog USA, Oct 30, 2025). Vermont: zero. NOTE: Live Insurance News attributed this to "Zutobi" — the Streetsblog piece credits Pickett. Use Pickett/Streetsblog attribution. (source: streetsblog)

6. **Five deadliest Halloween states per FLBR Motorsport analysis of NHTSA FARS: Wyoming, Maine, Rhode Island, Alabama, Kentucky.** RI: 2.68 deaths/million (3rd). RI's deadliest single hour: 1:00-1:59 a.m. — a party hour, not a trick-or-treat hour. Massachusetts safest. (source: liveinsurancenews FLBR piece)

7. **KYTC official Halloween warning (Oct 28, 2026):** last year Kentucky had 4,013 impaired-driver crashes, 1,887 injuries, 136 deaths. In the week leading up to and including Halloween: 84 alcohol-involved collisions, 2 deaths, 30 injuries. Sec. Jim Gray: "Walking while intoxicated can be just as dangerous as driving impaired." (source: k105 quoting KYTC Office of Highway Safety)

8. **NY enforcement campaign (Oct 25-31, 2026):** last year's Halloween initiative produced 44,545 citations, 1,210 impaired-driving, 8,701 speeding. Gov. Hochul announced increased patrols again this year. (source: ny-gov press release)

9. **JAMA Pediatrics, 42 years of federal crash data:** pedestrian death risk 43% higher on Halloween than comparison nights; children 4-8 face 10x the risk. MoneyGeek (federal records 2010-2024): more children killed walking on Halloween than any other date. (sources: driveteam, ny-gov)

10. **Whitley Law Firm holiday report (Oct 2026, FARS-based):** Halloween danger score 104 (normal day = 100), 124.1 killed/day, 31% drunk-driver share. Mississippi deadliest state on Halloween. (source: michiganmamanews)

11. **NHTSA 2019-2023:** 198 killed on Halloween night with some alcohol in system. (source: streetsblog)

13. Halloween-on-Saturday occurrences: 2020, 2026 (last), next 2037. So the 2019-2024 NHTSA window contains exactly one Saturday Halloween — 2020, during COVID, when driving patterns were anomalous.

## Original contribution (the compound-risk estimate)

**Claim:** A Saturday Halloween should produce roughly 45 drunk-driving deaths in one night, vs ~29 on a typical (mostly weekday) Halloween.

**Method:**
- Baseline: 176 deaths / 6 Halloween nights (2019-2024) = 29.3 deaths per Halloween night at BAC >= 0.08.
- Saturday effect: Saturdays carry 22% of annual alcohol-impaired fatal crash deaths vs 14.3% under a uniform distribution, so Saturday multiplier = 22/14.3 = 1.54.
- Expected Saturday-Halloween toll = 29.3 x 1.54 = 45.1.

**Inputs and assumptions:**
- Assumes the Saturday effect and the Halloween effect multiply (independence — untested).
- The 22% Saturday figure comes from a single secondary report of NHTSA data; treat as approximate.
- The 2019-2024 sample includes 2020 (Saturday Halloween during COVID), which may distort both baseline and multiplier.
- FARS 2026 data won't exist for months; this is a projection, not a count.

**Cross-check:** AAA says half of 2023 Halloween-night fatalities (a Tuesday) were alcohol-related. If the Tuesday baseline is already ~50% impaired and Saturday multiplies the death rate by ~1.5x, the Saturday toll lands in the same neighborhood as the compound estimate. Roughly consistent.

## Strongest counterargument
The calendar coincidence may be a statistical curiosity, not a mechanism. Halloween's real killer is the pedestrian surge (43% higher risk per JAMA), which concentrates at 5-8 p.m. — hours before the bar rush. The 176 deaths are real but spread across one night nationally; enforcement campaigns produce citations, not proven fatality reductions. And 2020's Saturday Halloween is the only template, from a year when everything about driving was weird. The honest prediction: we'll know in about 18 months when 2026 FARS closes.

## What we did NOT prove / limitations
- No 2026 FARS data exists yet; the 45 figure is a projection with wide error bars.
- The 22% Saturday share is single-sourced (secondary report of NHTSA data).
- State rankings differ by study window (Pickett: KY #1 over 4 years; FLBR: WY/ME/RI/AL/KY over 3 years). Both use FARS; both are small-sample.
- Cannot attribute any specific death to the calendar coincidence.
- NY citation counts measure enforcement activity, not prevented deaths.
