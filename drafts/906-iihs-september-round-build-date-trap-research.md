### Core facts (from Autoblog, Sept 3 2026; ConsumerAffairs, Sept 10 2026)
- IIHS released ratings for 7 models: Kia Telluride (2027), Tesla Model Y (2026-27), BMW 3 Series, BMW i4, GMC Canyon crew cab, Lexus IS, Toyota Crown Signia.
- Only 2 earned Top Safety Pick+: 2027 Kia Telluride, 2026-27 Tesla Model Y. Both got max Good ratings in every crash and crash-prevention test.
- Telluride award applies ONLY to models built after June 2026, when Kia improved headlights. Earlier builds got Marginal headlights. Actionable: check door-jamb build date sticker.
- Model Y got a Marginal rating for seat belt reminder effectiveness — but seat belt reminder ratings are NOT part of TSP award criteria. The irony: the safest pick can nag you poorly about buckling up.
- Five fell short:
  - BMW 3 Series: Acceptable moderate overlap, Marginal vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention.
  - BMW i4: Poor rating for STANDARD vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention; available headlights Marginal to Poor.
  - GMC Canyon crew cab: Marginal moderate overlap (rear-seat), Poor vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention. gmauthority: chest index 40, shoulder belt tension 5.7 kN on rear dummy; good driver protection, rear dummy took it.
  - Lexus IS: Marginal moderate overlap, Acceptable side.
  - Toyota Crown Signia: Marginal moderate overlap kept it from an award despite Good side, headlights, both crash-prevention systems.
- 2026 TSP criteria: Good in small overlap, moderate overlap, side; Acceptable/Good headlights on all trims; Good pedestrian front crash prevention; Acceptable+ in updated vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention 2.0 (for the +).

### The updated moderate overlap test (IIHS official)
- Added a rear-seat dummy (small woman / 12-year-old size) behind the driver dummy after research showed fatal-injury risk is now HIGHER for belted rear occupants than front occupants in newer vehicles. Front seats got safer (better airbags, advanced belts); rear seats did not keep up.
- Common failure: submarining (dummy slides under lap belt), high shoulder-belt position, excessive head excursion.
- The back seat remains the safest place for young children (front airbag risk).

### Novel angle
- Not just "two won, five lost" — three consumer traps inside one IIHS round:
  1. The build-date trap: a 2027 Telluride on a dealer lot could be a non-award car if built before June 2026. Award ratings have manufacturing-date cutoffs.
  2. The criteria loophole: Model Y earns the institute's highest honor with a Marginal seat belt reminder because reminders are not in the award math.
  3. The rear-seat pattern keeps repeating: the test designed to expose rear-seat protection gaps is now failing vehicles across segments (Canyon, Lexus IS, 3 Series, Crown Signia all dinged on moderate overlap).
- FARS-free article; all numbers from IIHS and named press coverage. Crash-test data is the primary source here.
