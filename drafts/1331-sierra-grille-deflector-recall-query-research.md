# Research: The Recall That Didn't Stick (Article #1331)

**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (Existential Dread Columnist)
**Kicker:** Existential Dread
**News peg:** NHTSA opened Recall Query RQ26003 on October 9, 2026 (publicly disclosed Oct 12), reviewing whether GM's 2025 recall of Sierra grille deflectors actually worked. Fresh angle: a recall of a recall.
**Kill test verdict:** PROCEED. Newsworthy: federal regulator publicly questioning whether a completed recall's remedy holds, 7.6x population expansion, 21 owners whose "fixed" trucks came apart again anyway. Never covered before. Novel angle: the two questions every recall quietly rests on, and the one case where both are now in doubt.

## The facts

### RQ26003 (current)
- NHTSA Office of Defects Investigation opened Recall Query RQ26003 on **October 9, 2026**; publicly disclosed Oct 12.
- Subject: upper grille deflector detachment on **2022-2024 GMC Sierra 1500**; estimated population **535,639** trucks.
- ODI has **118 owner complaints** of the deflector detaching while driving; **21 of those 118** allege detachment AFTER recall 25V-060 had been performed.
- Complaints describe: deflector coming off at speed; cracked windshields; surrounding vehicles swerving to avoid the piece.
- Opening resume lists **no crashes, injuries, or fatalities**.
- ODI stated purpose: "to assess the adequacy of the scope and remedy for Recall 25V-060." Two questions: did the recall cover enough trucks, and does the fix hold up?
- Resume describes detachment as occurring "without detectability"; people at risk per the filing: the truck itself, nearby vehicles, motorcyclists, pedestrians.
- Sources: gm-trucks.com (Oct 13, quotes resume + gives GM customer service 1-800-462-8782), daxstreet.com (Oct 15), gmauthority.com (Oct 12), motorbiscuit.com, theautowire.com (Oct 15), autopostglobal.com (Oct 14).

### Recall 25V-060 (the original, Feb 2025)
- GM filed safety recall 25V-060 on **February 6, 2025** (GM campaign N242490120); covered **70,768** 2022 Sierra 1500 trucks built with a chrome front grille deflector after the 2022 mid-cycle refresh.
- Defect per GM: the eight attachment points holding the deflector could fracture over time; if all eight let go, the bar detaches while the truck is moving, becoming a road hazard.
- GM's own estimates: **~3% of the recalled trucks actually had the defect** (0.03 x 70,768 = ~2,123 trucks); drivers might hear a rattle before separation.
- Remedy: dealers "reinforce or repair" all attachment points on the chrome deflector, ~1 hour, free; GM's instructions told techs to check every retainer and specifically NOT to replace the front grille unless necessary.
- GM issued a stop-delivery order covering affected trucks in new and used dealer inventory; GM Certified Pre-Owned trucks lost certification until repaired.
- Sources: gm-trucks.com coverage of the 2025 filing, daxstreet.com, gmauthority.com.

### Why 2023-2024 were excluded, and why that matters now
- GM told NHTSA that trucks built after the 2022 model year used deflectors from outside the supplier's suspect production window. That was the scope logic.
- RQ26003 names 2022 non-chrome, all 2023, and all 2024 Sierra 1500s. The complaint count now includes those model years, and testing the scope logic is exactly what the query does.
- Neither NHTSA nor GM has publicly theorized why a repaired truck would lose its deflector again. As of Oct 12 press (GM Authority), GM had not announced a broader recall.
- Note: GMC also sells the deflector concept on Silverado; the query names Sierra 1500 only.

## Original contribution: the census math

1. **Scope ratio:** 535,639 / 70,768 = **7.57x**. If ODI forces an expansion, the new population would be more than seven times the original.
2. **Post-repair complaint share:** 21 / 118 = **17.8%** of complaints in the record involve trucks that already received the remedy. This is a share of complaints, NOT a repair failure rate: the denominator that matters (trucks actually repaired) is unknown, so no failure rate can be computed. Both facts must be stated together.
3. **GM's own defect math vs. the record:** GM estimated ~2,123 of the 70,768 trucks had fracturing attachment points. 21 post-repair detachments is ~1% of that estimate, but the comparison is apples-to-oranges (repair completions unknown, complaints self-selected). Use to size the phenomenon, not to judge the remedy.
4. **Kinetic-energy estimate (methodology):** Assume a deflector mass of ~2 kg (typical large plastic trim bar; labeled as estimate) and highway speed 70 mph (31.3 m/s). KE = 1/2 x 2 x 31.3^2 ≈ **980 J, roughly 1,000 joules**. For scale: that is roughly 6x the ~160 J impact energy in common pedestrian-headform test regimes, and comparable to a brick falling off an overpass. The point is qualitative: this is not litter; it is a projectile.
5. **Timeline math:** stop-delivery Feb 2025 to recall query Oct 2026 = ~20 months. GM's remedy had a year and a half of field time before the feds asked the question.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)

A recall query is not a finding of a defect, and nothing in RQ26003 says the recall failed. 118 complaints across a 535,639-vehicle probe population is **0.022%** - a rounding error, and complaint data is self-selected upward. The 21 post-repair cases cannot produce a failure rate without a repair-completion denominator; the 2023-2024 inclusion may reflect GM's supplier-window reasoning being wrong, or may reflect complaints from trucks that were never at risk at all. No crashes, no injuries, no fatalities in the record. If ODI closes this with no action, the headline will have been: a regulator did its job by checking, and the recall was fine. The article must say this plainly, not as a throwaway.

## Limitations

- This desk has not read the actual ODI opening resume; the summary language ("without detectability," risk groups, ODI purpose quote) is quoted through six press outlets that all report the same figures, but the resume itself was not independently pulled.
- The deflector mass is an estimate; the KE figure inherits that uncertainty.
- The number of 25V-060 repairs actually completed (the true denominator) is not public.
- GM's public position beyond the original filing was not available as of Oct 12 press; no comment was reported.

## Actionable takeaways

- Own a 2022 Sierra 1500 with the chrome grille: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. If 25V-060 shows open, get it done. GMC customer service: 1-800-462-8782.
- Had the recall repair and now hear a rattle from the top of the grille, or the deflector looks loose: report it to your dealer AND file a complaint with NHTSA. Owner complaints are literally what opened this query.
- Own a 2023 or 2024 Sierra 1500: no recall covers your truck yet. If the deflector is loose or rattling, have the dealer inspect it and file with NHTSA anyway - scope expansion depends on those complaints.
- Shopping a used 2022-2024 Sierra: check the recall history, then physically look at the deflector. A completed-recall record tells you a repair was recorded, not that the part is still attached.
