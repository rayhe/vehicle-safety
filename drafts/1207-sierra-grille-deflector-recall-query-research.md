# Research: GM's Sierra Grille Fix Failed (article #1207)

## Story angle
GM recalled 70,768 Sierra 1500s for a grille deflector that flies off at speed. NHTSA just opened recall query RQ26003 (Oct 9, 2026): 118 complaints, 21 of them from trucks that ALREADY got the recall fix, and the probe covers 535,639 trucks across 2022-2024 model years, more than 7x the original recall population. The fix didn't fix, and the recall didn't cover. Plus a timeline of GM closing its own investigation in May 2024 saying "no trend," then reopening it when warranty claims climbed.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. NHTSA opened RQ26003 nine days ago (Oct 9, 2026). Fresh federal action, no defect finding yet, meaning this story lands BEFORE the outcome, which is when readers need it.
- Novel? Site has covered failed remedies (ram-rollaway, gm-l87-v8, recall-fix-double-failure) but never this incident. The novel contribution: RQ vs PE distinction (recall query audits the remedy itself), plus the arithmetic GM doesn't want in print (13.2% of the probe population got the recall; 17.8% of complaints are post-repair).
- Would a reader share it? "Your fixed truck's grille is still falling off" is visceral. Windshield cracked by flying chrome is a detail that travels.

## Primary sources (3+)
1. **NHTSA ODI Recall Query RQ26003 opening resume** (opened Oct 9, 2026): 118 complaints of upper grille deflector detachment on 2022-2024 GMC Sierra; 21 of 118 allege detachment AFTER recall 25V-060 was performed; estimated population 535,639; stated purpose "to assess the adequacy of the scope and remedy for Recall 25V-060"; no crashes/injuries/fatalities listed. Component: STRUCTURE:BODY:BUMPERS:ACTIVE SHUTTERS/GRILL. Source page: https://oemdtc.com/investigation/RQ26003/ (mirrors NHTSA action record).
2. **GM Recall 25V-060 Part 573 filing** (Feb 6, 2025): 70,768 units of 2022 Sierra 1500 with chrome upper grille deflector, built Nov 9, 2021 - Sep 29, 2022 (post mid-cycle refresh). Root cause: one or more of the eight attachment points can fracture over time; all eight failing lets the deflector detach while driving. Pre-recall warning: rattle from the front of the truck. GM counted 1,225 complaints and four accident allegations by end of 2024; no known injuries. Remedy: dealers reinforce/repair attachment points at no charge.
3. **GM's internal timeline** (per GM's filing, reported by The Auto Wire, Oct 15, 2026): GM employee flagged a detached deflector via Speak Up for Safety Jan 19, 2024; GM opened an investigation Feb 27, 2024; CLOSED it May 23, 2024 finding no defect trend; REOPENED Dec 11, 2024 when warranty claims climbed. Recall filed Feb 6, 2025.
4. **Repair completion rate**: ~64% of the 70,768 recalled trucks had the repair as of January 2026 (per GM Authority reporting). That leaves roughly 25,500 recalled trucks unrepaired, and an unknown number of 2023-2024 trucks outside the recall entirely.
5. **News coverage**: The Auto Wire (Oct 15, 2026), MotorBiscuit (Oct 14, 2026), GM Authority (Oct 13, 2026), GM-Trucks forum reporting (Oct 13, 2026), 4wdtalk.com summary.

## Key numbers (original calculations for article)
- Original recall (25V-060) covered 70,768 of the 535,639 trucks in the probe = **13.2%** of the population now under review.
- Probe population is **7.57x** the original recall population (535,639 / 70,768).
- 21 of 118 complaints = **17.8%** are alleged post-repair failures. (Caveat: denominator is complaints, not repaired trucks; can't compute a repair failure rate. Must say this explicitly.)
- Repair rate 64% of 70,768 = ~45,300 repaired; ~25,500 recalled trucks still unrepaired as of Jan 2026.
- Timeline: 13 months from employee flag (Jan 19, 2024) to recall filing (Feb 6, 2025), with GM closing its own investigation for 7 of those months (May-Dec 2024).

## Strongest counterargument
- NHTSA has NOT found a defect and has NOT ordered a new recall. A recall query is an audit of an existing recall, and many close without action. 118 complaints across 535,639 trucks is 0.022%, and 21 post-repair complaints is a small absolute number. The 2023-2024 trucks GM excluded may genuinely have different hardware; GM told NHTSA the later trucks used parts outside the supplier's suspect window. The post-repair failures could reflect repairs done before the final procedure, or unrelated damage.
- Say this at full strength in the article. The story is "NHTSA is asking whether the fix worked," not "GM shipped a death trap."

## Limitations
- No FARS fatality data underlies this story; it's a recall/O investigation story, which the site's template supports but with less statistical depth. The "original contribution" here is the arithmetic + timeline synthesis, not a data cross-tab.
- We have not seen the actual NHTSA opening resume PDF, only faithful summaries from oemdtc.com and multiple outlets; the Part 573 filing details come via press reporting of GM's filing.
- Complaint counts are self-reported owner complaints, not adjudicated failures.
- ODI describes detachment as occurring "without detectability" per the Auto Wire's summary of the opening resume.

## Actionable takeaways (hard gate)
- If you own a 2022 Sierra 1500 with the chrome grille: check nhtsa.gov/recalls with your VIN; if recall 25V-060 is open on your truck, get it done. If the fix is done, listen for a rattle from the front at speed.
- If you own a 2023-2024 Sierra 1500: your truck was excluded from 25V-060; the recall query may or may not expand coverage. File a complaint at nhtsa.gov if the deflector rattles or detaches.
- ~25,500 recalled trucks were still unrepaired as of Jan 2026: the fix only works if you get it.
