# Research — #1313: Sienna Pretensioner Unlocks Second Row

## Angle (1 sentence)
Toyota is recalling 228,367 Siennas because the seat belt pretensioner — the device that yanks you safe in a crash — fires so hard it vibrates the second-row seatback's recliner mechanism loose, unlocking the seat underneath the passenger.

## Kill test
Newsworthy? YES. Announced Oct 27-28, 2026; NHTSA campaign 26V690000; the defect is a safety device defeating another safety device (pretensioner vs. seatback lock); Toyota found it by accident during future-product sled testing with zero consumer complaints or injuries; fix is a software patch that retimes the pretensioner. Novel angle: the rescuer is the attacker.

## Facts (all traced)
- **Scope:** 228,367 units, certain 2022–2026 Toyota Sienna Hybrid, 7-passenger models only (second-row captain's chairs with integrated seat belts + pretensioners). 8-passenger bench-seat models NOT included.
- **Production window:** Sept 16, 2021 – July 20, 2026. (2021 MY, the first hybrid-only year, excluded.)
- **NHTSA campaign:** 26V690000 (26V-690). Toyota internal codes: 26TB24 / 26TA24.
- **Mechanism:** During frontal collision, seat belt pretensioner activation generates forces that create "high-magnitude oscillations between the locking cam and pawls inside the seat's outboard recliner mechanism." Under specific combinations of frontal impact force, seat-back recline position, and pretensioner deployment timing, the oscillation rotates the internal cam, allowing the recliner to unseat from its locked position. Seatback may fall back or fold forward.
- **Discovery:** NOT from consumer complaints. Toyota engineers observed a rear seatback recliner disengage during prototype sled tests (Jan–Nov 2025) evaluating modified seat designs for future vehicles. Engineering investigation ran dozens of sled + full-scale crash tests late 2025–Oct 2026, varying floor acceleration, internal friction, pretensioner firing sequences.
- **Decision:** Voluntary recall initiated Oct 14, 2026. Reported to NHTSA Oct 20, 2026.
- **Field data:** As of Oct 12, 2026 — zero field technical reports, zero warranty claims in the U.S. No reported injuries/fatalities.
- **Fix:** Standard fix = free SRS (supplemental restraint system) ECU software update that retunes pretensioner deployment timing, eliminating the locking issue. ~200 vehicles with the "Mobility as a Service" (MaaS) package get both second-row seatbacks physically replaced (free).
- **Notifications:** Owner letters expected ~Dec 7, 2026.
- **Supplier detail:** NHTSA filing references "certain recliner assemblies by a certain supplier during a certain production period equipped with a seat containing a certain seat belt pretensioner by a certain supplier" — supplier names not disclosed in public reports.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you own a 2022–2026 Sienna Hybrid with 7 seats: check your VIN now at nhtsa.gov/recalls — do NOT wait for the Dec 7 letter.
- 8-passenger bench-seat Siennas are not affected.
- The fix is free and takes one dealer visit (software flash); MaaS-package vans get hardware replacement.
- This is a pre-complaint, engineering-caught recall — no injuries reported. Act on the recall anyway: the failure mode is real (validated in full-scale crash tests), it just hasn't happened on the road yet.

## Limitations (what we do NOT know)
- Toyota has not publicly named the recliner or pretensioner suppliers.
- Whether 2021 MY Siennas truly share no affected hardware, or just fall outside the production window by luck.
- Real-world failure rate: with zero field reports, the probability of the exact force/timing/recline combination in an actual crash is unknown — NHTSA and Toyota both treat it as plausible enough to recall, but neither has published a failure-probability estimate.
- FARS data here is 2014–2023 and covers no Sienna-specific recent trends; this article is recall/news-driven, not FARS-driven. Verifiability rests on NHTSA filings and press coverage.

## Counterargument
"Zero complaints, zero injuries, discovered in a lab — this is a phantom risk and Toyota is over-recalling." Fair point in one sense: the recall is precautionary. But the failure mode was validated in full-scale crash tests, not just simulation; pretensioner-fire timing interacts with real seating positions. Toyota recalled because they could reproduce the unlock, not because they modeled it. Over-caution on seatbacks in a family vehicle carrying kids is cheap insurance.
