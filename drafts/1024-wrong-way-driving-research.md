# Research: #1024 — Wrong-Way Driving

**Slug:** 1024-wrong-way-driving-iceberg
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — fatality investigations, paradoxes)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Working headline:** "500 People Die Every Year Driving the Wrong Way. Most Wrong-Way Drivers Just Turn Around."

## Kill test
**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** Wrong-way crashes kill ~500 Americans a year in head-on highway collisions, yet Massachusetts' new detection network found that most wrong-way entries end with the driver self-correcting — the fatal crashes are the tip of a much larger near-miss iceberg. Meanwhile NHTSA is investigating Tesla's Full Self-Driving for driving the wrong way, meaning the robots have now joined the drunks and the disoriented.

**Challenge:** After 1,000+ articles, is this just another data dump? No. Three things make it novel: (1) the near-miss iceberg is newly visible — MassDOT's trailer network (deploying ~5 locations/week, 1,000+ wrong-way events logged in two years) is the first systematic count of wrong-way *entries*, not just fatal crashes; (2) a July 2026 Massachusetts law now mandates a statewide data-driven wrong-way strategy, the most aggressive state response to date; (3) the wrong-way driver is no longer always human — NHTSA's PE25012 probe (opened Oct 7, 2025) covers Teslas driving against traffic with FSD engaged.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Primary sources (5)

1. **AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety, "Heading the Wrong Way with Wrong-Way Driving" (March 2021)** — analysis of FARS data: 2,008 deaths from wrong-way crashes on divided highways, 2015–2018 (~502/yr), up 34% from 375/yr (2010–2014). Three risk factors: alcohol impairment (6 in 10 wrong-way crashes involved an alcohol-impaired driver), older age (70+), driving without a passenger (87% of wrong-way drivers were alone). URL: https://newsroom.aaa.com/2021/03/heading-the-wrong-way-with-wrong-way-driving/

2. **NTSB Wrong-Way Driving Special Investigation Report (2012)** — cited by AAA; NTSB Office of Highway Safety: "Alcohol impairment is, by far, the single most significant factor in the majority of wrong-way driving crashes, which unfortunately has not changed since the NTSB issued its wrong-way driving special investigation report in 2012" (Dr. Rob Molloy). AAA+NTSB countermeasures: ignition interlocks, sobriety checkpoints, driver refresher courses for older adults, more-visible signs/signals.

3. **TAPCO / MassDOT press release (Sep 29, 2026)** — "Massachusetts Deepens Commitment to Road Safety with Mobile Wrong-Way Detection Trailers": decade toll of 4,164 fatal wrong-way crashes and 5,730 deaths nationally, with wrong-way fatalities rising faster than fatal crashes overall. Gov. Maura Healey signed a law in July 2026 requiring MassDOT to build a statewide, data-driven wrong-way strategy (infrastructure, technology, education, annual reporting on high-risk locations). MassDOT installing detection at ~5 locations/week; 1,000+ wrong-way citations logged in two years. MassDOT Assistant Administrator Neil Boudreau: "most drivers self-correct" — the data is reshaping engineering and education. Trailers: solar-powered, radar detection + video + LED-enhanced wrong-way signage, real-time alerts to traffic management and law enforcement; four uses: construction/detour coverage, post-collision deployment, backup when fixed systems fail, and risk mapping. URL: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/massachusetts-deepens-commitment-to-road-safety-with-mobile-wrong-way-detection-trailers-302892069.html

4. **NHTSA Preliminary Evaluation PE25012 (opened Oct 7, 2025)** — defect probe into ~2.88 million Teslas with Full Self-Driving (Supervised)/FSD Beta for two behaviors: running red lights and driving against the direction of travel. At opening: 58 incidents, 14 crashes, 23 injuries; ~80 reports by December. Some cars ran lights after stopping correctly first — a software behavior, not a detection failure. (Secondary writeup: theweeklydriver.com, July 2026.)

5. **Arizona DOT wrong-way detection system (2018)** — $4 million system on 15 miles of I-17 in central Phoenix; received 2019 FHWA award. Precedent for the detection-tech approach. (Via AASHTO Journal, March 2021.)

Supporting: Caltrans — wrong-way collisions ~1% of crashes on the state highway system but ~37 deaths/yr on California freeways on average (AAA SoCal, 2021).

## Key numbers for the article
- 2,008 deaths, 2015–2018 → **~502/yr**, up 34% from 375/yr (2010–2014). (AAA Foundation)
- 4,164 fatal wrong-way crashes / 5,730 deaths over a decade → **~573/yr**; rising faster than fatal crashes overall. (TAPCO/MassDOT release, Sep 2026)
- 60%+ of wrong-way crashes involve an alcohol-impaired driver; BAC ≥ 0.08 in ~60% of wrong-way drivers in the table (1,757 of 2,922 in the AAA BAC breakdown).
- 87% of wrong-way drivers were alone — passengers are a protective factor.
- Drivers 70+ measurably more likely to be wrong-way drivers.
- Massachusetts: 1,000+ wrong-way citations in 2 years; ~5 new detection locations/week.
- NHTSA PE25012: ~2.88M Teslas; 58 incidents at opening (14 crashes, 23 injuries).

## Original contribution (novel analysis)
The article will compute and present the **near-miss ratio**: Massachusetts logged 1,000+ wrong-way *events* in two years while fatal wrong-way crashes in the state number in the dozens per year — implying the fatal crashes are a small fraction of total wrong-way entries. Combined with MassDOT's "most drivers self-correct," this reframes wrong-way driving from a rare freak event to a routinely occurring error with a low but catastrophic conversion rate. The article will also juxtapose the three human risk factors (drunk, old, alone) against the new fourth factor: software (FSD wrong-way probe).

## Limitations (to state in article)
- AAA's figures cover **divided highways only** (2015–2018, published 2021) — dated and excludes undivided roads.
- The decade figures (4,164 crashes / 5,730 deaths) come from a **vendor press release** (TAPCO, a detection-equipment maker with an interest in the problem looking big), not a peer-reviewed analysis; treat as directional.
- "Most drivers self-correct" is MassDOT's characterization of its detector data, not a published national statistic.
- FARS has no universal "wrong-way" flag; AAA's method (divided-highway head-on analysis) is an approximation.

## Strongest counterargument (to state at full strength)
Wrong-way deaths are ~1.3% of the ~39,000 annual US traffic deaths. Speeding kills ~10,000+, impairment ~12,000+. Every dollar Massachusetts spends on wrong-way detection trailers is a dollar not spent on pedestrian infrastructure, rural road widening, or interlock programs that would save more lives per dollar. The rational-VMT-maximizing critic says this is a vivid, terrifying, statistically minor problem — and the detection network is security theater with solar panels.

## Actionable takeaways
- If you realize YOU are the wrong-way driver: pull to the shoulder, stop, hazards on, call 911 — do not try to whip a U-turn across lanes at speed.
- If you see headlights coming at you: slow down, move right (wrong-way drivers tend to hug "their" right = your left... actually advise: move right, flash lights, call 911).
- Older drivers/families: night-driving refresher, avoid unfamiliar highway interchanges after dark.
- Passengers: speak up — 87% of wrong-way drivers were alone; a second pair of eyes is a safety device.
- Check whether your state maps wrong-way hotspots (Massachusetts now must publish annual high-risk location reports).
