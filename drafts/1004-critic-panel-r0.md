## 1. General Editor — 9.0/10
Hook lands immediately ("safer on paper and deadlier on the road") and the inverted pyramid holds: paradox → exposure math → rival controls → mechanism → counterargument → limitations → actionable close. The COVID objection folded into the controls paragraph rather than standing alone; the merge reads naturally because both paragraphs make the same move (rivals as the control group). The pull-stat (2.0×) tees up the real number (5×) without spoiling it. Deduction: the leap from impairment stats to the pandemic objection inside one paragraph asks the reader to track two rebuttals at once; it works, but a breath between them would be kinder.

## 2. Voice (Mia Crumplezone) — 9.2/10
Signature moves present: the coroner line, "Not a drunk-driver story — a car story," "the volume cars never earned it," the closing "Usually, it wasn't yours." Direct, forensic, wry without cruelty. No banned phrases, no AI-slop tells. The "Let's talk about" opener is conversational in a way that suits the desk. Deduction: "Steel yourself" was cut in revision and its absence is felt slightly — the pandemic pivot now arrives without its old warning flare.

## 3. Ethics — 8.8/10
Fair to the manufacturer: the piece distinguishes trims (GT's LEDs are praised, not buried), cites primary sources for every factual claim, and the limitations paragraph discloses what the data cannot prove. No victims named or identifiable. The original draft's "the bodies are in the LX" asserted trim-level deaths FARS cannot confirm; revised to "the volume cars never earned it" plus an explicit FARS-has-no-trim caveat in the limitations paragraph. Remaining risk is inherent to the genre: a 5× relative-risk framing will read as "the Forte kills" no matter how careful the caveats. The disclaimer and methodology note mitigate; they cannot eliminate.

## 4. Social — 9.0/10
Headline is contrarian and specific — awards vs. body count is a built-in share tension. The 2.0× pull stat is clean for cards; the 5× exposure-adjusted figure is the quote-tweet number and it appears in paragraph two, early enough to travel. The "ask which trim earned it" closer is actionable advice that survives excerpting. Deduction: the piece has no single-sentence "nut graf" quotable under 200 characters for platforms that punish context; the closest candidate ("The car IIHS tested is not the car most people bought") is good but sits mid-article.

## 5. Legal — 8.9/10
All factual claims about Kia are either cited to primary sources (IIHS ratings via the site's own prior investigation, award citations quoted verbatim from MotorTrend/The News Wheel) or presented as the author's disclosed calculation with methodology in the disclaimer. The 5× figure is framed as "roughly" with the proxy disclosed. No allegation of fraud or concealment — the piece argues the award system measures the wrong configuration, which is criticism of IIHS methodology as much as of Kia. The trim-inference revision (r0 fix) removed the one statement that could have read as an unsupported factual assertion about where deaths occurred. Standard opinion-and-true-facts posture; no red flags.

## 6. Rigor — 9.3/10
Novel contribution verified: the model-year-split exposure adjustment (FARS 2014–2023 × GoodCarBadCar sales) appears original to this piece. Three rival controls (Elantra 0.49, Civic 0.31, Corolla 0.47) rule out segment-wide effects; the impairment control (20.1% vs 18.6–20.4%) rules out the drunk-driver confound; the pandemic counterargument is addressed via the same rival-control logic. Math checked: 296,535/220,475 = 1.345 ✓; (182/296535/3.2)/(91/220475/12) ≈ 5.6, reported as "roughly five" ✓; 45 mph = 20.1 m/s, 22.5/20.1 = 1.12 s ✓. Limitations paragraph covers FARS fatal-only scope, sales-as-fleet proxy, scrappage noise, demographic confound, and trim inference. Deduction: exposure years (12 vs 3) are approximate midpoints rather than computed from registration data; disclosed as approximate, but a sensitivity note (±1 year moves 5× to ~4–7×) would have been stronger.

## 7. Data Presentation — 9.1/10
Every number is sourced inline or in the disclaimer, which helpfully tabulates all raw model-year counts (Forte 91/182, Elantra 364/179, Civic 729/225, Corolla 278/592... correction: Corolla 592/278). The 22.5 m → 1.12 s → 2.5 s reaction-standard chain is the piece's best data moment: three numbers that build a physical intuition. Deduction: the disclaimer lists "Corolla 592/278" in new/old order while the body gives 0.47 (new/old) — consistent, but a reader cross-checking must notice the order convention; an explicit "(new/old)" tag would remove the ambiguity.
