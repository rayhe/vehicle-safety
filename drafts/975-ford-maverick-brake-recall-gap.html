15 Recalls, Zero for the Brakes: The 2024 Maverick Hybrid's Blind Spot

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
September 23, 2026
☕ 4 min read

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better. NHTSA's defect-petition resume for investigation DP26007, opened August 24, lists exactly one incident behind its probe of the 2024 Ford Maverick Hybrid: an owner who says the truck lost antilock and regenerative braking and crashed, one incident, one crash, zero injuries, which is the entire official evidentiary basis for a federal investigation covering an estimated 82,040 vehicles.

8
Brake complaints on the 2024 Maverick in NHTSA's own database, versus the 1 incident counted in the petition resume

Meanwhile, the same agency's complaints database, queried this month, already holds eight more brake complaints on the 2024 Maverick, three of them crash-flagged (37.5%). Both numbers are right, in a sense: the petition resume is a screening snapshot, the complaints database is the living record ODI is supposed to screen, and the gap between the two is the story.

Start with the petitioner: one owner, four repair attempts, more than 50 days off the road. Fault codes C052E and U0418 pulled from Ford's own OASIS diagnostic system, according to the petition. The core allegation: intermittent loss of antilock and regenerative braking, the handoff between the electric motor slowing the truck and the hydraulic brakes taking over, and that the 2024 model year was improperly excluded from related brake-control software recalls.

Then open the complaints file. I pulled every 2024 Maverick complaint NHTSA's API returns, 72 of them, and eight mention the service brakes, a full 11% of all complaints on this truck. The petitioner himself shows up three times (ODI 11715308, 11726612, and 11731032, the last a supplemental filing explicitly requesting the investigation). Around him: a pedal that sank to the floor at 470 miles, no warning lights, no fluid leaks. A daughter who rear-ended another car at roughly 35 mph after what she described as a brake-booster failure, the knee airbag the only one to fire. A driveway rollback that ended against a neighbor's parked car while the pedal hissed and went soft. These are self-reported, unverified owner accounts, but they are also precisely the kind of reports ODI exists to read.

0
Brake-related recalls among the 15 NHTSA recalls issued for the 2024 Maverick

Now the number that stopped me cold. The 2024 Maverick has been recalled fifteen times: fuel tanks, tail lights, trailer brake controllers, transmission software, lower ball joints, engine block heaters, and zero of them touch the service brakes. A truck can be recalled for its windshield wiper motor and its engine block heater while the system that stops it carries an open federal defect petition. Exhaustive paperwork, everywhere except the one place it matters.

Zoom out one more level and the boundary pattern gets unmistakable. September 2022: Ford recalls 2022 Mavericks (campaign 22V730) because the ABS module could illuminate the brake lights with no foot on the pedal. March 2025: it redid the fix on trucks repaired incorrectly the first time (25V124). November 2025: it recalled certain 2025 Mavericks and Escapes (25V787) after a brake-booster ECU cover could overheat the circuit board and kill antilock braking, stability control, traction control, or brake power assist, a recall whose population was two vehicles, just two. And now DP26007, covering 82,040 2024 hybrids, sits in evaluation. Four filings, four model-year slices, one family of brake-control hardware. A recall population is a legal artifact drawn around build dates and supplier batches. The engineering boundary is wherever the shared hardware went, and on a hybrid platform the module that blends regenerative and friction braking went everywhere.

2 vs 82,040
Vehicles in Ford's 2025 brake-booster recall versus the estimated population of the 2024 Maverick Hybrid now under petition review

Fairness requires the other side of the ledger, because Ford's boundaries are not obviously drawn in crayon. The 2022 ABS-module recall was a brake-light illumination software error, an FMVSS 108 lamps violation, not a braking-loss defect, so excluding 2024 from it is arguably engineering-correct. The 2025 booster recall was a supplier-batch cover defect physically incapable of affecting 2024 trucks. And the petitioner's fault codes may reflect a single-vehicle wiring or module fault. One persistent owner is not a defect population, and eight complaints against well over 100,000 2024 Mavericks sold is a thin signal. ODI denies most petitions, and it may well deny this one.

But here's what the denials wouldn't erase: the screening summary counts one incident while the agency's own database already holds more, and the model year with an open brake petition has fifteen recalls and not one for brakes. If DP26007 dies quietly, it dies with the paperwork having been precise about everything except the hardware in question.

If you own a 2024 Maverick Hybrid, nothing requires action today: there is no recall, no free repair. What you can do: stop trusting a clean model-year recall search and check your actual VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, because the investigations and manufacturer communications a model-year search hides are exactly the ones that matter here. If your truck throws ABS, traction, or brake-assist warnings, or shudders during the regen-to-friction handoff, get it documented in writing at the dealer every single time, even when the shop can't reproduce it on the spot. Intermittent electrical faults get dismissed at 20,000 miles and recalled at 200,000. And if a fault recurs, file the NHTSA complaint yourself, because the database is the screen. Feed it.

What this doesn't prove: the API's 72 complaints are the record set NHTSA returned on September 23, 2026, and may not capture everything the agency holds. Complaints are self-reported and unverified; none of this establishes a defect, and DP26007 remains an open petition, not a finding. That two-vehicle population for 25V787 comes from the Part 573 filing as reported by The Auto Wire, not from my direct read of it. I did not prove ODI's screening missed the additional complaints, only that its public resume summary predates reports now visible in its own database.
