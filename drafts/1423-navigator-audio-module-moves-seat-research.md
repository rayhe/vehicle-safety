# Research Notes — #1423: Your Navigator's Stereo Can Move Your Seat. Ford's Fix Is to Delete the Feature.

## Angle (1 sentence)

Ford just recalled 36,994 Lincoln Navigators because the rear *audio* module can command the front passenger seat to slide, tilt, or recline on its own, and the fix is not to repair the logic but to delete the chauffeur-mode feature entirely — the second time in eight months Ford has fixed a self-moving Navigator seat by removing a feature instead of fixing it.

## Verified facts (3+ primary sources)

1. **The new recall — 26V691 (NHTSA recalls API, api.nhtsa.gov, queried November 7, 2026 — primary source):** Ford Motor Company is recalling certain 2025-2027 Lincoln Navigator vehicles. "The Rear Audio Control Module (RACM) may inadvertently command the chauffeur seat feature, causing the front passenger seat to adjust forward, rearward, or recline unexpectedly without occupant input." Consequence: "A front passenger seat that moves unexpectedly can contact second-row passengers, or force the front passenger into an unsafe seating position, increasing the risk of injury." Remedy: "Dealers will update the accessory protocol interface module (APIM) and rear audio control module (RACM) software to disable chauffeur mode, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed in November 2026." Ford's number: 26S77. Report received October 20, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from October 26, 2026.
2. **Population — 36,994 vehicles (U.S. DOT public dataset, via vehiclesafetyrecalls.com record for 26V691000, refreshed October 26, 2026):** "36,994 vehicles potentially affected." Component: Seats. Recall type: Vehicle.
3. **Canada — Transport Canada recall 2026510 (recalls-rappels.canada.ca, published October 20, 2026, updated October 28):** same defect, same remedy (update RACM and APIM software to disable chauffeur mode), approximately 2,292 vehicles in Canada. Adds one detail the U.S. record omits: "the seat may continue moving even if there's an obstruction."
4. **Chauffeur mode, defined (Lincoln owner's manual, via Get CyberTrucked, November 2026):** a feature that "allows occupants in the second row to adjust the front passenger seat's forward and backward position through rear touchscreen controls." The RACM is the module behind the rear-seat entertainment/audio controls. The fix disables the feature; it does not say ordinary front-passenger seat adjustments will be disabled.
5. **The March precedent — 26V120 / Ford 26S11 (NHTSA recalls API, queried November 7, 2026; Ford dealer letter RCMN-26V120-3146.pdf via static.nhtsa.gov — primary sources):** Ford recalled certain 2026 Lincoln Navigators because "the obstacle detection software for the driver seat may malfunction at low speeds, resulting in the seat reversing unexpectedly while driving." Population: 1,189 vehicles built November 26 – December 16, 2025, at Kentucky Truck Plant. The dealer letter explains the mechanism: the driver's seat module had not been programmed to cancel automatic reversal when the manual switch was operated or above 3 mph (5 km/h), so a seat that hit an obstacle could reverse all the way to the rearmost position, moving the driver away from the pedals. Remedy: update the driver's seat module software to remove the automatic reversal feature. Owner letters mailed March 13-27, 2026. Supplier of the module/software: Gentherm of Novi, Michigan. Investigation opened January 15, 2026, after Global Common Quality Indicator System reports; recall approved late February 2026. No crashes or injuries reported.
6. **The filing-day context (NHTSA recalls API, queried November 7, 2026):** October 20, 2026 was a crowded filing day. Ford filed 26V688 (11,623 Nautilus/Corsair hybrids, unexpected neutral shift), 26V689 (30,123 F-150/Super Duty tailgate cables), and 26V691 (this recall) the same day; Toyota filed 26V690 the same day (228,367 vehicles, seat back may unlatch during a crash). The Navigator seat recall is the only one of the four whose defect is a module commanding a system it has no business commanding.

## Computations (original to this piece)

- **Scale ratio:** 36,994 / 1,189 = **31.1x**. The October passenger-seat recall is thirty-one times the size of the March driver-seat recall.
- **2026 Navigator seat-recall total:** 36,994 + 1,189 = **38,183 vehicles** recalled in one calendar year for seats that move on their own, across two campaigns and both front seats.
- **Model-year coverage:** the new recall spans 2025, 2026, and 2027 — every model year of the redesigned fifth-generation Navigator (all-new for model year 2025). A buyer cannot dodge this one by picking a different year of the current generation.
- **Feature-deletion count:** both 2026 Navigator seat recalls were remedied by *removing* a feature (automatic seat reversal in March; chauffeur mode in October), not by correcting the underlying logic. Two for two.
- **Canada share:** 2,292 / (36,994 + 2,292) = **5.8%** of the North American population is Canadian; the U.S. recall is 94.2%.

## Limitations

- No crash, injury, or field-report count tied to 26V691 appears in the NHTSA record or the sources consulted. That is absence of a published count, not a claim of zero incidents. The March recall explicitly reported no crashes or injuries; do not transfer that claim to the October recall.
- The precise software failure sequence inside the RACM is not published. Transport Canada's notice and the NHTSA summary both describe the outcome, not the mechanism. The article must not invent a root cause (e.g., do not claim a specific signal, voltage fault, or CAN-bus message).
- The 36,994 figure is the population of potentially affected vehicles, not the number with the defect. NHTSA records do not publish a defect rate for this campaign.
- The owner-letter date ("expected to be mailed in November 2026") is a plan, not a completed mailing. Frame as expected.
- The Aviator/Explorer second-row easy-entry recall (26V-451, ~388,000 vehicles) is a related Ford seat-software story but a different defect (a switch that sticks) on different models; cite only as a one-line pattern note if at all, and do not fold its population into the Navigator totals.

## Strongest counterargument

The system is working as designed, and the fix is honest engineering. Ford found the defect, filed promptly, named the exact module, and the remedy is free and software-only — no dealer visit for parts, no hardware swap, no owner cost. Nobody has reported a crash or an injury from either Navigator seat recall, and the March recall's own record says so explicitly. Disabling chauffeur mode is the correct call: a convenience feature that lets a rear passenger slide the front seat is a luxury, not a safety system, and removing it eliminates the hazard entirely rather than patching around it. The "two recalls, two deleted features" framing is a narrative convenience — the two defects involve different modules (driver's seat module vs. rear audio control module), different seats, and different failure modes, and linking them implies a single systemic cause the record does not support. What they share is a manufacturer willing to delete a feature rather than defend it, which is arguably the right instinct. The article must say all of this.
