# Research — Article #846: Wheel-Off (Loose Lug Nuts as Projectiles)

## Angle
Wheels detach from moving vehicles and become high-speed projectiles. The person who dies is almost always in the OTHER vehicle. Federal data cannot count these deaths — FARS has no wheel-off code; NHTSA itself admits "our crash data are limited to the general category of tire failure."

## Kill test
- Fresh anchor: Aug 3, 2026 fatal wheel-separation crash, I-20 Ward County TX (Texas DPS), one month old. PASS.
- Uncovered topic on site (1003 slugs scanned: no wheel-off, lug, separation, runoff, blowout coverage). PASS.
- Novel angle: the asymmetry (victim in other lane) + the data invisibility (NHTSA can't count what it doesn't code). PASS.

Chevrolet 3500 westbound, front left tire and wheel separated due to mechanical failure, rolled across median into eastbound lanes, struck windshield and roof of Toyota 4Runner. Crash under investigation.

2. **Georgia DPS Fact Sheet #26 "Wheels in Flight"**: "In recent years, incidents of wheel separation have become a more frequent cause of Commercial Motor Vehicle accidents." "Most commonly result from improper tightening of wheel fasteners and wheel bearing failure — both are the result of improper maintenance practices." Inadequate tightening = failure to follow manufacturer's recommended wheel maintenance practices: torque wrench, proper tightening procedures, compatible components, removing paint/debris/oil/rust between fasteners, threads, mating surfaces.

## Original contribution
1. FARS has no wheel-off code: wheel-separation deaths are statistically invisible in federal fatality data. The data gap itself is the finding.
2. The asymmetry thesis: in every documented case (Odessa 2026, I-280 minivan, Miami school bus 1991), the dead person was in the other vehicle, not the vehicle that lost the wheel. Loose lug nuts are a hazard you inflict on strangers.

## Limitations
- No federal count of wheel-off deaths exists; cannot compute a per-mile fatality rate or compare against other causes.
- All cases are anecdotal/mechanistic; can't separate passenger vs. commercial vehicle risk from these sources.
- Georgia DPS fact sheet is undated (cites 1991 case; speaks to mechanism, not current incidence).

## Strongest counterargument
Most wheel-off events cause no injury at all — a wheel bouncing into an empty median is a story nobody hears. Impairment and speeding kill orders of magnitude more people, and singling out rare mechanical freaks could distort risk perception. The fix is also cheap and mostly someone else's job (the shop's torque wrench).

## Actionable insights
- After ANY tire service (rotation, new tires, brake work): re-torque lug nuts at 50–100 miles. This is standard shop practice and the single most effective prevention.
- Watch for warning signs: steering vibration at speed, a rattling/clunking from a wheel, or visibly crooked wheels.
- Keep the shop's torque spec in your glove box; cheap torque wrench + 10 minutes after a rotation.
- The check costs almost nothing; the failure mode is a 100-pound projectile crossing a median at highway speed.
