GM Fixed This Grille Once. Then the Fix Fell Off.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 23, 2026

On October 9, NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened recall query RQ26003 into the upper grille deflector on the 2022–2024 GMC Sierra 1500, an estimated 535,639 trucks. Owners filed 118 complaints of the wide trim bar across the top of the grille detaching while driving. Twenty-one of those complaints say it came off after the truck already had the recall repair.[1]

7.6×
The query covers 535,639 trucks while the original recall covered 70,768: roughly 13% of the population now under review.

A recall query is NHTSA's way of asking whether a recall actually worked. This one challenges the fix on both fronts: did it cover enough trucks, and does the remedy hold? The original campaign, 25V-060 filed February 6, 2025, applied only to 2022 Sierra 1500s with a chrome deflector built after the mid-cycle refresh, about 70,768 trucks.[2] Non-chrome 2022s and every 2023 and 2024 Sierra sat outside it.

GM's repair, per the owner letter, had dealers "reinforce or repair" the attachment points in about an hour, and specifically told them not to replace the grille unless necessary. Translation: the old clips got reinforcement, not replacement.[2] Twenty-one post-repair detachments suggest the reinforcement isn't surviving highway vibration, and with roughly 64% of recalled trucks repaired as of January 2026 (about 45,300 vehicles), that's hard to wave away as noise.[4]

Don't dismiss this as cosmetic. Eight attachment points hold the bar; when all eight fracture, the deflector leaves the truck at speed, sometimes preceded only by a rattle. ODI's summary lists the people at risk as the truck itself, nearby vehicles, motorcyclists, and pedestrians. A rigid bar at hood height on one of America's best-selling trucks is debris that matters most to the motorcyclist behind you.[1]

Fair is rare in this column, but a recall query is not a defect finding, zero crashes appear in ODI's summary, and 21 complaints is small against 45,300 repaired trucks. Many queries close with no action, but GM's timeline does it no favors: an employee flagged the defect through the Speak Up for Safety program in January 2024, GM closed the case that May for lack of a trend, and reopened it that December when warranty claims climbed. That is a threshold problem, and it is now NHTSA asking the questions.[2]

What to do. Walk around your truck and push gently on the top grille bar. If it shifts, buzzes, or rattles over bumps, get it looked at now, no letter required. If your truck already had the 25V-060 repair and the deflector loosens again, file it through NHTSA's reporting portal: owner complaints are what opened this query. If you drive a 2023–2024 or a non-chrome 2022 Sierra, you are not under any recall yet, so keep repair receipts. Used-truck shoppers: run the VIN through NHTSA's recall lookup and check the bar by hand before signing anything.[6]

Limitations: ODI complaint counts are self-reported and unverified, so this is not a detachment rate. No FARS fatal-crash data is implicated here, since none exists for this defect. Whether 2023–2024 trucks use different attachment hardware is unconfirmed; GM's claim that those trucks used parts outside the supplier's suspect window is precisely what the query is testing. The outcome, which could be an expanded recall, a revised remedy, or nothing, is months away.
