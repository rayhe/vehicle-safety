# Research Notes — 963: Poor Headlights, Used-Car Blind Spot

**Journalist:** Clara Rollover
**Article #:** 963
**Status:** research complete, proceeding to DRAFT

## Angle
Your car's headlights have a safety rating you've probably never seen, scored on a different scale by a different organization than the five-star crash score you do know. One in three rated vehicles scores Poor or Marginal. At Cadillac, it's four in five. And the fix is concentrated in new cars — the used market still carries the old numbers.

**Kill test:** Zero prior headlight coverage across 962 articles. Fresh news peg (ForCar brand analysis, Sept 2026). Novel angles: (1) headlights are the fastest-improving safety metric in IIHS history because IIHS tied them to Top Safety Pick — yet the improvement never reached the used lot; (2) trim-level trap — the rating you read may belong to a trim the car in front of you doesn't have; (3) brand divergence is uniquely American (GM/Stellantis top the bad list at 2x+ the market rate). **Verdict: proceed.**

## Primary sources (3+)

1. **ForCar / Denis Kataev, "A Third of Cars Have Headlights Rated Poor or Marginal. At Cadillac, Four in Five."** (Sept 2026) — analysis of 1,643 IIHS-rated vehicles: 665 Good, 436 Acceptable, 309 Poor, 233 Marginal. Poor or Marginal total: 542/1,643 = 33.0%. Brand table (min 25 rated vehicles): Cadillac 22/28 = 79%, Chevrolet 69/90 = 77%, GMC 35/46 = 76%, Jeep 48/78 = 62%, Chrysler 21/35 = 60%, all rated 542/1,643 = 33%. Model-year trend: 2022 24% (161 rated), 2023 15% (181), 2024 14% (198), 2025 8% (202), 2026 9% (181). Credit to IIHS Top Safety Pick award requirement driving the fix. URL: https://forcar.org/blog/headlights-rated-poor-by-brand/

2. **IIHS, first-ever headlight ratings news release** (March 30, 2016, mediaroom.iihs.org) — of 80+ headlight systems evaluated, only one (Toyota Prius v) earned Good. David Zuby quote: "If you're having trouble seeing behind the wheel at night, it could very well be your headlights and not your eyes that are to blame." Context: about half of traffic deaths occur in the dark or in dawn/dusk conditions; government (NHTSA FMVSS 108) standards are lab-based and allow huge variation in real-road illumination. URL: http://mediaroom.iihs.org/download/IIHS_news_033016.pdf

3. **IIHS headlight effectiveness + glare study** (via Automotive World summary) — vehicles with Good-rated headlights for visibility are involved in 19% fewer nighttime single-vehicle crashes and 23% fewer nighttime pedestrian crashes than vehicles with Poor-rated headlights (police-reported crash study). Progress: 51% of 2025-MY headlights rated Good vs ~1% in 2016; ~16% rated Marginal or Poor today vs 82% in 2016. Glare-related crashes have NOT increased despite complaints; the glare-producing vehicle is usually not in the crash, so attribution is hard. URL: https://www.automotiveworld.com/news-releases/iihs-headlight-complaints-abound-but-glare-related-crashes-havent-increased/

4. **mywebanswers / IIHS headlight overview (Sept 2026)** — only 46% of 2026-MY vehicles earn Good; ~17% Marginal/Poor. LED replacement assemblies $800-2,000 vs $50-200 halogen; LEDs last 15,000-25,000 hours vs 500-1,000 for halogen. Premium trim bundling: buyers who don't select the right package get inferior nighttime safety. ## Original contribution (STORY_GUIDE requirement)
1. **Velocity framing, computed:** Poor/Marginal rate fell from 82% of evaluated systems (2016) to 8% of 2025-MY vehicles — a ~90% relative reduction in nine model years. No other IIHS-rated safety metric improved that fast, and it happened because IIHS made Good headlights a Top Safety Pick requirement — i.e., an award criterion fixed in four years what regulation hadn't fixed in decades.
2. **Trim-trap arithmetic:** IIHS rates fitted hardware, so a model can carry two different ratings across trims/packages. The rating attached to a nameplate in a used listing is meaningless unless matched to the exact year + trim + headlight package — a check that takes two minutes on iihs.org/ratings but that essentially no used-car buyer performs.
3. **Brand risk multiplier:** Cadillac buyers face 79% bad-headlight odds vs 33% market average — a 2.4x exposure to the exact condition IIHS links to 19% more nighttime single-vehicle crashes.

## Limitations (dedicated, honest)
- FARS doesn't encode headlight ratings, so the article cannot compute fatality rates per headlight grade — the 19%/23% figures come from IIHS's police-reported crash study, not FARS, and police reports undercount glare involvement.
- IIHS tests headlights as-delivered from the dealer. Owner-replaced bulbs, aftermarket LEDs, and mis-aimed lamps (very common on used cars) are outside the rating.
- ForCar's brand table uses a 25-vehicle minimum; low-volume brands are excluded, and the dataset (1,643 vehicles) is a snapshot, not a longitudinal panel.
- Nighttime crash reduction figures are associations, not randomized trials — vehicles with better headlights may correlate with wealthier, more cautious owners.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The story above is mostly a story about the past. New-car buyers in 2026 face a 9% bad-headlight rate, down from 82% a decade ago — the problem this article describes has largely been solved where the buyer has leverage. The trim trap is also partly a rational market outcome: headlights bundled into premium packages reflect real cost ($500-2,000 per vehicle for LED/ADB systems), and forcing base trims to carry them raises prices for the most price-sensitive buyers. If you're buying new, the IIHS award system already did the work for you.

## Actionable insight
Shopping used (2016-2021 especially): look up the EXACT year, model, and trim on iihs.org/ratings before the night test drive — the headlight rating never appears in listings and never changes with mileage. If the car has Marginal/Poor headlights, budget for a dealer headlight aim check and fresh bulbs; on the worst offenders, the option-package headlights are a legitimate negotiating lever.
