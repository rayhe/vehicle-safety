# Research: NHTSA Closes Jeep Parked-Fire Probe (Sep 29, 2026)

**Article #1032** | Journalist: Axle McScatter (Data Visualization Editor) | Kicker: By The Numbers
Slug: `1032-jeep-parked-fire-probe-closed-108m`

## Story in one line
On September 29, 2026, NHTSA closed its two-year investigation into Jeep Wranglers and Gladiators catching fire while parked with the ignition off — because Stellantis's June recall already covers the population. The park-outside warning still applies to unrepaired vehicles, and the recall population (1,076,999) is 38% bigger than the probe ever examined.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Announced yesterday (Reuters, Sep 29, 2026). A federal probe into spontaneous vehicle fires closing is news.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. Queue covered the June recall announcement (#981, park-outside guidance) and has a "closed the file" template (#1012, Odyssey airbags) — but this story's angle is the closure arithmetic: the recall population outgrew the probe by ~295,000 vehicles, and the closure changes nothing for owners. The article's original math: probe 781,500 -> recall 1,076,999 (+37.8%); fire rate ~4.7 per 100,000 vehicles; and the Part 573 timeline oddity (first field report dated July 13, 2019, predating the US build window that starts June 24, 2020).
- **Data legs?** Yes. Reuters (Sep 29 closure; Jun 9 recall), NHTSA campaign 26V363000 (government primary), FCA Part 573 Safety Recall Report (company filing via NHTSA; mirrored publicly), FCA June 9, 2026 statement.

## Key facts (with attribution)

1. **NHTSA closed its preliminary evaluation on Sep 29, 2026** into Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator underhood fires while parked with ignition off. Opened Sep 2024 covering ~781,500 vehicles (2021-2023). Closure rationale: the recall "addressed the vehicles and conditions examined in the probe"; NHTSA will continue monitoring field performance. (Reuters, Sep 29, 2026)

2. **NHTSA campaign 26V363000** (Chrysler/FCA US, LLC, announced June 9, 2026): 1,076,999 US vehicles — ~787,887 Wrangler + ~289,112 Gladiator, model years 2021-2025, built June 24, 2020 - Dec 2, 2024 (Wrangler) / Aug 18, 2020 - Dec 2, 2024 (Gladiator). Includes Wrangler 4xe plug-in hybrid. (NHTSA 26V363000; FCA statement)

3. **Defect**: electrical connection issue in the electric-hydraulic power steering pump wiring. A loose or improperly seated connector raises electrical resistance; at high current that generates heat that can melt the connector and ignite nearby material. **Fires can occur with the ignition off — a parked car.** (NHTSA recall summary; FCA Part 573 report)

4. **Fire counts**: FCA aware of 63 customer assistance records, 0 warranty claims, 72 field reports (35 confirmed to originate at the EHPSP interface), 12 other service records potentially related, across all markets, receipts from July 13, 2019 to March 28, 2026. Zero accidents, 1 injury potentially related. (FCA Part 573 Safety Recall Report 26V363) — Note discrepancy: openclassactions/NHTSA cites "51 fires and one injury likely related."

5. **Consumer advisories in the Part 573**: **Do Not Drive + Park Outside**. FCA advised owners to park away from structures and other vehicles until the remedy is obtained. (Part 573; FCA statement)

6. **Remedy**: free dealer inspection and, if necessary, repair/replace wiring harness and/or electric-hydraulic power steering pump. Owner notification began ~July 9, 2026. (Part 573)

7. **Global scope**: ~1.08M US + ~106,000 Canada + ~23,000 Mexico + ~125,000 other markets = 1.3M+ worldwide. (Reuters, Jun 9, 2026)

8. **Defect determination date**: May 28, 2026 (FCA Vehicle Regulations Committee). Remedy availability anticipated by July 2026.

## Original contribution (novel analysis)

Three Axle-style calculations:

a) **The probe-to-recall growth**: 781,500 (Sep 2024 probe) -> 1,076,999 (Jun 2026 recall) = +295,499 vehicles, +37.8%. The closure was based on a recall covering vehicles NHTSA's probe never examined. Stated carefully: expansion covered 2021-2025 vs probe's 2021-2023.

b) **Fire rate**: 51 fires / 1,076,999 vehicles = ~4.7 fires per 100,000 vehicles over the defect window. Frame with the caveat that this counts only reported fires attributed to the defect, and each fire could involve a garage/house, which is why the park-outside advisory matters even at a low per-vehicle rate.

c) **The 2019 anomaly**: FCA's own Part 573 says field reports were received starting July 13, 2019 — nearly a year before the US recall population's build start (June 24, 2020). That means either an early report outside the population, a misattribution, or the defect reaches older vehicles. FCA's 35 confirmed-at-interface figure vs 72 field reports is also a gap: less than half of field reports are confirmed to originate at the pump interface. Present as questions, not conclusions.

## Strongest counterargument
- 51 fires across 1.08 million vehicles is ~0.0047% — an individual owner's fire probability is tiny. The park-outside guidance is "out of an abundance of caution" (FCA's words), not a statistical emergency.
- NHTSA closing the probe is routine process: once a recall covers the population, keeping the PE open adds nothing. It is not an exoneration and not a scandal — it's how the system works.
- Stellantis expanded the recall beyond the probe's population voluntarily; the expansion to 2024-2025 models is a sign the system worked, not that it failed.
- FCA determined the defect and filed the recall within ~11 days of the NHTSA notice (June 4 -> recall announced June 9); remedy promised by July 2026. That's a fast response by recall standards.

## Limitations
- Recall remedy completion rate is not yet public for 26V363 (quarterly reports not yet filed/reported as of Sep 30, 2026); we cannot say how many of the 1.08M have been fixed. Do NOT claim a completion number.
- The 51-fire figure comes from secondary reporting of NHTSA data; the primary Part 573 cites 72 field reports / 35 confirmed at interface. Report both, label which is which.
- No FARS connection: FARS tracks fatal crashes; these fires produced zero accidents and 1 injury per FCA. This is a recall/regulatory story, not a fatality-data story — say so explicitly.
- The closure announcement is from Reuters quoting NHTSA; the ODI closing resume was not independently read for this draft.

## Actionable insight
If you own a 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for campaign 26V363000 (FCA recall 21D). If open, park outside and away from structures until the dealer inspects the power-steering pump wiring — free repair. If buying used, an unrepaired recall vehicle can still be sold to you; the VIN check is the only screen.
