# Research: #1025 — GM L87 V8: The Fix Was Thicker Oil

**Slug:** 1025-gm-l87-engine-fix-failed-ea26005
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — vehicle design analysis, safety tech, slightly judgmental about bad design)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Working headline:** "GM's Fix for 600,000 Failing V8s Was Thicker Oil. 499 of Them Failed Anyway."

## Kill test
**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** In April 2025 GM recalled nearly 600,000 trucks and SUVs because machining defects in the 6.2L L87 V8 could seize the engine and kill propulsion at speed. For the 97% of vehicles that passed a diagnostic scan, the federally approved remedy was changing the oil to a thicker grade. On August 20, 2026, NHTSA opened Engineering Analysis EA26005 into 997,743 vehicles, because 499 owners reported engine failures *after* the recall repair — including 26 whose engines had been fully replaced.

**Challenge:** After 1,000+ articles, is this just recall news? No. Three things make it novel: (1) the remedy-effectiveness story is almost never the headline — the "oil change as safety fix" for a crankshaft machining defect is the kind of engineering decision Mia Crumplezone's beat exists to interrogate; (2) the post-remedy failure counts (473 oil-remedy + 26 full replacements) mean even the "real" fix failed measurably; (3) 191 failures involve engines built *after* GM's stated supplier process-improvement date, which undercuts the recall's entire root-cause narrative (a closed March 2021–May 2024 production window). The regulatory escalation ladder — PE → recall → recall query → engineering analysis — is a case study in a fix failing in public.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Key numbers for the article
- **597,571** US vehicles recalled Apr 2025 (25V-274; GM campaign N252494000); 721,000 worldwide.
- **28,102** field complaints reviewed before recall; **14,332** alleged loss of propulsion; 12 crashes, 12 minor injuries, 42 fires.
- Production window: engines built **Mar 1, 2021 – May 31, 2024**; supplier process improvements claimed before **June 2024**.
- Remedy split: **~3%** estimated to need engine replacement (failed P0016 scan); **~97%** got 0W-20 → 0W-40 oil change.
- Recall Query RQ26001 (Jan 2026): **36** VOQs alleging remedy failures.
- Engineering Analysis EA26005 (Aug 20, 2026): **997,743** vehicles, 2021–2026 MY; **499** ODI post-remedy complaints (473 oil remedy, 26 replacement engines); **6,953** post-remedy complaints to GM; **191** failures in post-window engines.
- Post-remedy failure rate on ODI complaints: 499 / 597,571 ≈ **0.084%**; GM's internal count: 6,953 / 597,571 ≈ **1.16%** — a 14x gap between manufacturer-reported and regulator-received complaints.

## Original contribution (novel analysis)
1. **The undercount ratio, again:** The original recall took 14,000+ owner complaints to happen (gmauthority). Now GM itself reports 6,953 post-remedy failure complaints while ODI has only 499 — the same 14x manufacturer-to-regulator reporting gap, now on the remedy itself. If history rhymes, the remedy is failing at a rate far above what the 499 headline number suggests.
2. **The replacement engines failed too:** 26 of the 499 post-remedy failures were in vehicles that received *full engine replacements* — the remedy's strongest branch. The failure mode survived even a new rotating assembly in those cases (cause unconfirmed; could be replacement-engine quality, installation, or unrelated failures — but the number is in the federal filing).
3. **The 191 post-window failures undercut the root-cause narrative:** The recall's stated cause was a closed supplier manufacturing defect (Mar 2021–May 2024), with process improvements claimed before June 2024. Engines built after that window are failing at NHTSA's doorstep anyway. Either the defect window is wider than GM said, or the "fix" was never the whole story.

## Limitations (to state in article)
- VOQs are **self-reported and unverified** — 499 complaints is not 499 confirmed failures; some may involve unrelated engine problems.
- **No public data on remedy completion rate** — we don't know how many of the 597,571 recalled vehicles actually received the remedy, so failure-rate denominators are estimates.
- **GM's 6,953 figure vs ODI's 499** likely reflects different counting (dealership contacts, duplicates, repeat visits), not necessarily 6,953 distinct engine failures.
- NHTSA has **not concluded** a defect exists in the remedy; EA is an investigation, not a finding, and EAs routinely close without new recalls.
- The 26 replacement-engine failures and 191 post-window failures have **no published teardown data** — root cause is unconfirmed.

## Strongest counterargument (to state at full strength)
499 complaints across 997,743 vehicles is 0.05% — a fraction of a fraction. Thicker oil is a legitimate engineering mitigation for marginal bearing clearances: it raises the hydrodynamic film strength where the defect is worst, and GM did replace every engine that failed the P0016 scan. Post-window failures could be unrelated wear in a million-vehicle population of hard-worked truck engines. And NHTSA escalations like this are routine process — the agency opens Engineering Analyses all the time and closes most of them without demanding a thing. The feds are asking questions; they have not found an answer.

## Actionable takeaways
- **Check your VIN** at nhtsa.gov/recalls for recall 25V-274 (GM campaign N252494000). The investigation is engine-specific, not model-specific: a Silverado with the 5.3L L84 is not involved.
- **Identify your engine:** RPO code **L87** on the glovebox sticker (or "6.2L EcoTec3 V8" on the window sticker).
- **Warning signs** per GM's own filing: knocking or banging, check-engine light, hesitation, abnormal shifting, reduced propulsion, no-start. Don't nurse a knocking L87 down the highway to "see if it clears up."
- **Keep the paperwork:** recall completion records, dealer inspection results, oil-change receipts, dates and mileage when symptoms appeared — exactly what NHTSA's file asks owners to preserve.
- If symptoms are severe, **tow it**; a seized V8 at 70 mph is a loss-of-propulsion event, which is the entire reason this is a safety file and not a warranty file.
