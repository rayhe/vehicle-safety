# Research — #1272: Congress wants to repeal DC's motorcycle helmet law; DC is the test case for a national rollback

**Journalist:** Axle McScatter | **Kicker:** Investigation
**Slug:** `1272-ride-in-freedom-dc-helmet-repeal`
**Headline:** "Congress Picked the One City It Controls to Kill Helmet Laws. DC's Motorcycle Deaths Just Tripled."

## What happened / the data

**The bill.** Rep. Derrick Van Orden (R-WI) and Sen. Tim Sheehy (R-MT) introduced the "Ride in Freedom Act" on August 6, 2026: H.R. 10067 in the House and S. 5274 in the Senate. It would prohibit Washington, DC from requiring protective helmets for motorcycle operators and passengers age 18 and older, effectively converting DC's universal (all-rider) law into an under-18-only law matching Wisconsin's. The House bill was referred to the House Oversight Committee; as of September 4, 2026 it had no House cosponsors. Neither sponsor represents DC residents, who have no voting member of Congress.

**The stated strategy.** Van Orden's spokeswoman Grace Kim told the Washington Post: "D.C. is an important place to start. If Congress can change the policy here, it opens the door to having a larger conversation about the federal government's role in helmet mandates across the country." Van Orden, a Harley rider who rides to Capitol Hill votes and co-chairs the Congressional Motorcycle Caucus, calls a motorcycle "a symbol of freedom" and says riders "should have the choice to wear a helmet or not."

**DC's body count, right now.** Six people died in DC motorcycle crashes in 2026, versus two in all of 2025, per WTTG/Fox 5 DC reporting (a tripling). DC's registered motorcycle fleet is tiny (4,610 vehicles in the NHTSA/IIHS 2023 tabulation), which already made its per-registration fatal-crash rate the chart's outlier; six deaths against that fleet implies roughly 13 deaths per 10,000 registered motorcycles, about 1.8x the 2023 national average of 7.1. Small-N caveat applies: one crash moves the DC rate by ~2.2 points, so treat the level as noisy and the direction (tripled year-on-year) as the signal.

**The national baseline.** NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts 2024 Data: Motorcycles (DOT HS 813 824, 2026): 6,228 riders killed in 2024, injuries up 5% year-on-year; Florida leads the nation. IIHS Fatality Facts 2024: motorcycle deaths are up 23% since 2019. NHTSA's 2023 rate figures: motorcyclists die at 31.39 per 100M VMT versus 1.13 for car occupants, roughly 29x. NHTSA estimates helmets cut rider fatality risk by roughly 22-42% and brain-injury risk by 41-69%.

**What repeals actually do (the literature).** The Community Guide systematic review of universal helmet laws (PMC6918948): enacting a universal law raises helmet use by a median 49 percentage points and cuts fatalities by a median 32%; repealing one drops helmet use by a median 41 points and raises fatalities by a median 42% (20 study arms). Natural experiments: Texas repeal, helmet use -30%, fatalities +21%; Kentucky +50% deaths after its 1998 repeal; Louisiana +100% after its 1999 repeal. Syracuse University Lerner Center (1999-2019): universal-law states ran a 33% lower age-adjusted head-fatality rate than no-law/partial-law states, every one of 21 years.

**The map.** As of April 2026, 17 states plus DC have universal helmet laws (National Safety Council); 47 states have some form of helmet law (GHSA); only Illinois, Iowa, and New Hampshire have none. DC's neighbors Maryland and Virginia both have universal laws, so a DC repeal would create a helmet-optional island inside a universal-law region.

**The history.** In 1967 the federal government required states to enact universal helmet laws to qualify for highway safety funds; by 1975 all but three states had complied. In 1976 Congress revoked the penalty authority, and states began weakening their laws to young/novice riders only. The Ride in Freedom Act inverts that history: instead of using federal leverage to spread helmet laws, Congress would use its power over DC to delete one.

**Pushback.** DC Council Member Charles Allen, chair of the Council's Transportation Committee, called the bill "an unserious and unsafe proposal." IIHS director of statistical services Eric Teoh: "Weakening that will result in people dying." The Motorcycle Riders Foundation supports the bill; its VP Jay Jackson argues DC is where "freedom of choice" belongs at "the pinnacle."

## Limitations

- The bill is in committee with no House cosponsors as of September 4, 2026; it may never reach a vote. The article covers a strategy and its predicted effect, not an enacted law.
- DC's 2026 death count (6 vs 2) is small-N: one crash swings the implied rate by ~2.2 points per 10,000 registered. The tripling is direction, not a stable rate.
- Helmet-use status of the six 2026 DC victims is not public in the cited reporting; the article does NOT claim helmets would have saved any specific victim.
- Repeal-effect medians come from state-level studies (Texas, Kentucky, Louisiana, etc.), not a city-level repeal; DC's small fleet and commuter influx (Maryland/Virginia riders) limit direct transfer.
- The 2024 national file is NHTSA's Annual Report File, subject to slight revision with the 2025 annual report; injury counts are sample estimates, not tallies.
- The ~13-per-10,000 DC implied rate divides 2026 deaths by the 2023 registered fleet (4,610); the fleet size may have changed. Labeled illustrative wherever used.

## Actionable insights (hard gate)

- **Wear a DOT FMVSS 218 helmet regardless of what the law says.** The standard requires a hard shell, thick rigid lining, strong chin strap, and 105 degrees of peripheral vision per side. A helmet law repeal changes the ticket, not the physics.
- **Beware novelty helmets with counterfeit DOT stickers.** They meet no standard and fail exactly like wearing nothing. Snell certification (sticker inside) is the stricter voluntary check.
- **If you ride into DC from Maryland or Virginia, the border is the trap.** Both neighbors require helmets for all riders; a DC repeal would make the District the one place on your commute where the law stops requiring the thing that saves your life. Keep the helmet on across the line.
- **For DC residents: the decision is not yours to vote on.** The bill's sponsors answer to Wisconsin and Montana voters. The local lever is the DC Council and the District's nonvoting delegate, plus the House Oversight Committee where H.R. 10067 sits.
- **Check the medical-insurance exemption trap before relying on it.** Partial-law states that let adults ride helmetless with insurance (Florida, Michigan, Missouri models) leave riders with bills the policy does not cover; the exemption is a paperwork shield, not a medical one.
