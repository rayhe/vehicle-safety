# Research Notes: GM Sedan Lineage — Cavalier → Cobalt → Cruze

## Angle
GM's compact sedan lineage tells a three-act story: the Cavalier was bad (2.43), the Cobalt was a catastrophe (5.10), and the Cruze was genuinely good (0.63). Then GM killed the Cruze in 2019 and exited sedans entirely. The company finally built a sedan that didn't kill people — and discontinued it.

## FARS Data (Primary Source)

### Death Rates per 100M VMT (sedans with 400+ deaths)
- Chevrolet Cavalier (1982-2005): 1,225 deaths, rate 2.43, fleet 437,500
- Chevrolet Cobalt (2005-2010): 1,540 deaths, rate 5.10, fleet 262,500
- Chevrolet Cruze (2011-2019): 638 deaths, rate 0.63, fleet 875,000

### Key Ratios
- Cavalier → Cobalt: 2.1x WORSE (2.43 → 5.10)
- Cobalt → Cruze: 8.1x BETTER (5.10 → 0.63)
- Cavalier → Cruze: 3.9x BETTER overall (2.43 → 0.63)

### Cruze Ranking Among All Sedans (400+ deaths)
- Cruze (0.63) is the 4th safest sedan in the FARS database
- Only Kia Forte (0.40), Toyota Prius (0.55), and Kia Optima (0.58) are lower
- Sedan class average: ~1.15
- The Cruze beats the Camry (2.03), Civic (2.25), Accord (3.07), Corolla (1.85)

### Model Year Deaths
Cobalt: 2005(169), 2006(328), 2007(292), 2008(306), 2009(220), 2010(225) = 1,540
Cruze: 2011(49), 2012(84), 2013(71), 2014(103), 2015(64), 2016(69), 2017(102), 2018(55), 2019(34) = 631

### Impairment Comparison
- Cobalt: any 22.4%, alcohol 17.1%, drug 9.3%
- Cruze: any 20.2%, alcohol 15.1%, drug 8.9%
- Cavalier: any 22.4%, alcohol 16.2%, drug 9.5%
(Similar impairment rates across all three — the death rate difference isn't about drunk driving)

## Context
- GM ignition switch scandal (2014): 124 deaths attributed to defective ignition switches in Cobalt and Saturn Ion
- GM paid $900M criminal penalty, $595M civil settlement
- The Cobalt was recalled ~30M times across multiple campaigns
- The Cruze was built at Lordstown, Ohio (plant closed 2019)
- GM discontinued the Cruze in March 2019 as part of a shift to SUVs/trucks
- GM also killed the Malibu (2024), Impala (2020), Sonic (2020), Volt (2019)
- GM now sells zero sedans in the US
