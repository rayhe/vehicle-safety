# Research Notes — Article #901 (Rex Driverton, Investigation)

## Working angle
America's roads are the safest they've been in a decade for people inside cars — and the deadliest in 40 years for people on bicycles. In the same week that gap hit the news, the federal government was caught deleting bike-safety resources from the FHWA website and restricting bike-lane funding in BUILD grants. Two members of Congress wrote a letter on Sep 10, 2026 asking why. The question the data asks: why is the one road-user group still dying in record numbers the same group losing its federal safety programs?

## Kill test: is this genuinely newsworthy?
YES. (1) It is a genuine paradox: national traffic deaths at a decade-low pace, cyclist deaths at a 40-year high. (2) It has a fresh news peg: the Sep 10, 2026 Foushee/Thompson letter opposing FHWA's removal of bike safety resources and BUILD grant bike-lane funding restrictions — no Crash Report story has covered this. (3) It names a concrete policy mechanism (funding eligibility for bike lanes) tied to the specific demographic (1 in 5 traffic deaths is a walker or biker). Novel vs. the three prior cyclist stories (they covered AEB exclusion, the 2025 decline divergence, and sub-1.0 rate caveats — none covered the funding fight).

## Sources (primary, 5)

URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/trumps-transportation-department-reminds-drivers-that-speeding-catches-you
2. **Carscoops, Jul 2026 (citing Reuters/NHTSA data)** — bicyclist fatalities rose 4% in 2025 to 1,148 deaths, near highest levels in 40+ years; 2026 early report has no breakdown by user type yet. URL: https://www.carscoops.com/2026/07/nhtsa-traffic-deaths-decline/
3. **Rep. Valerie Foushee (NC-04) press release, Sep 10, 2026** — Foushee and Rep. Mike Thompson (CA-04) sent a letter to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and FHWA Administrator Sean McMaster opposing removal of bike safety resources (PSCM pages) from the FHWA website and the USDOT cancellation of grants including bicycling facilities plus stated intent to limit bike-lane funding eligibility within the BUILD Grant Program. Letter quotes: "more than 36,000 people killed on our streets in 2025. One in five traffic deaths is a person walking or biking. Since 2010, bicyclist fatalities in the U.S. have risen by 87 percent." 4. **League of American Bicyclists benchmarking (NHTSA data)** — 62% of bicyclist deaths with reported speed limits occurred on roads where the legal speed was 40 mph or more; the most common speed limit for the striking driver in a fatal bike crash was 45 mph; majority of the increase in deaths occurred on roads with speed limits of 40+ mph. URL: https://data.bikeleague.org/new-nhtsa-data-speed-data-shows-lethal-legal-speed-limits-involved-in-most-pedestrian-and-bicyclist-deaths/
5. **League of American Bicyclists on NHTSA NCAP proposal** — NHTSA's proposed pedestrian crashworthiness assessment will use automaker self-assessment, won't inform the 5-star rating, and excludes bicyclists entirely (despite the League urging their inclusion); the US is one of only two NCAP regions without vulnerable-road-user impact testing. URL: https://bikeleague.org/as-nhtsa-joins-rest-of-world-on-pedestrian-safety-tests-bicyclists-are-left-out/

## Actionable insight (HARD GATE requirement)
Rex's column must tell readers what to do: if you're a cyclist, your highest-risk roads are 45-mph arterials — the exact roads that rarely have protected bike lanes, and now federal money for those lanes is restricted; support is local now (city/county bike plans, not DC). If you're a driver, NHTSA's own new NCAP pedestrian-crashworthiness proposal doesn't even grade bicyclists and doesn't affect the 5-star rating — so "5 stars" tells you nothing about how your car treats a cyclist. Check the League's benchmarking data for your city's cyclist deaths before trusting the "safest year" headlines.
