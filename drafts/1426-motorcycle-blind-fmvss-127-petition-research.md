# Research — #1426: The Federal Brake Test Has Never Seen a Motorcycle. Riders Just Filed to Change That.

## The news hook

On October 29, 2026, the Motorcycle Riders Foundation and other national rider groups sent NHTSA and Congress a formal list of priorities demanding that motorcycle detection be written into the federal automatic emergency braking rules. The ask: dedicated sensor validation in FMVSS 127, with test protocols that make a car prove it can spot a two-wheeler in different riding and lighting conditions, not just hit a broad vehicle-detection benchmark. The timing is a response to NHTSA's late-October releases: the updated AV safety and lighting frameworks, including the FMVSS 108 NPRM (signed Oct 22, Federal Register Oct 26, docket NHTSA-2026-1519, comments due ~Nov 25).

## Verified facts (all traced)

1. **The filing (Oct 29, 2026).** The Motorcycle Riders Foundation and other national rider groups sent NHTSA and Congress a formal list of priorities. Ask: dedicated sensor validation written into FMVSS 127, with test protocols that make a car prove it can spot a two-wheeler in different riding and lighting conditions. Source: Biker Nation, Oct 29, 2026.
2. **The standing agenda behind it.** MRF's 2026 Legislative Priorities (adopted Sept 2025, Meeting of the Minds, Shreveport): under Autonomous Vehicles, high priority — "Endorse rulemaking to set new standards specific to seeing, detecting, and properly reacting to [motorcycles]" and "Advocate a mandate that autonomous vehicles must detect and respond to motorcycles." The Oct 29 filing is the agenda being delivered to the regulator in response to the October frameworks, not a new idea invented last week. Source: MRF 2026 Legislative Priorities PDF (mrf.org via Wild Apricot CDN).
3. **What FMVSS 127 actually tests.** Final rule May 9, 2024 (89 FR 39686): "An AEB system must detect and react to an imminent crash with both a lead vehicle or a pedestrian." Test procedures: lead-vehicle AEB and pedestrian AEB (daylight and darkness), plus two false-activation tests. No motorcycle test device, no motorcycle scenario, anywhere in the standard. Compliance: Sept 1, 2029 (small-volume Sept 1, 2030). NHTSA projects at least 360 lives and 24,000 injuries prevented per year at full implementation. Sources: Federal Register May 9, 2024; DOT final-rule response to petitions (Nov 26, 2024, 89 FR 93199); Nelson Mullins analysis (Apr 2026).
4. **The rule is already slipping.** Auto Innovators sued Jan 17, 2025 (D.C. Cir. No. 25-1026); DOT got the case held in abeyance Feb 28, 2025. March 23, 2026 DOT status report: "the Department is currently preparing a notice of proposed rulemaking that would, if finalized, amend certain aspects of the rule," extending compliance lead time by two years. Source: Nelson Mullins, Apr 22, 2026 (also covered in site #999, Sept 27, 2026).
5. **What happens when a motorcycle IS tested.** IIHS's updated vehicle-to-vehicle front crash prevention evaluation (introduced 2024) includes a motorcycle target alongside a passenger-car target and a semitrailer. In the first-ever commercial-vehicle ratings (Oct 8, 2026), IIHS said "several systems did not issue warnings or slow at all in most of the motorcycle trials." The Rivian RCV delivery van avoided the car target in four of six runs and warned in every car and semitrailer run, then recorded zero speed reduction and zero timely warnings in all six motorcycle runs. Sources: IIHS ratings pages (V2V 2.0 test description); site #1150 article text (Axle McScatter, Oct 13, 2026).
6. **The stakes (NHTSA's own numbers).** Traffic Safety Facts, Motorcycles: 2024 Data (DOT HS 813 824, July 2026): 6,228 motorcyclists killed in 2024, down 2.2% from 6,366 in 2023; motorcyclists were 16% of all traffic fatalities and 20% of all motor-vehicle occupant fatalities, while motorcycles are about 3% of registered vehicles. Fatality rate 28.00 per 100M VMT, almost 27 times the passenger-car occupant rate (1.05). Injury rate 391 per 100M VMT, ~4.5x the car-occupant rate (86).
7. **The framework the groups responded to.** NHTSA NPRM, "Modernization of FMVSS No. 108 to Accommodate ADS-Equipped Vehicles" (Docket NHTSA-2026-1519, RIN 2127-AM70), signed Oct 22, 2026, Federal Register Oct 26, 2026, 30-day comment period closing ~Nov 25, 2026. Proposes optional cyan ADS marker lamps and drops manual-control requirements for vehicles without steering wheels or pedals. Source: NHTSA NPRM PDF; covered on this site as #1308 (Oct 28, 2026).
8. **Same week, second thread.** NHTSA launched its Fall 2026 recall campaign Oct 26 (through Nov 1), aimed at riders: check your motorcycle's VIN for open recalls (brakes, fuel leaks, steering hardware). Source: Biker Nation, Oct 29, 2026.

## Original calculations (show work in article)

- **17x:** 6,228 motorcyclist deaths in 2024 / 360 lives per year FMVSS 127 is projected to save = 17.3. The annual motorcycle body count is 17 times the entire projected benefit of the federal auto-brake mandate, and the mandate's test procedures never include a motorcycle.
- **27x:** 28.00 / 1.05 = 26.7, NHTSA's own "almost 27 times" phrasing. Per mile, the road user the federal test ignores is the one most likely to die.
- **0-for-6:** Rivian RCV motorcycle trials: 0 timely warnings, 0 speed reductions in 6 runs; car target: 4 of 6 avoided, warnings in every run. Same sensors, same van, different target shape. The system doesn't fail at braking; it fails at seeing.
- **16% from 3%:** motorcyclists are ~3% of registered vehicles and 16% of traffic deaths. The test protocol covers the 97% and skips the 3% that produces a sixth of the deaths.

## Strongest counterargument (state at full strength)

The petition may be asking the wrong instrument to do the job. FMVSS 127's test procedures are compliance tests for light-vehicle AEB, and most fatal motorcycle crashes are not rear-end collisions by a car into a motorcycle: the dominant fatal pattern is a car turning left across an oncoming bike (about 26% of fatal two-vehicle motorcycle crashes, 2017-2021, Traffic Injury Prevention), a geometry no forward-facing AEB test, motorcycle target or not, would catch. Adding a motorcycle target to a rear-end test would fix a real but narrow slice of rider deaths. And the 360-lives figure is the incremental benefit of mandating AEB on the remaining unequipped fleet, not the total benefit of AEB: most new cars already have AEB, so comparing 6,228 annual rider deaths to 360 incremental lives overstates the gap. The honest version of the argument is narrower: the federal test should include the road user it currently pretends doesn't exist, because a compliance test that never shows a motorcycle teaches manufacturers that motorcycles are optional in their sensor validation. That is the claim. The left-turn numbers are the limit of it.

## Limitations

- The Oct 29 filing's exact form (letter vs. docket comment vs. legislative priorities document delivered to staff) is not specified in the available reporting; the article describes it as a formal list of priorities sent to NHTSA and Congress, per Biker Nation, and does not claim a docket number.
- FMVSS 127's 360-lives projection is an incremental steady-state benefit estimate at full fleet implementation; it is not directly comparable to a single year's total motorcycle deaths, and the article says so.
- IIHS motorcycle-target results come from the commercial-vehicle ratings (vans and Class 3 pickups), not light passenger vehicles; light-vehicle V2V 2.0 motorcycle results exist but the cited 0-for-6 is a van. The article names the vehicle.
- 2024 motorcycle figures are from the FARS Annual Report File and may be revised in the Final File.
- MRF's 2026 agenda predates the Oct 29 filing (adopted Sept 2025); the filing is the delivery mechanism, and the article says so.

## Actionable takeaways (required)

- Riders: NHTSA's Fall 2026 recall campaign runs through Nov 1; check your bike's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. The FMVSS 108 comment docket (NHTSA-2026-1519) is open until ~Nov 25, 2026; rider groups are filing there and the docket takes public comments.
- Car buyers: IIHS's V2V 2.0 ratings include a motorcycle target. If you share roads with bikes, check the motorcycle line, not just the overall score. A system rated Good overall can still be blind to a two-wheeler.
- Everyone: the federal auto-brake mandate's test has two targets, a car and a pedestrian. Until a motorcycle joins them, "my car brakes itself" does not mean "my car sees the bike."
