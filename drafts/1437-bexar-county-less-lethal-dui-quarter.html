The Gap

Bexar County Crashed More Than Dallas and Killed Fewer. Drunk Driving Was One in Four.

Axle McScatter • Data Visualization Editor
November 8, 2026

Bexar County, Texas logged 46,744 crashes in 2025, more than Dallas County’s 43,946, and buried 190 people to Dallas’s 274.[1] Same state, same year, same database, and the county with more crashes killed 84 fewer people. Divide deaths by crashes and the gap sharpens: 4.06 deaths per 1,000 crashes in Bexar against 6.99 statewide.[2]

4.06
Deaths per 1,000 crashes in Bexar County, 2025 — 42% below the Texas average of 6.99 (TxDOT county tables)

Run the same division across the six biggest Texas counties and Bexar lands near the bottom of the lethality table, not the top. Harris County sits at 4.68, El Paso at 3.92, the lowest of the six, and Dallas and Tarrant tie at 6.24. Travis County sits at the top at 8.97, more than double Bexar’s rate.[1] Bexar also tied Tarrant for the third-most deaths in the state, which is what makes the table worth building: raw counts say Bexar is one of the deadliest counties in Texas, and per-crash arithmetic says its crashes are among the least likely to kill.

2025 was an improving year, and the improvement outran the state. Crashes fell 3.7 percent, deaths fell 11.6 percent, suspected serious injuries fell 15.4 percent.[1] Statewide deaths fell 9.2 percent on the same county-table editions, the national estimate fell 6.7 percent, and Bexar beat both.[3] Crashes also got less lethal, not just fewer: 4.43 deaths per 1,000 crashes in 2024, 4.06 in 2025.

Then there is the number that refuses to improve. Alcohol-involved crashes were 1,621 of Bexar’s 46,744 crashes, 3.5 percent. They produced 46 of its 190 deaths, 24.2 percent.[1] A drunk-driving crash in Bexar County was roughly seven times as likely to kill as the average crash in the county, measured as a share ratio, not a causal rate. Speed-involved crashes killed 72 people by the separate speed table, and the two factors overlap, so the counts do not add.[1] Bexar’s 1,621 DUI crashes were the third-most of any Texas county, behind Harris at 3,090 and Dallas at 1,986.

Give the good news its proper discount. County totals carry no exposure adjustment: no miles driven, no population, no road mix. A county whose crashes skew urban and low-speed will look less lethal than one whose crashes happen on rural highways, and the tables cannot say how much of the gap that explains. A 25-death swing on a baseline of 215 is about 1.7 standard deviations, which is consistent with a real improvement but not proof of one. Serious injuries carry the stronger signal: down 138 on a baseline of 897, about 4.6 standard deviations, which is not noise.

Inside the county, the danger concentrates on a small set of streets. San Antonio’s own High Injury Network study, five years of crash data from 2019 through 2023, found 65 percent of fatalities and severe injuries on just 3.8 percent of the streets in the network.[4] For pedestrians the pattern tightens further: 52.9 percent of serious crashes on 0.7 percent of the network. That study covers a different window than the 2025 county tables, so the two cannot be compared head to head, but the implication survives the caveat: most of Bexar’s dying happens on a small set of streets, and the city publishes a map of them. Commercial vehicles are a footnote here: 2,581 crashes, 16 deaths, 8.4 percent of the county’s dead from 5.5 percent of its crashes.[1] What you can do with this starts with the map, and continues with the obvious: 3.5 percent of crashes, 24.2 percent of deaths. Sober is the cheapest safety feature in the county.
