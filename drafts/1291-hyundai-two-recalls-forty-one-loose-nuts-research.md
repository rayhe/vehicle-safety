# Research: #1291 — Hyundai's two loose-nut recalls, 41 cars total

**Slug:** 1291-hyundai-two-recalls-forty-one-loose-nuts
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — car-buying guidance beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date researched:** 2026-10-26

## Kill test
PASS. Two NHTSA campaigns filed in the same October 2026 window, both
"insufficiently tightened fasteners," both 2026 model-year Hyundais from the
same Ulsan plant in South Korea, both using fasteners from the same supplier
(Samjin Nut), and both tiny: 24 cars and 17 cars. Combined, 41 brand-new cars
that could lose steering or lose a wheel because somebody did not tighten a
nut enough. The novelty is the pairing, not either recall alone: one recall
is a rounding error, two recalls with the same cause at the same plant is a
pattern. Add the third under-torqued-fastener recall Autoblog cites, 26V314
(172 Ioniq 5 / Ioniq 9 cars, Georgia plant), and the pattern is 213 cars
across two plants and two countries, all for fasteners not tightened enough.
No prior Crash Report draft covers 26V692, 26V693, or 26V314 (checked
2026-10-26: no drafts, no queue entries).

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- NHTSA campaign **26V692**: 24 vehicles, 2026 Hyundai Elantra (20) and
  2026 Hyundai Tucson Hybrid (4). Front strut assemblies may not have been
  properly secured to the front steering knuckles due to insufficiently
  tightened fasteners; separation of suspension components increases crash
  risk. Hyundai aware of **1 incident, 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injuries**.
  (Autoblog, Oct 24, 2026.)
- NHTSA campaign **26V693**: 17 vehicles, 2026 Hyundai Venue (8), Elantra
  (4), Elantra Hybrid (4), Elantra N (1). Wheel lug nuts may be
  insufficiently tightened and loosen, allowing a wheel to detach; detachment
  causes loss of control and a road hazard. Hyundai aware of **0 incidents**.
  (Autoblog, Oct 24, 2026; ConsumerAffairs Oct 26 roundup; ProblemsByVin
  Oct 25 roundup.)
- Combined population: **24 + 17 = 41 vehicles**. Both recalls affect only
  2026 model-year vehicles imported from South Korea, built at Hyundai's
  **Ulsan plant**; affected fasteners come from the same supplier,
  **Samjin Nut**. Hyundai attributes both to manufacturing lapses, not
  defective parts. (Autoblog.)
- Remedy (both): dealers inspect and retighten fasteners/lug nuts, replace
  associated hardware as necessary, plus wheel alignment (26V692) or hub
  bearing replacement (26V693) as needed, free of charge. Owner notification
  letters for both begin **December 19, 2026**. (Autoblog; ProblemsByVin.)
- 26V693 specifics: Hyundai recall number **310**; customer service
  **1-855-371-9460**; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from **October 23, 2026**.
  (ProblemsByVin.)
- Pattern extension: recall **26V314** covers insufficiently tightened rear
  suspension fasteners in **172 examples of the 2025 Ioniq 5 and 2026 Ioniq 9**,
  built at Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America in Georgia, not Ulsan.
  (Autoblog.) Combined three-campaign total: **41 + 172 = 213 vehicles**.
- Context: Hyundai had issued **22 recalls in 2026** at the time of
  Autoblog's Oct 24 piece, fifth among major automakers; Ford led with 81.
  (Autoblog.)

## Secondary / attributed-only details (NOT stated as NHTSA facts)
- One secondary account of 26V693 traces the lug nuts to a temporary manual
  torquing process at Ulsan used while wheel-tightening tooling was changed,
  ending Sept 16, 2026. Another secondary account of 26V692 mentions torque
  data mis-recording and new monitoring/sign-off from Sept 9, 2026. These
  are NOT in the Autoblog/ConsumerAffairs/ProblemsByVin material verified
  above, so the article does NOT assert them as fact; it attributes the
  general cause to Hyundai's own "manufacturing lapses" framing only.

## Novel contribution
The cross-campaign arithmetic (41 combined; 213 with 26V314) and the
same-plant/same-supplier/same-failure-mode triangulation across two
campaigns. Neither recall alone is a story; together they show a torque
process problem, not a parts problem, and the Georgia recall shows it is
not confined to one plant.

## Strongest counterargument
41 cars is nothing. No crashes, no injuries, one incident total, and both
recalls were caught before anyone got hurt. A reasonable reader could call
this the system working: a tiny population, a free fix, letters in December.
That objection is fair on the body count and wrong on the lesson: the same
failure mode in three campaigns, at two plants, means the next under-torqued
batch may not be 41 cars, and a wheel or a steering knuckle does not fail
gently when it does fail.

## Limitations
All defect-cause and supplier claims come from Hyundai via press coverage
of the filings, not from the Part 573 PDFs read directly in this run; counts
and splits are as reported by Autoblog and cross-checked against the
ConsumerAffairs/ProblemsByVin campaign lists. No FARS fatality data is
involved, so no death rate is computed or implied. The 213 total mixes a
Georgia-plant campaign with a different assembly and fastener location; it
is a pattern total, not a single-defect population.

## Actionable insight
If you bought a 2026 Elantra, Elantra Hybrid, Elantra N, Venue, or Tucson
Hybrid, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now instead of waiting for the
December 19 letter; if your car is one of the 41, the fix is a retorque and
it is free. Shopping a 2026 Hyundai used, check both 26V692 and 26V693, not
just one, because the two campaigns overlap on the Elantra.
