NHTSA Told Ten Fire Engines to Stop Driving Before the Wheels Fall Off.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
October 14, 2026

A fire engine has exactly one job: get to the emergency fast. So it takes a special kind of week for the federal government to tell ten of them not to move at all. On October 2, Rosenbauer America filed campaign 26V640000 with NHTSA, covering certain 2024-2025 Commander and 2024 Warrior fire engines, because a cracked rim weld can separate the wheel and deflate the tire. Owners are advised not to drive their vehicles until the remedy is completed, and the NHTSA record carries the rare parkIt flag, the bureaucratic equivalent of a red tag.[1][2]

10
Fire engines under a federal do-not-drive order in October 2026, because their wheels might separate

Ten trucks is a small recall. It is also, in a way, the purest kind: the defect is one cracked weld away from a fire engine shedding a wheel on the way to a fire, which is the sort of sentence you read twice. Dealers will replace the wheels and tires free of charge. Owner letters go out October 19, and Rosenbauer's line is 1-651-462-1000 for anyone who would rather not wait for the mail.[1]

Do-not-drive is not standard recall language. NHTSA reserves the designation for the small club of defects it considers genuinely dangerous right now, a step above the usual "schedule service at your convenience" choreography.[4] When the notice says the truck stays parked, it means the truck stays parked, and for a vehicle whose entire purpose is urgent motion, that is about as loud as a recall gets.

Now widen the lens, because Rosenbauer has been here before. In December 2023 the company recalled Commanders and Warriors because a firmware fault could let overloaded circuits overheat: a fire engine that could start a fire.[3] In August 2024 it recalled Commanders whose front-suspension bolts could turn brittle, break, and send a bolt head flying as a projectile.[3] In August 2026 it recalled Commanders whose touchscreen display for the emergency lights could simply fail to power on, which is a strange way to arrive at an emergency.[3] Four campaigns in three years, and each one disabled a different thing a fire engine needs: power, suspension, lights, wheels. Rosenbauer’s emergency vehicles keep having emergencies of their own.

Fairness requires the other half of the ledger, and it is short. Ten vehicles. No crashes or injuries cited in the campaign record. Rosenbauer found the defect, filed the paperwork, and is replacing hardware free before the letters even go out. That is the system working, not failing, and a do-not-drive on ten trucks is proof the severity scale exists, not proof of a fleet-wide menace. Small recalls are cheap to file and cheap to fix, which is exactly why they should be filed and fixed fast.

What to do: if your department or fleet runs 2024-2025 Rosenbauer Commanders or 2024 Warriors, park them until the wheel and tire replacement is done, and call Rosenbauer at 1-651-462-1000 rather than waiting for the October 19 letters. Then run every apparatus VIN through nhtsa.gov/recalls against all four Rosenbauer campaigns, because the same truck can easily be in more than one. A fire engine is allowed exactly one emergency at a time: yours.

Limitations

The 10-vehicle count comes from Fleet Maintenance’s reading of the NHTSA documents; the live API record confirms the campaign and the do-not-drive flag but not the count. No complaint or incident counts are cited for this campaign, so we cannot say whether any wheel actually separated. FARS tracks roadway fatalities and cannot speak to a ten-vehicle recall with no reported injuries. Rosenbauer is a fire-apparatus upfitter, so fleet managers should check chassis-OEM recalls separately. The four-campaign “pattern” is an inference across three years of filings, not a root-cause finding, and the populations differ enormously in size.
