# Research: Mack Trucks Shedding Grille Bezels (26V631)

**Slug:** 1119-mack-flying-grille-bezels-cable-tie-fix
**Article #:** 1119
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (consumer safety advocate — direct, practical, furious on behalf of everyone sharing the highway)
**Kicker:** Body Count

## The story

Mack Trucks is recalling **3,096** of its newest flagship tractors because the intake and grille bezels — the cosmetic/aero trim ringing the truck's face — can detach at speed and become road hazards for everyone else on the highway.

The fix? Dealers will inspect, replace the bezels, and **install cable ties**. Zip ties. On a six-figure flagship semi.

## Verified facts (primary sources)

- **NHTSA campaign 26V631**; Mack internal recall number **SC0501** (oemdtc.com NHTSA Part 573 summary, posted 2026-10-01).
- **Population:** certain 2026–2027 Mack Anthem AN(4) and 2025–2027 Mack Pioneer PR(4) — 3,096 units.
- **Component:** STRUCTURE:BODY:BUMPERS:ACTIVE SHUTTERS/GRILL.
- **Defect:** intake and grille bezels may detach from the vehicle; a detached bezel can become a road hazard for other vehicles, increasing crash risk.
- **Remedy:** dealers inspect, replace the intake and grille bezels, and install cable ties as necessary, free of charge.
- **Timeline:** owner notification letters mailed **November 30, 2026**; Mack customer service 1-800-866-1177; VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from **October 8, 2026**.

## The angle (kill test: PASS)

1. **The truck is eating its own face.** The Pioneer is Mack's newest flagship line. Its aerodynamic bezel trim — hardware sold as efficiency and polish — is flying off and becoming the very road debris truckers dodge.
2. **The remedy is cable ties.** A zip-tie fix on a premium Class 8 tractor is grim-funny and a perfect Crash Report detail.
3. **Fresh, uncovered territory.** Full-queue keyword sweep (2026-10-10): zero hits for "bezel", "grille bezel", "26v631". The adjacent Mack tanker-braking story (#1098, 26V618-era software) covered braking, not body-panel detachment.

## Limitations / what we don't know

- NHTSA's filing summary does not say how many field reports or warranty claims triggered the recall, whether any crashes or injuries occurred, or at what speed the bezels detach.
- We don't know the exact attachment mechanism that fails (clip? adhesive? fastener?) — only that the fix adds cable ties.
- FARS doesn't map "struck by detached truck panel" cleanly as a vehicle model, so no death-rate stat for this one; the piece stands on the recall record, not FARS.

## Counterargument (stated at full strength)

Three thousand trucks out of ~200,000+ Class 8 trucks sold annually is a rounding error, no injuries have been reported, and a bezel is lightweight plastic that mostly annoys rather than kills. A skeptical reader could call this a nuisance recall dressed up as a safety story. Fair. But a detached panel at 70 mph still forces a 40-ton surprise into somebody else's windshield lane, and "cable ties" as the factory remedy for a flagship tractor is the kind of detail that tells you how the sausage is held together.

## Actionable takeaways

- Drive a 2025–2027 Mack Pioneer or 2026–2027 Anthem? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable since Oct 8, 2026); letters go out Nov 30, 2026.
- Everyone else: if you see a Mack with a half-hanging grille face, give it room — that trim is one bump away from the interstate.
