Five Trucks, One Loose Connector, and a 212,000-to-1 Ratio

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
October 10, 2026

I ran the numbers. Then I ran them again. They didn't get better. Stellantis has issued a safety recall covering five 2026 Ram 1500 pickups. Five: not five thousand, not five percent of anything. Five individual trucks, built between February 28 and May 18, 2026, each of which may carry a seat-wiring connector for the driver or front-passenger side airbag that was never properly secured, a defect that could keep those airbags from deploying as intended in a crash. NHTSA logged it as campaign 26V622; inside Chrysler it runs as recall 94D, and it fails the vehicles on two federal standards: FMVSS 208, occupant crash protection, and FMVSS 214, side impact protection.[1]

5
Trucks in NHTSA campaign 26V622. Ram's previous airbag-wiring recall covered 1,060,531 of them.

For scale, consider what Ram's airbag wiring recall history looks like at the other end of the spectrum. In 2015, the company recalled 1,060,531 pickups from model years 2012 through 2014 because a steering-wheel wiring harness could chafe against a spring, short circuit, and fire the driver airbag without warning, a failure that produced two reported injuries.[4] The ratio between that campaign and this one is 212,000 to 1. Eleven years apart, same brand, same wiring family, and the defect footprint shrank from a million trucks to a parking lot.

There is a charitable interpretation and it deserves to be stated plainly: a five-vehicle recall means traceability worked. Somewhere in the supply chain, a quality team narrowed a suspect build window to eleven weeks and then narrowed the suspect population to five VINs, which is the kind of precision that usually only appears in aerospace, and that is genuinely good manufacturing. It also means, statistically, that almost nobody reading this is affected, and no injuries or crashes have been reported. This is paperwork that happens to wear a scary word: recall.

The cynical interpretation also has a seat at the table. Tiny recalls occasionally function as containment narratives, a way to tell regulators the problem was found, bounded, and fixed while the rest of the line hums along, and I cannot verify any of that here. The root cause, supplier, and shift are all undisclosed. One early draft of the news coverage claimed specifics that were later retracted as unverified, and they stay retracted here.[1] What I can verify is the math: five trucks, two failed federal standards, zero reported injuries. If you own a 2026 Ram 1500, the play is thirty seconds of your time. Type your VIN into nhtsa.gov/recalls, where these VINs have been searchable since October 6. A match means a free dealer inspection; reference 94D when you call 800-853-1403.[2]
