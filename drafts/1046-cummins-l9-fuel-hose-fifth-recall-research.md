# Research — #1046: Cummins L9 Fuel Hose, Fifth Recall (26E066)

## Angle
On September 28, 2026, Cummins filed NHTSA equipment recall 26E066 (Cummins C7311): 39,052 L9 diesel engines whose remote fuel filter hoses can separate from their fittings, spilling diesel — engine stall, fire risk. The news is not the hose. The news is that this is the FIFTH Cummins recall of the same L9 remote fuel filter hose in five years, and the 2026 filing explicitly voids the previous three remedies: "Engines remedied under Recalls 23E055, 24E038, or 25E004 will need to have the new redesigned parts installed." The fixes kept failing. Engines built as recently as April 10, 2026 are affected — production kept shipping the defect through every prior "correction."

**Self-critique gate:** Surprising after 1,000+ articles? Yes — a five-deep re-recall chain on a single hose, in fire trucks, school buses, motorhomes, and city buses, with the newest recall recalling the remedies. ## Facts

1. **The current recall:** NHTSA 26E066 (Cummins C7311), report received Sep 28, 2026, record created Sep 29. Equipment recall. 39,052 Cummins L9 diesel engines, part number D563027BX03, built Dec 7, 2020 – Apr 10, 2026. Component: fuel system, diesel delivery hoses/lines/fittings — "fuel carrying hose with quick connect fittings," part numbers 6316754, 6316757, 6316899 (Autoblog lists filter/hose PNs FF90942/91044 PN6316754, FF90943 PN6316757, FF90944 PN6316899). Defect: remote fuel filter hoses may separate from the fitting → fuel leak + engine stall. "A fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source increases the risk of a fire. An engine stall increases the risk of a crash." Cause per Autoblog's reading of the report: "insufficient crimping of the connector, possibly resulting in insufficient hose retention."
2. **The voided remedies:** NHTSA record states verbatim: "Engines remedied under Recalls 23E055, 24E038, or 25E004 will need to have the new redesigned parts installed." Remedy: dealers replace the fuel hoses free of charge. Owner notification letters expected mailed November 20, 2026. Cummins customer service 1-800-286-6467.
3. **Round 1 — 21E063 (Jul 13, 2021, Cummins C2465):** certain MY 2021 L9 engines built Mar 27, 2019 – Jun 15, 2021. "A fuel leak may occur in the fuel hoses between the fuel pump and remote filter head... may increase the risk of a fire." Remedy at the time: "replace the subject fuel hoses... with fuel hoses of a new design... The new fuel hoses will have a unique part number and are visibly different." Production corrected Jun 15, 2021. (NHTSA RCMN via static.oemdtc.com; lindseyresearch mirror of RCLRPT-21E063.)
4. **Round 2 — 23E055 (2023):** "L9 Remote Mount Stage 2 Fuel Hose Safety Campaign" (Cummins field campaign C2717): replace the two subject fuel hoses with hoses having "improved connector crimps." Covered >1,100 products (Paccar's 23V-617 — ~900 Kenworth/Peterbilt trucks — was part of this; Land Line Media Sep 14, 2023). Root cause identified as "a poor quality crimp on the fuel hose." NHTSA estimated 3% actually affected.
5. **Round 3 — 24E038 (May 21, 2024, Cummins C7032):** L9 engines built Nov 7, 2022 – Mar 29, 2023 with remote Stage 2 fuel filter (FF90943/FF90944), two pressurized hoses may separate from the fitting. Population only 230 (quarterly report RCLQRT-24E038: 37 remedied by Oct 2024). Owner notification Jul 8, 2024. (NHTSA RCMN-24E038-6844.pdf, Shyft Group letter May 28, 2024.)
6. **Round 4 — 25E004 (2025):** same hose family (named in 26E066 as a voided remedy).
7. **The production-overlap tell:** 26E066's build window (Dec 7, 2020 – Apr 10, 2026) overlaps the 2021, 2023, and 2024 recall windows — engines built AFTER the 2021 "new design" and 2023 "improved crimps" fixes are still in the 2026 population. The defect outlived two corrections in production.
8. **Who drives these engines:** per Autoblog/NHTSA, affected OEMs include Big Rig, Battle Motors, Grove Worldwide, Temsa, Tiffin Motorhomes, Shyft Group, Sutphen, Nova Bus, Autocar, New Flyer, Gillig, DTNA, PACCAR. Applications: fire trucks (Sutphen, Shyft), school/transit buses (Nova Bus, New Flyer, Temsa, Thomas Built via DTNA), motorhomes (Tiffin), medium-duty vocational trucks. An equipment recall means Cummins notifies OEMs, OEMs file their own vehicle recalls — the owner hears from their RV/bus/truck maker, not Cummins.

## Actionable takeaway
If you own a motorhome, bus, or medium-duty truck with a Cummins L9 built between Dec 2020 and Apr 2026: don't wait for the Nov 20 letter — and don't assume a prior fuel-hose recall repair means you're clear, because Cummins says engines fixed under the 2023–2025 recalls need the new redesigned parts installed. Call Cummins at 1-800-286-6467 with your engine serial number, or check with your vehicle OEM (Tiffin, New Flyer, PACCAR, DTNA, etc.), since equipment recalls reach owners through the vehicle maker. Watch for the warning signs NHTSA lists: fuel smell, visible wetness near the remote filter head, or the engine malfunction lamp.

## Counterargument (strongest)
Five recalls of the same part can mean the surveillance is working, not that the engineering is broken: each round narrowed the population (230 engines in 24E038), and Cummins kept finding the edge cases instead of declaring victory. The L9 is one of the most-produced medium-duty diesels in North America; a hose fitting that fails at low rates across a huge installed base will generate exactly this kind of iterative campaign. The honest frame: the re-recall chain is Cummins refusing to let the defect age out — but it is also Cummins failing, four times, to actually fix a hose.
