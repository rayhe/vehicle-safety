## Core numbers (BizzyCar analysis of NHTSA data, via NIADA, Nov 2, 2026)
- Q3 2026: more than 8.8 million vehicles recalled across 85 campaigns — the
  highest-volume third quarter in BizzyCar's five-year window, though below Q1 and Q2 2026.
- Year-to-date 2026: 32.1 million vehicles — already exceeds full-year totals for 2022,
  2024, and 2025; trails only 2023's 33.6 million. Q4 needs just 1.5 million more to set a
  five-year record.
- Chrysler (Stellantis): ~3.4 million vehicles, 38.6% of Q3 volume.
- Ford: ~1.9 million vehicles, 21.5% of volume, and led in campaign count with 19.
- Top component categories: back-over prevention systems (rearview cameras) ~23% of
  affected vehicles, followed by electrical systems and seat belts.
- Largest single Q3 recall: ~1.27 million Ram 1500 pickups, second-row seat belt buckle
  anchors (see below).
- Five campaigns covering 519,187 vehicles carried "Park Outside" advisories.
- Remedy mix: ~77% of recalled vehicles required dealer remedies; over-the-air-eligible
  remedies rose to 14.7% of volume, up from 3.1% in Q2.

## The Ram anchor recall (NHTSA 26V495, Chrysler 67D)
- 1,271,294 model-year 2019–2026 Ram 1500 pickups, built Feb 17, 2018 through Apr 27, 2026
  (an eight-year, eight-model-year build window).
- Second-row center and left-side (driver-side) seat belt buckle anchors may not have been
  properly attached to the body structure: the anchor bolt was under-torqued or missed
  entirely on the assembly line. An assembly miss, not a defective part.
- No visible warning: an affected belt looks and feels normal until a crash tests it.
  Noncompliant with FMVSS 210 (seat belt assembly anchorages).
- Global total just over 1.5 million (156,138 Canada, 15,088 Mexico, ~74,000 elsewhere).
- Chronology: Transport Canada flagged a 2025 Ram 1500 concern Jan 20, 2026; FCA's Vehicle
  Regulations Committee decided July 23; Part 573 filed July 30; VINs searchable Aug 6;
  owner letters Aug 18 through Sep 24, 2026.
- No injuries or fatalities confirmed linked to the defect in the NHTSA filing.
- Remedy: dealers inspect and attach the anchors as needed, free.

## Why cameras lead the list: the backover mandate
- NHTSA final rule (March 31, 2014): all vehicles under 10,000 lbs manufactured on or after
  May 1, 2018 must have rear visibility technology covering a 10-by-20-foot zone behind
  the vehicle (FMVSS 111).
- Pre-mandate toll cited by NHTSA: ~210 fatalities and 15,000 injuries per year from
  backover crashes; children under 5 accounted for 31% of backover fatalities, adults 70+
  for 26%. Projected savings: 58–69 lives per year once the fleet turned over.
- This site's own May 2026 article found the mandate cut child backover deaths 78% at a
  Level 1 pediatric trauma center. The cameras work. They are also now the most-recalled
  safety system in America, which is the paradox at the center of this story.

## What the article does NOT prove
- Recall volume is not defect volume: 32.1 million "potentially affected" vehicles is a
  population estimate, not a count of broken parts (the Ram recall covers 1.27M trucks for
  a condition Chrysler's filing ties to an assembly miss affecting a small share).
- BizzyCar's category shares are its own analysis of NHTSA data, not an NHTSA publication.
- Q4 2026 could still come in under 2023's 33.6M; the "record pace" claim is arithmetic
  (1.5M to go), not a forecast.

## Actionable takeaways (required gate)
- Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, not when the letter arrives — Q3 alone added
  8.8 million vehicles, and 519,187 of them carry park-outside advisories.
- Ram 1500 owners (2019–2026): the anchor inspection is free and takes a dealer visit;
  a rear belt that looks fine is exactly what this defect looks like.
- If your car offers an over-the-air recall fix, take it: OTA-eligible remedies jumped
  from 3.1% to 14.7% of recall volume in one quarter, and a camera fix you skip is a
  federally required safety display you are driving without.
