A Pallet Stopped Moving in Slovakia. 371 Defenders Got Recalled.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 3, 2026

Let’s talk about what happens in the first 150 milliseconds of a crash. Your seatbelt locks, the webbing catches your chest, and several thousand newtons of force go somewhere that is not your spine. Now imagine the stitching holding that webbing together was formed wrong at the factory: the belt doesn’t restrain you, it comes apart. That is the defect Land Rover just recalled 371 Defenders for.[2]

371
Defenders built in a three-day window, all recalled. JLR estimates 100% carry the suspect belts.

It started with one photo: on August 4, 2025, the owner of a 2024 Defender told NHTSA that a second-row seatbelt had come unstitched, and attached pictures. NHTSA called the webbing stitching “improperly formed.” Four months later, the Office of Defects Investigation opened Preliminary Evaluation PE25-019.[1] So far, this is the recall system working exactly as designed: a citizen complaint with evidence becomes a federal file.

Then it got weird, because Jaguar Land Rover’s safety committee spent the next six months pulling second-row belts out of 2024 Defenders, checking serial numbers, and finding nothing wrong. Nothing was wrong with the inspection; the problem was logistics. Because the belts are not sequenced to specific vehicle identification numbers, there was no way to know which trucks carried the bad batch. And the suspect pallet had, in the Part 573 report’s own deadpan words, stopped moving lineside at the Nitra assembly plant in Slovakia.[2] Meanwhile the factory kept harvesting belts from the flow while the defective batch sat still, so of course they found nothing. Nobody was looking at the one place the defect was sitting: a pallet that wasn’t moving.

Here is the part that should bother you. Call it what it was: not a scandal, but a traceability gap. Seatbelts, the single most effective safety device in the vehicle, ship to the line in batches that cannot be tied to the VINs they end up in. When that matters, the only fix is the blunt one. On September 10, 2026, JLR determined where the pallet had stalled, and the committee treated the six-month miss pattern as an urgent review rather than a coincidence. Out of it came recall D130, NHTSA campaign 26V615: every 2024 Defender built between January 31 and February 2, 2024, three days at roughly 124 trucks a day, all presumed to carry belts from the suspect batch at a 100 percent defect estimate.[2] Autoliv Switzerland supplied the belts, and the fix is a free replacement once dealers check your serial number.[3]

Now the honest part, because I owe it to you. This might be the recall system’s best week. One complaint became a federal investigation, a manufacturer hunted the defect for half a year, and when the hunt exposed a traceability hole instead of a pile of bad belts, it recalled the entire production window at maximum caution, with no crashes, no injuries, no deaths, and nobody hurt by this defect as far as any record shows. You could fairly say the headline is “Land Rover recalled 371 cars as a precaution,” and that punishing a company for transparency would be perverse, which is all true.

But the precaution was forced by the gap: if seatbelts were sequenced to VINs, this would be a twelve-car recall, not a 371-car one. NHTSA credits seat belts with saving an estimated 14,955 lives in 2017.[5] That device should be the most traceable part in the building, but it isn’t. That is the design judgment I cannot get past: the deadliest failure mode in the car gets the loosest paperwork.

What to do: Own a 2024 Defender built in the last days of January or first days of February 2024? Run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, not when the interim letter arrives November 20; the check is free and the remedy is free. Any 2024 Defender owner can look at their own second-row belt stitching tonight. If it looks loose, uneven, or is coming apart, file a vehicle owner questionnaire at nhtsa.gov. One photo started this recall, and yours could start the next one.[4]
