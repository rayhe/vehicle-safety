# Research: NHTSA Door Rulemaking — Decade Delay

## Core Story
NHTSA on July 24, 2026 simultaneously:
1. DENIED a petition to investigate Tesla Model 3 emergency door releases as a defect
2. GRANTED a separate petition to begin rulemaking for emergency door egress standards

These are contradictory postures. Denying the defect investigation means Tesla's doors are not considered defective. Granting the rulemaking means NHTSA admits current rules don't address the problem. The difference: a defect investigation can force a recall in 1-2 years. Rulemaking takes 5-10 years.

## Novel Contribution
**The NHTSA rulemaking timeline calculation:**
- Average NHTSA rulemaking: NPRM takes 2-4 years, final rule 2-3 years after NPRM, implementation 2-3 years after final rule. Total: ~7-10 years from petition grant to enforceable standard.
- Tesla Q2 2026 sales: 467,762 Model 3/Y = ~1.87M annualized
- At ~1.87M/year × 7 years minimum = 13.1 million additional Tesla vehicles sold before any door egress rule takes effect
- Those vehicles will remain on roads for ~15 years = ~196 million vehicle-years of exposure
- Compare: defect investigation → recall → fix in 1-2 years, covering existing fleet

**The body count that doesn't exist in FARS:**
FARS tracks whether someone died in a crash. It does NOT track whether someone died because they couldn't escape AFTER the crash. Post-crash entrapment deaths could be coded as fire deaths, smoke inhalation deaths, or drowning deaths. The regulatory gap has a data gap to match — we literally cannot measure how many people die because they can't open a door.
