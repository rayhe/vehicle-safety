## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10
No victim exploitation; the dead are aggregated, never individualized. The piece resists the easy moral (Ford lied!) and lands on the harder one (nobody lied; the confound is structural). No self-congratulation. Positions are earned by the data.

## 4. Social/Shareability — 8.8/10
Headline is the whole pitch and it travels: "safest truck" + "the one Ford sold the fewest of" is a paradox with a mechanism, ideal for sharing. Pull stat (-45.8%) is quotable. The chart-less format is a minor weakness for a data piece, but the desk's format is prose-first and the numbers are simple enough to survive without a visual.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10
All external claims cited: Ford's Jan 2009 5-star/Top Safety Pick/AdvanceTrac claims [4], F-Series annual sales [5], IIHS ESC effectiveness [2], FMVSS 126 phase-in [3]. The "mandatory September 2011" claim is correctly scoped to light vehicles under FMVSS 126. Wikipedia sales table is cited as citing Ford reports — appropriate attribution, not laundered. No legal claims that need case law. One nit: "twin NHTSA five-star ratings" is shorthand for frontal + side; the source supports it.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.2/10
Original contribution is real and checkable: per-model-year FARS deaths normalized by years-on-road, four-year windows, seven-model cross-brand comparison, with the Silverado-as-control (redesigned 2007, not 2009) as the falsification test. The 2013-14 rebound observation closes the causal loop. Limitations are explicit and honest: fatal-only, calendar-year-sales proxy for model-year production, attrition bias (correctly noted as working against the finding), small-n Titan/Tundra, and the redesign/recession confound stated as unresolvable with FARS alone. Strongest counterargument given full strength (ESC really works; Ford earned the stars). Methodology transparent enough to reproduce.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.8/10
Key numbers inline and bolded where they matter (45.8%, 690,589 -> 413,625, 763,402). The seven-model comparison table lives in the research file; the article renders it as prose, which keeps flow but loses scanability. Pull stat + pull label carry the headline number. Verdict: adequate for the format, not exceptional.
