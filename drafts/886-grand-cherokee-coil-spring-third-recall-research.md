# Research — #886: The Spring That Wouldn't Stay Fixed

## Angle (1-2 sentences)
The same rear coil springs on the 2021-2023 Jeep Grand Cherokee have now been recalled THREE times: the original defect (23V413, 2023), a recall of the botched remedy (26V051, 2026), and now a third campaign (89D/26V562, Sept 2026) replacing both. 754,001 recall notices issued for one component on roughly one fleet of Jeeps.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: yes. A triple recall on the same component across three years, with the middle recall explicitly caused by dealers installing springs wrong DURING the first recall's remedy. The system caught its own failure twice and is now fixing the same cars a third time. Novel data angle: cumulative-notice math + timeline of failure.

## Primary sources (3+)
1. USA Today, Sept 8, 2026 (Taylor Ardrey): 328,381 vehicles (2022-2023 GC, 2022-2023 GC 4xe, 2021-2023 GC L), FCA recall 89D, replaces 26V051 and 23V413, no accidents/injuries, dealers replace rear coil spring lower isolators free, letters Sept 29.
2. Carscoops, Feb 2026: 26V051 covered 80,620 vehicles (29,139 standard GC + 51,481 GC L, air suspension excluded); 23V413 (FCA 64A) covered 345,000+; during 23V413 remedies springs were installed incorrectly or left incomplete; Jeep knew of 20 customer assistance records + 284 warranty claims at filing.
3. Cox Media (WHIO/WPXI/WFTV), Feb 2026: 26V051 explicitly replaced 23V413; "Any Grand Cherokee fixed under the earlier recall will need to be repaired again."
4. DelawareOnline, Sept 8, 2026: VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov from Sept 9, 2026; remedy = replace rear coil spring lower isolators; interim letters Sept 29; FCA 1-800-853-1403.
5. Autoblog, Sept 6, 2026: NHTSA 26V562; frames it as "a problem two recalls didn't fix."

## Key numbers (all cited, none fabricated)
- 23V413 (June 2023): ~345,000 vehicles
- 26V051 (Feb 2026): 80,620 vehicles (29,139 GC + 51,481 GC L)
- 89D / 26V562 (Sept 2026): 328,381 vehicles
- Cumulative notices: 345,000 + 80,620 + 328,381 = 754,001
- Unique fleet: roughly the 23V413 cohort (~345K); the 328K third campaign is nearly the full first cohort again
- 20 customer assistance records, 284 warranty claims tied to botched 23V413 remedies (at 26V051 filing)
- Zero accidents or injuries reported in any filing
- Timeline: June 2023 -> Feb 2026 -> Sept 2026 (3 years, 2 months)

## Novel contribution
The 754,001 cumulative-notice calculation: NHTSA's public-facing "vehicles affected" numbers look like three separate fleets, but the populations overlap almost entirely — the real story is ~345K Jeeps each averaging 2.2 recall notices for one spring. Also the irony arc: the middle recall (26V051) was not a new defect discovery but an admission that the DEALER REMEDY for 23V413 was performed wrong, making this a "recall of a recall" plus a third "recall of the recall of the recall."

## Counterargument (full strength)
This is the system working. NHTSA and Stellantis caught the bad remedy through warranty data (284 claims) and customer reports (20 records) and escalated rather than burying it. Three escalating campaigns over three years with zero reported injuries is exactly how a self-correcting safety regime is supposed to behave. The "third recall" framing is scarier than the risk warrants: a detached spring mostly endangers the car BEHIND the Jeep, and Jeep reports no crashes.

## Limitations
- FARS cannot attribute fatalities to this defect; zero accidents reported, so this is a "near-miss/luck" story, not a body-count story. Say so explicitly.
- Population counts (345K/80.6K/328K) are potentially affected, not actually defective; the real defect rate is unknown.
- Unique-fleet estimate (~345K) is approximate; FCA/Stellantis has not published a VIN-overlap reconciliation.
- The 4xe models appear in the third campaign but were not separately broken out in the first; hybrid-specific overlap is uncertain.

## Actionable takeaways
1. Even if your Grand Cherokee was "fixed" under 23V413 (2023) or 26V051 (2026), you must go back AGAIN for 89D — the new remedy replaces the lower isolators.
2. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — VINs became searchable Sept 9, 2026; interim letters mail Sept 29.
3. 2021-2023 GC L, 2022-2023 GC, 2022-2023 GC 4xe (non-air-suspension) are in scope.
4. FCA customer service: 1-800-853-1403, free remedy.
