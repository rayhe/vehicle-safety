The Interlock Was Never Added. Eight School Buses Could Drive Off With the Lift Down

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 13, 2026

If your kid rides a school bus in a wheelchair, this one deserves sixty seconds of your time. Greenpower Motor Company has recalled eight 2021 Nano Beast electric school buses because the interlock logic that prevents the bus from moving while the wheelchair lift is deployed was, in the manufacturer's own words, "not properly added" on these units.[1] Not broken. Not degraded. Never added. Eight buses left the factory missing a safety function that federal law has required since 2004.

100%
Estimated share of the 8 recalled 2021 Greenpower Nano Beast buses that have the defect, per Greenpower's own Part 573 filing

Read that stat twice. Greenpower's defect notice to NHTSA, filed October 5, 2026, estimates the percentage of the eight buses with the defect at 100 percent.[1] The campaign is 26V643000; Greenpower's internal number is GP2604002. The official consequence language is blunt: if the driver does not set the park brake, a bus on an incline can start rolling on its own. If the driver releases the park brake with the lift down, the bus can roll, be shifted into drive or reverse, and moved. With a wheelchair user on the lift.[1] The failure mode is not hypothetical confusion between two electronic modules. It is a school bus that can drive away from the curb mid-boarding.

Now the part that should make school boards sit up straight. This is not a one-bus weirdness. The same filing's corrective-action section points at interlock requirements S6.10.1 and S6.10.2 of 49 CFR 571.403, the platform-lift safety standard, and describes corrective action across roughly 102 vehicles spanning three model lines: the 2018 through 2024 EV Star, the 2020 through 2023 EV Star Mobility Plus, and the 2021 Nano Beast.[1] And it arrived in the same week as a separate Greenpower recall, same failure family: a control-module interlock software error on 94 EV Star and EV Star Mobility Plus buses that could also let the bus move with the lift deployed.[2] That is a pattern at one manufacturer, around one function, in one week.

It is also not a new rule that caught a company off guard. Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 403 has required platform lifts to include interlocks preventing vehicle movement with the lift in play since December 27, 2004.[3] We have seen this movie before: Collins Bus recalled school buses in 2008 when a lift-interlock module could fail to lock the shifter in park with the lift door open, with the same stated consequence, a vehicle moved with the lift deployed.[4] Eighteen years of precedent said this exact failure was on the list of things to get right, and an electric bus built in 2021 went out the door without the logic at all.

These buses matter beyond their count because of who bought them. The Nano Beast line, including the Access variant with a BraunAbility rear curbside lift and room for 3 or more wheelchair securements, was the bus Greenpower sold to West Virginia school districts under the EPA Clean School Bus program, a $15 million public purchase.[5] Public money bought electric buses for rural districts, which is a fine policy outcome, and somewhere in that supply chain a lift-equipped bus shipped with the interlock logic missing. Federal money should come with a check on the federal interlock, but that audit is on the district, not the manufacturer.

Here is what to do. The fix is a free software update at the dealer: updated control-module logic that allows lift operation only with the park-brake interlock engaged.[1] Owner notification letters are scheduled October 12 through 26, 2026. If your district operates any Greenpower EV Star, EV Star Mobility Plus, or Nano Beast, run every VIN through nhtsa.gov/recalls this week and ask the transportation director, in writing, whether the interlock update has been applied. Until the fix is confirmed: drivers should set the park brake before any lift deployment and keep it set until the lift is stowed, because the federal backstop on these eight buses was never installed.[1]
