# Research Notes: NY vs CA Fatality Divergence

## Core Data (NSC Preliminary, April 2026)
- **National:** April 2026 deaths estimated 3,960 — down 5% from 2025, down 14% from 2024
- **Mileage death rate:** 1.06 per 100M VMT (April 2026), down from 1.11 (2025), 1.25 (2024), 1.36 (2023)
- **New York*:** -78%, 150 fewer deaths (asterisk = likely incomplete reporting, but trend is real)
- **California:** +39%, 243 more deaths through April 2026
- **Net divergence:** 393 deaths between two states going opposite directions

## New York Interventions
1. **Congestion pricing** (launched Jan 5, 2025):
   - 87,000 fewer vehicles/day entering lower Manhattan
   - 12% fewer vehicles in zone
   - Crashes down 14% in congestion relief zone
   - Traffic injuries down 15%
   - Pedestrian fatalities at historic lows (matching 2018)
   - Source: Governor Hochul press release, NYC DOT data

2. **24/7 Speed cameras** (expanded to nighttime/weekend enforcement):
   - Speeding down 30% average at camera locations
   - Houston Street, Manhattan: speeding down 96%
   - Fatalities down 25% in speed camera zones during newly covered hours
   - 95% reduction in deadly speeding at camera locations
   - Source: NYC DOT Commissioner Rodriguez announcement

3. **Sammy's Law** (20 mph default zones):
   - City-wide implementation completed end of 2025
   - Mayor Mamdani reducing to 15 mph in 700 school speed zones in 2026
   - Source: Vital City NYC, NYC.gov

4. **"Super Speeders" legislation** (proposed):
   - Drivers with 20+ speed camera violations: 5× crash risk
   - Drivers with 30+ violations: 50× crash risk
   - Would require intelligent speed assistance devices
   - Source: NYC.gov Vision Zero announcement

5. **Infrastructure changes:**
   - Parking spaces removed near 300 intersections (improves visibility)
   - 23 miles of protected bike lanes
   - Pedestrian and cyclist-focused redesigns
   - Source: Vital City NYC

## NYC-Specific Numbers
- Vision Zero: traffic deaths near lowest levels in recorded history (Q1 2026)
- Pedestrian injuries decreased 12% since congestion pricing
- Motor vehicle occupant injuries decreased 34%
- Total injuries decreased 14%
- Source: NYC.gov, Mamdani administration press release

## California — Why Up 39%?
- No statewide speed camera program (AB 645 authorized only pilot in 6 cities, started July 2024)
- No congestion pricing
- VMT likely increasing (national VMT up 0.7% in April, CA may be higher)
- Possible factors: population growth, sprawl, higher speeds on freeways, less transit investment
- Need to acknowledge: 243 more deaths is absolute, percentage could shift with final data

## FARS Connection
- Top-killing vehicles in FARS (Silverado, F-150, Accord, Civic, Camry) are common everywhere
- Speed cameras target the behavior (speeding) not the vehicle
- Each 6% reduction in driver assistance tech = one fewer death (JAMA Pediatrics study)
- Vehicle age matters: older vehicles = fewer ADAS features = higher death risk

## Limitations
- NSC data is PRELIMINARY — final FARS numbers could differ significantly
- New York asterisk suggests incomplete reporting, true decline likely lower than 78%
- California increase could partially reflect faster reporting, not necessarily more deaths
- Congestion pricing benefits concentrated in Manhattan — statewide effect unclear
- Correlation ≠ causation: other factors (weather, gas prices, demographics) could contribute

## Strongest Counterargument
- New York's 78% figure is probably inflated by reporting lag — actual decline might be 20-30%
- Even so, the directional contrast with California is real
- California's AB 645 speed camera pilot only covers 6 cities — too limited and too new to show effect
