# Research: #949 — NHTSA Cybercab Special Order: prove it under oath

**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (investigations, paradoxes, deadpan)

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** RESEARCH complete

## The story
On **September 10, 2026**, NHTSA escalated its Cybercab investigation from an audit query to a **formal Special Order**: 21 detailed requests demanding Tesla prove, under oath, that the Cybercab — a vehicle with no steering wheel, no pedals, no mirrors — was legally certified as FMVSS-compliant. Tesla has until **September 30** to respond. Failing to answer fully and truthfully carries penalties of **up to $139 million**; falsifying a sworn response can bring criminal penalties — a fine or up to 15 years in prison. Signed by NHTSA Chief Counsel **Peter Simshauser**, addressed directly to Tesla's head of litigation and associate general counsel for regulatory affairs.

This is NOT a recall and NOT a safety finding. The car is not accused of being dangerous. The paperwork is accused of being fiction.

## Timeline (the velocity is the story)
- **Sept 3, 2026:** Tesla begins commercial Cybercab service in Austin. NHTSA opens Audit Query AQ26002 the same day (announced Sept 4). ~1,000 vehicles estimated; 45 Cybercabs registered in Texas of 420 Tesla autonomous vehicles.
- **Sept 10, 2026:** NHTSA issues Special Order — 12 days after the audit opened. (Reported Sept 15 by Electrek, Sept 17 by TechTimes.)
- **Sept 30, 2026:** Tesla's sworn response due.
- Contrast: Zoox self-certified its control-less robotaxi in 2022 → NHTSA special order (2022) → formal audit query (2023) → Part 555 exemption granted **July 2026** — ~4 years. Tesla got 12 days from audit to sworn order.

## Verified facts (primary/credible sources)
1. **Electrek, Sept 15, 2026** (verified full article read): Special Order = 21 requests, signed by Chief Counsel Peter Simshauser, addressed to Tesla head of litigation + associate GC for regulatory affairs. Response due Sept 30 under oath by a responsible officer; incomplete/untruthful answers → up to $139M penalties; falsifying/withholding → criminal (fine or up to 15 years prison).
   - NHTSA quotes the Safety Act back at Tesla: "A person may not issue" a certification "if, in exercising reasonable care, the person has reason to know the certificate is false or misleading in a material respect."
   - **Request 10:** presses Tesla on which standards it deems inapplicable — Tesla's likely defense is that human-driver rules (mirror adjustment, turn signals) were never meant for a car with no driver. NHTSA's counter: until rules are formally rewritten, they still apply, and "inapplicable" isn't a box the manufacturer checks for itself.
   - **Request 19:** FMVSS No. 135 — "The service brakes shall be activated by means of a foot control." The Cybercab has no foot control. NHTSA notes it has publicly stated a manufacturer without a foot-activated service brake could not certify to that standard — then asks Tesla to explain how it did exactly that.
   - **"Make inoperative" trap:** requests probe whether Tesla used temporarily attached human controls to certify compliance and then stripped them out. The Safety Act's "make inoperative" prohibition: you cannot certify a car with required safety equipment installed and remove it before delivery. Order defines "subject vehicles" as those "lacking permanently attached, conventional manual controls" (brake pedal, accelerator, steering wheel, mirrors, telltales).
   - Standards walked through: FMVSS 101 (controls/telltales), 102 (shift-position display), 108 (turn signals self-cancel by steering-wheel rotation), 111 (mirrors/rearview images), 126 (stability-control telltales).
   - NHTSA's own 2022 rule said further FMVSS changes would "likely be necessary" before an ADS-only vehicle could be manufactured for sale — unless the automaker holds a Part 555 exemption. **Tesla has no Part 555 exemption.**
   - June 2026: NHTSA proposed amending the FMVSS 135 foot-brake rule for automated vehicles. Proposal is not law. NHTSA is enforcing the old rule while proposing to delete it.
2. **Zoox contrast (Electrek, Sept 15):** In July 2026, NHTSA granted Amazon's Zoox a temporary Part 555 exemption covering eight FMVSS — including the foot-brake rule (135) and mirror rules (111) Tesla is now being pressed on. Exemption lets Zoox charge for rides in no-controls pods, caps fleet at 2,500 vehicles/year, runs through 2028. Zoox applied, made its safety case, waited. Tesla certified itself compliant and started charging Sept 3. No application, no exemption, no cap.
3. **NHTSA ODI, Audit Query AQ26002 resume (Sept 3, 2026, Investigator Syed Rahaman):** 1,000 estimated Cybercab vehicles; prompted by public information; examines "the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab," including "the extent to which Tesla's certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable."
4. **TechTimes, Sept 17, 2026:** confirms sworn-oath requirement; lays out the three post-Sept-30 outcomes: (a) NHTSA accepts → validates inapplicability-as-pathway for the whole industry; (b) NHTSA rejects → modifications, retroactive Part 555 application, recall, or civil penalties; (c) NHTSA finalizes the FMVSS amendments first → Request 19's legal basis evaporates.
5. **Twin docket:** Sept 9, 2026 — Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) wrote DOT citing 43 videos of drivers apparently asleep with Autopilot/FSD engaged (17 posted in 2026), tied to engineering analysis EA26002 covering ~3.2M FSD-equipped vehicles; asked five questions by Sept 30, including whether NHTSA data points to a defect requiring a recall. (USA Today, Sept 10, 2026; goodcarbadcar Sept 14.) So NHTSA simultaneously investigates: (a) a car certified on the premise no driver is needed, and (b) drivers in cars that require one who have stopped driving. Two dockets, opposite assumptions, one company.
6. **IIHS, March 12, 2024 (safeguard ratings):** 14 partial-automation systems rated — 1 acceptable, 2 marginal, 11 poor, none good. Tesla FSD (Beta) worst overall, poor in 6 of 8 categories.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** Yes. Sworn Special Order is a Sept 10/15 development (this week); deadline Sept 30 (11 days out); criminal penalties in play. No site draft covers the Special Order — #839/#844/#887 covered the Sept 3-4 audit query; the escalation is new.
- **Novel angle?** Yes. Everyone is covering "can the Cybercab drive itself." The novel story is the paperwork trial: NHTSA is not asking whether the car is safe — it is asking Tesla to swear under oath that declaring human-driver standards "inapplicable" was legal. Plus the velocity paradox (12 days vs Zoox's 4 years) and the twin-docket paradox (car assumes no driver; drivers assume no driving). Electrek's line captures it: "wanting the rules to change isn't the same as the rules having changed."
- **Data angle?** Yes. 21 requests, 12 days, $139M, 15 years, 2,500-vehicle cap math (again: the exemption path caps the fleet; self-certification is the only way to scale a robotaxi business — now under sworn review), the six named FMVSS numbers, 43 asleep-driver videos.

## Actionable insights (for the article)
- The Cybercab's legality is being litigated on paper, not on pavement. If you rode one in Austin, the vehicle you were in was certified via Tesla's own inapplicability determinations — NHTSA is now testing those under oath, with an answer due Sept 30.
- The 2,500-vehicle Part 555 cap is the arithmetic behind the whole fight: the exemption path cannot scale a robotaxi fleet, so Tesla bet the company on self-certification. Watch the Sept 30 filing — outcome (a) above would validate the bet for the entire industry.
- If NHTSA rejects the certification, remedies range from retroactive exemption applications to recall of deployed vehicles.
