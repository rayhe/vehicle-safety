Jeep Told 1.08 Million Owners to Park Outside in June, and the Fix Still Isn't Here

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 23, 2026

Your Jeep lives in the driveway now, because Stellantis is afraid it might burn your garage down. That was the arrangement in June: temporary exile, fix coming. A company spokesman said a remedy was expected by the end of July.[1] It is late September, and 1,076,999 Wranglers and Gladiators are still banished to the curb, with no repair available.[2]

0.1%
Estimated share of recalled Jeeps that actually carry the defective wiring, while the other 99.9% park outside anyway

Start with the electric hydraulic power steering pump wiring: during factory assembly, out-of-tolerance terminals took excessive insertion force, which spread the connector terminals and left a high-resistance connection that can heat up and ignite nearby combustible material.[3] The unnerving part is the trigger condition. None. A fire can start with the vehicle parked and the ignition off, which is why NHTSA's instruction is not "drive carefully" but "park outside and away from structures until repaired."[4] Roughly 72 fires have been reported, with one potential injury and no crashes or fatalities.[3]

Do the arithmetic. 72 fires across 1,076,999 vehicles is about 6.7 fires per 100,000 Jeeps. Stellantis estimates only around 0.1% of the recalled population actually has the defective connection, which works out to roughly 1,077 vehicles. So a million Jeeps sit in driveways because a thousand of them might smolder. The numbers smirk, but the failure mode explains the thousand-to-one caution: an ignition-off fire gives the owner nothing to react to. No warning light, no smoke on startup, no chance to pull over; the first sign is the garage going up.

Then there is the timeline, which is where this story stops being a recall notice and starts being a case file. Stellantis first investigated these fires from May 2023 to April 2024, then closed the case, citing a low rate of occurrence.[3] The fires did not get the memo. After a resurgence of reports, the company reopened the investigation in August 2024, and NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened a formal probe the following month.[5] Stellantis confirmed the defect in May 2026 and filed the recall on June 4, 2026.[6] From first fire reports to recall: nearly three years, including four months where the official position was that nothing needed doing.

In fairness, the defense deserves its full hearing. A 0.1% defect estimate means 99.9% of affected owners are parking outside over a connector their Jeep never had. No one has died, and no crash has been attributed to this. Stellantis drew the recall population from a suspect production window (build dates June 2020 through December 2024), not from confirmed-defective parts, so the net was deliberately, enormously wide. Read generously, Stellantis is erring on the side of caution with a fire risk it cannot yet isolate. Read less generously, it spent three years deciding whether a low fire rate was low enough, and owners are now paying for that deliberation one driveway at a time, past a remedy deadline the company set for itself.

If you own a 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator, the drill is simple. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls against campaign 26V363, or call FCA US at 1-800-853-1403 and reference recall 21D.[4] Park outside, away from the house and other vehicles, until the repair is done. Watch for a "Service Power Steering" warning or any loss of steering assist; owners who saw those symptoms were the ones whose connectors had already started failing.[7] Shopping used? A 2021-2025 Wrangler on a lot right now carries this open recall until a dealer documents the repair, so make the VIN check part of the test drive, not an afterthought. And keep an eye on whether Stellantis publishes a remedy before the calendar runs out on another promised month.
