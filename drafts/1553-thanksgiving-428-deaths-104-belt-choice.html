428 People Will Die This Thanksgiving. 104 of Them Just Need to Click a Belt.

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
November 21, 2026

The National Safety Council put a number on this Thanksgiving weekend: 428 people will die on American roads between Wednesday evening and Sunday night. Read the fine print before you repeat that number, because it arrives with a confidence interval of 372 to 487, a spread of 115 deaths. Last year’s estimate was 439. Eleven fewer deaths, inside an interval ten times wider than the improvement. Call it what it is: flat, within error, dressed up as a 2 percent decrease.[1][2]

104
Additional lives saved this Thanksgiving weekend if every occupant wore a seat belt, on top of the 160 already saved by belt use.

Here is the number inside the number that actually matters. Seat belts, when used, cut fatality risk 45 percent for front-seat car occupants. NSC figures 160 lives will be saved this weekend because people buckle up, and 104 more would be saved if everyone did. Add them up and 264 lives ride on a strap of webbing over the long weekend. That is 24 percent of the entire forecast, and unlike the 2 percent trend, it is not a rounding artifact. It is a choice, made one click at a time, in every car on every road in the country.[1]

Last Thanksgiving tells you what the forecast is really made of. NHTSA counted 286 passenger-vehicle occupants killed over the 2024 holiday weekend, and 128 of them, 45 percent, were not wearing a belt. After dark the share hit 50 percent. Alcohol ran through 37 percent of Thanksgiving fatalities, against 30 percent of traffic deaths in a normal year, and NHTSA counted 868 drunk-driving deaths across the five Thanksgiving periods from 2019 to 2023. Enforcement is already rolling: the national Click It or Ticket campaign runs November 23 through 29, which means the ticket is the nudge and the belt is the fix, in the same week as the forecast.[3][4][6]

Fairness check, because a forecast is not a body count. NSC’s 428 is a statistical projection, and the real number could land anywhere from 372 to 487. Last year’s 439 was also an estimate, so the year-over-year comparison is model output stacked on model output. The 104-lives figure assumes everybody buckles, which no campaign has ever achieved, and a belt does nothing for the drunk driver who wore one. Buckle up and drive impaired and you are still the problem. None of that shrinks the lever: among everything a single driver controls this weekend, after sobriety, the belt is the biggest one.[1][5]

So the ask is small and dated. Buckle up in every seat, every trip, back seat included, before the car moves. Sort the sober ride before the drinking starts, because the deadliest drunk-driving day of the holiday week is the Saturday after, not Wednesday. If you are driving the long haul home, check the belts before you check the mirrors. 428 is a guess. 104 is a choice.
