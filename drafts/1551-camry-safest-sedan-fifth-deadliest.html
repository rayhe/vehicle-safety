The Safest Sedan in America Has Killed 6,328 People

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 21, 2026

Every crash lab in America agrees: the 2026 Toyota Camry is the safest sedan you can buy. Good in every IIHS crash test, Top Safety Pick+, five NHTSA stars across the board.[6] Meanwhile the federal fatality file quietly disagrees, in the way only a body count can. 6,328 people died in Camrys between 2014 and 2023, the fifth-highest body count of any nameplate in the country. Both facts are true, and the space between them is where the story lives.[1][2]

6,328
People who died in Toyota Camrys, 2014–2023. Only four nameplates killed more: Silverado, F-150, Accord, Civic.

Start with the drivers, because that is where the easy explanation dies. Camry drivers in fatal crashes tested impaired 19.2 percent of the time, tied with the Corolla for the soberest drivers in the whole sedan comparison, and a full point below the Accord's. Yet the Camry's death rate runs 2.03 per 100 million miles, more than five times the Kia Forte's 0.40 and nearly four times the CR-V's 0.53, with impairment rates that barely move across the table. Sober drivers, ordinary rate, extraordinary pile of bodies. Volume explains part of it: 2.7 million Camrys on the road will produce a large number at almost any rate. Volume does not explain why the Accord, with a smaller fleet, kills 34 percent faster per mile, or why the Forte, at one-fifth the Camry's rate, needs five Camry lifetimes to match one Camry decade.[1]

Now look at the calendar, because the car in the lab is not the car in the file. Nearly two-thirds of the Camry dead, 64.3 percent, were in pre-2010 model years, and only 7.8 percent in 2018 or newer. IIHS tested a 2025 Camry, a hybrid-only redesign with a stiffer body and a full suite of standard driver assist, and gave it Good in the small overlap, the updated moderate overlap, and the updated side test, plus an Acceptable for rear-passenger restraints. NHTSA gave it five stars. None of that describes the 2007 Camry still commuting in 2026, which is the single deadliest model year in the file at 426 deaths, more than double its neighbors in 2006 and 2008, for reasons the data cannot explain and this article will not invent. A 2007 Camry is nineteen years old. The average car on American roads is 12.8 years old, a record, which means the fleet keeps aging into the danger zone faster than the lab can certify its way out.[2][3]

One more uncomfortable number before the advice. Adjust each recent model year for its years on the road inside the window, and the Camry's death pace barely moves: 2015 through 2021 run flat at roughly 20 to 26 deaths per exposure-year, with no clear improvement at all, while the Civic's newest cohort fell about 40 percent over the same stretch. Either the Camry's redesigns did not move the needle on the road, or the gains are still too small and too recent to see. Both readings are bad news for anyone who assumes a 2021 Camry is meaningfully safer than a 2015 one, because the file says it is not, yet.[1]

Fairness check, because the headline is doing a lot of work. Ranked by rate, the Camry sits 36th of 337 models, thoroughly mid-pack, and 20th of 145 sedans. Its drivers are among the soberest in the segment. The 6,328 is what 2.7 million cars and ten years produce at an ordinary rate, and the current car just earned the highest award the Institute gives. All true. Also true: ordinary is a choice of denominator. Against the Forte, the Camry kills five times faster per mile. Against the CR-V in its own showroom, nearly four times. A rate can be mid-pack and still be a public-health problem when the pack is this big, and a lab award can be perfectly deserved and still describe almost none of the cars actually on the road.

If you drive a Camry, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and check the model year before you check anything else. A 2025 or 2026 Camry is the safest sedan IIHS tested this cycle. A 2007 Camry is the single deadliest model year in this file, and a pre-2010 Camry of any year carries nearly two-thirds of the nameplate's dead. If you are shopping used, the safest Camry is the newest one you can afford, and the gap between a 2015 and a 2021 is smaller than the badge suggests. The lab has done its part. The fleet has not caught up.
