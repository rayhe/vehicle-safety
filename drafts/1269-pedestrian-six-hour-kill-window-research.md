# Research: #1269 Pedestrian deaths concentrate in six evening hours; clocks change Nov 1

**Slug:** 1269-pedestrian-six-hour-kill-window | **Journalist:** Dale Impactor III | **Kicker:** Sobriety Report

## News peg
October is National Pedestrian Safety Month, and daylight saving time ends Sunday, November 1, 2026, pushing the evening commute into darkness. FARS 2024 final tables show pedestrian deaths are not spread evenly across the day: nearly half land in a single six-hour evening window, and nearly three-quarters happen away from intersections.

## Verified facts (5 primary sources)
1. **FARS Encyclopedia, People - Pedestrians, 2024 (NHTSA):** 7,080 pedestrians killed in 2024. Time of day: 6:00-8:59 p.m. 1,703 deaths (24.1%); 9:00-11:59 p.m. 1,648 (23.3%). Combined 3,351 of 7,080 = 47.3% in six evening hours. Location: non-intersection 5,104 (72.1%); intersection 1,246 (17.6%); other/unknown 730 (10.3%). Age split: pedestrians 65-74 die at intersections at the highest rate (263 of 910, 28.9%); children under 5 die mostly away from intersections (38 of 56, 67.9% non-intersection). Source: http://www-fars.nhtsa.dot.gov/People/PeoplePedestrians.aspx
2. **IIHS, Pedestrians topic page (posted June 2026):** 7,080 pedestrian deaths in 2024 = 18% of all crash fatalities; pedestrian deaths up 72% since the 2009 low. Vehicles with pedestrian-detecting automatic braking had a 27% lower rate of pedestrian crashes than vehicles without it (Cicchino, 2022). Source: https://www.iihs.org/research-areas/fatality-statistics/detail/pedestrians
3. **NHTSA Pedestrian Safety Month guidance via Drive Safe Hampton Roads (Oct 2026):** in 2024 a pedestrian died every 74 minutes and one was injured every 7 minutes; 71,635 pedestrians injured in 2024; 7,080 killed, a 4% decrease from 2023. 76% of pedestrian fatalities in 2024 occurred in the dark. Alcohol use (driver and/or pedestrian) was reported in 43% of all fatal pedestrian crashes in 2024. Source: https://www.drivesafehr.org/pedestrian-safety-month/
4. **UC Berkeley SafeTREC, 2026 Traffic Safety Facts: Pedestrian Safety:** 7,080 killed nationally in 2024 (18% of all motor-vehicle fatalities); an average of 20 pedestrians killed per day; national pedestrian fatalities down 3.9% 2023-2024. California: 1,090 killed in 2024, 28% of the state's motor-vehicle fatalities, down 5.2%. Source: https://safetrec.berkeley.edu/traffic-safety-facts/2026-safetrec-traffic-safety-facts/2026-safetrec-traffic-safety-facts-0

## Limitations
- The 47.3% window share is a share of deaths, not a risk rate: it partly reflects exposure (more people walk and drive in the evening) as well as darkness and alcohol. The article says this, not just the raw share.
- The intersection split is also a share of deaths, not exposure-adjusted risk; crossing at a marked intersection remains the recommended behavior, and the point is that mid-block danger dominates the body count.
- The 43% alcohol figure counts crashes where alcohol was reported for the driver, the pedestrian, or both; it does not mean 43% of drivers were drunk.
- 2024 is the latest final FARS year; 2025 GHSA estimates (6,732, down further) are cited only as direction, not as the article's core data.
- The DST hook is about light conditions, not a claim that the clock change itself causes a measured spike; no causal claim is made.

## Counterargument (strongest)
The trend is genuinely improving: pedestrian deaths fell in 2024 and again per 2025 estimates, and pedestrian-detecting AEB cuts crash rates 27%. The evening concentration partly reflects when people are out, not a new danger. The honest version of the story is "a shrinking but still concentrated problem about to get darker," not "pedestrians have never been less safe."

## Actionable insights
- If you walk after 6 p.m.: assume you are invisible. Reflective gear, a light, and a marked crossing beat dark clothes and a mid-block dash; 72% of the dead were not at intersections.
- If you drive 6 p.m. to midnight: slow down in residential and commercial areas, and treat every unlit mid-block stretch as a crossing zone. This is the window, and next Sunday it goes dark an hour earlier.
- If you drink: do not drive, and do not walk home along a fast road in the dark. Alcohol shows up in 43% of fatal pedestrian crashes.
- If you are car shopping: pedestrian-detecting automatic emergency braking is worth specifying; IIHS ties it to a 27% lower pedestrian-crash rate.
- Check your own evening route: the danger is not the intersection you fear, it is the dark stretch between them.
