Ford Recalls 223,472 F-150s. The Cameras Were Watching Everything Except the Fuel Tank Straps

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 16, 2026

Here is a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute. On September 9, Ford recalled 223,472 F-150 pickups because the fuel tank might fall off while you are driving. Not leak. Detach. A 20-plus-gallon tank of gasoline departing the vehicle at highway speed, rolling down the interstate behind you, entirely unsupervised.

3.5 years
How long fuel tank strap verification ran without continuous camera use, January 2023 through August 2026

Ford's own recall report names the culprit with the bureaucratic frankness of a man pleading the fifth. In some trucks, the front fuel tank strap slots were never fully inserted into the frame rail during assembly. And the reason it went undetected for so long? Quote: the vehicles were built "when camera systems to verify proper fuel tank strap assembly were not in continuous use" at the F-150 plants. Not continuous use, for three and a half years. At Dearborn and Kansas City, the two plants that build the F-150.

Now the part that makes this art. Ford has a camera system built exactly for this job. Last summer the company showed it off: the Mobile AI Vision System, MAIVS, consumer smartphones lashed to 3D-printed mounts, staring down the assembly line, checking for 25 different defects. CEO Jim Farley talked up the quality overhaul. The press loved it, and the cameras were installed. They were just not, you know, continuously watching the straps that hold the fuel tank on.

Sixteen warranty claims globally surfaced the problem, with the earliest dating to 2018, which means the strap problem was appearing years before the January 2023 start of the camera gap Ford admits to, and the Free Press account of the recall report offers no explanation for why those early claims never triggered a fix. No accidents or injuries reported, which is either reassuring or the statistical equivalent of crossing a minefield without losing a leg. Ford notes the affected trucks are "less than 1% of the F-150 vehicle population," and it shrank the recall population to trucks that were unsold or had under three months in service as of late August, which reads like Ford's data says a loose strap shows itself early. Fine. Good. Comforting in the way that learning your parachute usually opens is comforting.

The same September 9 filing batch also swept up 1,945 Transit vans whose wireless seat belt reminder can display buckled when the rear passenger is not, meaning your dash can lie to you about whether your kids are belted. Plus 37 F-150s with the wrong rear axle tail bearing installed, a defect that can lock the rear wheel or separate the driveshaft. Plus 17 with a loose rear suspension stabilizer rod. The September 9 bundle totals 225,471 vehicles, and puts Ford at 71 recalls so far in 2026, down from 108 this time last year and still the most of any automaker. Credit where due: the trend is bending. But the trend line on recall counts and the trend line on trust are different graphs, and only one of them has a fuel tank rolling down the highway on it.

What you should do

If you bought or leased a 2023-2027 F-150 recently, or one sat on a dealer lot most of its life, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. VINs for this recall (Ford 26S69, NHTSA 26V578000) were searchable starting September 14, and Ford began mailing owner letters the same day. The dealer inspects the fuel tank straps and replaces them free. Do not skip the letter because nothing has fallen off yet. That is not how fuel tanks work.

What this did not prove

Sixteen warranty claims and one recall do not give us a failure rate. Sixteen is the numerator; the denominator, how many of the 223,472 trucks actually have a loose strap versus how many merely sat on the line during the unverified window, is unknown, so do not do per-truck probability math on this. Ford's data only captures warranty claims, not every loose strap in the wild, which means the true count sits somewhere between "tiny" and "nobody checked for three and a half years." The camera gap correlates with the defect window; it does not prove the cameras would have caught it. MAIVS was a pilot watching for 25 specific things, not an all-seeing eye, and Ford's recall count dropping year over year is a genuine improvement worth acknowledging before we laugh too hard.
