Ford Is Recalling 223,472 F-150s. Your Fuel Tank May Be Held On By Hope.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 16, 2026

The numbers don't lie, but they do occasionally smirk. Ford just told NHTSA that 223,472 F-150s might have fuel tanks that were never properly strapped to the frame, and it is not a bad sensor or a software glitch. The metal strap that holds the gasoline under your truck may not be holding the gasoline under your truck.[1]

~2,235
Trucks Ford estimates actually have improperly secured fuel tanks (1% of the recall population)

A detached fuel tank is a special kind of road hazard, because it leaks, it can stall the engine, and it can ignite. And before any of that, it becomes a forty-gallon projectile rolling loose through morning traffic, full of flammable liquid, looking for a lane.[1]

Ford's Critical Concern Review Group opened the investigation in July. What they found should bother you more than the defect itself. The affected trucks were built during periods when the camera systems installed to verify fuel tank strap engagement were not continuously in use.[2] Read that again. Ford built a verification system for this exact failure, then left it switched off. The strap is a metal band a worker or a machine engages into the frame rail; if it's not fully seated, the tank shifts. Sixteen warranty claims surfaced worldwide before the recall, out of an estimated 2,235. Ford found less than one percent of the problem it says exists.

That math cuts both ways, and a noir detective owes you the honest version. Problems caused by badly installed straps surface early in a truck's service life, according to Ford's filing. No crashes. No injuries. Ninety-nine percent of recalled owners will get inspected and drive home unchanged. This is the system working: a manufacturer catches a low-probability installation error through warranty surveillance and fixes it for free.[2] The smirk is that the camera was supposed to catch it on the line, three years before a warranty claim ever filed.

And one more detail from the police blotter: this recall (Ford 26S69, NHTSA 26V578000) was one of four Ford recalls issued in the same September week, covering incorrect rear axle tail bearings, a loose rear suspension stabilizer rod, this fuel tank, and the wireless seatbelt reminder on Transits that your Crash Report covered yesterday.[3] The 2026 F-150 appears in three of the four, so somebody in Dearborn is having a month.

What this article did not prove: the 1% figure is Ford's own estimate, and the method behind it lives in a Part 573 filing I haven't laid hands on. I couldn't verify which assembly plants built the affected trucks or how long the cameras were down. FARS data doesn't touch this story; there are no fatal crashes to analyze, only a hazard that Ford caught first. And the trucks in question span model years 2023 through 2027, built between January 4, 2023 and August 27, 2026, so model year alone won't tell you if you're on the list.[1]

Do this today. Grab your 17-character VIN off the windshield, plug it into nhtsa.gov/recalls, and check whether your F-150 is one of the 223,472. Owner notification letters don't go out until September 21, so the website knows before your mailbox does.[4] If yours is flagged, call a Ford dealer, reference recall 26S69, and the inspection costs you nothing. Same strap, same design, attached properly to the frame this time.
