# Research: #1092 — The RV Industry Keeps Dropping Its Water Tanks

**Slug:** 1092-rv-falling-water-tank-double-recall
**Journalist:** Rex Driverton (Senior Crash Correspondent — investigations, paradoxes, deadpan dark humor)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Working headline:** "Two Recalls, One Week, Same Falling Water Tank. The RV Industry Has a Dropping Problem."

## Kill test

**Proposed angle (1-2 sentences):** In the first week of October 2026, two Jayco sister brands — Starcraft RV (26V626) and Highland Ridge RV (26V627) — recalled 2022-2024 travel trailers because the fresh water tank's fasteners can fail and drop the tank onto the highway. It is the fourth documented "falling water tank" recall cycle in 13 years, and the second filing carries a campaign number nearly consecutive with the first (9902669 vs 9904669) and the same customer-service phone line: one corporate QA system, two brand names, same dropped tank.

**Challenge:** After 1,000+ articles, is this just recall news? No. Three things make it novel: (1) nobody has connected the four incidents — Monaco 2013 (4,009 vehicles), Forest River 2024/2025 (1,868+), Jayco double-filing 2026 — into a single recurring failure-mode story; (2) the sister-brand double filing in the same NHTSA weekly batch, with consecutive internal campaign numbers and identical contact numbers, is an original cross-tab showing how brand proliferation masks shared engineering; (3) the "when full" qualifier from the 2025 Forest River filing makes the physics explicit — the tank is heaviest exactly when the owner does the responsible thing (fill up before the trip), so the failure mode punishes preparation.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

Investigation began May 2, 2013 after a consumer NHTSA complaint of a tank "falling through the underbelly." Monaco: not aware of any accidents or injuries. Remedy: cross brace between mounting brackets. https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2013/RCDNN-13V284-4622.pdf
5. **Transport Canada recall RA-75534 (2024)** — Forest River Viking/Clipper trailers (2020-2024 model years): "the fresh water tank may not be secured properly. 6. **Thor Industries (Wikipedia)** — Starcraft RV and Highland Ridge RV are both Jayco, Inc. brands; Jayco (Middlebury, Indiana) acquired by Thor Industries in 2016. Same corporate parent, same engineering/quality systems. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Industries

## Key numbers
- 26V626 / 26V627: announced week of Oct 5, 2026; 2022-2024 model years; populations not yet published
- 25V708/25V727: 1,868 units (2021 XLR Toy Hauler)
- 2013 Monaco: 4,009 vehicles (2,250 US + 1,759 exported), 100% estimated defect rate in suspect population
- Water: 8.34 lbs/gallon. A 40-gallon fresh tank weighs ~334 lbs full — roughly the weight of a grand piano's action, or two adult passengers, falling off at 65 mph.
- Campaign numbers 9902669 and 9904669: filed as a pair, same phone line (800-283-8267)

## The novel cross-tab / observation
The RV industry's fresh-water-tank failures are not isolated: four distinct recall cycles (2013, 2024, 2025, 2026) across three corporate families (Monaco, Forest River, Jayco/Thor), all the same failure — tank mounts/fasteners/brackets fail, tank separates at speed, road hazard. The 2026 double filing is the tell: Starcraft and Highland Ridge are marketed as separate brands with separate model lines (Super Lite Maxx vs Range Lite Air), but the consecutive campaign numbers and shared phone line reveal a single manufacturing/quality pipeline. Brand diversity is marketing; the underbelly is one design.

## Limitations
- Populations for 26V626/26V627 not confirmed at research time; article will cite model years and defect, not unit counts.
- Exact fresh-water capacities of the Range Lite Air and Super Lite Maxx not verified; article uses generic gallon-to-pounds math labeled as illustration, not model-specific claims.
- No injuries/crashes attributed to the 2026 filings in the sources reviewed; the 2013 Monaco filing explicitly reports none known.
- "When full" qualifier comes from the 2025 Forest River filing, not the 2026 Jayco filings; article will attribute it to the right recall.

## Counterargument (strongest)
RVs ship ~300,000-500,000 units a year in the US; a few thousand recalled tanks over 13 years is a small absolute rate, and the system is working — manufacturers are self-reporting under Part 573 before injuries. Travel trailers also live brutal lives: owners overload tanks, skip maintenance, and tow on washboard roads. Some of these failures may be maintenance, not design. The honest frame: the failure mode keeps recurring across different companies and decades, which suggests the industry's tank-mount engineering margin — not any one brand — is the common denominator.

## Actionable insight
If you own a 2022-2024 Starcraft Super Lite Maxx or Highland Ridge Range Lite Air: run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, don't wait for the letter. Until the brackets are inspected, tow with the fresh tank empty or near-empty (the 2025 precedent failed "when full"). General used-trailer advice: crawl under any travel trailer you're buying and look at the fresh/gray/black tank straps and brackets — rust, cracked welds, or missing hardware are walk-away signals.
