# Research: Ford 7.3L Godzilla V8 Idle-Patch Probe (DP26011)

## Angle
The engine Ford sold to fleets as the simple, unkillable gas alternative to a diesel is now under a federal defect-petition investigation, and the most damning allegation is not the failures themselves. It is that Ford allegedly already knew, quietly pushed a software patch that raised idle speed, and never told owners why. A secret fix is an admission with the serial numbers filed off.

## Kill test
Genuinely newsworthy: NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened the probe October 26, 2026, one day before this draft. Novel angle for this site: no prior Crash Report article covers the Godzilla 7.3L lifter/cam failure (checked: no godzilla/7.3 slugs in queue or drafts). The work-vehicle stakes are distinct from consumer recalls: E-350 vans, F59 stripped chassis, and F-750 medium-duty trucks are ambulances, transit buses, delivery trucks, and utility rigs. A sudden loss of motive power at highway speed in a loaded commercial vehicle is a different hazard class than a consumer SUV stalling.

## Verified facts (3+ primary sources)
1. **NHTSA ODI defect petition DP26011, opened October 26, 2026.** The petition itself was filed in June 2026. ODI must respond to petitions alleging safety issues. Population: an estimated 54,787 vehicles: 2021-2022 Ford E-350, 2021 Ford F-59, 2021 Ford F-750, all with the 7.3-liter gasoline V8 (Godzilla). Sources: Reuters via TradingView (Oct 26, 2026), Autoblog (Oct 27, 2026), HotCars (Oct 27, 2026), CarBuzz.
2. **Alleged mechanism:** low oil pressure at idle, around 6 psi, starves the valvetrain; lifter rollers and camshaft lobes wear severely; failure can mean catastrophic engine failure and sudden loss of motive power while driving at highway speeds. Sources: petition summary as reported by Autoblog, CarBuzz, HotCars.
3. **Alleged secret patch:** the petition claims Ford knew about the low-oil-pressure problem and released a software update raising idle speed to about 1,000 rpm, without a recall or owner notification explaining the change. Sources: Autoblog, HotCars, CarBuzz (all reporting the petition's claims, not confirmed Ford admissions).
4. **Prior Ford action, CSP 24B27:** Ford ran customer satisfaction program 24B27 covering 2021-2022 models for low idle oil pressure. Owners and forums describe it as a software remedy; it was a customer satisfaction program, not a safety recall. Sources: NHTSA complaint 11701901 (Montachusett Regional Transit Authority) and Ford-Trucks forum threads on CSP 24B27.
5. **Fleet complaint evidence:**
   - A North Carolina fleet owner reported 5 of 11 Ford vehicles with the 7.3L V8 suffered engine failures, earliest at 45,745 miles, latest at 103,177 miles. Source: HotCars reporting the petition's complaints.
   - Montachusett Regional Transit Authority (Fitchburg, MA) filed NHTSA complaint 11701901: engine failure at 78,172 miles with excess camshaft-lobe and valve-tappet wear, 22 failures across its fleet, some engines changed twice, and a complaint that Ford "is not acknowledging" the problem. Source: NHTSA complaint via oemdtc.
6. **What ODI has NOT found:** Autoblog notes the agency's opening summary mentions a single reported incident, and no crashes, fires, injuries, or fatalities are attributed to the issue in that summary. The 54,787 figure is the population under review, not a failure count. This article must not present it as a failure count.
7. **Owner-forum corroboration (secondary):** Ford-Trucks threads (Sep 2026) discuss upper-engine ticking/tapping TSBs covering 2020-2025, hot-idle oil-pressure checks via FORScan, lifter part lc3z6500c and camshaft part pc3z6250a, and uncertainty over whether 2026-2027 engines carry revised hardware. Treat as color, not fact.

## What this article must NOT claim
- That NHTSA found a defect. It opened a petition review; defect determination comes later, if at all.
- That 54,787 engines failed. That is the population, not failures.
- That Ford admitted fault. The patch and knowledge claims are petition allegations.
- That CSP 24B27 was a recall. It was a customer satisfaction program.
- Any crash, injury, or death count tied to this failure. None is attributed in the opening summary.

## Actionable takeaways for readers
- If you run a 2021-2022 E-350, 2021 F-59, or 2021 F-750 with the 7.3L gas V8: ask your dealer whether CSP 24B27 was applied, and get a hot-idle oil-pressure reading at full operating temperature (owners report using FORScan; a dealer can do it).
- Ticking or tapping at idle is the early symptom owners report; do not wait for a failure at highway speed.
- File an NHTSA complaint if a dealer brushes off valvetrain noise; ODI petition reviews run on complaint volume.
- If buying a used 2021-2022 example of these vehicles, demand the CSP 24B27 service record and any engine-replacement history.
