# Research: Ten States Saved 421 Lives This Year. California Gave 242 of Them Back.

## Angle
NSC preliminary 2026 data (through May) shows a dramatic state-level divergence in traffic fatalities. Ten states cut deaths by 13% or more, saving a combined 421 lives. Seven states plus DC increased deaths by 10%+, adding 344. California alone added 242 — 70% of all increases from a single state. The national mileage death rate actually INCREASED 2.6% despite these gains.

## Data

### National (NSC, through May 2026)
- May 2026 estimated deaths: 3,420 (up 1% from 2025, down 9% from 2024)
- Mileage death rate: 1.17/100M VMT (up 2.6% from 1.14 in May 2025)
- Miles driven: down 0.7% from May 2025

### Key Calculations
- Net lives saved by 10 improving states: 421
- Net lives added by 8 worsening states/DC: 344
- Net net: 77 lives saved (the improvement nearly canceled out)
- California share of all added deaths: 242/344 = 70.3%
- Illinois alone saved 129 — more than any other state
- If California had held flat: net improvement would be 319 lives

### FARS context
- FARS national 10-year baseline covers 2014-2023
- Average national mileage death rate trend has been declining since 2016 peak
- The 2026 rate (1.17) is below 2023 (1.36) and 2024 (1.28) but above 2025 (1.14)
- The decline is decelerating — and California is the primary drag

## Original Contribution
1. Computing the net effect: 10 states' collective 421-life improvement nearly erased by 8 states' 344-life deterioration
2. Quantifying California's outsized weight: one state = 70% of all added deaths
3. The "Illinois mirror" — Illinois and California are comparable in size/urbanization but went opposite directions (IL -28% vs CA +25%)

## Counterargument
- Preliminary data matures (NSC says estimates tend to increase as fatalities are confirmed)
- Small states show huge percentages on tiny numbers (DC +129% = 9 deaths)
- Year-over-year comparisons are noisy; multi-year trends matter more
- California's increase could reflect population growth, VMT growth, or reporting timing differences

## Limitations
- NSC preliminary estimates are not comparable to NHTSA figures (different methodology)
- State-level data doesn't control for VMT, weather, construction, or policy changes
- We cannot attribute causation from this aggregate data
- Some states may simply be reverting to their pre-pandemic baseline
