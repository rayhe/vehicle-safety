Volkswagen Recalled Its New Cars 4 Times. Two Were Fires, One Was Caught by Canada, and the EV Has No Fix.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

Twelve brands into the recall census, Volkswagen files four. Four campaigns, 167,250 vehicles, and two of the four can catch fire. A Jetta ground wire that a worker forgot to connect, already responsible for three engine-compartment fires. An ID.4 battery that can burn for reasons Volkswagen still cannot explain, with no repair in sight. A Taos fuel tank that fails a rear crash test because its sensor wiring is 80 millimeters too short, discovered by the Canadians, not by Volkswagen. And an Atlas whose backup camera goes dark.[1][4][5][6][7]

4
NHTSA campaigns filed in 2026 touching a 2026 Volkswagen. Twelve-brand total is 144, and Volkswagen filed a third of the 12.0 average. Ford filed ten times as many.

Method first, because a count is only as honest as the query behind it. I ran 42 queries against the NHTSA recalls API for every 2026 Volkswagen model name, plus hybrid and plug-in hybrid variants. Thirty-seven came back HTTP 400. Not empty results. Errors. Five queries worked, and those five found all four campaigns, but the API has no working vocabulary for a 2026 Golf GTI, Golf R, Jetta GLI, or Tiguan, which is the same failure mode that made the Mazda census a zero built on outside sources instead of a query.[1] So the zeros for those four models do not rest on the API. They rest on CarBuzz, which pulled NHTSA's own file in October and counted zero recalls for the Tiguan, the Jetta GLI, the Golf GTI, and the Golf R, and on two independent recall trackers that list zero for the 2026 Tiguan as of this week.[2][3] Control queries prove the API itself works: the same spellings return real campaigns for 2025 models, including a Golf R recall and three Tiguan campaigns. A census that cannot query four of nine models and says so is worth more than a census that queries all nine and pretends it did.

Twelve-brand average is 12.0 campaigns, which puts Volkswagen eight below average, and the only brand quieter is Mazda, at zero, with no fires attached.[13] Four of nine 2026 models are clean: Tiguan, Golf GTI, Golf R, Jetta GLI. Five are touched, and the touches are not equal.

Start with the Jetta, because it is the smallest defect with the sharpest edge. Campaign 26V138, filed March 11, covers 48,165 Jettas from model years 2025 and 2026. A transmission ground wire may not have been connected during production at the Puebla plant, which leaves an open electrical circuit that draws higher current than the wiring was built for. Volkswagen traced it to human error, a worker who did not connect a wire, and by the time the company decided to recall, it knew of six claims in the United States and Canada: three melted wires and connectors, three engine-compartment fires. No injuries. Volkswagen estimates less than 1 percent of the recalled cars actually have the defect, which is reassuring arithmetic until you remember that the defect is a fire, and that the way Volkswagen found it was two field cases in December, not a test.[4]

Then the Taos, which is the one that should embarrass Wolfsburg. Campaign 26V258, filed in April, covers 38,710 Taos crossovers from 2025 and 2026. A fuel pressure sensor sits on top of the fuel tank, held in place by a wiring harness that is too short, and in a severe rear-end crash the tension can rip the sensor out of the tank, leaving a hole for fuel to escape. Volkswagen estimates that 100 percent of the recalled vehicles have the defect. Every single one. Not a statistical tail, not a bad batch, the whole population. And the company did not find it. Transport Canada found it, running a rear crash test on a 2025 Taos in March, watching the tank fail, and telling Volkswagen. A foreign regulator crash-tested a car and found a hole in its gas tank, and the fix is an 80-millimeter wiring extension that a dealer installs for free, which means the defect was 80 millimeters of wire away from not existing.[5]

Atlas owners get the least dramatic campaign and the most paperwork. Campaign 26V473, filed July 22, covers 57,851 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport SUVs from 2024 through 2026. A low-voltage electrical event can knock out the display screen, including the rearview camera image, which violates the federal rear-visibility standard. Volkswagen has 571 claims that may relate, and it estimates 1 percent of the cars have the defect. Fix is a software update, free, and owner letters went out in September.[6] What the census does not count, because it was filed last December, is the Atlas recall that actually scares me: 25V835, wrong wheel bolts installed during manufacturing on 2026 Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport, and ID.4 vehicles, wheels that can detach, with a do-not-drive warning from NHTSA. That is the most dangerous 2026 Volkswagen recall of the year, and it is not in this count, because the census counts filings in 2026 and this one was filed in 2025. CarBuzz counts it, which is why its table reads two for the Atlas while mine reads one. Both numbers are right. They count different things.[2][12]

Save the worst for last, because the ID.4 is the one with no ending. Campaign 26V630, filed October 1, covers 22,524 ID.4s from model years 2023 through 2026, built between January 2023 and February 2025, with battery cells from SK Battery America in Georgia. Certain charging profiles above 80 percent state of charge may trigger thermal propagation in the high-voltage battery. Thermal propagation is the polite term for a battery fire that spreads from cell to cell. Volkswagen lists the root cause as unknown and the remedy as still under development, which is the filing's way of saying it does not know why the batteries fail and has no repair. Interim fix: charge to 80 percent, enforced by an over-the-air cap pushed to the cars in late October. Volkswagen estimates 0.1 percent of the recalled cars have the defect, about 23 vehicles, against 22 claims on file. Twenty-three bad batteries it cannot identify, so 22,524 owners charge to 80 percent.[7]

Context makes it worse. 26V630 is the third ID.4 battery recall in eleven months, and the three together cover about 67,000 cars. The first two, filed in January 2026, covered 44,551 cars from model years 2023 to 2025 for misaligned electrodes in the same supplier's cells. Three recalls, one supplier, zero identified causes. Meanwhile Volkswagen ended ID.4 production at its Chattanooga plant in April 2026, refocused the factory on the gas-powered Atlas, and sold 989 ID.4s in the third quarter, down 92 percent from 12,470 a year earlier.[7][8][9] Nobody is fixing the ID.4. They are winding it down while its battery is still under investigation.

Now the clean cars, because a census that only counts the guilty misses the pattern in the innocent. Tiguan, Golf GTI, Golf R, Jetta GLI: zero 2026 recalls each. Tiguan is the best seller in the lineup, and it carries 30 owner complaints in NHTSA's file, more than any other 2026 Volkswagen, with phantom braking reported across the Jetta, the GLI, the Golf R, and the Tiguan.[2][3] Complaints are unverified owner reports, not findings, but the Tiguan's 2025 sibling was recalled twice in 2026: once because a body control module fault could switch the ignition off while driving, and once, in the steering-bolt campaign, for a bolt that can corrode and cost the car its steering. That is the shape a 2026 Tiguan recall would take if one comes, and the 30 complaints are the raw material it would be made from.

Give the skeptics their due, because their case is real. Four recalls is a small number, and a census that counts filings counts paperwork, not safety. Defect estimates are tiny: less than 1 percent for the Jetta, 1 percent for the Atlas, 0.1 percent for the ID.4, and only the Taos, at 100 percent, is a defect in every car. A skeptic will say the honest headline is that Volkswagen built a quiet year: the best seller has zero recalls, the enthusiast cars have zero, the GLI has zero, and the fires are three engine compartments out of 48,165 Jettas while the battery problem affects maybe 23 cars out of 22,524. That reading is mostly fair, and the article should say so. Counterweight: a defect rate of 0.1 percent is only comforting if you can identify the 0.1 percent, and for the ID.4 Volkswagen cannot, because it does not know the cause, has no fix, and is telling 22,524 owners to charge to 80 percent while it figures it out. A recall count of four is good news for Volkswagen. A battery recall with no cause and no fix is not good news for anyone who owns the car.

What this story does not prove matters too. It counts campaigns filed January 1 through November 12, 2026, so a campaign filed tomorrow would not appear. Four of nine models could not be queried through the API at all, so their zero rests on CarBuzz's October 16 table and the Tiguan trackers, plus control sweeps showing the same spellings return real campaigns for 2025 models. The 241,141 sales figure is US sales from January through September across all model years, not 2026-model-year production, so it is a denominator for scale, not a precise population, and one secondary source reports a different first-quarter figure that would move the total by about 10,000. Campaign populations are the manufacturers' own numbers in their Part 573 filings. Complaint counts are unverified owner reports, not findings of defect. Australia recalled 2025 and 2026 Golfs and Tiguans for a power-steering fault in September; there is no US equivalent, and it is not counted.

What to do with this: own a 2025 or 2026 Jetta? You are in 26V138, and the fix is an inspection and repair, free, but the defect is a fire, so check your VIN. Own a 2025 or 2026 Taos? You are in 26V258, every car has the defect, and the fix is an 80-millimeter harness extension, free. Own a 2024 through 2026 Atlas or Atlas Cross Sport? You are in 26V473 for the camera, and you are also in 25V835 for the wheel bolts, which carries a do-not-drive warning and is the urgent one. Own a 2023 through 2026 ID.4? Charge to 80 percent until Volkswagen develops a repair, and there is no date for one; interim letters go out November 27. Own a 2026 Tiguan, Golf GTI, Golf R, or Jetta GLI? As of today there is no safety recall on your car, but check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls anyway, and if your Tiguan brakes on its own, report it, because that is how complaints become investigations and investigations become recalls.[1][4][5][6][7]
