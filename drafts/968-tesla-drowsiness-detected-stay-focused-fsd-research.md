# #968 Research — "Drowsiness Detected. Stay Focused With FSD."

**News pegs (all September 2026):**
- Sept 9, 2026: Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) wrote to DOT Secretary Sean P. Duffy demanding answers by **Sept 30, 2026** about Tesla drivers filmed apparently asleep with Autopilot/FSD engaged.
- The letter cites an NBC News investigation (David Ingram, Aug 25, 2026): **43 videos** of drivers apparently asleep in moving Teslas, **17 posted this year alone**.
- The letter ties the pattern to **EA26002**, the engineering analysis NHTSA opened March 18, 2026, covering an estimated **3,203,754** Tesla vehicles equipped with FSD (concern: FSD's degradation-detection system and whether it gives adequate warning and response time in reduced visibility; 9 incidents, incl. 1 fatality and 1 injury).
- Meanwhile **AQ26002** (opened Sept 3, escalated Sept 16 via Special Order) probes the opposite question: how Tesla certified the steering-wheel-free Cybercab at all.

**The novel angle — the safety system prescribes the hazard:**
- Beginning with software update 2025.32.3, Tesla vehicles reportedly display: **"Drowsiness detected. Stay focused with FSD"** and "Lane drift detected. Let FSD assist so you can stay focused."
- The letter's core distinction: advising an alert driver to use a driver-assistance feature is one thing; encouraging a driver *the vehicle has detected as drowsy* to "stay focused with FSD" is a fundamentally different safety question, because FSD requires continuous human supervision.
- Wired (Aarian Marshall, Sept 27, 2025): "Tesla Is Urging Drowsy Drivers to Use 'Full Self-Driving.' That Could Go Very Wrong."
- Paradox: the impairment detector's recommended corrective action is *more* automation, not pulling over.

**Second paradox — May vs September:**
- May 7, 2026: NHTSA issued a press release announcing the Tesla Model Y as the **first vehicle to pass its new "Advanced Driver Assistance System" tests**.
- Four months later, a member of Congress is asking NHTSA whether the same company's driver-monitoring and drowsiness-detection systems constitute a defect requiring corrective action, including a recall.

**The five questions (letter, page 3):** whether NHTSA is aware of the videos and has assessed systemic driver-monitoring problems; whether it has communicated with Tesla about them; what data it has collected since Jan 1, 2024 via the Standing General Order; its assessment of the cabin-camera system's effectiveness (obstruction, sunglasses, hats); what findings would trigger a defect determination and recall.

**Limitations (must state in article):**
- 43 videos are anecdotal evidence of existence, not prevalence; NBC counted them, not NHTSA. The letter itself asks NHTSA how many such incidents it has identified since Jan 1, 2024 because the answer isn't public.
- EA26002's 9 incidents / 1 fatality are about FSD degradation-detection in low visibility, NOT driver-misuse cases — don't conflate the two dockets' incident counts.
- The "Drowsiness detected" messages were reported from a software update in 2025; Tesla has not publicly confirmed the exact trigger logic.
- The Sept 30 deadline is for a response to questions, not a recall decision.
- No FARS cross-tab for this angle; the analysis is regulatory-timeline arithmetic, not a novel data finding. State that.

**Strongest counterargument (state at full strength):**
- Tesla's position would be that "stay focused with FSD" is encouragement to keep supervising, not permission to sleep; that drowsiness detection plus supervised FSD is strictly safer than a drowsy driver with no assistance at all; and that 43 self-selected viral videos across a fleet of ~3.2M vehicles prove nothing about systemic failure. The cabin camera exists; the misuse is the driver's, not the system's. Say so.

**Actionable:** If you drive a Tesla with FSD: treat drowsiness alerts as pull-over signals, not permission slips. Check complaints/recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls; report misuse or alert-design concerns via NHTSA's complaint portal — that's the data EA26002 runs on.
