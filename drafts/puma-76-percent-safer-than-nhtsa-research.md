# Research: Pumas Reduce Deer-Vehicle Collisions by 76%

## Angle
A peer-reviewed study in Current Biology (Aug 5, 2026) found that pumas reduce deer-vehicle collisions by 76% in Washington's Olympic Peninsula simply by existing. That's a larger safety effect than most federal road safety interventions. The cosmic absurdity: we spend billions on crash avoidance tech while states debate whether to let people shoot the most effective deer-collision intervention in nature.

## Primary Sources (3+)

### 1. Current Biology study (Aug 5, 2026)
- Lead author: Justin Suraci, Conservation Science Partners, Truckee, CA
- Location: Olympic Peninsula, Washington
- Key findings:
  - Puma presence → 76% reduction in deer-vehicle collisions
  - Deer shift activity toward daylight hours where pumas are present
  - Deer 15% less likely to be near roads where pumas are present
  - 67% reduction in likelihood of deer-vehicle collision from behavioral changes
  - Mechanism: predation fear makes deer avoid roads and shift to daytime activity (when they're more visible to drivers)
- Mark Elbroch, Panthera: "Living alongside large carnivores isn't simply about protecting wildlife; it's about protecting the many ways they quietly benefit us every day."

### 2. FARS / IIHS / NHTSA deer-vehicle collision data
- ~2.1 million deer-vehicle collisions annually in the US
- ~200 human fatalities per year (Pew/IIHS estimate), up to ~440/yr (Conover/USGS)
- $10+ billion in annual damage
- 1.7 million insurance claims in 2024-2025 (State Farm/IIHS)
- Average claim: $5,620 (2024, high-risk regions)
- 1 in 139 odds of hitting an animal per year
- November = peak month
- Collisions 14x more frequent in 2 hours after sunset vs before

### 3. Pew Charitable Trusts / University of Washington
- 1-2 million crashes between vehicles and large animals annually
- 26,000 injuries per year
- Daylight saving time change: 16% collision spike in the week after fall clock change
- Making DST permanent would prevent 33 human deaths and 36,550 deer deaths annually
- Wildlife crossing structures: 85-95% effective but cost $150K-$600K each

## Novel Original Analysis
Comparison of deer-vehicle collision reduction effectiveness:
| Intervention | DVC Reduction | Cost | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deer fencing | ~87% | ~$10K/mile | Localized |
| Wildlife crossings | 85-95% | $150K-$600K each | Localized |
| **Pumas (existing)** | **76%** | **$0** | **Area-wide** |
| Speed reduction | ~40% (fatal DVCs) | Regulatory | Road-specific |
| Permanent DST | ~16% reduction in spike week | Legislative | National |
| Deer whistles | Negligible | ~$10/pair | Per-vehicle |
| AEB (current gen) | Limited deer detection | $1K+ (vehicle cost) | Per-vehicle |

**Original contribution:** Pumas are the third most effective deer-vehicle collision intervention ever measured, behind only physical fencing and wildlife crossings, but they cost nothing, require no construction, and work across entire landscapes rather than at specific points. Thirty states still allow or encourage puma hunting.

## Limitations
- Study covers one region (Olympic Peninsula, WA); effects may vary in other ecosystems
- FARS doesn't break out deer-vehicle crashes as a separate category in its standard model-level data
- The 76% figure measures collision frequency reduction, not fatality reduction specifically
- Puma populations can't be "deployed" — this is about conservation policy, not engineering
- Human-puma conflicts (livestock predation, rare attacks) are real tradeoffs not addressed here

## Strongest Counterargument
Mountain lions occasionally kill people. Between 1890 and 2020, there were 126 confirmed puma attacks on humans in the US and Canada, 27 of them fatal. That's ~0.21 fatal attacks per year vs. ~200-440 human deaths from deer-vehicle collisions per year. The math: for every person a puma kills, deer-vehicle collisions (which pumas suppress) kill roughly 1,000-2,000 people.
