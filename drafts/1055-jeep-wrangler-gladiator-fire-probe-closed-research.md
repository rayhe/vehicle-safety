## Key facts
- **The closure:** NHTSA closed its preliminary evaluation on September 29, 2026. The recall "doesn't disappear just because the federal probe is over" — NHTSA simply confirmed Stellantis's existing recall covers the vehicles and defect it was examining. The agency will keep monitoring field performance and can act again if new issues surface.
- **The park-outside order stands:** unrepaired vehicles should be parked outside and away from structures or other vehicles. The remedy became available in July 2026: inspect the connector, repair or replace parts as needed, free of charge.
- **The probe:** NHTSA opened the preliminary evaluation in September 2024 after reports of engine-compartment fires in parked Wranglers and Gladiators with the ignition off. Original scope: ~781,500 vehicles (2021-2023). Several fires reportedly started near the power steering pump on the passenger side of the engine bay.
- **The expansion:** by June 2026 Stellantis expanded the recall to all 2021-2025 Wranglers and Gladiators built June 24, 2020 – December 2, 2024: nearly 1.08M vehicles. That's a 38% expansion over the probe's original population (1.08M / 781,500 = 1.382).
- **The defect:** the electric-hydraulic power steering pump (electric motor driving hydraulic assist instead of a belt). The pump's electrical connector could be manufactured outside spec, making it harder to fully seat. A loose connection raises electrical resistance; at high current that generates heat — enough to melt the connector and ignite nearby material. A car that can catch fire while turned off, in your driveway, because of a connector that wasn't fully clicked in.
- **Stellantis's own closure:** before NHTSA ever opened its probe, Stellantis investigated (2023-early 2024), closed its investigation citing a "low fire rate," then reopened it in August 2024 after incidents increased. Over a year of forensic work — vehicle buybacks, CT scans, bench testing — before confirming the connector issue in April 2026.
- **The numbers:** 72 fires, 1 injury (Reuters, June 2026). 72 fires in ~1.3M vehicles worldwide = 0.006% incidence.
- **FARS context:** the site's FARS dataset shows the Jeep Wrangler at 1,842 deaths (2014-2023), ~184/year, death rate 0.84 per 100M VMT. Fire is terrifying but vanishingly rare next to the routine crash toll.

## Novel original contributions
1. **The closure that isn't an acquittal.** NHTSA closed the probe for administrative coverage reasons — the recall already covers the population — while simultaneously keeping the park-outside order in force and retaining the right to act again. Two entities closed investigations into these fires (Stellantis in 2024, NHTSA in 2026) and the trucks are still not supposed to be parked in your garage.
2. **The 38% expansion.** The recall population outgrew the investigation's original population by 38%, because the connector went into two extra model years of trucks. The probe drew a box; the defect was bigger than the box.
3. **Ignition-off as the unsettling detail.** A vehicle fire risk that doesn't require driving, speed, or a crash — the truck can be cold in your driveway. This is the fact the park-outside order exists for, and it's what separates this from every crash-data story on the site.

## Actionable insights
- Own a 2021-2025 Wrangler or Gladiator? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or Stellantis's Mopar recall system — matching the model year alone doesn't confirm your truck is in the recall window (build dates June 24, 2020 – December 2, 2024).
- Until the repair is done: park outside, away from structures and other vehicles. The remedy is free (connector inspection, harness or pump replacement) and has been available since July 2026.
- If you bought used: this applies to trucks now on their second or third owners.

## Strongest counterargument
72 fires out of 1.3 million vehicles is a 0.006% incidence rate — Stellantis initially argued (and initially convinced itself) the fire rate didn't justify a recall. The closure of NHTSA's probe can be read as validation: the system worked, the recall covers the defect, resources move elsewhere. The 1+ year forensic slog (CT scans, buybacks) is arguably diligence, not delay.

## Limitations
- The 72-fire / 1-injury figures are from Reuters' June 2026 filing coverage; the current count is likely higher but not published.
- Remedy completion rates for the recall are not publicly available — no way to say how many of the 1.08M have been fixed.
- FARS Wrangler data (2014-2023) covers crash deaths, not fire deaths; the comparison is contextual, not causal.
- Breakdown of ignition-off vs. driving fires is not in the published material.
