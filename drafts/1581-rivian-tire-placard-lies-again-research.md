# Research: #1581 — Rivian's Tire Sticker Is Wrong Again. The Fix Is Another Sticker.

**Slug:** 1581-rivian-tire-placard-lies-again
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — car-buying guidance and "what this means for you" are her beat; a wrong door-jamb sticker that owners must verify themselves is pure Clara territory)
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Date researched:** 2026-11-25
**Target ship_date:** 2028-11-10 (1 day after #1580's 2028-11-09)

## Story in one line
Rivian recalled 1,619 R1S, R1T, and R2 vehicles because the tire placard on the door jamb may list the wrong wheel, tire, and spare configuration — the second time in 29 months Rivian has failed the same regulation, FMVSS 110, by printing wrong tire information on the sticker, and owners cannot even check their VIN until January 18, 2027.

## Kill test
- **Genuinely newsworthy?** YES. Filed with NHTSA November 19, 2026; Reuters reported it November 25, 2026 — today. A $45,000-$100,000+ EV whose door-jamb sticker lies about its own tires, including the brand-new mass-market R2.
- **Novel angle on data?** YES. Full queue/drafts/status grep: zero prior coverage of 26V761, FSAM-1900, or any Rivian placard recall. The nearest neighbors are different defects: #1047/#1082/#1087/#1099/#1106/#1159 (R2 loose HV battery fasteners and loose bolts), #1022/#1036 (mentions in passing). Nobody on this site has covered a tire-placard recall, and nobody has connected the two Rivian FMVSS 110 failures into a repeat-offender pattern.
- **3+ primary sources?** YES. (1) NHTSA Part 573 filing for 26V761, quoted verbatim via WhichTrim; (2) Reuters, Nov 25, 2026; (3) NHTSA campaign 24V458 (June 2024) for the first placard failure; (4) NHTSA DOT HS 811 617 (2012) for tire-failure crash numbers; (5) GAO-07-246R for underinflation prevalence and the TPMS 25% trigger.

## Primary sources
1. **NHTSA Part 573 filing, campaign 26V761000 (Rivian FSAM-1900), filed Nov 19, 2026** — quoted verbatim at https://whichtrim.com/recalls/rivian-fsam-1900/ and https://whichtrim.com/recalls/26v761000/ — "The tire placard and/or certification label may not correctly list the wheel, tire, and spare tire configuration of the vehicle... The tires may be improperly inflated and/or the wrong size replacement tire may be installed after referencing the incorrect information, increasing the risk of a crash." Remedy: replace placard/labels free. Owner letters expected Jan 18, 2027. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov Jan 18, 2027. Rivian customer service 1-888-748-4261. Build window Oct 18, 2021–Aug 25, 2026.
2. **Reuters, "Rivian recalls over 1,600 vehicles over tire issue, NHTSA says," Nov 25, 2026** — https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026:newsml_L6N4760FM:0-rivian-recalls-over-1-600-vehicles-over-tire-issue-nhtsa-says/ — 1,619 vehicles, "tires may be improperly inflated or the wrong-size replacement tire may be installed after referencing incorrect information."
3. **NHTSA campaign 24V458000 (Rivian FSAM-1491), June 20, 2024** — https://auto-recalls.justia.com/rivian/r1t/2023/24v458000/index.html — 666 vehicles, 2022–2023 R1S/R1T: "The maximum vehicle capacity weight value on the tire placard label is incorrect... A vehicle with an incorrect maximum capacity weight value may be overloaded, which can increase the risk of a crash." Remedy was a mailed corrected overlay label; owner letters mailed Aug 8, 2024.
4. **NHTSA, "Tire-Related Factors in the Pre-Crash Phase," DOT HS 811 617, April 2012** — https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/811617 — estimated 414 fatalities, 10,275 nonfatal injuries, and 78,392 crashes annually due to flat tires or blowouts before TPMS; tires underinflated by more than 25% are about 3x more likely to be a critical pre-crash factor.
5. **GAO, "Underinflated Tires in the United States," GAO-07-246R, Feb 9, 2007** — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-07-246R/pdf/GAOREPORTS-GAO-07-246R.pdf — more than a quarter of cars and about a third of light trucks have a tire underinflated 8+ psi below recommended; TPMS warns only at 25% below the placard pressure; in 1999 underinflated tires contributed to 247 of 32,061 fatalities.

## Key verified facts (from the 26V761 filing)
- **Population:** 1,619 vehicles. 2022–2027 Rivian R1S, R1T, and 2027 R2. Built Oct 18, 2021–Aug 25, 2026.
- **Defect:** tire placard and/or certification label may not correctly list the wheel, tire, and spare tire configuration. Fails FMVSS 110, "Tire Selection and Rims."
- **Risk (filing's words):** "The tires may be improperly inflated and/or the wrong size replacement tire may be installed after referencing the incorrect information, increasing the risk of a crash."
- **Remedy:** Rivian replaces the tire placard and/or certification labels, free of charge. Owner notification letters expected January 18, 2027.
- **The blind window:** VINs involved will not be searchable on NHTSA.gov until January 18, 2027 — two months after filing. Owners cannot check today whether their truck is affected.
- **Rivian's number:** FSAM-1900. NHTSA's number: 26V761000.

## Original contribution
1. **Repeat-offender arithmetic:** Rivian has now failed FMVSS 110 placard accuracy twice in 29 months. June 2024: 666 vehicles, wrong maximum capacity weight (24V458). November 2026: 1,619 vehicles, wrong wheel/tire/spare configuration (26V761). The 2024 fix was a corrected sticker mailed to owners. The 2026 defect is a wrong sticker again, on more models, spanning five model years, and now including the R2 — Rivian's first mass-market vehicle, which is in a recall for wrong tire information before volume deliveries have even scaled.
2. **The TPMS blind spot:** the tire-pressure warning light is designed to fire at 25% below the placard pressure. The placard is the baseline the warning is anchored to. When the placard number is wrong, the warning itself is calibrated to a wrong number — the safety system trusts the sticker, and the sticker is the thing that is broken. A driver doing everything right (checking the door jamb, trusting the dash light) is still wrong.
3. **The two-month verification gap:** the filing went in November 19, but NHTSA's VIN lookup does not go live until January 18, 2027. For two months the only way an owner can verify the placard is to compare it by hand against the tire sidewall and the spare — a check almost nobody does, on a sticker almost nobody reads. The recall's own remedy (a new sticker) cannot even be requested until the letters go out.
4. **The spare-tire kicker:** the filing covers the spare tire configuration too. A wrong spare listing means a roadside swap can put the wrong size tire on the truck — the one moment an owner is guaranteed to trust the placard is the moment it matters most.

## Limitations
- NHTSA's filing states no crash, injury, or complaint counts for 26V761; do not imply any occurred. The risk is prospective, stated in the filing's own conditional language ("may be improperly inflated").
- The exact number of R2s in the 1,619 is not broken out in the public filing summary; do not invent a split.
- Tire-fatality figures (414/year, DOT HS 811 617) predate TPMS and describe flat tires/blowouts generally, not placard errors specifically; use them only to establish why inflation pressure matters, with that caveat stated.
- The TPMS 25% trigger is from GAO-07-246R (2007) and FMVSS 138; the article must present it as how the warning is designed, not as a claim that any specific Rivian's TPMS is miscalibrated.
- Rivian's 2024 placard recall (24V458) covered a different field on the label (capacity weight vs. wheel/tire/spare configuration); the repeat is the regulation and the failure mode (wrong printed tire information), not the identical error.

## Strongest counterargument
1,619 vehicles is a rounding error against the ~200,000 Rivians on the road, the defect is a misprinted label rather than a mechanical failure, no crashes or injuries are reported, and the fix is a free sticker. Rivian found the problem itself and reported it. The honest reply: the count is small, but the pattern is the story — the same company misprinted the same safety label twice, the label is the sole source of truth for inflation pressure and replacement tire size, and the two-month VIN blackout means affected owners are driving on unverifiable information through winter. Small recalls of cheap defects are exactly how systemic process failures announce themselves.
