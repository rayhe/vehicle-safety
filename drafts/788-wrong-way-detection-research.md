## Angle

## Core Data (All Primary Sources)

### Scale of the Problem
- **500 deaths/year** from wrong-way crashes on divided highways (AAA Foundation, 2015-2018 avg)
- Up **34%** from 375 deaths/year (2010-2014), AAA Foundation Mar 2021
- **3.7%** of all fatal crashes on divided highways (AAA Foundation, 2010-2018)
- **40x more likely to be deadly** than other crash types (ODOT, 0.3% of crashes but disproportionate fatalities)
- **12x fatality rate** vs other crash types (Caltrans 1989 study, cited by NTSB 2012)
- **22% fatality rate** vs **0.3%** for all highway accidents (Michigan study, 2012)
- 80%+ are **head-on collisions** at high closure speeds (NTSB 2012)
- **60% involve alcohol-impaired driver** (AAA Foundation, NTSB 2012)
- **15% of wrong-way drivers are 65+** vs 10% of all freeway fatal drivers (overrepresented, NTSB 2012)
- **52.8% of deaths = the wrong-way driver**, **41.1% = innocent other-vehicle occupants** (AAA, 2010-2018)
- Source: NTSB Special Investigation Report, Dec 2012; AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety, Mar 2021

### Connecticut — The Model (Best Data)
- **Trigger:** State Rep. Quentin Williams, 39, killed by wrong-way driver Jan 2023
- **Legislation:** Required CTDOT to install detection on 120 high-risk exit ramps
- **Installed:** 211 systems since August 2023
- **Cost:** **~$120,000 per installation**, $4,500/year maintenance
- **Results (since Aug 2023):**
  - 680 wrong-way drivers detected at equipped ramps
  - **82.5% self-corrected** when they saw flashing signs
  - 17.5% continued on → alerts sent to Highway Operations Center + State Police with **live video**
- **Fatality trend:**
  - 2022: 13 wrong-way crashes → 23 fatalities
  - Last year (after systems): 6 wrong-way crashes → 13 fatalities
  - This year so far: 1 fatal crash → 1 fatality
- Source: GovTech, Nov 2024 (John Tarr presentation)

### Ohio — Camera System
- 30 cameras total, **$3.4M** → ~$113K per camera
- Reduced detection from "miles and minutes" to "feet and seconds"
- Camera uses 3-box trigger — car crosses boxes in wrong order → alert
- ODOT Traffic Management Center always staffed → confirms on video → calls law enforcement
- Source: Columbus Dispatch, Jun 1 2026

### Rhode Island — Proven Zero-Crash Record
- $600K for 24 high-risk locations → **$25K per location** (detection only)
- Part of $2M broader investment (signing, striping, 145 locations)
- **47 wrong-way incidents detected → ZERO crashes at equipped locations**
- Source: Traffic Technology Today, 2018

### Kentucky — Large-Scale Pilot
- $9.5M for 6 corridors (Fayette + Jefferson counties)
- Federal grant ($5.14M) + state match
- 4-layer system: detection → deterrent → alert → mainline monitoring
- Source: Parsons, Feb 2024

### Arizona
- $4M for 15 miles of I-17 in Phoenix (2018)
- FHWA award-winning
- Source: AASHTO Journal, 2021

## The Math (For Article)
- ~500 deaths/year
- Connecticut proves ~$120K/interchange works
- US has ~65,000-80,000 highway interchanges (FHWA data)
- Even targeting only highest-risk 10% = 6,500-8,000 interchanges
- Cost: ~$780M - $960M for highest-risk 10%
- IIJA provided $7.5B for highway safety improvement programs (2021 law)
- **Connecticut spent $25.3M (211 × $120K) and went from 23 deaths/year to possibly 1-2**

## Counterargument & Limitations
- **Small sample size on fatality reduction:** Connecticut's fatality decline could be trend noise
- **60% involve alcohol:** Detection doesn't prevent impaired driving, only catches it mid-event
- **Cost scales:** Full national deployment at $120K/interchange × 80K = $9.6B (expensive)
- **But:** Targeted deployment at highest-risk ramps (near bars, high-incident) is cost-effective
- **Spike strips dismissed:** ODOT says directional spike strips only work in low-speed parking garages; debris maintenance on highway ramps makes them impractical
