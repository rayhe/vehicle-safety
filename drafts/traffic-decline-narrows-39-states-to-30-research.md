## Story Angle

NHTSA early estimates show national traffic deaths falling for 15 consecutive quarters, from 39,254 in 2024 to 36,640 in 2025 (down 6.7%, rate 1.10 second-lowest ever), to 17,140 in H1 2025 (down 8.2%, rate 1.06 lowest mid-year since 2014), to 7,770 in Q1 2026 (down 4.3%, rate 0.99 second-lowest Q1 ever). Good headline numbers.

The breadth is shrinking: 39 states + DC + PR saw declines in full-year 2025, 38 + DC + PR in H1 2025, but only 30 + PR in Q1 2026. That's 75% of jurisdictions down to 58% down, while national deaths still fall. Improvement is concentrating in fewer, larger states while more states go the wrong way.

This is the opposite of a broad-based safety recovery. It's a narrow one.

Original contribution: Calculate breadth narrowing (39/52=75% to 38/52=73% to 30/52=58%), show VMT rising each period (+29.8B in 2025, +12.1B in H1 2025, +11B in Q1 2026) meaning more driving but fewer deaths only in certain geographies, and link to FARS model-year/vehicle-age data showing newer vehicles (<10yr) down 12% vs older down 5%, suggesting fleet turnover explains concentration.

## Limitations

- Early estimates, not Final File - will be revised early 2027 when FARS ARF 2025 available. Counts slightly revised per NHTSA note.
- State-level breakdown not provided in press releases - only counts of states up/down, not which states. Cannot identify which 11 states up in 2025 or which 20+ up in Q1 2026 without full state tables (not in these releases). Must state this explicitly.
- VMT preliminary from FHWA - subject to revision.
- Breadth metric (number of jurisdictions down) does not weight by population or VMT - 1 small state = 1 large state in count, so narrowing may overstate concentration if small states fluctuating.
- Correlation of newer vehicle improvement (12% vs 5%) to geographic concentration is inferential, not proven - need registration data by state not provided here.
- FARS 2014-2023 data used for context only, not directly comparable to 2025-2026 early estimates (different methodology).

## Strongest Counterargument

Best case against thesis: Number of states down is noisy small-sample metric. 39 vs 30 could be random fluctuation in small states with 20-30 deaths per quarter, where 2-3 additional crashes flips a state from down to up. National decline driven by large states (CA, TX, FL account for ~30% of deaths) is expected and not evidence of narrow recovery - large states improving always drives national numbers. Also early estimates have ±5-10% uncertainty at state level, so 39 vs 30 may not be statistically significant. Finally, Q1 is winter months with different exposure patterns than full-year, so Q1 2026 30 states down vs full-year 2025 39 down may not be comparable seasonality.

We will state this explicitly and note that only full-year 2026 final data will confirm if narrowing persists.

## Actionable Insights

- If you live in CA, TX, FL, NY, NC, etc.: Your state likely drove national improvement - check your state highway safety office report for local trends, but don't assume local roads are safer - national decline doesn't guarantee your county.
- If you live in small/rural state: Your state may be one of the 11 up in 2025 or 20+ up in Q1 2026 - check your state DOT fatality report (search "[your state] traffic fatalities 2025 NHTSA").
- For all drivers: Newer vehicles (<10yr) down 12% vs older down 5% - if you drive a 2015 or older vehicle, you are in the cohort improving half as fast. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and consider AEB, ESC, side airbags (IIHS 49% reduction for ESC).
- For policymakers: Breadth narrowing suggests enforcement/education investments may be working in some states but not others - $665M NHTSA grants available (eGrants system) for distracted, unbelted, impaired, speeding programs.
- For cyclists/pedestrians: Pedestrians down 8% but pedalcyclists up 4% (from subcategory report) - bike infrastructure still lagging.
