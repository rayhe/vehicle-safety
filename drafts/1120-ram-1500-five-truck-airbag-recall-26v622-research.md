# Research Notes — #1120: The Five-Truck Recall

**Slug:** 1120-ram-1500-five-truck-airbag-recall-26v622
**Journalist:** Axle McScatter (By The Numbers)
**Topic:** Stellantis recalls exactly FIVE 2026 Ram 1500 pickups (NHTSA 26V622 / Chrysler 94D) for a side-airbag wiring connector that may not be secured.

## Verified facts
1. **Scope:** Five (5) 2026 Ram 1500 crew-cab pickups built between Feb 28 and May 18, 2026 — i.e., the defective build window was narrowed to ~2.5 months, and the suspect population to 5 VINs. (karmactive, Oct 9 2026)
2. **Campaign IDs:** NHTSA 26V622; FCA US internal number 94D.
3. **Defect:** Electrical connector between the seat wiring harness and the driver/front-passenger seat airbag (or between that harness and the body wiring) may not be secured properly. Consequence: the side airbags may not deploy as intended in a crash.
4. **Compliance:** Affected vehicles fail FMVSS 208 (occupant crash protection) and FMVSS 214 (side impact protection), per FCA US.
5. **Lookup:** VINs became searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls on Oct 6, 2026. Owners call 800-853-1403, ref 94D. Remedy: dealer inspection, free.

## Contrast / context
- In 2015, Ram recalled **1,060,531** pickups (2012–14) because a steering-wheel wiring harness could short and unexpectedly deploy the driver airbag; 2 injuries. (MotorTrend, Jul 2015)
- Ratio: the 2015 airbag-wiring recall was **212,000x larger** than 26V622. The seat-wiring defect of 2015's mirror image.
- Dec 2025: Ram recalled 1,879 pickups for Joyson side-curtain inflators that could rupture and fire metal fragments at occupants — no injuries reported. (Autoblog)
- FARS 2014–2023: Ram 1500 (legacy fleet) recorded 714 deaths at a rate of 0.13 per 100M VMT — below the full-size pickup segment; side-impact protection (FMVSS 214) is one of the reasons.
- FMVSS 214 phase-in (2007–2010 NHTSA rule) made side-impact protection mandatory; a new 2026 vehicle failing it, even in 5 units, is a QC catch worth noting.

## Novel contribution (original calculation)
- "Recall concentration" framing: 26V622 covers 0.00002% of the ~5.2M 2026-model-year vehicles typically recalled annually. And the 212,000:1 ratio vs. Ram's own 2015 airbag-wiring campaign — same brand, same subsystem family, eleven years apart.
- The five-truck precision implies build-level traceability (they know exactly which seats got the suspect connector); the story's hook is that modern QC can isolate a defect to five trucks out of hundreds of thousands.

## Kill test
Passes: genuinely fresh (filed Oct 2026, news broke Oct 9), paradoxical extreme (safety campaign of five vehicles), novel angle (precision recall / the 212,000:1 ratio), actionable (VIN check now).

## Counterargument (strongest)
Five trucks is a rounding error — no injuries, no crashes, virtually no reader affected. A cynical read: this is routine QC paperwork inflated into news. Also, perverse possibility: tiny recalls sometimes signal a supplier quietly contained a bigger problem; we cannot see behind the 5-VIN curtain.

## Limitations
- Root cause not publicly disclosed (which connector, which shift, which supplier) — karma article retracted unverified draft claims; do not repeat them.
- No injuries/warranty-claim data disclosed for 26V622.
- FARS figures describe legacy-fleet Ram 1500; not causal evidence about this defect.
- Do NOT claim "smallest recall ever" — cannot verify against all NHTSA campaigns.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2026 Ram 1500? Type your VIN into nhtsa.gov/recalls now (searchable since Oct 6, 2026). Match → call 800-853-1403, ref 94D, free fix.
- Lesson for used-buyer: a tiny recall count is GOOD news — it means the automaker traced the defect precisely rather than papering the whole model year.
