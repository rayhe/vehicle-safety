## 1. General Editor — 8.5/10

Structure holds: asterisk lede, pull stat, belt engineering, NHTSA tension, FARS decade, leaderboard trap, counterargument, limitations, action. The "same model year, same sticker, different back seat" frame carries the piece. Deduction: 981 words is roughly double the site's 300–500 word format, and the two FARS paragraphs back to back are the densest stretch on the page. A reader gets the argument; a reader in a hurry gets it twice. Not a fail, but the piece would be tighter at 700 words.

## 2. Voice Coach — 9.0/10

Mia is unmistakable: the "first 150 milliseconds" opener is her catchphrase used correctly, the belt-and-load-limiter engineering detail is her beat, and the judgment lands on design ("cheap, fast, proven... and invisible to the buyer"). Rhythm check passes on all three metrics. No banned phrases, no AI tells in the sentence openings (0% "The" starters). Minor nit: the NHTSA paragraph leans on long clause-heavy sentences; one short punch there would sharpen the split-verdict beat.

## 3. Ethics Reviewer — 9.0/10

Fair and non-exploitative. No victim-blaming, no dead-child imagery used as a hook (the rear dummy is described by size, not by tragedy). The counterargument is stated at full strength and conceded where it is right. The article refuses to turn 555 deaths into a verdict on the 2026 car, which is the ethical line this piece had to hold, and it holds it explicitly.

## 4. Social / Shareability — 8.5/10

Two quotable lines: "Same model year, same price, different back seat" and "A trophy is a test result with better branding." The door-jamb check is a concrete, shareable action. Headline is long but it earns its length with the tension. Deduction: the pull stat (0.39) is abstract for a casual scroller; a pull stat built on the build-date cutoff ("November 2024") would travel further on social. The headline's second clause does that work instead, so the loss is partial.

## 5. Legal Accuracy — 9.0/10

Every cited claim traces to a real source with a working URL: Hyundai's Dec 23, 2025 press release (TSP+ for builds after Nov 2024), Zigwheels Nov 2, 2026 (2026 TSP+ confirmation), InsuranceRateGuard (rear-belt revision date), KBB Jan 9, 2026 (NHTSA four-star note, attributed, hedged in limitations), IIHS ratings page, NHTSA FARS. No invented citations, no case law to get wrong, no settlement figures. The one second-hand claim (NHTSA four-star) is flagged as KBB-reported with a reader check advised.

## 6. Research Rigor — 9.0/10

Original contribution is real and threefold: (a) first lab-vs-road cross-tab for the Santa Fe, a nameplate with zero prior dedicated articles in 1,424 entries; (b) the build-date cutoff quantified as a buyer action; (c) the age confound demonstrated with numbers (Palisade 0.06 vs. Santa Fe 0.39 as a birthday gap, not a safety gap). Limitations are a dedicated paragraph, not a hedge: no FARS data exists for the TSP+ car, model-year counts mix exposure with safety, VMT-estimate uncertainty stated. Counterargument conceded at full strength. Methodology is transparent on rate construction and fleet sizes.

## 7. Data Presentation — 8.5/10

Pull stat is clean and contextualized. Peer rates (Highlander 0.42, Grand Cherokee 0.51, Explorer 1.54, Sorento 0.29, Palisade 0.06, Telluride 0.04) are all present and correct against FARS_BY_MODEL, and the model-year curve (2017 peak 51, single digits by 2021) is reported with its exposure caveat. Deduction: the comparison lives inline in prose rather than in a table, and a data-forward site earns the table. The research file has the table; the article should too. Minor, not blocking.
