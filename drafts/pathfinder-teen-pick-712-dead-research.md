# Research: Pathfinder Named "Best Vehicle for Teens" — FARS Says 712 Dead

## Angle
The 2026 Nissan Pathfinder just earned IIHS Top Safety Pick+ and was named "Best New Vehicle for Teens" by IIHS/Consumer Reports. But FARS data shows the Pathfinder nameplate has 712 deaths at a rate of 0.93 per 100M VMT — 3.2× the Honda Pilot's rate (0.29) and 2.2× the Toyota Highlander's rate (0.42). Same segment. Same school parking lot.

## FARS Data (2014-2023)
- **Nissan Pathfinder:** 712 deaths, 1,428 crashes, rate 0.93, fleet 612,500, impairment 19.1%
- **Honda Pilot:** 514 deaths, rate 0.29, impairment 19.4%
- **Toyota Highlander:** 1,106 deaths, rate 0.42, impairment 16.4%
- **Ford Explorer:** 3,797 deaths, rate 1.54, impairment 19.5%
- **Dodge Durango:** 356 deaths, rate 0.54, impairment 22.5%

## Generational Breakdown (model year deaths in 2014-2023 crashes)
- Gen2 (R50, 1996-2004, body-on-frame): 281 deaths
- Gen3 (R51, 2005-2012, body-on-frame): 186 deaths
- Gen4 (R52, 2013-2020, unibody + CVT): 146 deaths

## Key CVT History
- 2013-2021 models used Xtronic CVT — notorious for judder, loss of acceleration, stalling
- 870+ NHTSA complaints on 2013 model alone
- Class-action lawsuits filed (2019-2021 Pathfinder and Infiniti QX60)
- 2005-2010 models had coolant-into-transmission-fluid leak defect (NHTSA denied investigation probe)
- 2013-2016 models recalled for occupant classification system failure (3.3M vehicles across Nissan)
- 2025 model recalled for brake caliper that could break (1,430 vehicles)

## 2026 Model — Genuinely Different?
- Switched from CVT to 9-speed automatic
- Earned IIHS Top Safety Pick+ (2026)
- Named "Best New Vehicle for Teens" by IIHS/CR
- 5-star NHTSA overall rating
- Base price $39,900
- Still uses 3.5L V6

## Novel Cross-Tabulation
Comparing the **current IIHS "Best for Teens" recommendation** against the **historical FARS nameplate death rate** reveals a paradox: the vehicle being recommended as safest for teens has a 3.2× higher death rate than the Pilot — a vehicle in the same segment, same price range, same school parking lot.

## Strongest Counterargument
The 2026 Pathfinder is a fundamentally different vehicle than the 2013-2021 models driving the FARS body count. The CVT is gone. The platform is new. The IIHS award evaluates the CURRENT vehicle, not the nameplate's history. Judging the 2026 by its predecessors' death toll is like blaming a current CEO for a previous CEO's crimes.

## Limitations
- FARS data covers 2014-2023; the 2022+ R53 generation has minimal exposure time in the data
- Fleet estimates have ±15% uncertainty for mid-volume models
- Impairment rates are similar across competitors (~19%), so driver behavior isn't the differentiator
- The 0.93 rate includes all model years on the road, not just current-gen
