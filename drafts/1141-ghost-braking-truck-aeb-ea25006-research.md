# Research: Ghost Braking — EA25006 (truck AEB false activation)

## The story in one line

Congress ordered NHTSA to mandate automatic emergency braking on heavy trucks. Five years later, the rule is in limbo while NHTSA's own defect investigators hold an open Engineering Analysis into trucks that slam on their brakes for nothing — 24 crashes, 13 injuries, one fatality incident in the file.

## Kill test: PASSED

- Newsworthy: yes. A congressionally mandated safety rule (Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, 2021) is effectively stalled; the same agency's Office of Defects Investigation is simultaneously investigating the mandated technology for phantom braking.
- Novel angle: everyone covered the mandate when proposed (2023) and the delay. Nobody on this site has covered the EA25006 escalation (Oct 23, 2025) as a lens on the mandate's fate, or paired it with IIHS's Oct 8, 2026 commercial-vehicle ratings showing work trucks are poor at avoiding *real* crashes. The symmetry: trucks brake for steel plates, but can't avoid a motorcycle.

## Verified facts (all traceable)

1. **EA25006** — NHTSA ODI Engineering Analysis, "Automatic Emergency Braking Errors," opened 10/23/2025, prompted by PE23010. Investigator: Andrew Brady. Manufacturer: Daimler Trucks North America. Subject vehicles: 2017–2022 Freightliner Cascadia and 2017–2022 Western Star 5700. Estimated population: 250,000. Problem description: "The automatic emergency braking system on the subject vehicles may inaccurately identify an object and command the vehicle to stop unexpectedly, resulting in a hazard to other motorists."
   - Source: NHTSA ODI Resume (static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2025/INOA-EA25006-23182.pdf), mirrored at oemdtc.com.
2. **347 reports reviewed; 24 crashes; 13 injury incidents (8 injuries); 1 fatality incident (0 fatalities).** Detroit Assurance 2.0, 4.0, 5.0 and Wabco OnGuard systems; ODI analysis showed Detroit Assurance systems fail at similar rates, OnGuard significantly lower claim rate despite comparable duty.
   - Sources: ODI resume EA25006; The BRAKE Report (Oct 2025).
3. **PE23010 opened May 2023** on 18 Vehicle Owner Questionnaires alleging AEB activation "without an actual roadway obstacle"; braking ranged from momentary partial application to full application bringing the truck to a complete stop in the travel lane.
   - Sources: FreightWaves (May 2023), Carscoops, Overdrive.
4. **Steel trench plate test:** NHTSA's Vehicle Research and Test Center tested an AEB-equipped Cascadia; during the "steel trench plate" scenario the truck initiated hard braking when the plate was detected. DTNA told NHTSA the steel trench plate was not representative of real-world driving.
   - Sources: FreightWaves, Carscoops, Overdrive (all quoting NHTSA documents).
5. **The 2023 mandate NPRM** (NHTSA-2023-0023 / FMCSA-2022-0171, June 2023): proposed AEB + ESC on heavy vehicles (GVWR > 10,000 lb). Projected benefits: ~60,000 rear-end crashes/year with heavy vehicle as striking vehicle; once implemented, 19,118 crashes prevented, 155 lives saved, 8,814 injuries prevented, 24,828 property-damage-only crashes eliminated annually.
   - Source: nhtsa.gov press release + Federal Register docket.
6. **Stalled:** 2026 regulatory agenda expected a joint supplemental NPRM "as soon as this month" (July 2026). FMCSA's March 2026 report lists the project as a supplemental NPRM with next action date undetermined. It has not published as of Oct 12, 2026. Once it does: new public comment period, final rule well into 2027 or 2028.
   - Sources: Land Line Media (July 2026), freedomforallamericans.org explainer (Sep 30, 2026).
7. **OOIDA opposition:** "the agencies were mandating a technology 'without sufficiently addressing false activations, properly consulting with professional truck drivers or completing ongoing research programs.'" Jay Grimes, OOIDA director of federal affairs: "NHTSA is currently investigating 250,000 heavy-duty trucks equipped with AEB that may inaccurately identify objects and cause unexpected stops."
   - Source: Land Line Media.
8. **Carrie Moore anecdote:** Oct 2023 Land Line Magazine story; truck driver in snowy Michigan highway, false AEB activation caused jackknife, nearly crashed into a median. Cited by Rep. Troy Nehls (R-TX) in Dec 2023 grilling of FMCSA/NHTSA administrators.
   - Source: Land Line Media (Sep 30, 2026 retrospective piece).
9. **NHTSA rulemaking backlog:** Congressional Research Service 2026 report — only 38 of 57 mandated rules completed under the last three transportation bills (2012, 2015, 2021). NHTSA staff cut 30% from its 2024 peak; funding cut 7%.
   - Source: Land Line Media (Sep 30, 2026).
10. **IIHS contrast (Oct 8, 2026):** first-ever commercial-vehicle front crash prevention ratings — no Class 3 pickup earned Good in either vehicle-to-vehicle or pedestrian tests; Chevrolet Silverado 3500HD, Ford F-350, GMC Sierra 3500 all Poor for vehicle-to-vehicle; 6,215 people killed in 2024 in crashes involving medium/heavy-duty vehicles or light vans (IIHS).
    - Sources: IIHS news release; Autoblog; fleetmanagementweekly.

## Original contribution (Rigor requirement)

- **The mandate-vs-defect timeline calculation:** Bipartisan Infrastructure Law signed Nov 2021 → PE23010 opened May 2023 → NPRM June 2023 → EA25006 escalated Oct 23, 2025 → still open Oct 12, 2026 (355 days as an EA, ~3.4 years since the PE opened). The false-activation probe has outlived the expected supplemental-NPRM schedule it helped stall.
- **Benefit/risk arithmetic:** the mandate's headline projection is 155 lives saved and 19,118 crashes prevented per year; the defect file holds 24 crashes and 13 injuries attributed to the technology *working wrong*. Both numbers are small relative to 60,000 annual heavy-vehicle-strikes rear-end crashes — the stakes are real on both sides, and neither camp has the clean win it claims.
- **The symmetry nobody stated:** NHTSA's own files contain both directions of the failure. In a lab, a Cascadia emergency-braked for a steel plate lying in the road. On the track, IIHS watched Class 3 pickups fail to brake for a car target at 31 mph. The technology fails toward stopping when it should roll and fails toward rolling when it should stop.

## Limitations (must be stated in article)

- EA25006 is not a recall and not a defect determination; Engineering Analysis is the most serious stage of a defect investigation but can end without action.
- Complaint counts understate real-world frequency (most false activations are never reported); they also overstate attribution (drivers may misattribute a jolt).
- Detroit Assurance vs OnGuard claim-rate comparison is claim data, not controlled failure-rate testing.
- The 155-lives projection assumes full fleet equipage over years; the 24 crashes are observed in one 250,000-truck population.
- The supplemental NPRM's absence may reflect many factors (staff cuts, politics, comment volume), not only the EA.

## Strongest counterargument (must be stated at full strength)

Advocates: 60,000 rear-end crashes a year with a heavy vehicle as the striking vehicle, 155 lives a year, and every year of delay is measurable body count. Cars already carry AEB and it works — NHTSA's own FMVSS 127 for light vehicles passed on the same logic. Phantom braking is real but rare relative to fleet miles, and no technology ships perfect; a rule that waits for perfection is a rule that never ships.
