Dodge Has Recalled the Durango's Rear Spoiler Three Times. The Fix Keeps Failing. They Still Don't Have a New One.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
August 25, 2026

In 2021, Stellantis quietly recalled 503 Dodge Durangos because the rear liftgate spoiler could detach while driving. Dealers inspected them, replaced what needed replacing, and everyone moved on. In 2023, the same defect came back: recall number two covered 18,163 vehicles. Same fix. Inspect, replace, close the ticket. Now it is August 2026, and NHTSA campaign 26V528000 covers 74,578 Durangos from the 2021 through 2023 model years.[1] Three recalls. Same part. A population that grew 148 times. And Stellantis says the remedy is "under development."

74,578
Durangos recalled for the same spoiler defect, third time in five years

They issued a recall without a fix. Dealers received notice on August 20. Owner letters start going out September 10. What those letters will say is, effectively, "your spoiler might fly off at highway speed, and we will contact you again when we know how to stop it."

An assembly tolerance problem at Jefferson North put spoilers slightly too high relative to the roof. Every time a liftgate cycles, the spoiler's forward edge scrapes the roof's trailing edge. Do that a few hundred times and the fasteners give up. At 70 mph on I-94, a detached spoiler becomes a projectile aimed at whoever is behind you.

Stellantis logged 116 warranty claims and 45 additional field reports tied to the issue between May 2022 and June 2026.[2] No crashes. No injuries. But the pattern matters more than the count: three years of production shipped with the defect, two prior fixes failed, and the recall population keeps expanding. Vehicles built after March 2023 no longer have the over-flush condition, meaning Stellantis eventually fixed the assembly line but never went back and fixed the 74,578 already on the road.

Stellantis has also recalled its backup camera software seven separate times across Jeep, Chrysler, Dodge, and Ram because radio firmware prevents the rearview image from displaying.[3] In 2024, the company recalled 2.6 vehicles for every one it sold.[4] At some point, "recall" stops meaning "we found a problem and we are fixing it" and starts meaning "we shipped it broken and we will keep shipping it broken until someone notices."

If you own a 2021 to 2023 Dodge Durango, listen for the spoiler contacting the roof when you open or close the liftgate. A rattling sound from the rear at speed means it is already loose. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Do not wait for the letter. If you are shopping used, ask whether both previous spoiler recalls were completed and know that the third fix does not exist yet.

Counterargument: Zero crashes and zero injuries across 161 field reports suggest low actual risk. Issuing a recall before finalizing the remedy prioritizes disclosure over delay. Expanding the population may be precautionary, not proof of a worsening defect rate.

Limitations: NHTSA does not disclose completion rates for the 2021 and 2023 campaigns. Warranty claims may undercount incidents. Field reports are voluntary.
