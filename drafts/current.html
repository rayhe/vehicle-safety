70% of America’s Safety Gains Came From One Road Type. The Roads Didn’t Change.

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
July 27, 2026

NHTSA released its 2025 subcategory breakdown this month, and one number swallows the rest: urban arterials shed 1,843 deaths in a single year, falling from 15,394 to 13,551.[1] That 12% decline accounts for 70.5% of the entire national fatality reduction. Seven out of every ten lives saved last year were saved on stroads and suburban boulevards.

70.5% of the national fatality decline occurred on urban arterials alone

Nobody widened a shoulder. Nobody dropped a speed limit from 45 to 35. Nobody installed a protected left turn. The concrete is the same concrete it was in 2024, when those roads killed 15,394 people. What changed was the metal rolling over it. Newer vehicles with forward collision warning, automatic emergency braking, and lane departure systems are compensating for road geometry that was designed to move cars fast, not keep people alive.[2]

Compare that to rural interstates, supposedly the safest roads per mile traveled. They were the only road category where deaths increased in 2025: 1,806 to 1,823, a small but conspicuous bump in the wrong direction.[1] The pattern is counterintuitive until you think about closure speeds. AEB works at 40 mph approaching a red light. At 75 mph closing on a jackknifed semi, the physics outrun the software.

Rural arterials barely budged: down 1%, or 92 fewer deaths. Urban collectors and local roads dropped just 4%. None of this is distributing evenly. ADAS delivers its benefits precisely where the road design is worst and speeds are most forgiving for automated braking systems.

Urban arterials still killed 13,551 people last year. That is 37% of all traffic fatalities, concentrated on roads that represent a fraction of total lane-miles. Every suburban six-lane boulevard with a 45-mph limit and a crosswalk painted directly into the kill zone is still standing. The cars just got better at preventing the crashes those roads were always going to produce.

If you commute on a multi-lane arterial, your vehicle’s ADAS suite matters more than the road beneath you. Check whether your model earned current IIHS ratings with AEB at iihs.org/ratings. A five-star NCAP crash score means the cabin survives impact. It says nothing about whether the car can prevent impact in the first place. And if you drive a vehicle older than model year 2018, most of this technology simply does not exist in your car. Your road has not gotten safer. Your neighbors’ cars have.
