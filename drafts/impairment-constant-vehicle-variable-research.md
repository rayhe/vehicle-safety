# Research Notes: Impairment Is a Constant. The Vehicle Is the Variable.

## News Peg
- NHTSA "Drive Sober or Get Pulled Over" campaign (July 2026) + $665M safety grants

- Speeding deaths -11% in 2025 (10,035), overall deaths -6.7% (36,640)
- Q1 2026 fatality rate: 0.99/100M VMT, 15th consecutive quarterly decline

## Original Finding
Pearson correlation between vehicle death rate (per 100M VMT) and driver impairment rate (% of fatal crash drivers impaired) across 200 vehicles with 100+ deaths:

**r = -0.195 (weakly negative)**

This means: vehicles with HIGHER death rates tend to have LOWER impairment rates. The deadliest cars are killing sober people.

## Quartile Analysis (50 vehicles each)

| Quartile | Avg Death Rate | Avg Impairment | Total Deaths |
|----------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| Safest 25% | 0.33 | 19.5% | 18,667 |
| Below average | 0.69 | 20.7% | 23,048 |
| Above average | 1.20 | 20.4% | 56,272 |
| Deadliest 25% | 2.92 | 19.6% | 86,103 |

Key insight: Death rate varies 9x from safest to deadliest quartile. Impairment moves from 19.5% to 19.6%. It's effectively a CONSTANT.
