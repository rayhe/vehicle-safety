# Research Notes: 1249 — Maryland's Traffic Deaths Fell 21% While Crash Counts Stayed Flat

**Article #1249** | **Journalist:** Rex Driverton | **Started:** 2026-10-23T03:30:00-07:00

## News Peg (fresh, dated)
- CNS Maryland published Oct 19, 2026: analysis of Maryland Department of State Police crash data finds traffic **fatalities declined 21%** compared to the same period last year (290 deaths as of end of September 2026), while **total reported crashes rose 0.3%**. Same crashes, fewer deaths.
- Fatal crashes: ~600 (2023, 10-year high) → 470 (2025, lowest in a decade) → 2026 on track lower still.
- Fatal crashes as share of all crashes: 0.5% (2024) → 0.36% (2026 YTD). Property-damage-only crashes: 72% of all crashes in each of the past three years.

## Primary Sources (4)
1. **CNS Maryland analysis** (cnsmaryland.org, 2026-10-19): headline figures above; methodology published; datasets on GitHub. Quote from Tim Kerns, director of the Maryland Highway Safety Office: "We kind of look at it as a pyramid... our first target are the ones that wind up in a fatality or a serious injury, and those have been coming down."
2. **Maryland State Police data** (via CNS/GitHub): 2024-2026 dashboard (analytics.mdsp.org), 2019-2023 dashboard, 2015-2018 via email.
3. **Zero Deaths Maryland dashboard** (zerodeathsmd.gov): five contributing-cause categories (speed, impairment, aggressive, distracted, unrestrained). Fatal crashes fell in ALL FIVE categories vs. this time in 2024. Dashboard differs from MDSP data (includes open investigations + US Park Police).
4. **NHTSA FARS**: Maryland ranked 39th in deaths per 100,000 residents (2024, most recent state figures). Neighbors: Virginia 32nd, D.C. 48th.

## The Angle (novel, Rex-shaped)
Not "Maryland is safe now." The paradox: **the crashes didn't stop. The dying did.** The same number of metal-bending events; the lethal ones got filtered out. Kerns's pyramid framing gives the thesis: property-damage crashes are the immovable base; the top of the pyramid (fatal + serious-injury crashes) is where intervention works.

Original contribution (arithmetic the CNS piece didn't spell out):
- 290 deaths through end of Sept 2026 → ~21% decline implies ~367 for the same period of 2025. Full-year 2026 pace: 290/9*12 ≈ **387** vs 470 (2025) vs ~600 (2023). That is a ~35% drop from the 2023 peak in three years.
- Impairment crashes fell **nearly 43% statewide** — the sharpest of any contributing-cause category. Prince George's County is the outlier (impairment crashes up 9.1% vs 2024, though down from a 2025 spike; overall fatal crashes down ~17% since 2024).
- The Baltimore caveat is a data-quality story in itself: MDSP recorded 7 Baltimore fatal crashes in 2026 (as of Oct 6) vs Baltimore Police's own count of **22** — because Baltimore PD only reports "totally completed" investigations. The decline is real, but the 2026 figure is undercounted in the most crash-prone city.
- Maryland passed a Vision Zero law in 2019 (zero deaths by 2030); Highway Safety Office credits enforcement + education + engineering (sober-ride-home campaigns, seat-belt messaging, high-visibility enforcement).

## Actionable takeaways
- Maryland's pyramid thesis is the action: if your state/county publishes contributing-cause data, the impairment slice is the one that moves first (Maryland cut it 43%). That is where Vision Zero dollars appear to pay off fastest.
- For drivers: the five fatal-crash categories are all human-behavior categories (speed, impairment, aggression, distraction, belts) — vehicle-tech doesn't explain the decline alone.
- Caveat to readers in other states: Maryland was already 39th-lowest in 2024; "on track" numbers are preliminary and will revise upward as open investigations close (see the Baltimore 7-vs-22 gap).

## Limitations / Counterarguments
- Preliminary data: 2026 figures exclude open investigations; final numbers will be higher. The 21% decline will likely shrink on revision.
- Causation is unclaimed: Kerns credits enforcement+education+engineering, but the CNS piece doesn't isolate which intervention mattered, nor does it rule out EMS response improvements, vehicle-fleet turnover, or reporting-completeness changes.
- Correlation with national trend: TRIP's September 2026 report found national rates also improving; Maryland may be riding a national wave rather than leading one. Maryland ranked 39th — it is improving from an already-low baseline.
- Property-damage reporting: a stable 72% property-damage share assumes reporting thresholds are stable; if minor crashes are increasingly unreported (insurance-filing behavior changes), the denominator could be understated, inflating the "share of crashes that are fatal" improvement.
