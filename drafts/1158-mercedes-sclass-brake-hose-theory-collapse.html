Mercedes Said Its Brake Hoses Only Failed in Hot, Humid Places. The Rest of the World Disagreed.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
September 12, 2027

In February 2025, Mercedes filed a recall for 20,339 S-Class sedans with a confident diagnosis: the front brake hoses were failing after use in hot, humid climates.[2] Then the hoses kept failing in places that were not hot or humid. Campaign 26V638 expands the recall to 30,064 vehicles and puts the estimated defect share at 100 percent.[1]

100%
Estimated share of the 30,064 recalled vehicles carrying defective front brake hoses (NHTSA 26V638 Part 573 report)

Its chronology reads like a slow-motion retraction. March through August 2025: field reports arrive from markets outside the US, involving vehicles built outside the original production range and driven outside the climatic regions the theory was based on. November 2025: Mercedes begins coordinating a field engineering activity. March 2026: the collection protocol goes live, and suspect hoses start coming back to the lab. July 2026: the test results get reviewed, the durability specs come up short, and an improved hose design (already in series production since November 29, 2024) is identified. On September 25, 2026, the decision to expand arrives. Nineteen months after the first filing, the climate theory is dead.[3]

Part number A2234201800 is the suspect: a front brake hose built by Continental Automotive Technologies of Frankfurt that, per the filing, missed its durability specifications. Accumulated thermal and mechanical stress over a vehicle's lifetime can fatigue the rubber until it ruptures and leaks brake fluid, bleeding pressure from one of the two hydraulic circuits. One car dominates the recall population: 16,881 S 580 4MATICs, 56 percent of all affected vehicles, the volume flagship, followed by 7,143 S 500s.[1]

For scale: the S-Class logged 60 deaths over ten years in FARS, a rate of 0.4 per 100 million miles.[8] This is a car that protects its occupants well. None of that structure helps when the pedal goes soft without warning, and the filing is explicit that there is no warning before failure. That red brake-fluid light on the cluster is a post-mortem, not a warning.[1]

Give Mercedes its due. This is the recall system working: it monitored the field after 25V116, collected hoses, ran lab tests, and expanded voluntarily. Five warranty claims and two field reports in the expanded population, with zero crashes, zero injuries, zero property damage. Nobody died. Credit where due: this is precaution with a paper trail, and a carmaker that spends money on a 100-percent-defect recall is doing the expensive thing right.[3]

What we do not know: FARS does not code brake-component failures, so no public dataset links these hoses to actual crashes. Lab results are not public, and Continental has said nothing on the record. Treat the filing's 100 percent figure as an estimate, not a measurement of all 30,064 hoses. And the 20,339 cars in the original recall's narrower population spent up to 19 months driving on suspect hoses while the theory held.[8]

If you drive a 2021 to 2025 S-Class, Maybach S, or AMG S 63: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls tonight.[7] Do not wait for the November 27 letter. Free front brake hose replacement at the dealer is the remedy.[1] This is one defect where the first symptom is also the last warning.
