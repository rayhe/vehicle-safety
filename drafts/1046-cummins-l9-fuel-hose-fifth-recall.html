Cummins Recalled the Same Fuel Hose Five Times in Five Years. The Fifth Recall Is for the Fixes.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
October 2, 2026

Here's a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute: somewhere on the road right now, a fire truck is carrying a fuel hose that has been recalled five times in five years, not five hoses, one hose, five recalls. Cummins just filed the fifth one, covering 39,052 L9 diesel engines under NHTSA campaign 26E066, and the paperwork contains a sentence you almost never see in a recall filing: engines already fixed under the 2023, 2024, and 2025 recalls have to be fixed again, because the fixes didn't fix it.[1]

5
Recalls of the same Cummins L9 remote fuel filter hose since 2021

Read the timeline like a confession: in 2021 Cummins recalled the L9's remote fuel filter hoses for leaking and the remedy was hoses "of a new design"; in 2023 it recalled them again for "improved connector crimps";[5] in 2024 again, a small one, just 230 engines; then again in 2025.[2][3] And now, in September 2026, the same hose, the same separation, the same fuel leak, the same fire risk: five campaigns, one hose.

What makes this more than a warranty comedy is where the hose lives: these hoses are plumbed into fire apparatus, school buses, city transit buses, motorhomes, and medium-duty work trucks, the vehicles you most want to neither catch fire nor stall. The defect is exactly as dumb as it sounds, a crimped connector that doesn't crimp hard enough, so the hose walks off its fitting and dumps diesel, and NHTSA's warning signs read like a poem about the end of the world: fuel smell, visible wetness, the malfunction lamp.[4] Diesel doesn't ignite as eagerly as gasoline, which is the only reason this story is about stalls instead of fireballs. Your fire truck may smell like fuel, and good luck with that.

In fairness, the defense deserves its hearing: five recalls of one part can mean the surveillance is working, each round narrowed the population, and Cummins kept hunting edge cases instead of declaring victory. Round three covered 230 engines, and that's diligence, but diligence with a body count of zero so far is still a manufacturer that needed five tries to crimp a hose, and worse, the 2026 population includes engines built in April 2026, five years after the "new design" was supposed to end this.[1] That is either the most diligent quality program in the industry or the most patient failure, and the difference is mostly marketing: surveillance worked, the hoses didn't.

So if you own a motorhome, bus, or vocational truck with a Cummins L9 built between December 2020 and April 2026, do not assume a previous fuel-hose recall repair cleared you, because Cummins says those engines need the new redesigned parts. Call Cummins at 1-800-286-6467 with your engine serial number, or check with your vehicle maker, because equipment recalls reach owners through the OEM, Tiffin, New Flyer, PACCAR, and the rest, not through Cummins directly. Owner letters go out November 20, and presumably the hose will be recalled again sometime after that.
