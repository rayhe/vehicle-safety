Thomas Built Recalled School Buses Three Times in One Year. Every Time, Hot Coolant Was Leaking Into the Cabin.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
October 27, 2026

In October, regulators in the United States and Canada widened a school-bus recall that was supposed to be finished in April. NHTSA campaign 26V697 and Transport Canada recall 2026513 both cover 2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 buses whose heater hoses can rupture and leak, and the Canadian notice says plainly that it expands an earlier recall for the same issue.[1][2] Count them: three separate waves of Thomas Built heater-hose recalls in 2026, all ending with hot coolant inside the passenger compartment, where children sit.

2,895
Thomas Built school buses recalled in February 2026 after heater-hose clamps were not tightened properly (2,843 + 52 across two campaigns)

Let's talk about what happens in the first seconds after a heater hose lets go. A school bus heats its cabin by routing hot engine coolant through hoses into the bus body, which means the plumbing runs where passengers sit rather than staying under the hood with the engine. Pressurized coolant escaping inside a cabin is not a puddle problem or a smell problem. It is a scald problem, delivered at close range to people who cannot drive themselves away from it, and Daimler Truck North America's own April letter warns that the hose "may rupture and leak without prior warning" and "can release hot coolant within the passenger area."[3] No warning. No drip first. Just a rupture.

April's recall, NHTSA 26V-109 under Daimler number F1031, looked surgically precise. It covered 2027 Saf-T-Liner C2 buses built from December 8 to December 12, 2025, a five-day production window, with a manufacturing error in the hose as the cause.[3] Remedy was a date-code inspection: a dealer checks which batch a bus's hoses came from and replaces them if they match, a job of one to eight hours, free of charge.[3] Batch-specific defects get batch-specific fixes, and on paper this one was contained.

February had already shown the other way this exact failure happens. Two campaigns that month covered 2023-2026 Saf-T-Liner EFX and HDX buses, 2,843 in one and 52 in the other, because heater-hose clamps "may not have been tightened properly, causing the hose to disconnect and leak heating coolant inside the passenger compartment."[4] Add them up, 2,843 plus 52, and you get 2,895 buses, all with the same consequence spelled out in the same words: contact with hot heating coolant may burn occupants.[4] One of those two campaigns is documented as NHTSA 26V087 under Daimler number F1030, reported February 13, 2026, with owner letters expected April 14, 2026.[5] So 2026 gave us a clamp that was never tightened right and a hose that was never manufactured right, and both failure modes end with a child sitting next to a burst of hot coolant.

October is where the pattern turns from unfortunate to instructive. An expansion recall means the first sweep's population was wrong, and here the miss took about six months to surface: April's five-day window did not hold, so both countries widened the net in the same week.[1][2] Canada describes the new population only as "a small number" of buses, and no US population count for 26V697 appears in the sources found for this article, which is itself worth noting, because a recall expansion that will not say how many vehicles it expands to is asking owners to trust a process that just demonstrated it miscounted. For scale, this failure mode is not new at this company: Daimler's 2016 campaigns FL708 and FL709 covered Thomas Built HDX heater hoses that could fail and leak hot coolant inside the bus, a decade before this year's three waves.[6]

Fairness requires the counterargument at full strength. School buses are the safest vehicles children ride in, by a wide margin, and nothing in the sources found for this article attributes a single burn injury to any of these heater-hose recalls. Populations here are small and batch-specific, the remedy takes hours and costs nothing, and a district that pulled buses off routes over a hose recall would trade a small, known, fixable risk for the much larger risk of children walking or riding in cars instead. None of that erases the repetition, though. One manufacturer has now needed three attempts in one year to contain one failure mode, and the third attempt exists because the first two did not finish the job.

What you can actually do: fleet operators and districts running 2027 Saf-T-Liner C2 buses should check VINs against both NHTSA 26V109/F1031 and the new 26V697, plus Transport Canada 2026513 and the earlier 2026-083 in Canada, and call Daimler Truck North America at 1-800-547-0712 rather than assuming an April inspection clears a bus covered by the October expansion.[5] Districts running 2023-2026 EFX or HDX buses should check the February clamp recalls, including 26V087/F1030. Drivers: a sweet smell, steam, or wetness inside the cabin is a stop-and-evacuate signal, not a drive-to-the-depot signal, because hot coolant burns on contact. Parents: ask your district whether its Thomas Built buses have cleared all three 2026 heater-hose actions, not just "the recall."

What this article did not prove: no US count for 26V697 and no Canadian count for 2026513 appears in the sources found, so neither is stated here, and no burn, crash, or fatality count is attributed to these recalls, because none appears in those sources either. Absence of a count is not evidence that none occurred. One February campaign number is also left unstated, because only the 52-unit leg's number (26V087) was verified for this article. Finally, the US and Canadian October actions are described as parallel moves on the same issue in the same week, matching model, model year, defect, and timing, not as one filing causing the other.
