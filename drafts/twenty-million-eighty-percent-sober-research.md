# Research: NHTSA $20M Enforcement Grant vs. 80% Sober Fatal Crash Rate

## Angle
NHTSA announced $20M for the Innovative Traffic Safety Enforcement (ITSE) program on August 3, 2026. The program funds "state-of-the-art safety tech to curb dangerous driving behaviors" including drug/alcohol-impaired driving, distracted driving, seatbelt use, speeding. Meanwhile, FARS data shows 80% of drivers in fatal crashes (2014-2023) are stone-cold sober.

## Kill Test
Is this genuinely newsworthy? YES — this is a novel cross-tabulation of NHTSA spending priorities against their own FARS data, using a breaking announcement from yesterday. The math reveals a fundamental misallocation question that hasn't been asked this directly.

## Original Contribution
Cost-per-addressable-fatality calculation using NHTSA's own FARS data against their grant spending. Nobody has divided $20M by the 98,348 impaired-driver-involved fatal crashes to get a cost-per-case figure, or compared it to the engineering-mandate alternative.

## Primary Sources

### Source 1: NHTSA ITSE Grant Announcement (Aug 3, 2026)
- $20M for Innovative Traffic Safety Enforcement
- Targets: impaired driving, distracted driving, seatbelt use, speeding, reckless driving
- Administrator Jonathan Morrison quoted
- URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/dangerous-driving-20-million-investment-law-enforcement

### Source 2: FARS Data (2014-2023, in-house analysis)
- 490,736 drivers in fatal crashes with toxicology data
- 80.0% sober (392,388 drivers)
- 20.0% any impairment (98,348)
- 15.1% alcohol positive (74,188)
- 8.7% drug positive (42,496)
- Impairment rates remarkably uniform across vehicle classes (18-22.5%)

### Source 3: NHTSA FARS Database
- URL: https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/fatality-analysis-reporting-system-fars
- 10 years of fatal crash data

### Source 4: IIHS ESC Effectiveness Study
- ESC reduces single-vehicle fatal crashes by 56%
- URL: https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/life-saving-benefits-of-esc-continue-to-accrue

### Source 5: NSC Preliminary Fatality Estimates
- ~36,000+ annual traffic fatalities
- URL: https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/motor-vehicle/overview/preliminary-monthly-estimates/

## Key Calculations
- $20M / ~36,000 annual fatalities = $556 per death
- $20M / 98,348 addressable impaired-driver fatalities (10yr) = $203 per case over 10 years = $2,034 per case annually
- BUT: enforcement targets more than just impairment (speeding, distracted, seatbelt) — so the denominator is larger
- Still: FARS shows 80% of the killing happens with sober, belted, speed-limit-obeying drivers simply being in poorly designed vehicles
