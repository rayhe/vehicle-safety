# Research: #869 — June Spoiled the Victory Lap

Journalist: Rex Driverton | Kicker: Trend Watch | Date: 2026-09-10

## Angle (1-2 sentences)

NSC's June 2026 preliminary estimates (updated Sept 9, 2026) show the national monthly death rate INCREASED 2.5% vs June 2025 — more miles (+0.9%), more deaths, higher rate — even as the H1 2026 cumulative still reads down 2%. Q1 2026 was the second-best quarter in history (0.99/100M VMT); June broke the streak right as summer, the statistical killing season, peaked. 28 states + DC posted worse Junes than last year.

## Kill test

- Newsworthy: YES. Data is 1 day old (NSC updated Sept 9, 2026). It complicates the "2026 on pace for fifth straight year of declines" narrative (Reuters, Jul 8, 2026).
- Novel: YES. Zero coverage on vehicle-safety.org of the June monthly rate reversal. #861 (Sept 9) covered the H1 2026 cumulative (18,460, -2%) and the Illinois concentration, and explicitly dismissed the June state split as "volatility, not reversal." This piece argues the national June rate reversal (+2.5%, deaths AND mileage up together) is a different and sharper claim than the state-count noise.
- Honest caveats: one month is thin; NSC warns preliminary estimates mature upward (June's increase could grow, not shrink); summer is always the deadliest season. Frame as "complication," not "collapse."

## Verified facts (all from this run's tool output, 2026-09-10)

1. NSC June 2026 preliminary estimates (injuryfacts.nsc.org, "Last Updated: 1 day ago" per Sept 9 crawl): June 2026 mileage +0.9% vs June 2025; "combined with an increase in number of deaths, the June 2026 death rate increased by 2.5% compared to June 2025." [PRIMARY]
2. NSC June 2026: "22 states reported fewer deaths compared to June 2025 preliminary reports and 28 states and the District of Columbia reported more deaths in June 2026 than in June 2025." [PRIMARY]
3. NSC H1 2026 (preliminary-estimates page, verified 2026-09-10): 18,460 deaths, down 2% from 18,846 (2025 H1); mileage +0.8%; rate 1.12/100M VMT, down 3.4% from 1.16. [PRIMARY]
4. NHTSA Q1 2026 early estimates (press release, via Reuters Jul 8, 2026): 7,770 deaths, -4.3% vs Q1 2025; rate 0.99/100M VMT — second-lowest Q1 rate in US history (record 0.98). [PRIMARY via NHTSA; Reuters for narrative]
5. Reuters Jul 8, 2026: "2026 is on pace to be the fifth straight year of declines." 2025 full-year: 36,640 deaths, rate 1.10 — second-lowest yearly figure. [news]
6. NSC methodology: deaths follow a seasonal pattern, peaking in summer/fall; state DOT reporters supply monthly estimates; NSC compares comparable-maturity months; "fatality estimates tend to mature over the course of the year. Numbers often increase as fatalities are confirmed." [PRIMARY]
7. NSC state splits H1 (from #861's verified research, Sept 9): 13 states down 10%+ saved 499 lives; Illinois alone = 134 fewer deaths, 34.7% of the 386-death national H1 decline. (Context only; cite #861-era NSC release via preliminary-estimates URL.)

## What to NOT claim

- Do NOT invent the absolute June 2026 death count (NSC page gives rate/mileage changes, not the count).
- Do NOT claim the full-year trend reversed (H1 is still -2%; one month is one month).
- Do NOT use the Maryland MDOT anecdote (MDOT YTD 318 vs 290 conflicts with NSC's Maryland -13% through June; different periods/methods, unresolved).

## Actionable insight

Summer is the statistical killing season (NSC: death rates highest in summer/fall), and June's rate just ticked up 2.5%. Readers heading into the rest of the 100 Deadliest Days window should treat the seasonal peak as real: the three behaviors NHTSA keeps naming — speeding, impairment, no belt — are the levers. Check open recalls by VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before a long summer drive. One month doesn't erase H1's gains, but June is the month that matters most, and it went the wrong way.
