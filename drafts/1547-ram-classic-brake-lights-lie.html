Your Ram Classic's Brake Lights Are Lying to Everyone Behind You

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

Stellantis is recalling 82,843 Ram 1500 Classic trucks because the anti-lock brake module can misread the brake pedal. Read that again. The computer that decides whether your brake lights are on can get the pedal wrong, and when it does, three things happen at once. Your brake lights lie to the driver behind you. Your truck can move when it should be parked. And the systems meant to catch all of that, stability control, AutoPark, and forward collision warning, go dark with it.[1][2]

83,512
Total affected units in NHTSA campaign 26V756: 82,843 Ram 1500 Classic trucks, plus 669 Mopar ABS modules sold as replacement parts.

This is the sequel. In September 2024, recall 75B pulled more than a million new-generation Ram 1500s for an ABS software malfunction that could kill stability control, a failure to conform to the federal stability control standard. Same failure family, same fix, a dealer software update. Twenty-six months later, the old truck gets its own version. Stellantis kept selling the previous-generation Classic alongside the redesigned 1500 as the budget work truck, and the budget truck kept the budget bug.[4]

Worse, the defect did not stay inside the trucks. Another 669 units in this recall are Mopar ABS control modules sold over the counter as replacement parts. That is 0.8 percent of the recall, and it is the scariest 0.8 percent, because a module bought to fix a truck can carry the same fault into a truck that never had it. If your Classic ever had an ABS module swapped, ask the dealer whether the part was one of the 669.[2]

Fairness check: no crashes or injuries show up in the public record for this campaign, and 82,843 trucks is a small recall next to the million-truck predecessor. The Ram 1500 is also a statistically safe truck by body count, 714 deaths in FARS 2014-2023, a rate of 0.13 per 100 million miles. None of that changes the failure mode. A parked truck that rolls and brake lights that lie are the kind of defect that needs exactly one bad moment, not a big population.[5]

What to do: VINs become searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls on November 26, and the letter does not arrive until January 14, so check the VIN yourself. Watch for brake lights acting strange, the truck creeping when it should be parked, or warnings for stability control, AutoPark, or forward collision warning. Any Chrysler dealer will flash the fix free of charge. Until then, park on flat ground and set the parking brake every single time. Do not trust AutoPark to hold a truck whose computer cannot read its own brake pedal.[1][3]
