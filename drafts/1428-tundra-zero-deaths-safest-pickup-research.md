# Research — #1428: The Safest Full-Size Pickup in America Is the One Nobody Buys

**Slug:** 1428-tundra-zero-deaths-safest-pickup
**Journalist:** Clara Rollover (Consumer Safety Advocate — car-buying guidance beat)
**Kicker:** The Gap
**Date:** November 7, 2026

## The angle (1-2 sentences)
A November 2026 study of America's 50 best-selling vehicles found the Toyota Tundra had zero driver deaths in its 2022 and 2023 models — the safest large pickup in the country, and one of only six models in the IIHS data with a clean record. Our own FARS pull confirms it from a second angle: zero deaths in crashes involving model-year 2022-2023 Tundras, while the F-150, Ram 1500, Silverado, and Tacoma all piled up double-digit body counts in the same window.

## Kill test
- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes. A zero in real-world death data for a full-size pickup is a first for this site, and it lands the same week the Tundra also stands alone as the only gasoline full-size pickup with a 2026 IIHS award.
- Novel? Zero "tundra" slugs in 1,427 prior articles. The site has covered the F-150, Silverado, and Ram 1500 obsessively; the Tundra has never once been the story. The FARS model-year cross-check (MY2022-23, same exposure window, peer comparison) is an original contribution.
- Verdict: PROCEED.

## The numbers
### The zero, two ways
- **IIHS driver deaths (Walner/IIHS):** 2022-2023 Tundra CrewMax 4WD: **0** driver deaths per million registered vehicle years. Large-pickup class average: **29**. That is -100% vs. the class.
- **FARS all-deaths, same exposure window (MY2022-2023 vehicles, crashes in 2022-2023):** Tundra **0**. Ford F-150 **30** (21 + 9). Ram 1500 **39** (28 + 11). Chevrolet Silverado **23** (23 + 0). Toyota Tacoma **17** (11 + 6). Note the metrics differ (IIHS counts driver deaths only; FARS counts all deaths in crashes involving the vehicle), but both point the same way.

### The expected-deaths check (rough, assumptions stated)
- MY2022-2023 Tundras sold: ~229,000 (104,246 + 125,185 calendar-year sales). Rough exposure: MY2022 trucks on the road for calendar 2022 and 2023, MY2023 trucks for 2023 only, ~330,000 vehicle-years at ~13,500 miles/year = ~4.5 billion miles.
- At the Tundra nameplate's own 2014-2023 average rate (0.94 deaths per 100M VMT), roughly **40 deaths** would have been expected in that window. Zero showed up.
- This is a back-of-envelope estimate, not a precise expectation: model-year and calendar-year sales do not align exactly, and the 0.94 rate mixes the old generation with the new one. The peer comparison above is the cleaner evidence; the estimate only shows the zero is not a small-sample shrug.

### The history the zero erases
- The Tundra nameplate is not historically safe: 1,223 FARS deaths 2014-2023, rate 0.94, mid-pack among pickups (pickup median 0.80). The story is the redesigned 2022 third generation, not the badge. Deaths by model year fell steadily as the old generation aged out (2014: 43 down to 2021: 8) and hit zero the year the redesign arrived.
- The redesigned truck also fixed the lab tests: Good across all IIHS crashworthiness tests and a 5-star NHTSA overall rating, where the F-150 — America's best-selling truck at 750,789 F-Series sold in 2023 — could not earn a 2026 IIHS award at all.

## What this means for buyers (actionable)
- Shopping for a full-size pickup: the 2022-and-newer Tundra is the only one in the segment with both a zero in real-world driver-death data and a current IIHS award. The specific configuration in the IIHS data is the CrewMax 4WD; Double Cabs were not NHTSA-assessed.
- The zero is two model years of data, not a lifetime guarantee, and it covers driver deaths (IIHS) and all crash deaths (FARS) in a short window. Treat it as the strongest real-world signal in the segment, not a promise.
- If you already own a 2022+ Tundra, nothing to do. If you own a 2021-or-older one, the generational break is the point: the old truck's record (43 deaths for MY2014 alone) is not the new truck's record.

## Caveats (state plainly in the article)
- The Walner Law report is a personal-injury law firm's study, not a peer-reviewed paper. Its underlying driver-death data is IIHS data, and the site's own FARS pull independently confirms the direction of the finding; the law-firm framing is not the evidence.
- Zero is a point estimate over a short window (MY2022-23, at most two years of exposure in the FARS file ending 2023). A single fatal crash involving a 2022 Tundra in 2024 data would end the zero. The claim is "zero so far," not "zero forever."
- FARS counts all deaths in crashes involving the vehicle; IIHS counts driver deaths only. The two zeros are different metrics that agree, which strengthens the finding, but they are not the same number.
- FARS model-year coding for some rivals (GMC Sierra, Nissan Titan) is ambiguous in this file, so the peer comparison is limited to models with stable name coding (F-150, Silverado, Ram 1500, Tacoma). The Sierra and Titan are excluded from the comparison rather than assumed to be zero.
