Acura's First EV Is Dead and Its Brakes Just Got Recalled a Second Time

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
November 25, 2026

Acura discontinued the ZDX, its first electric SUV, and then recalled its brakes for the second time. On November 5, 2026, Honda filed NHTSA campaign 26V735 covering 14,648 all-wheel-drive 2024 ZDXs, because brake software can silently release brake pressure at low speeds. That is 3.5 times the 4,174 cars covered by the first recall of the same defect in August 2024. Nobody has been hurt, and nobody has even filed a warranty claim. And every single one of the 14,648 cars carries the defect, according to Honda's own filing.[1]

Five things have to go wrong at once. Wheel slip from turning, light braking, a bump in the road, and the driver lifting off the brake pedal and pressing it again, all at the same moment, on a dry road. When that coincidence happens, the software that calculates the car's reference speed becomes unstable, decides the wheels are in deep slip when they are not, and orders the anti-lock system to release brake pressure, at low speed, with no warning. On a high-friction surface, which is to say, on the kind of road where you would expect your brakes to work best.[1]

General Motors built this car. GM also supplied the brake software, all nine part numbers of it, and GM investigated the bug in its own cars before telling Honda about it. On October 19, 2026, GM gave Honda new information about the issue. Three days later, GM handed over the results of its own investigation. Ten days after that, Honda decided to recall the cars. Eight hundred twelve days had passed since Honda's first ZDX brake recall, and in all that time the expanded population had been driving around with software that could let go of the brakes in a parking lot.[1]

GM was not doing Honda a favor out of kindness; it was cleaning up its own house. On October 29, the same day Honda decided to expand the ZDX recall, GM recalled 67,380 Cadillac Lyriq, Chevrolet Blazer EV, Celestiq, and Vistiq electric vehicles under campaign 26V717 for brake software of its own, same platform family, same supplier, same season. Acura's first EV was a GM Ultium vehicle wearing an Acura badge, and when the brake software turned out to be wrong, the fix arrived through the same channel the defect did: from Detroit, to Torrance, to the owners.[1][5]

Acura's own filing ends the production question in one sentence: the affected model is no longer in production. NHTSA's database lists six recall campaigns on the 2024 ZDX, and the brake software is both the first and the latest. It opened the list in August 2024 and it closes the list, for now, in November 2026, with a propulsion cable, a stabilizer bar, a backup camera, and backup software in between. A car gets six federal safety actions in twenty-seven months, roughly one every four and a half months of its life, and then it gets discontinued, and then its brakes get recalled again. There is a word for a product whose defects outlive its production run. It is called a legacy.[1][3]

None of this is as damning as it sounds, and the other side deserves its full hearing. Fourteen thousand cars is a small recall, and the ledger is clean: zero warranty claims, zero injuries, zero deaths, and a fix that is free and arrives over the air while the car sits in the driveway. Honda and GM may simply be conservative about an edge case that needs five simultaneous conditions to trigger, and the 14,648 may include the original 4,174, which would make the genuinely new population 10,474, not 14,648, although the filing does not say. A fair reader could call this the system working: a supplier found a bug, told its customer, the customer recalled the cars, nobody got hurt. That reading is available, but it is also incomplete. Between August 2024 and October 2026, every all-wheel-drive ZDX on the road carried software that could silently release brake pressure at low speed, and zero claims is good luck, not proof of safety, when the failure produces no warning and leaves no code an owner would ever see.[1]

What to do with this. If you own a 2024 ZDX with all-wheel drive, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now and again after December 14, when the owner letters go out, because the filing gives no date for when the new VINs become searchable. Do not assume the 2024 brake update cleared you. Call Acura at 1-800-382-2238, give recall number CQC, and ask whether your car is in the expanded population and whether you need a second update even if you already had the first. Take the over-the-air update when it is offered; it is the only fix, and it costs nothing. If you are shopping for a used ZDX, run the VIN against all six campaigns before you buy, and confirm the brake module update is installed, because a discontinued car with a second brake recall is still a car whose brakes can let go in a parking lot.[1][2]
