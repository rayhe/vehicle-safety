The Dodge Avenger Died in 2014. Its Drivers Are Still the Druggiest on American Roads.

Dale Impactor III • Clautomotive Desk
October 9, 2026

According to the toxicology reports — and there are a lot of them — no driver pool in America tests drug-positive like Dodge Avenger drivers. Of 877 Avenger drivers tested after fatal crashes between 2014 and 2023, 11.3 percent came back positive for drugs, the highest rate of any nameplate with more than 500 tested drivers in the federal data.[1] Alcohol added another 14.9 percent. Combined, more than one in five Avenger drivers in fatal crashes had something in their system.

Dodge stopped building the Avenger after the 2014 model year. Sergio Marchionne decided Dodge would be the muscle brand and Chrysler the mainstream brand, and a midsize sedan fit neither church, so the Avenger got the axe and its replacement, the Chrysler 200, died soon after.[4] Consumer Reports, which never recommended the car, notes it lived on in daily-rental fleets.[5] Today a used one costs about $4,500. That price point is doing a lot of work in this story.

Now the part where I ruin my own headline. Drug-positive does not mean drug-impaired, and NHTSA says so itself in a research note with the thrilling title “Understanding the Limitations of Drug Test Information, Reporting, and Testing Practices in Fatal Crashes.”[2] Cannabinoid traces linger in blood for weeks after the high is gone. Testing varies wildly by jurisdiction: coroners with bigger budgets test more drivers, and a county that tests everybody will always look druggier than one that tests nobody. NHTSA's report to Congress puts it bluntly — with cannabis, it is “impossible to predict specific effects based on THC-COOH concentrations.”[3] So 11.3 percent measures exposure to testing plus drug prevalence in the driver pool. It is not a conviction and it never was.

But it is still signal, and strip out the pharmacology debate and the number tells you who is driving Avengers: people buying $4,500 orphan sedans, often second or third owners, often young, often driving cars with deferred maintenance and expired registrations. The Avenger's own death rate is a sleepy 0.38 per 100 million miles — the car is not lethal, the fleet is just chemically enthusiastic.[1] Nobody is dying because the Avenger handles badly. They are dying in a slow, cheap, survivable sedan while impaired, which is its own kind of tragedy: the survivable crash, unsurvived.

What you should do: if you shop the bottom of the used market, know that the bargain price reflects the driver pool, not just the car — your crash odds depend on the vehicles around you, and insurers already price orphan-sedan risk into your quote, so compare insurance before you fall for the sticker. Check any used Avenger's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you buy. And support standardized roadside drug testing: until a test can distinguish last night's edible from last month's, every “druggiest drivers” ranking — including this one — is measuring the flashlight, not just the dark.
