Your Luxury Sedan Has Been Kicked Out of the Garage. Twice.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
November 18, 2027

Before you read another word: if you drive a 2015 or 2016 Hyundai Genesis, or a 2017 through 2020 Genesis G80, go move your car: outside, away from the house, not the driveway under the carport, not the garage with the door cracked. Hyundai just told 94,622 of you to do exactly that, because a stuck front-seat switch can overheat the power seat motors and start a fire while the car is parked or driving.[1]

2,302,534
Hyundai and Genesis vehicles told to park outside since 2021, and your luxury sedan counts twice

This is the second time your specific car has been evicted from the garage. In 2021, these exact model years were recalled under 21V160000 for an ABS module that could short-circuit and set the engine compartment ablaze while parked or driving, same park-outdoors advisory, owner letters mailed July 2021, and five years later the seat switch is doing the burning: different part, same exile.[2]

Here is the census of exile. Hyundai's park-outside recalls since 2021: 567,912 Santa Fe and Santa Cruz models for tow-hitch harness fires in March 2023, roughly 1.64 million more for ABS module fires that September, and now 94,622 luxury sedans for seat-switch fires, 26V-664, bringing the running total to 2,302,534 vehicles. Count Kia's 1.73 million from the same ABS debacle and the family tally passes 4 million. That is the population of Los Angeles being told their driveway is safer than their garage.

Hyundai will argue, fairly, that this looks bad partly because it self-reports fast. After the Theta II engine and ABS scandals turned silence into a corporate strategy, aggressive early recalls became the company's atonement, and a manufacturer that recalls early pads its tally while competitors bury theirs, though transparency still beats the alternative. But two park-outside recalls in five years on one model range is not a paperwork artifact; it is a pattern.

And the remedy does not exist, which deserves a moment to land. This recall was announced October 15, and as of now there is no fix: Hyundai says so itself, and there is nothing your dealer can install today. The defect estimate is around 1% of the recalled population, with two alleged U.S. fire incidents and no crashes or injuries reported. But "only 1%" is cold comfort when the failure mode is a parked car becoming a fire.[3]

Start today: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, then park outside, away from structures, the house, the other car, the garage full of paint cans and old rags. Do not sleep in it, and do not assume a service appointment next week accomplishes anything, because there is nothing to install yet. A recall announcement is not a repair; until it is one, your driveway is the safest room in the house.
