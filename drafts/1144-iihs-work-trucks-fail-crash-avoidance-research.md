## Primary sources
1. **IIHS news release, Oct 8, 2026** — "Cargo vans and work pickups struggle in IIHS crash avoidance tests." First commercial-vehicle front crash prevention ratings: 11 vehicles (7 cargo vans, 4 Class 3 pickups, GVWR 10,001–14,000 lbs), tested at the Vehicle Research Center, Ruckersville, VA. Same protocols as passenger vehicles. <https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/cargo-vans-and-work-pickups-struggle-in-iihs-crash-avoidance-tests>
2. **Advocates for Highway and Auto Safety, president Cathy Chase, statement Oct 8, 2026** — ratings pose "a clear warning that too many commercial vehicles lack adequate front crash prevention," especially the two tested vehicles with no AEB. Cites NHTSA's June 2023 estimate: a federal AEB requirement for heavy vehicles could save 155 lives and prevent 8,814 injuries and 19,000+ crashes annually, saving close to $2B/year. Via Detroit Free Press. <https://www.freep.com/story/money/cars/2026/10/08/work-trucks-and-cargo-vans-face-1st-safety-tests-by-iihs-the-results/92137073007/>
3. **NHTSA FARS 2024** — 6,215 people killed in crashes involving heavy- or medium-duty vehicles or light vans; 16% of all US roadway fatalities (per IIHS release; underlying NHTSA data). <https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/fatality-analysis-reporting-system-fars>

## Verified facts (from IIHS release + named coverage)
- **Scope:** 7 cargo vans + 4 Class 3 pickups. Vans: 2025-26 Chevrolet BrightDrop 400, 2025-27 Chevrolet Express 2500, 2025 Ford Transit T250, 2025-27 GMC Savana 2500, 2025 Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 2500, 2025-27 Ram ProMaster 2500, 2025 Rivian RCV-Delivery 500. Pickups: 2025-26 Chevrolet Silverado 3500HD, 2025 Ford F-350, 2025-26 GMC Sierra 3500, 2026-27 Ram 3500.
- **Vehicle-to-vehicle:** Good — Sprinter 2500 (only one). Acceptable — BrightDrop 400. Marginal — Rivian RCV-Delivery 500, Ram 3500. Poor — Express 2500, Savana 2500, Transit T250, ProMaster 2500, Silverado 3500HD, F-350, Sierra 3500.
- **Pedestrian:** Good — BrightDrop 400, Rivian RCV-Delivery 500 (both electric). Acceptable — Sprinter 2500, F-350, Ram 3500. Marginal — Transit T250, ProMaster 2500, Silverado 3500HD, Sierra 3500. Poor — Express 2500, Savana 2500.
- **Express 2500 and Savana 2500** earn Poor in BOTH tests because they lack AEB entirely; optional forward collision warning only. These are still-sold, ancient-platform vans.
- **Motorcycle target** was the biggest challenge: "Several systems did not issue warnings or slow at all in most of the motorcycle trials" (IIHS).
- **Child dummy:** acceptable-rated F-350 and Ram 3500 had a harder time detecting the child in daylight than an adult at night; in some cases the child was detected at distance then "disappeared" as the vehicle approached. Likely cause per IIHS: child dummy height + tall front ends + sensors near the rearview mirror.
- **Nighttime:** poor/marginal systems failed to slow enough in most nighttime adult trials; some performed substantially worse on low beams than high beams.
- **F-350 specifics (coverage):** avoided a passenger-car target only at 31 mph; in four higher-speed tests failed; in one 43-mph test failed to reduce speed at all (Autoblog). Transit pedestrian warnings came only 0.6–1.6 seconds before impact (Ford Authority).
- **Silverado 3500HD:** failed to avoid the passenger car target in all six V2V tests (Autoblog).
- **Context:** program launched June 2026 with driver-protection info (airbags, belts — standards that often don't apply to commercial vehicles); IIHS will keep evaluating Class 3 through this year and next, adding headlights and rear AEB; Class 4-6 trucks phased in later. Tied to IIHS 30x30 vision (30% fewer fatalities by 2030).
- **Quote (IIHS President David Harkey):** "These results are a wake-up call for commercial vehicle makers." — keep to this short excerpt only.

## Original contribution (Crash Report cross-tabs)
1. **EV sweep:** 2 of 2 electric vans tested (BrightDrop 400, Rivian RCV-Delivery 500) earned Good pedestrian ratings. 0 of 9 combustion vehicles did (3 Acceptable, 4 Marginal, 2 Poor). Framing: the two best vehicles at not running over pedestrians are both electric delivery vans — the newest designs in the group. Note the caveat: n=2 EVs is small, and both are clean-sheet designs; correlation with EV-ness vs. design recency is unproven.
2. **Exposure math:** 6,215 deaths = 16% of all US road fatalities involve these vehicle classes. Per IIHS's passenger-vehicle comparison context, 2026-model passenger cars need Good V2V for Top Safety Pick+ — while 7 of 11 commercial vehicles rate Poor V2V. The vehicles most dangerous to others have the weakest avoidance tech.
3. **The motorcycle gap:** passenger-vehicle TSP+ V2V criteria include motorcycle targets (per IIHS 2026 awards tightening). Commercial vehicles' motorcycle blindness stands out against the passenger standard many of these same automakers already meet.

## Limitations
- Only 11 vehicles tested; fleet market is wider (box trucks, Class 4-6 coming later).
- Ratings test avoidance systems, not crashworthiness; a Poor-avoidance truck may still protect its own driver well (the June driver-protection round addressed that separately).
- EV-vs-combustion cross-tab is n=2 — suggestive, not conclusive.
- Test-dummy scenarios are standardized; real-world performance varies with maintenance, loading, weather.

## Strongest counterargument
Commercial vehicles operate in harder conditions (heavier, taller, loaded weight varies), sensor packaging is genuinely harder, and these ratings are brand-new — manufacturers haven't had rating-driven years to improve, unlike passenger cars with 20+ years of IIHS pressure. The Good performers (Sprinter, BrightDrop, Rivian) prove the bar is reachable now, but judging a first-ever round like a mature one overstates the failure.

## Actionable insights
- Fleet buyers: the Sprinter 2500 is the only Good V2V pick; for pedestrian-dense routes (residential delivery), BrightDrop 400 or Rivian RCV-Delivery 500. Avoid spec'ing Express/Savana 2500 — no AEB at all.
- Consumers: the Amazon van (Rivian RCV platform) has better pedestrian tech than your plumber's F-350.
- Motorcyclists/cyclists/pedestrians: assume work trucks and vans cannot see you — especially motorcycles; several systems never warned or braked at all.
- Check VIN/ratings at iihs.org/ratings before buying a work vehicle.
