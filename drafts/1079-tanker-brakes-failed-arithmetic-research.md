# Research Notes — #1079: Mack/Volvo Tanker Brake-Software Recall

**Slug:** 1079-tanker-brakes-failed-arithmetic
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone
**Number:** 1079
**Kicker:** Investigation
**Angle:** The tanker application package calculated axle loads wrong, making braking worse. The remedy is a software update that turns the dynamic calculation OFF. The fix for the computer that couldn't do math is less computer.

## Facts

- **Mack recall:** 850 tractors, 2026–2027 Anthem AN (4) and 2026–2027 Pioneer PR (4) equipped with the tanker application package. Inaccurate axle load calculations may cause reduced braking performance while transporting smoothbore tanker trailers. Longer stopping distances, increased crash risk. (TheTrucker.com, Oct 5, 2026; The Brake Report)
- **Volvo recall:** 392 tractors, 2025–2027 VNL (4) and 2026–2027 VNR (4) with the tanker application package. Same defect, same failure mode. (The Brake Report)
- **Campaign numbers:** NHTSA 26V620 (Mack, mfr SC0500), NHTSA 26V619 (Volvo, mfr RVXX2610).
- **Remedy:** Dealers install new software that DISABLES the dynamic trailer braking calculations, free of charge. (Both brands.)
- **Owner notification:** letters expected mailed November 27, 2026. VINs searchable on NHTSA.gov starting October 5, 2026.
- **Customer service:** Mack 1-800-866-1177; Volvo Trucks 800-528-6586.
- **Same-week sibling recall:** Mack also recalled 3,096 trucks (2026–2027 Anthem AN (4), 2025–2027 Pioneer PR (4)) because intake and grille bezels may detach and become road hazards. NHTSA 26V631, Mack SC0501. Letters Nov 30, 2026. (Truck Parts & Service; ConsumerAffairs; TheTrucker)
- **Week total:** 850 + 392 + 3,096 = 4,338 trucks recalled across Mack and Volvo this week. The Brake-software trucks are 1,242 of the 4,338 (28.6%).
- **May 2026 precedent:** Volvo recalled 2,800 VNL/VNR (26V260, RVXX2605) and Mack recalled 2,337 Pioneer/Anthem (26V261, SC0494) for rear brake modulator electrical connectors that could short-circuit and kill ABS + electronic stability control — same model lines. Letters mailed June 22, 2026. (Overdrive; Truckers News)
- **Corporate connection:** Mack Trucks is owned by Volvo Group (Volvo Trucks North America) — the two "different" brake recalls this week are one defect in one shared software package, sold under two badges. (Public corporate fact.)

## Original calculations (verifiable)

- 850 + 392 = 1,242 brake-software trucks; + 3,096 bezel trucks = 4,338 total this week. Brake trucks = 28.6% of the week's recalls.
- May 2026 brake-modulator recalls: 2,800 + 2,337 = 5,137 trucks. Combined with this week's 1,242 brake-software trucks: **6,379 trucks recalled for braking defects across Volvo Group brands in 2026** — all on the same four model lines (Anthem/Pioneer/VNL/VNR). Some VINs likely appear in both campaigns.
- Ratio of this week's bezel recall to brake recall: 3,096 : 1,242 ≈ 2.5 : 1. The cosmetic-adjacent defect (falling grilles) outnumbered the braking defect 2.5-to-1 in the same week.

## Kill test

- Genuinely newsworthy: yes — reduced braking performance on 80,000-lb tanker trucks (smoothbore = liquid cargo that sloshes; these are the trailers where accurate axle-load math matters most).
- Novel angle: the remedy is deletion of the feature, not repair of it. The industry's standard "update the software" became "turn off the software." Plus the May 2026 ABS/ESC recalls on the same model lines make this a pattern: Volvo Group's braking systems have been recalled twice in five months.

## Counterargument (stated at full strength)

- Disabling the dynamic calculation is not a lobotomy — it is the correct engineering response. If the dynamic trailer braking calculation was producing WORSE braking than the standard trailer braking baseline, then switching it off makes every affected truck SAFER than it was yesterday. Mocking the fix as "dumber" ignores that the alternative — leaving known-bad math in the loop on loaded tankers — is strictly worse. The trucks revert to the industry-standard braking behavior every other truck uses. Nobody is losing a capability that worked; they are losing a capability that was actively hurting them.

## Limitations

- The recall summaries do not quantify HOW wrong the axle-load calculations were — no published figure for stopping-distance increase, no deceleration data, no test results. "Reduced braking performance" is the full extent of the public technical description.
- No incidents, crashes, injuries, or warranty claims are cited in the published summaries as of this writing; the defect was caught before the body count, or at least before anyone counted publicly.
- We do not know whether the same VINs appear in both the May brake-modulator recalls and this week's brake-software recalls (same model lines, same brands — plausible overlap, unverified).
- Smoothbore tanker context: smoothbore trailers lack internal baffles, so liquid cargo surges more. The summaries specify smoothbore trailers; we infer the calculation error is specific to that trailer type, but the technical mechanism is not public.

## Actionable takeaways

- Fleet operators and owner-operators running 2026–2027 Mack Anthem/Pioneer or 2025–2027 Volvo VNL/VNR tractors with the tanker application package: check VINs at nhtsa.gov/recalls (searchable from Oct 5, 2026). Letters mail Nov 27. Remedy is free: Mack 1-800-866-1177 (ref SC0500), Volvo 800-528-6586 (ref RVXX2610).
- Same trucks without the tanker package are not affected by this campaign — but check the May 2026 brake-modulator recalls (26V260/26V261) too, since they covered the same model lines.
- General lesson: when a recall remedy is "we turned the feature off," ask what the feature was doing before it was turned off — and how long it was doing it.
