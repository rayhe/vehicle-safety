The Corvette Is the Drunkest Car in America. FARS Has the Receipts.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
September 28, 2026

Here's a fun fact that will ruin your morning commute. Of 168 vehicle nameplates with enough fatal crashes to measure, the Chevrolet Corvette has the highest alcohol-positive rate of any car in America: 21.3%. Two hundred forty-four of the 1,147 Corvette drivers in fatal crashes between 2014 and 2023 had alcohol in their system. More than one in four had alcohol or drugs.[1]

21.3%
of Corvette drivers in fatal crashes tested alcohol-positive, vs. 15.1% nationally

This is not a rounding artifact. Second place belongs to the Cadillac CTS at 20.6%, then the Infiniti G37 at 19.6%. The Corvette beat the entire field, and it beat its own family: the Camaro sits at 17.1%, the Challenger at 17.5%, the Mustang at 16.4%. Chevrolet builds the fastest American car and, apparently, the one most likely to be piloted by someone who should not have been behind the wheel.[1]

Reality keeps confirming the spreadsheet. In February, a speeding Corvette in Newport Beach struck and killed 21-year-old Steven Alcaraz, who was celebrating his birthday; the driver fled on foot and was arrested for DUI.[3] In Las Vegas, Henry Ruggs drove his Corvette at a documented 156 mph with a blood alcohol level of 0.161, twice the legal limit, and killed Tina Tintor. He pleaded guilty and got three to ten years.[4] Apparently the universe has a type. It is a two-seater with a small-block V8.

A note on what the numbers don't claim: "alcohol-positive" means BAC above zero, not necessarily above the 0.08 legal limit, and FARS only records fatal crashes, so this is the drunkest slice of the drunkest dataset, not a survey of Corvette owners at large.[1] And there's a real causal tangle here, stated at full strength: a Corvette crash is more likely to be fatal in the first place, because speed, and nighttime high-speed driving correlates with drinking, so part of that 21.3% may measure the physics of the crash rather than the sobriety of the owner. Testing rates vary by state too, but all true, and still #1 of 168.

If you are shopping for a used performance car, read the toxicology table like a menu: these machines concentrate risk-taking drivers, so budget for a driving school alongside the tires. If you are the friend at the bar watching the Corvette keys come out, be the friend who takes them. And check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before your next oil change. The recall database can't fix the driver, but it's the part of this equation you actually control.[5]
