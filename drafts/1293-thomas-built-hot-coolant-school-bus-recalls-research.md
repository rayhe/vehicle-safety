# Research: #1293 — Thomas Built's school buses keep leaking hot coolant into the cabin

**Slug:** 1293-thomas-built-hot-coolant-school-bus-recalls
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — safety tech / design beat)
**Kicker:** Trend Watch
**Date researched:** 2026-10-27

## Kill test
PASS. In October 2026, NHTSA campaign 26V697 and Transport Canada recall
2026513 (Oct 21, 2026) expanded an earlier heater-hose recall for 2027
Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 school buses: a hose that can rupture and dump
hot coolant inside the passenger compartment, where children sit. That
expansion is the third wave of Thomas Built heater-hose coolant-in-cabin
recalls in 2026 alone: February brought heater-hose clamp recalls (2,843 +
52 buses) for hoses that disconnect and leak the same hot coolant into the
same passenger compartment, and April brought the original rupture recall
(26V109/F1031) for 2027 C2 buses built over a five-day window in December
2025. Two mechanisms, one consequence, one year, and the October expansion
means the first sweep missed buses. Killed angles this run: IIHS Oct 8
commercial-vehicle ratings (covered #1110, #1121, and others), IIHS Oct 24
Kicks/Q3 round (covered #1278), Ford F-Series cluster recall 26V653
(covered #1223), Hyundai 26V692/26V693 (covered #1291), Cummins L9 fuel-hose
26V699 Autocar follow-on (parent equipment recall 26E066 covered #1046),
Ram 1500 TPMS recall (TPMS covered #855, #911, #1010, #1107), Toyota Land
Cruiser fuel leak (covered #1230, #1248). Queue/drafts grep for
thomas/saf-t-liner/heater-hose: zero prior coverage.

## Verified facts (3+ primary/primary-adjacent sources)
- **October 2026 peg — NHTSA 26V697:** Daimler Trucks North America,
  2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2, "Heater Hose May Rupture and Leak."
  (ConsumerAffairs weekly NHTSA roundup, week of October 26, 2026.)
- **October 2026 peg — Transport Canada 2026513 (Oct 21, 2026,
  RA-82856):** certain 2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 school buses made
  by DTNA; heater hoses "may not have been made properly" and could fail
  and leak coolant into the bus; "A coolant leak could create the risk of
  a burn injury." This recall EXPANDS an earlier one for the same issue,
  Transport Canada recall no. 2026-083. DTNA notifies owners by mail;
  buses go to an authorized service facility for hose inspection and
  replacement if needed. The Canadian notice does not state how many
  buses are affected in Canada; it describes "a small number."
  (Transport Canada via Canadian Press syndication, Oct 21-24, 2026.)
- **April 2026 — NHTSA 26V-109 / DTNA F1031 (school bus), owner letter
  April 2026:** certain 2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 vehicles built
  December 8, 2025 to December 12, 2025 — a five-day production window.
  "Due to a manufacturing error, the heater hose may rupture and leak
  without prior warning. The heater hose can release hot coolant within
  the passenger area, which may increase the risk of burn injury to
  passengers." Remedy: DTNA authorized service facility inspects the
  date code of the heater hoses and replaces as needed; 1 to 8 hours;
  free of charge. (DTNA owner notification letter, NHTSA file
  RCONL-26V109.)
- **February 2026 — heater-hose clamp recalls (different mechanism,
  same consequence):** DTNA recalled certain 2023-2026 Saf-T-Liner EFX
  and HDX school buses (2,843 units) and certain 2023-2025 EFX /
  2023-2026 HDX buses (52 units): heater hose clamps "may not have been
  tightened properly, causing the hose to disconnect and leak heating
  coolant inside the passenger compartment. Contact with hot heating
  coolant may burn occupants." (Trucks, Parts, Service, NHTSA commercial
  vehicle recalls, week of Feb 23, 2026.)
- **February 2026 detail — NHTSA 26V087 / DTNA F1030 (the 52-unit leg):**
  report received February 13, 2026; 2023-2025 Saf-T-Liner EFX and
  2023-2026 Saf-T-Liner HDX; same clamp defect and burn consequence;
  remedy was under development at filing; owner letters expected April
  14, 2026; DTNA customer service 1-800-547-0712. (NHTSA acknowledgment
  letter, Feb 19, 2026.)
- **2016 precedent (one line of context only):** DTNA campaigns FL708 /
  FL709, NHTSA 16V-323/324/325/326: Thomas Built HDX heater hoses could
  fail and leak hot coolant inside the bus. The coolant-in-cabin failure
  mode is a decade old at this company.
- **Arithmetic:** February clamp legs total 2,843 + 52 = 2,895 buses.
  The April rupture population (five-day Dec 2025 build window) and the
  October expansion population are NOT stated in the sources found for
  this article; do not invent counts for 26V109, 26V697, or 2026513.

## Original contribution
1. **The three-wave census:** February (clamps, 2,895 buses), April
   (rupture, five-day build window), October (expansion of the rupture
   recall). Three separate NHTSA/Transport Canada actions in one
   calendar year, all ending with hot coolant where children sit.
2. **Two mechanisms, one consequence:** a clamp that was never
   tightened right and a hose that was never manufactured right both
   end the same way — pressurized hot coolant released inside the
   passenger compartment, with no warning in the rupture case ("without
   prior warning" is DTNA's own wording).
3. **The expansion is the story:** an expansion recall means the first
   sweep's population was wrong. The April letter's five-day build
   window looked surgically precise; by October, both US and Canadian
   regulators were widening it. Precision in a recall population is a
   claim, and this one did not hold for six months.

## Angles / what to verify in draft
- Mia's engineering voice: what a heater hose does (engine coolant
  routed into the bus body to heat the cabin), why a rupture inside the
  cabin is worse than one under the hood (passengers sit next to the
  routing, coolant runs near 200 degrees Fahrenheit in a pressurized
  system — do NOT state a precise temperature unless sourced; describe
  it as hot, pressurized engine coolant), and why a date-code inspection
  is the remedy (the defect is batch-specific, so the fix is identifying
  which hoses came from the bad batch).
- The school-bus safety paradox, stated fairly: buses are the safest
  way for children to travel, and none of these recalls carries a burn
  injury count in the sources found. The risk is narrow and the
  populations are small. The story is the repetition, not a body count.

## Limitations (for the article)
- No US population count for 26V697 and no Canadian count for 2026513
  appears in the sources found; the article states this gap rather
  than filling it.
- No burn injury, crash, or fatality count is attributed to any of
  these heater-hose recalls in the sources found; absence of a count is
  not evidence that none occurred.
- The 2,843-unit February campaign number is cited from the trade
  roundup; its NHTSA campaign number was not verified for this article
  and is not stated.
- The relationship between US 26V697 and Canadian 2026513 (parallel
  expansions of the same underlying recall) is inferred from matching
  model, model year, defect, and timing; the article says the two
  regulators acted on the same issue in the same week, not that one
  filing caused the other.

## Strongest counterargument
School buses are, by every measure, the safest vehicles children ride
in, and these are small, batch-specific populations with a remedy that
takes hours and costs nothing. No burns are reported in the sources
found. A district that pulls buses off routes over a hose recall would
be trading a small, known, fixable risk for the much larger risk of
children walking or riding in cars. The honest headline is narrower
than "school buses are dangerous": one manufacturer has now needed
three attempts in one year to contain one failure mode, and the third
attempt exists because the first two did not finish the job.

## Actionable takeaways (required)
- Fleet operators and school districts running 2027 Saf-T-Liner C2
  buses: check VINs against NHTSA campaigns 26V109/F1031 and 26V697,
  and against Transport Canada 2026513/2026-083 in Canada; call DTNA
  at 1-800-547-0712. Do not assume an April inspection clears a bus
  covered by the October expansion.
- Districts running 2023-2026 EFX/HDX buses: check the February clamp
  recalls (26V087/F1030 and the 2,843-unit companion campaign).
- Drivers: a sweet smell, steam, or wetness inside the cabin is a
  stop-and-evacuate signal, not a drive-to-the-depot signal; hot
  coolant burns on contact.
- Parents: ask your district whether its Thomas Built buses have
  cleared all three 2026 heater-hose actions, not just "the recall."
