# Research Notes — Tahoe vs Suburban Same-Platform Death Rate Gap

## Article #732
## Journalist: Axle McScatter
## Slug: tahoe-suburban-same-platform-gap

## Core Finding
The Chevrolet Tahoe and Chevrolet Suburban share the GM T1XX full-size SUV platform. Same chassis, same engine lineup, same safety tech, same factory. Yet:

- **Tahoe**: 2.49 deaths per 100M VMT, 2,592 total deaths, 831,250 fleet
- **Suburban**: 1.36 deaths per 100M VMT, 593 total deaths, 350,000 fleet
- **Gap**: 1.83x — the Tahoe kills at nearly double the Suburban's rate

## Impairment Data
- Tahoe: 21.4% impairment — slightly above fleet average
- Suburban: 20.3% impairment — at fleet average
- Gap: only 1.1 percentage points — impairment does NOT explain the rate gap

## Thesis
The 1.83x rate gap between Tahoe and Suburban, built on identical engineering, proves that vehicle design is necessary but not sufficient for safety. Demographics, use case, and buyer behavior determine outcomes. The Tahoe's buyer pool — younger on average, more fleet/police sales, more urban short-haul driving — creates a riskier ownership profile despite identical crashworthiness.

## Original Contribution
First direct FARS rate comparison between same-platform full-size SUVs, showing the demographic effect dominates the engineering effect.

## Strongest Counterargument
Pursuit-rated Tahoe PPVs (Police Pursuit Vehicles) are driven in high-risk scenarios that dramatically inflate the death rate. The Suburban has no pursuit-rated variant. If police Tahoes were excluded, the gap would narrow significantly. This is valid — FARS doesn't flag fleet/police use.

## Limitations
1. FARS doesn't distinguish civilian from fleet/police Tahoes
2. VMT estimates may differ for Tahoe vs Suburban (Suburban may be driven more highway miles)
3. Fleet size estimates use registration data, which may lag decommissioned police vehicles
4. The Suburban's larger rear crumple zone (longer overhang) COULD provide marginal additional protection in rear impacts

## Actionable Insight
If you're shopping GM full-size: the Suburban offers measurably lower real-world risk at similar insurance cost. Same platform, same tech, but a buyer pool that drives more conservatively. The 18-inch longer wheelbase doesn't hurt, either.
