Altec Recalled the Same Bucket Trucks Twice in Seven Days. Both Times, Something Was Loose.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 20, 2026

Altec Industries recalled 3,083 of its aerial devices on November 6 because the hardware holding the jib tilt cylinder pin in place may never have been tightened.[1] Seven days later it recalled 1,100 more because a valve handle was assembled without thread-locking compound.[2] Both campaigns name the same trucks, 2025 and 2026 AM and AN Series bucket trucks, the machines that lift lineworkers into the air above live power lines.

4,183
Bucket trucks recalled by Altec in November 2026, across two campaigns filed seven days apart.

Start with what each defect does, because the two failure modes rhyme in a way that should make a fleet manager sit up. In the first, loose retention hardware can fall out and release the cylinder pin, letting the jib swing freely while the winch line drops up to 10 feet. In the second, a handle at the lower controls can loosen and separate, letting the valve eject hot oil. One defect drops things from height while the other sprays hot oil at the person standing at the base of the machine. Neither requires a crash to hurt someone, which is the whole point of a bucket truck recall: the workplace is the road, and the road is 60 feet up.

Now the pattern, because a single recall is a defect and a pair is a story. This is the second time in five weeks that Altec has recalled the same product family twice in seven days. In October it was a badly welded access step (6,780 trucks) and boom-stow wiring that could let a driver pull away with the boom still up (143 trucks).[5] November brings a different symmetry: both new defects are the same failure class, hardware that was not tightened. Thread-locking compound is, to put it plainly, glue for threads, and its absence from an assembly line is not a design mystery but a process step someone skipped.

Zoom out and the census gets worse. NHTSA's recalls database returns eight campaigns touching the 2025 AM Series aerial device between September 2025 and November 2026, roughly one every seven weeks for fourteen months, spanning exhaust placement, emergency-stop wiring, steps, fasteners, and valves.[2] That is not eight defects on one specific truck, since several campaigns span multiple models and years, but it is eight times the federal record has had something to say about this product family in just over a year, and the last two times it spoke, it said the same thing twice in one week.

Fairness requires the other side, and the other side is not weak. Altec found both defects itself, filed both campaigns as manufacturer-initiated recalls with no NHTSA investigation cited, and both remedies are free and simple: inspect and tighten the hardware, check a valve lot number and replace the valve if it matches. No injuries, crashes, or deaths are reported in either public record. Four thousand trucks is a rounding error against the millions of vehicles recalled in a typical year, so a reasonable reader could call this the recall system working exactly as designed, a manufacturer catching assembly escapes before anyone gets hurt.

Reasonable, and incomplete, because a system that catches its own mistakes is good while a system that keeps making the same class of mistake, on the same trucks, in pairs, five weeks apart, is a system with a process problem wearing a detection success as a disguise. Tightening is the cheapest possible fix in manufacturing. When it fails twice in a week, the question is no longer whether the bolt was tight. It is who, on the line, was supposed to check.

What to do: if your fleet runs 2025 or 2026 Altec AM, AN, or AA Series aerial devices, check both campaigns, 26V738 and 26V752, by VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A truck can plausibly sit in both populations, and the remedies are different inspections, so one check does not cover the other.[4] Do not wait for the owner letters, expected January 4 and January 8, 2027; the 26V738 remedy is an inspection and a tightening, and Altec will provide the instructions free. And if a manufacturer files a second recall on the same model family within a week of the first, audit the whole truck, not just the named part. The second filing is the signal, and the first one was the warning.
