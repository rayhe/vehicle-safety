Ram Recalled 263,845 Trucks in Five Weeks. Every Failure Was Silent

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
November 4, 2026

Stellantis recalled 263,845 Ram pickups in five weeks, and not one of the three defects announces itself. Back up, and the camera shows nothing; check the tires, and the gauge stays quiet; slide, and the stability control has already left. Nobody crashed, nobody got hurt, and that is precisely the problem: when safety equipment fails by saying nothing, the driver keeps driving as if it were still there.

263,845
Ram pickups recalled across three campaigns in five weeks, with zero reported crashes or injuries

Start with the big one, because it is the strangest. Under NHTSA campaign 26V560, Chrysler recalled 239,131 model-year 2025 and 2026 Ram 1500 pickups, built October 6, 2023 through February 23, 2026, after a radio software error learned to swallow the rearview camera image.[1] Not a broken camera, not a cut wire, not a cracked lens: the entertainment system ate a federally required safety display, and the truck answered with a blank screen where the view behind the bumper should have been, failing Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111 on rear visibility.[1] Fix is free, over the air or by a dealer; letters went out September 24, 2026.

Next comes the quiet gauge. Under campaign 26V623, Chrysler recalled 16,385 model-year 2026 Ram 1500 pickups because their tire pressure sensors, supplied by Sensata Technologies, may fail to detect low pressure and may also fail to light the dashboard warning lamp, a two-part failure that violates FMVSS 138.[2] A tire going soft on one of these trucks produces no light, no chime, no message, just a clean dashboard and a driver concluding everything is fine. Suspect trucks were built July 1, 2025 through March 17, 2026, a window opened by a tire-and-wheel assembly process change, and the recall summary estimates 100 percent of the recalled population carries the suspect sensors.[2] Owner letters went out October 27, 2026.

Finally, the heavy-duty entry. Under campaign 26V651, Chrysler recalled 8,329 model-year 2026 Ram 2500 trucks because a defective steering column control module can cause a sudden loss of Electronic Stability Control, violating FMVSS 126.[3] Stability control catches a slide before the driver finishes noticing it, so losing it without warning on an 8,000-pound truck is a handling change discovered mid-emergency. Dealers replace the module free, with owner letters scheduled by November 24, 2026.[3]

Do the arithmetic, because it sharpens the story. Add the three populations and you get 263,845, split 90.6 percent camera, 6.2 percent tire sensors, 3.2 percent stability control, and every one is a federal-standards noncompliance case, which means Ram shipped three trucks that each failed a different federal safety standard inside the same five-week window.[1] Precedent makes the camera recall sting twice: Stellantis recalled roughly 273,000 Rams in October 2024 because their rearview cameras failed to show an image, so the 2026 recall, at 88 percent of that size, is the second time in two years the view behind a Ram went dark, with a different root cause wearing the same symptom.[5]

Fairness demands the other side get its full hearing. Three recalls in five weeks is not automatically a pattern: suppliers differ, systems differ, and a company selling hundreds of thousands of trucks generates more recall paperwork than a boutique brand, while the response here looks competent, with the camera fix shipping over the air, a clean March-to-September tire-sensor investigation, and zero crashes attached to any campaign.[3] All true, yet the shared shape is hard to unsee: in all three cases the thing that broke was an electronic intermediary standing between driver and reality, and intermediaries fail the way software fails, silently, completely, without leaving a mark on the dashboard.

What this article did not prove matters too. Crash and injury counts are reported-to-date figures, not a guarantee none occurred, and the 239,130 versus 239,131 unit discrepancy is unresolved at the unit level, though 239,131 reconciles exactly with the published 263,845 total. No FARS angle exists for 2025 and 2026 trucks yet, so this is a recall-architecture story, not a body-count story. So check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls today instead of waiting for the letter, especially for the stability-control module, whose letters run to November 24. If your truck took the over-the-air radio update, shift into reverse and confirm the camera image actually appears before trusting it behind a child in a parking lot. Until the tire sensors are replaced, check pressures monthly with a handheld gauge, because the dash cannot be trusted to tell you, and if a stability-control warning appears or handling feels wrong in a slide, treat it as a real loss of the system and get the module replaced before winter roads.
