# Research: Great Dane landing-gear collapse petition (DP26012) + weld/coupler pattern

## Story angle
NHTSA logged defect petition DP26012 on **October 23, 2026** — five days ago — asking the agency to
evaluate **landing gear collapse on 2017 Great Dane refrigerated trailers with Jost landing gear**.
A defect petition is a citizen request that NHTSA open a defect investigation; ODI now has ~120 days
to grant or deny it. This is the FOURTH Great Dane structural-integrity action in ~22 months:

1. **24V-922** (Dec 11, 2024) — 225 units, 2025 Champion dry freight trailers, **missing kingpin
   welds**; trailer may detach. Letters mailed Dec 19, 2024. (Justia)
2. **24V-637** (Dec 6, 2024) — 1,757 units, 2025 Everest SS refrigerated trailers, **insufficient
   welds on support gear bracing mounts**; could lead to trailer collapsing. Built Jan 2–Oct 2, 2024
   at the Wayne, Nebraska plant. Great Dane estimated 5% with defect. (Part 573 report via oemdtc)
3. **26V-656** (report received Oct 6, 2026) — 69 units, 2027 Champion dry van trailers, **upper
   coupler incorrect bottom plate, kingpin may protrude**; do-not-use advisory. Letters expected
   Dec 4, 2026. (automotor.news, The Trucker Oct 26, 2026)
4. **DP26012** (opened Oct 23, 2026) — defect petition: landing gear collapse, 2017 Great Dane
   refrigerated trailers, **Jost landing gear** named. (NHTSA ODI via oemdtc investigations list)

Transport Canada 2024663 (Nov 1, 2024) mirrors 24V-637: support gear bracing mounts improperly
welded on refrigerated trailers; trailer may collapse **when parked**; guidance: do not use the
trailer without a tractor attached or without safety stands until repaired.

## Why newsworthy / novel
- No pipeline article on landing-gear collapse; no pipeline article on the Everest SS weld recall.
  (#1298 covered only the Champion coupler recall, with a different pattern angle: second coupler
  recall in 22 months.)
- The petition names a **supplier component** (Jost landing gear) — failure may sit in the supply
  chain, not just the builder's welds.
- Structural failures now span THREE load paths on one brand: coupling (detach), support-gear
  bracing (collapse), landing gear (collapse). Two involve welds at the same Wayne NE plant.
- Physical stakes: a semi-trailer's landing gear is the pair of crank-down legs holding tens of
  thousands of pounds of loaded trailer off the ground during drop-and-hook. Collapse = the front
  of the trailer slams down on whoever is working under it.
- Trailers are federally regulated as vehicles but get a fraction of tractor scrutiny.

## Kill test: PASS
Genuinely newsworthy (fresh federal petition, five days old) and a novel pattern no outlet has
drawn: four structural actions against one trailer maker in 22 months, now touching the legs the
trailer stands on. Not a duplicate of #1298 (coupler recall, different event) or #749 (Ford trailer
module race condition).

## What the data does NOT show (limitations)
- DP26012 is a petition review, not a granted investigation. ODI may deny it.
- No public complaint/crash/injury counts are attached to DP26012 yet.
- The 2017 model-year scope suggests age, fatigue, and maintenance history could explain
  individual collapses — not necessarily a manufacturing defect.
- Great Dane is one of North America's largest trailer builders; four actions may partly reflect
  fleet volume. Two of the four were company-initiated recalls (the system working).

## Strongest counterargument
The welding QC problems were all at one plant (Wayne, NE), were self-reported, and were
recalled — that is the regulatory system functioning. A 2017 reefer is nine model years old;
landing gear collapses on a decade-old trailer point more plausibly at maintenance and duty
cycle than at a design defect. And a defect petition is cheap to file; ODI denies most.
