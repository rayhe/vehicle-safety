Nissan Recalled Its New Cars 7 Times. The Kicks Carried Three of Them.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 12, 2026

Same method as the Ford, Stellantis, GM, Toyota, Hyundai, Honda, and Kia censuses before it: every canonical model name in NHTSA's database, every hybrid and plug-in variant, every spelling the API might hide a campaign under. 64 queries. Two makes. Nissan plus Infiniti recalled their 2026 models 7 times, the quietest count in the entire series, and one subcompact SUV carries three of the seven. Kicks 3, Leaf 2, Sentra 1, Frontier 1, Rogue 1, Armada 1, QX80 1. Altima, Ariya, Murano, Pathfinder, Z, and QX60: zero. Add the brands up and you get 8, because the biggest campaign crosses makes, and the company total after dedupe is 7.[1]

7
Recall campaigns filed in 2026 touching Nissan's and Infiniti's 2026-model-year vehicles, deduped across two makes. One campaign covers 168,149 vehicles; another covers 15 cars

Start with the Kicks, because the engineering story there is genuinely interesting. Three campaigns, three different suppliers, three narrow production windows. Campaign 26V331, filed May 22, covers 51,598 Kicks whose combination-meter software can blank the instrument cluster on a cold start, which means no speedometer, no warning lights, no telltales at all, on a screen that is the entire dashboard. Nissan estimates 0.2 percent of them actually have the defect, and it took 205 warranty claims and seven technical reports to get there. Campaign 26V023, filed January 21, covers 26,432 vehicles whose door striker wire loops were improperly welded by Magna Winmex, a defect Nissan estimates at 1 percent, discovered when a technician noticed a striker wire separated during a routine door fit check. Campaign 26V145, filed March 13, covers about 15 cars, five Sentras and ten Kicks built across two weeks in December, whose front seat frames were insufficiently laser-welded by TACHI-S in Mexico. Fifteen cars. Free replacement seat assemblies, all of them. Nissan caught all three early and recalled narrowly, which is the part of this story that deserves credit.[2][3][4]

Then there is the Leaf, which carries the other two campaigns and the only fire in the count. Campaign 26V188 covers 51 Leafs, and Nissan estimates 100 percent of them are defective: damaged cathode edges in the 78-kilowatt-hour battery can fold over inside the cell, short-circuit the module, and start a thermal event. Two have already had one, one in Japan in February and one in the United States in March, both while parked. Owners were told to park outside, away from structures, and not to charge. Fifty-one cars is the smallest population in this census and the only one where the defect can burn a garage down. Campaign 26V425, filed July 2, covers 3,785 Leafs whose rear outboard seat belt retractors may deactivate early, which can keep a child restraint from securing properly, a quieter failure with a worse failure mode if it ever matters.[5][6]

By population, the biggest campaign is the least dramatic one. Campaign 26V455, filed July 16, covers 168,149 Armadas, QX56s, and QX80s with 22-inch wheels, spanning model years 2011 through 2027, because the certification label prints the wrong gross axle weight rating. Nobody's axle snapped. No mechanical component failed. Remedy is a corrected sticker, mailed to the owner or installed free, and it matters because a driver loading a truck to its printed rating could overload it without knowing. Campaign 26V419, filed July 1, is the one I would actually lose sleep over: 5,823 Rogues built in a five-week window whose inhibitor switch can fail inside the transmission control valve, wiping the gear indicator off the instrument panel and potentially leaving the driver unable to shift out of Neutral. Nissan logged 106 warranty claims and 48 technical reports in under three months. Rollaway risk, 59 percent defect estimate, no crash reported yet.[7][8]

Now the honest accounting, because 7 flatters Nissan and undersells it at the same time. It flatters, because the 2026 lineup is thin: eleven models, with Titan, Versa, and GT-R gone, so there are fewer nameplates to recall, and a count partly measures a shrinking catalog, not improving quality. It undersells, because the method has a hole the Kicks exposed: my first pass printed 6, not 7, since the recalls API returns an error for the VPIC name "Kicks MPV" and only answers to "Kicks", hiding the entire instrument-cluster campaign behind a spelling difference, the same vocabulary trap that hid the ProMaster in the Stellantis census. And the census excludes the two campaigns that matter to the most Nissan owners, because they cover older cars: roughly 324,000 Rogues recalled in 2026 for VC-Turbo engine bearing failure, and roughly 319,000 more for a fracturing throttle body gear, about 642,000 older Rogues in the same year this census counts 255,853 vehicles across all seven campaigns. Both numbers are true. Neither means much without the other.[1][9][10]

What to do with this: if you own a 2025-2026 Kicks, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 26V331, 26V023, and 26V145. Dashboard fix is a free 30-minute software update, and do not drive with a blank cluster, since you cannot see your speed or your warning lights. If you own a 2026 Leaf, check for 26V188 and 26V425; if yours is one of the 51, park outside and do not charge until the battery modules are replaced, and use the LATCH system for child restraints until the retractors are fixed. If you own a 2026 Rogue, check for 26V419, and if the gear indicator ever vanishes, do not assume the car is in Park because the lever says so. If you own an Armada or QX80 with 22-inch wheels, check for 26V455 and read the label before loading the truck to its rated capacity. If you own a 2023-2025 Rogue with the 1.5-liter VC-Turbo engine, check for 26V080 and 26V081, the two campaigns this census excludes. And if you run a recall census of your own, never trust a VPIC name to match the recalls API, and never trust a 400 error to mean zero. A 400 is not always a zero, and a make is not always a company.
