## Key numbers (all from source 1 unless noted)
- 49% of drivers agree: "It's safer for me to drive with the flow of traffic than obey the speed limit" (moral-justification item)
- 49% agree: "Going a little bit over the speed limit isn't really 'speeding'" (euphemistic labeling)
- 44% agree: "When I do speed, I don't go so fast that I worry about dying if I get in an accident"
- 47% of drivers show moderate moral disengagement on the speeding scale
- Social threshold: ~10 mph over the posted limit viewed as acceptable and unlikely to be enforced; up to 25 mph over acceptable "in certain circumstances"
- Enforcement expected to begin 10-12 mph over, depending on roadway
- Top motivations: get around slower drivers (31%), reach destination faster (25%), avoid holding up traffic (21%)
- Five driver mindsets (latent class analysis): Strict Compliance Advocates 21%, Safety-Focused Realists 14%, Principled Pragmatists 26%, Tolerant but Non-Compliant 23%, Autonomy-First 16%
- Countermeasures: 88% support increased police presence in high-potential crash areas; nearly 3/4 support automated enforcement there; <50% support infrastructure countermeasures; ISA acceptance ranges from ~1/4 (non-overridable limiter in own car) to >1/2 (advisory), waning with invasiveness
- Reasons to slow down (national, via Insurance Journal summary): avoid a ticket 79%, avoid a crash 72%, bad weather 60%
- TSCI 2025: 58% believe they'd be caught at 15 mph over, yet ~half sped that much in the past month; only 46% support residential speed cameras

## Novel contributions (original to this article)
1. **Crash-energy arithmetic:** KE scales with v^2. At 75 in a 65 zone (+10, the "acceptable" threshold): (75/65)^2 = 1.33 → 33% more crash energy. At the 12-over enforcement threshold (77 in a 65): 40% more. At +25 (90 in a 65, "acceptable in certain circumstances"): (90/65)^2 = 1.92 → 92% more, nearly double. None of the study coverage ran this math.
2. **The de facto speed limit:** posted limit + 10-12 mph = the real, enforcement-ratified limit. On a 65-mph highway the actual legal regime is 75-77.
3. **79% have a speeding philosophy:** only 21% are Strict Compliance Advocates; the other four mindsets (79%) all contain a rationalization framework.
4. **The deterrence hierarchy:** drivers rank avoiding a ticket (79%) above avoiding a crash (72%) as reasons to slow down. Enforcement beats physics in the driver's mind — which is exactly why automated enforcement (supported by ~3/4 in high-crash areas) is the lever.
5. **Strongest counterargument (stated at full strength):** the 49% are not entirely wrong. Speed *variance* kills — the Solomon curve (1964) and subsequent research show crash risk rises with deviation from mean traffic speed, so driving 55 in a 75-mph flow is genuinely dangerous. And speed-limit credibility is real: limits set below the 85th-percentile speed invite contempt, a theme the report itself documents. The honest answer is not "obey harder" but that variance is the killer and uniform compliance (via ISA/automated enforcement) is the fix — which is also what ~3/4 of drivers already support at high-crash locations.

## Limitations
- Survey self-report: attitudes, not observed behavior; social-desirability bias cuts both ways (underreported speeding, overreported virtue).
- The moral-disengagement scale is bespoke to this study (adapted from Swann et al. 2017), not a validated clinical instrument.
- No crash-outcome linkage: the study does not show that the 49% crash more.
- Focus groups were small and qualitative; quotes illustrate, they don't generalize.
- NHTSA's "~29% of fatalities speeding-related" is a contributing-factor coding from FARS, subject to officer judgment at the scene.

## Actionable insights
- The tolerated band is real (10-12 over), but physics doesn't grade on a curve: 10 over on the highway = one-third more crash energy. Drive 9-over if you must surf the band; know the price.
- "Flow of traffic" is not a legal defense and not a physics defense. If traffic is doing 80 in a 65, the danger is the variance — back off, increase following distance, exit the pack.
- If your car offers advisory ISA or an adjustable speed limiter, turn it on; it's the countermeasure drivers themselves prefer over hard limiters.
- Where your city debates speed cameras: ~70% of drivers nationally support them at high-crash locations. That's the rare safety intervention with both evidence and public permission.
