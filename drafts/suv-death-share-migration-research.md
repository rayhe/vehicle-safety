# Research: America's Death Share Migration — Sedans to SUVs

## Angle
As America's fleet shifted from sedans to SUVs, the body count didn't drop — it migrated. FARS model year data shows SUVs now claim 40% of fatal crash deaths in 2020+ model years, up from 22% pre-2000. Sedans dropped from 54% peak to 41%. The two classes are now functionally equal in their share of road deaths.

## Primary Sources

### 1. FARS Model Year Cross-Tabulation (Original Analysis)
Deaths by vehicle class and model year era:

| Era | Sedan% | SUV% | Pickup% | Sports% | Van% |
|-----|--------|------|---------|---------|------|
| pre-2000 | 35.7 | 22.2 | 36.0 | 3.3 | 2.8 |
| 2000-04 | 39.0 | 27.5 | 26.5 | 2.6 | 4.4 |
| 2005-09 | 53.9 | 19.2 | 19.5 | 2.4 | 5.0 |
| 2010-14 | 57.0 | 20.6 | 14.7 | 3.7 | 4.0 |
| 2015-19 | 48.7 | 29.8 | 14.2 | 4.0 | 3.3 |
| 2020+ | 41.1 | 40.0 | 12.8 | 3.7 | 2.3 |

Key finding: SUV death share nearly DOUBLED from pre-2000 (22.2%) to 2020+ (40.0%). Meanwhile sedan share rose then fell, settling at 41.1% — nearly equal to SUVs.

### 2. IIHS Study: "Supersizing vehicles offers minimal safety benefits — but substantial dangers" (Feb 2025)
- Safety benefits of heavier vehicles plateau above fleet-average weight
- Adding 500 lbs to a heavier-than-average vehicle increases danger to others without improving own occupant safety
- Source: https://www.IIHS.org/news/detail/supersizing-vehicles-offers-minimal-safety-benefits--but-substantial-dangers

### 3. IIHS 2026 Award Criteria Changes
- AEB now MANDATORY for any safety award (TSP or TSP+)
- Pedestrian AEB required: good for TSP+, acceptable/good for TSP
- 63 vehicles qualified under tougher 2026 criteria (up from 48 at same point in 2025)
- Source: https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/tougher-crash-avoidance-rules-shape-2026-iihs-awards/

### 4. US Fleet Market Share Context
- Light trucks (SUVs + pickups + vans) now ~78% of US new car sales (2025-2026)
- Traditional passenger cars: ~22%
- The death share data (SUV 40% of 2020+ deaths) is BELOW their ~50% market share, suggesting per-vehicle improvement
- BUT the absolute death count didn't decline proportionally

### 5. FARS Aggregate Data
- Total deaths across all model years in dataset: 187,058
- Sedan total: 87,406 (46.7%)
- SUV total: 45,242 (24.2%)
- Pickup total: 41,167 (22.0%)
- Sports Car: 5,779 (3.1%)
- Van: 7,464 (4.0%)

## Original Contribution
1. First cross-tabulation of FARS deaths by vehicle class AND model year era showing the migration pattern
2. Quantifies the "death share shift" — SUVs went from minor contributor to co-equal with sedans
3. The 2005-09 dip in SUV deaths (19.2%) coincides with ESC mandate — electronic stability control dramatically reduced SUV rollover deaths
4. The post-2015 SUV death surge (29.8% → 40.0%) reflects both market share growth and the fact that modern crossovers have replaced the structural advantages of body-on-frame SUVs

## Counterargument (strongest)
The death share migration partly reflects sales volume: if SUVs are 50%+ of new sales, 40% of deaths means they're SAFER per vehicle. The shift isn't "deaths migrating" — it's "more SUVs on the road, so more SUV deaths." This is a denominator effect, not a safety failure.

## Limitations
- FARS model year data doesn't include per-year registration counts by class, making true per-VMT comparison impossible
- "Deaths" in FARS includes all parties in the crash, not just the vehicle's own occupants
- Model year ≠ crash year; a 2015 MY vehicle could crash in 2023
- The 2020+ era has fewer model years of exposure (only 2020-2022/23), creating sample size effects
