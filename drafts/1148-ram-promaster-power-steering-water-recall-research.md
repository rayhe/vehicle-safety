# Research — #1148: Ram ProMaster Power Steering Water Recall

**Slug:** 1148-ram-promaster-power-steering-water-recall
**Journalist:** Mia Crumplezone (Safety Engineering Editor — design/defect analysis beat)
**Date:** 2026-10-12

## The facts

- Chrysler (FCA US) recalled **265,512** Ram ProMaster vans — 2022-2026 Ram 1500 / 2500 / 3500 ProMaster and 2024-2026 Ram 3500 Electric (EV) ProMaster.
- Model breakdown: 44,391 (1500), 147,204 (2500), 72,043 (3500), 1,874 (3500 EV).
- Defect: water can enter the electric power steering wiring harness, damaging the wiring and **disabling power steering assist**. Drivers may get **no warning before assist disappears**.
- Stellantis acknowledges **one accident (no injuries)** linked to the defect (spokesperson Frank Matyok to The Detroit News).
- Chrysler recall number **92D**. VINs searchable at nhtsa.gov/recalls since Oct 6, 2026. **Interim notification letters mailed Nov 17, 2026; final remedy not yet available** — owners are told about a risk nobody can yet fix.
- Related same-day action: 16,385 2026 Ram 1500s recalled for TPMS sensors that may fail to warn of low tire pressure (Chrysler 51D, letters Oct 27).
- Same week context: Volkswagen recalled ~208,000 SUVs over bolts that could cause steering loss. Steering is having a week.

## Kill test

- Genuinely newsworthy? Yes — quarter-million work vans, silent assist loss, no remedy.
- Novel angle vs. 835 published? No ProMaster stories on the site; prior Ram recall rejections (#1120 26V622, etc.) were different defects. PROCEED.
- The "interim letter with no fix" angle is the hook: the official remedy process is a letter telling you your van might not steer, signed "good luck."

## Original-contribution angle

- Five model years (2022-2026) carry the defect — it shipped for half a decade, and the 2024-2026 EV ProMaster inclusion proves it is not a legacy problem, it is in current production.
- Counterpoint baked in: losing power steering is NOT losing steering. At speed, a ProMaster remains steerable; the danger concentrates at parking-lot speeds and for smaller drivers, where unassisted effort on a ~4,500-lb van can exceed what a driver can produce in a panic maneuver. That's the honest version.

## What this does not prove (limitations)

- Only one known crash; the true incident rate is unknown. 265,512 units affected is the exposure population, not the failure count.
- The interim letter contains no remedy — the actionable guidance is: check your VIN now at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and commercial fleet owners should brief drivers that assist can vanish without a dashboard warning.
