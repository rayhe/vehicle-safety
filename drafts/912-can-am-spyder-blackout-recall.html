Plug In Your Phone, Lose Your Speedometer: Can-Am Recalls 14,014 Bikes Whose Screens Can Go Blank

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
September 16, 2026

On August 31, BRP told federal regulators it was recalling 14,014 Can-Am motorcycles and three-wheelers because the touchscreen instrument cluster can reset, freeze, or go blank mid-ride. The trigger, according to the company's own investigation, is a communication failure and memory leak tied to rider use of USB and Bluetooth connections. Plug in your phone, stream your music, and the screen that shows your speed can forget what it is for.

14,014
Can-Am motorcycles and three-wheelers recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V575000

This is not a broken speedometer; it is a broken everythingometer. On the affected Spyder F3, Spyder RT, Canyon, Origin, and Pulse models, one touchscreen is legally the speedometer, the brake-system display, and the warning-light panel — the recall cites violations of both FMVSS 122, "Motorcycle Brake Systems," and FMVSS 123, "Motorcycle Control and Displays." And it gets worse. During a cluster reset, the turn signals and hazard lights "temporarily deactivate or fail to respond," per the recall documentation; read that again, because you lose your gauges and your ability to signal a turn in the same instant.

Now the timeline, because it is doing a lot of work here. BRP first saw complaints about intermittent display problems in the summer of 2024. It investigated, found no common root cause, and closed the case, apparently; then the reports kept coming, the company re-opened the investigation in June 2026, and this time traced the failures to the USB and Bluetooth link. That is roughly 25 months from first complaint to recall initiation, with 125 field reports logged by early September. Whether that reads as careful engineering diligence or two years of watching screens flicker depends entirely on your charity level, but if you own one of these bikes, you have been riding the experiment the whole time.

The arithmetic deserves a pause. 125 reports across 14,014 vehicles is a 0.89 percent complaint rate, which sounds small until you notice the trigger is behavioral: the defect strikes riders who actually use the connected features, which means your phone charger is the defect vector. Somewhere a product manager is having a very bad week.

Fairness requires the other side of this, and it is real: no crashes and no injuries appear anywhere in the recall record, and BRP recalled voluntarily, closed the loop on a genuinely hard-to-reproduce software fault, and is pushing the fix over the air so owners do not have to find a dealer. An experienced rider can judge speed from traffic flow and engine note, and that is all real; it is also real that a memory leak tied to a USB cable took two years to find, and that during every one of those months a rider mid-reset had no speedo, no warnings, and signals that may or may not signal.

If you own a 2024–2027 Spyder F3 or Spyder RT, a 2025–2027 Canyon, or a 2025–2026 Origin or Pulse with the touchscreen cluster, do three things. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls starting September 22, when the campaign becomes searchable. Accept the over-the-air software update when BRP pushes the instructions. And if your screen ever blanks mid-ride, do not trust the turn-signal stalk until the display returns — pull over, restart the bike, and only then tell traffic where you are going. The remedy is free, but the lesson is not: one screen now does the job of five gauges, and when it forgets, it takes the whole dashboard with it.
