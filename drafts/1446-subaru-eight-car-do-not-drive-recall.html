Subaru Recalled Eight Cars and Told Them Not to Drive. That's the Whole Recall.

Clara Rollover • Consumer Safety Advocate
November 9, 2026

Eight cars. Four Crosstreks, four Foresters, every one a 2026 model built in a ten-day window in August. Subaru recalled all eight of them and told their owners not to drive them, because a single castle nut on the left-front ball joint may never have been tightened to spec. One nut, eight cars, zero driving allowed.[1]

8,422x
How much bigger GM's brake recall was the same week: 67,380 vehicles vs. Subaru's 8. (Vehicle Service Pros, NHTSA recalls Nov. 2-6, 2026)

Start with the part. A castle nut holds the ball joint to the lower control arm on the left-front suspension. If it was never tightened to spec, the joint can separate from the arm while the car is moving, and the driver loses control. Subaru says there may be no warning before it happens. Dealers will inspect the assembly and replace it free of charge, and Subaru's number for the recall is WRI26.[1][4]

Now the part that should impress you. Subaru found these eight cars from a single field report, dated September 15, of a ball joint that had separated while driving. Supplier TOA (USA) in Mooresville, Indiana, makes the part, number 20202FN03A. Its process and traceability records narrowed the suspect population to four Crosstreks built August 4 to 6 and four Foresters built August 5 to 13. Ten years ago, this defect would have meant recalling every Crosstrek and Forester built that quarter, out of an abundance of caution. In 2026 it meant eight cars and a phone call.[3][5]

Here is where the numbers get strange. Subaru's recall is the smallest in the November 2 to 6 roundup and the only one carrying a Do Not Drive advisory. GM recalled 67,380 electric Cadillacs and Chevys the same week because a brake software defect can take the brakes away, and those owners can keep driving. Hyundai recalled 37,901 cars over an airbag computer that can corrupt itself, and those owners can keep driving. Dodge recalled 13,330 Durangos with tail lights that can fail, and those owners can keep driving. Eight cars, zero driving allowed, and severity and scale have nothing to do with each other.[6]

Fairness requires saying the obvious. Eight cars is not a crisis, it is a rounding error, and the system worked exactly as designed. One failure, one supplier, eight VINs, a voluntary recall decided 25 days after the field report, no injuries, free fix. If you drive a 2026 Crosstrek or Forester that was not built in that ten-day window, this recall has nothing to do with you. And if you want to worry about something, worry about GM's 67,380 cars losing their brakes, not Subaru's eight parked in driveways with their owners already contacted.[5]

If you own a 2026 Crosstrek or Forester built in August 2026, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls right now. Do not wait for the letter. Owner notification letters are not mailed until December 16, 58 days after the filing reached NHTSA, and a Do Not Drive car should not sit in your driveway for two months while the mail catches up. If your VIN matches, park it and call Subaru at 1-844-373-6614 or your dealer and ask about recall WRI26. And the general rule, worth taping to the fridge: a Do Not Drive advisory is the rarest line in a recall notice, and it does not scale with the size of the recall. Eight cars can carry one. Read the advisory line, not just the vehicle count.
