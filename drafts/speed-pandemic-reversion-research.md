# Research: Speeding Deaths Return to Pre-Pandemic Levels — 5 Years Too Late

## Primary Sources

### 1. NHTSA Press Release (July 2026) — "Speeding Catches Up with You" Campaign
- **2024:** 11,288 speeding-related deaths (29% of all traffic fatalities), ~316,757 injuries
- **2025 estimate:** ~10,035 speeding-related deaths (11% decline)
- This represents a return to **pre-pandemic 2019 levels** — the first time since COVID
- **2025 full-year speeding deaths as percentage of all fatalities:** speeding remains the #1 behavioral factor
- NHTSA campaign ran July 6–31, 2026
- Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/trumps-transportation-department-reminds-drivers-that-speeding-catches-you

### 2. NHTSA Q1 2026 Traffic Fatality Estimates
- Q1 2026: 7,770 deaths, rate **0.99 per 100M VMT** (second-lowest Q1 in history)
- Q1 2025: 8,055 deaths, rate 1.05 (12th consecutive quarterly decline)
- H1 2025: 17,140 deaths, rate 1.06 (lowest mid-year since 2014), 8.2% decline
- Jan-Sep 2025: 27,365 deaths, 6.4% decline, rate 1.10
- Americans drove 25+ billion MORE miles in 2025 but deaths dropped
- Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/nhtsa-reports-sharp-drop-traffic-fatalities-first-half-2025

### 3. FARS Vehicle Data (2014-2023)
- **Sports cars:** 6,134 deaths, class-average rate 15.93/100M VMT — 3.2x SUVs (5.06)
- **Top sports car killers:** Mustang 2,739 deaths (rate 6.02), Camaro 1,204 (3.44), Veloster 598 (8.54)
- **Sports car impairment average:** ~22% (Corvette leads at 26.2%)
- **But 78% of sports car fatal crashes involve SOBER drivers** — speed is the standalone killer
- **High-rate sedans match sports cars:** Maxima 5.11, Cobalt 5.10, Impala 5.00 — these aren't sports cars
- **Sedan class average rate:** 11.86 — the highest of all classes (even higher than pickups at 8.86)

## Novel Contribution

Cross-referencing NHTSA's national speeding trend data (macro) with FARS vehicle-level death rates (micro) reveals:

1. **The "sedan-as-sports-car" phenomenon.** Three sedans (Maxima, Cobalt, Impala) have death rates matching or exceeding sports cars like the Camaro and Corvette. These aren't marketed as performance vehicles. Their driver demographics suggest that speed kills in everyday cars just as effectively.

2. **The 78% sober problem.** In sports car fatal crashes, nearly 4 out of 5 drivers are sober. This undermines the "drunk drivers speed" narrative. Many speed deaths are deliberate driving choices by unimpaired people.

3. **The pandemic speed tax was 5 years of excess death.** From 2020-2024, speeding fatalities ran ~1,000-2,000 above 2019 levels annually. Rough estimate: 5,000-8,000 excess deaths over 5 years attributable to the behavioral shift that COVID created on American roads.

## Limitations
- FARS data covers 2014-2023 and doesn't directly flag "speed as factor" — using vehicle death rates as proxy
- NHTSA's speed-related coding is separate from vehicle-level data
- The 10,035 estimate is preliminary and subject to revision
- Vehicle type correlates with speed-related driving but doesn't prove causation
- "Pre-pandemic levels" framing ignores that 2019 was itself a bad year (10,000+ speed deaths)

## Strongest Counterargument
The drop from 11,288 to 10,035 may be data noise in preliminary estimates. NHTSA has revised early projections before. And "returning to 2019 levels" is an arbitrary baseline — 10,035 deaths is still catastrophic, and the secular trend before the pandemic was already rising (from ~9,378 in 2015 to ~9,478 in 2019).
