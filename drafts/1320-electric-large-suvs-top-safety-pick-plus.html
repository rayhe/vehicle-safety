The Gap

The Two Safest Large SUVs in America Are Electric. Not One Gas Model Cleared the Bar.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 29, 2026

Before you sign that lease on a Tahoe, look at the scoreboard. IIHS has rated ten large SUVs under its 2026 criteria, and exactly two earned the Top Safety Pick+ award. Rivian R1S. Volvo EX90. Both are fully electric, and not one gas-powered model cleared the bar.[1]

0
Gas-powered large SUVs with an IIHS Top Safety Pick+

I pulled the full class table from IIHS this morning, and the gas column is a crime scene. Six of the eight gas models failed a required category outright. GM's Tahoe scored Poor in the moderate overlap front test and only Acceptable in the small overlap. Ford's Expedition earned Marginal in both front crash tests. Toyota's Sequoia, the Audi Q8, and the Jeep Grand Wagoneer all flunked the updated moderate overlap test, the one that finally grades back-seat protection like it matters. Audi's Q7 aced every crash test and still missed the Plus, because its vehicle-to-vehicle crash prevention scored Poor. That leaves the Armada and the QX80, which hold the regular Top Safety Pick with key categories still marked "not tested."[1]

Now the physics, because this is not a coincidence. Rollovers caused 28 percent of passenger-vehicle occupant deaths in 2024, and 32 percent for SUVs specifically.[3] A battery pack bolted under the floor drops the center of gravity exactly where a tall, heavy SUV needs it most, and in a two-vehicle crash the heavier vehicle sheds less speed, which is what keeps its occupants alive.[4] Both winners are also among the newest designs in the class, carrying the latest crash-avoidance tech that the 2026 criteria reward.[2]

Fairness requires the other side. The Armada and QX80 have not finished testing, and either could still earn the Plus. Some of this gap is newness, not batteries. And the weight that protects your family makes a 6,000-pound SUV more lethal to everyone else on the road, a trade IIHS's own research documents.[4]

What this means for you

Read the full IIHS class table before you buy, not the badge on the tailgate. Regular Top Safety Pick is a full tier below Plus, and three gas models, the Q7, Armada, and QX80, sit in that tier. If your kids ride in the second row, the moderate overlap test is the one to check, because that is where most of the gas models bled points. And know the blind spots: IIHS crash tests do not score battery-fire risk, real-world death rates for 2026 models will not exist for years, and both winners start near $80,000.[5]
