# Research: 1267 — Kalmar Ottawa T2 Terminal Tractors, Park-Outside Fire Recall (26V648)

**Slug:** 1267-kalmar-ottawa-t2-park-outside-fire
**Journalist:** Vin Wreckage (existential dread / paradox beat)
**Hook date:** Oct 5, 2026 (NHTSA filing); acknowledged Oct 16, 2026
**Kill test:** PASS. A vehicle that never leaves the yard got a federal recall because it can catch fire while parked with the ignition off. The remedy for a truck designed to live inside warehouses and terminals is to sleep outside. And the fix for a fire risk is a fuse and a rewire, which raises the question of why the circuit was ever unfused. First Ottawa/Kalmar/terminal-tractor article in the queue (grepped titles: no Ottawa, Kalmar, terminal tractor, or yard truck coverage in 1,266 prior entries).

## Facts (all sourced)

- **NHTSA campaign 26V648**, filed Oct 5, 2026 by Kalmar Solutions, LLC (Ottawa, KS); NHTSA acknowledgment letter dated Oct 16, 2026.
- **Population: 16,127 units.** Certain 2014-2026 Ottawa T2 4x2 and 2016-2026 T2 6x4 DOT/EPA terminal tractors. Kalmar's own release adds the Ottawa T2 Off Road configuration.
- **Defect:** the printed circuit board / power distribution board in the cabin can suffer a breakdown of electrical insulation resistance, resulting in a short circuit.
- **Consequence (NHTSA wording):** "A short circuit increases the risk of a fire even when the vehicle is parked and the ignition is in the 'Off' position."
- **Interim advice:** owners are advised to park outside and away from structures until repaired.
- **Remedy:** Kalmar will rewire the cabin power and add a 5A fuse to the PCB circuit, free of charge. Kalmar's release describes it as adding a remote ground stud (RGS/fuse) to completely isolate certain electrical circuits and prevent thermal events.
- **Owner letters:** expected to be mailed by December 4, 2026. Kalmar customer service: 1-888-229-6300, option 4, then option 2. NHTSA hotline: 1-888-327-4236.
- **Kalmar's frequency claim:** thermal events occurred in "very rare circumstances" and involved "less than 0.001% of total units" of the Ottawa T2 fleet. Note: the denominator is the total fleet, not the 16,127 recalled units, and Kalmar does not state either number. 0.001% of 16,127 would be 0.16 units, so the fleet denominator must be much larger; the per-recall rate is unstated.
- **What a terminal tractor is:** a yard truck (also called a yard spotter or shunt truck) that moves semi-trailers and containers inside ports, warehouses, and distribution terminals. It rarely travels on public roads. Its workplace is the facility itself, which is why a parked-off fire is a building fire, not a highway crash.
- **Prior Ottawa recall on the same model line:** 15V-177 (2015), 52 units of 2014-2015 Ottawa T2, rear-axle ABS wiring connected left-to-right reversed, risking instability under braking. Different defect, same nameplate, eleven years earlier.
- **Scale context:** 16,127 yard tractors spread across US ports, warehouses, and distribution centers. A single thermal event inside a trailer yard can involve adjacent trailers, fuel, and warehoused goods, so consequence scales with location, not with the truck.

## Novel contribution (original analysis)

1. **The inverted recall:** almost every recall in this publication's history is about a vehicle failing while in use. This one fails while doing nothing. Parked, off, key out, and still a fire risk. The hazard state requires no driver, no speed, no road, which breaks the usual mental model of vehicle safety entirely.
2. **The remedy is the punchline:** a 5-amp fuse and a rewire. Fuses exist to do exactly one job, sacrifice themselves instead of the wiring. A power distribution board that can short and burn without a fuse in the circuit is a protection device missing its protection. The fix costs less than the paperwork that announced it.
3. **Park-outside, for a vehicle that lives indoors:** the interim order tells fleet managers to park outside a machine whose entire job is working inside a facility. The order is correct and absurd at the same time, which is the point. Compliance means reorganizing yard operations around the trucks that cannot go home.
4. **Kalmar's percentage hides the rate that matters:** "less than 0.001% of total units" sounds like zero, but it divides by an unstated global fleet instead of the 16,127 trucks actually recalled. NHTSA's park-outside order, not the percentage, is the honest risk statement. When the regulator tells you to move the truck away from the building, the denominator debate is over.
5. **Location is the multiplier:** a car fire on a highway endangers its occupants. A yard-tractor fire inside a distribution terminal endangers the building, the trailers, and the night shift. The recalled population is small; the blast radius of one bad parking spot is not.

## Counterargument (at full strength)

Kalmar's framing has real weight: thermal events are genuinely rare, under 0.001% of the fleet by its count, and no injuries or deaths are attached to this campaign in the published material. A 5A fuse and a rewire is a cheap, complete fix, and the company filed voluntarily and quickly (Oct 5). Yard tractors are commercial equipment maintained by professionals, not passenger cars driven by teenagers, so detection and response are better than average. It is entirely fair to call this a well-handled low-frequency electrical correction rather than a scandal: small population, rare events, free fix, no reported harm.

## Limitations

- No crash, injury, or death counts are published for this campaign; "thermal events" is Kalmar's term and no count is given.
- The actual number of fires is unknown; Kalmar's <0.001% figure cannot be converted to a per-recall rate without the fleet denominator.
- Whether any fire occurred inside a building (vs. outdoors) is not stated in the sources.
- FARS data does not apply: terminal tractors rarely appear in fatal highway crash data, and no fatal crashes are tied to this defect.
- The remedy schedule beyond the Dec 4, 2026 letter date is not published; fleet repair timing depends on Kalmar's service network.

## Actionable insights

- Fleet managers with Ottawa T2 tractors: park them outside and away from structures until the fuse/rewire work is done. Do not assume a switched-off truck is a safe truck.
- Check whether your units are in campaign 26V648 via NHTSA recalls or Kalmar at 1-888-229-6300 (option 4, then 2).
- If a tractor smells of hot plastic or shows electrical faults, treat it as a fire risk, not a maintenance ticket, and move it away from buildings and trailers first.
- Buyers of used yard equipment: a 2014-2026 Ottawa T2 without the fuse retrofit documented is still carrying the defect, whatever the hour meter says.
