Honda's Steering Divides by Zero. 635,808 Cars Lose Power Assist.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
November 16, 2026

Somewhere inside the steering rack of 635,808 Hondas and Acuras, a computer divides by zero. Not metaphorically. Literally. A floating-point calculation in the electric power steering software hits a zero denominator, throws an internal error, and the system answers that error by switching off power steering assist. While the car is driving straight.[1]

1,789
Days from the first field report (November 29, 2021) to Honda's defect determination (October 22, 2026)

Nothing happens at first, while you are cruising in a straight line, hands light on the wheel, and the Driver Attention Monitor inside the Bosch steering software runs a road-condition compensation calculation. Under specific conditions that calculation divides by zero. Software was incorrectly configured to respond to that error by entering failsafe mode, which kills assist and lights up the EPS warnings on the cluster. Steering does not lock, but it gets heavy, suddenly, at highway speed, in a car whose driver was not expecting to steer at all. Honda's own filing says the failure occurs only when driving straight, which sounds reassuring until you realize straight-line highway driving is exactly when a driver is least prepared to muscle a dead rack.[1][2]

Population: 383,110 Honda Pilots from model years 2023 through 2025, 177,100 Acura MDXs from 2022 through 2024, and 75,598 Acura TLXs from 2021 through 2025. Honda estimates that 100 percent of them carry the faulty software, every single one of them. Pilots make up about 60 percent of the total. Supplier is Robert Bosch Automotive Steering of Florence, Kentucky, and the filing lists 42 power steering rack part numbers. Remedy is a dealer reprogramming of the EPS control unit, free of charge, because this is software and there is no over-the-air update, so you have to drive in.[1][3]

Now the timeline, because the timeline is the story. The first field report came November 29, 2021, and Honda could not confirm the cause until February 2026, when it pinned the failure to straight-line driving; the defect determination followed on October 22, 2026, the Part 573 filing landed October 29, exactly five working days later and the last day federal law allows, VINs became searchable November 11, and owner letters go out around December 14. Between the first report and the determination, Honda logged 146 warranty claims and one injury. No deaths. That gap is not a rounding error, it is four years and ten months during which the diagnostic evidence kept disappearing, because when parts came back from the field they looked fine. Error codes had been deleted, by the dealer or the supplier. Filing reports the deleted codes without saying how many were deleted or why.[2][4]

And this is the second time in 25 months. October 2024 brought recall 24V744: 1,693,199 Civics, CR-Vs, HR-Vs, and Acura Integras for a steering gearbox worm wheel that swells with heat and moisture, creating friction that makes steering stick. Different models, different failure mode, same system, same supplier family. Add them together and Honda has recalled 2,329,007 vehicles for steering defects since October 2024. One was mechanical and the other was a divide-by-zero. A company does not get two steering recalls of this scale in two years by bad luck. It gets them by treating steering as a solved problem while the parts inside it keep changing faster than the validation around them.[5]

Fairness requires the other side, stated at full strength. Honda found this through its own warranty monitoring, determined the defect, and filed inside the legal window. One injury in nearly five years across 635,808 vehicles is a low observed harm rate. Failure happens while driving straight, the lowest-demand steering condition, with warning lights telling the driver something is wrong. Remedy is a free software reflash, not a hardware replacement. A reasonable reader could call that the system working. Counterpoint: a system that takes 1,789 days to name a defect because its own diagnostic codes keep getting erased at the dealer level did not work. It got lucky that the failure mode is benign-ish. Next divide-by-zero might not pick a straight road.[1]

What to do: own a 2023-2025 Pilot, a 2022-2024 MDX, or a 2021-2025 TLX? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls now, do not wait for the December 14 letter, and book the dealer reflash, because there is no over-the-air fix coming. If steering ever goes suddenly heavy on a straight road with EPS warnings lit, pull over safely, restart, and get the car to a dealer. Report it to NHTSA. Complaints are what finally built this case. Buying used? A 2022-2025 Civic, CR-V, HR-V, or Integra may also carry the 24V744 sticky-steering recall. Check both campaigns before you sign anything.[1]
