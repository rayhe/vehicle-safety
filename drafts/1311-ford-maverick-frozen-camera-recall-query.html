Ford Recalled 144,516 Mavericks to Un-Freeze Their Backup Cameras. The Cameras Froze Again.

Rex Driverton • Clautomotive Desk
February 13, 2028

NHTSA opened a file on the Ford Maverick on October 23, 2026, and the file is not really about a defect. It is about a repair. The recall query, numbered RQ26004, covers about 273,301 2022–2024 Mavericks and Maverick Hybrids, every one of them equipped with a backup camera that can freeze in reverse, and it asks the question regulators dread asking: did the recall work?[4]

273,301
2022–2024 Ford Mavericks under recall-query review — nearly double the original recall

September 2024 brought the original recall, NHTSA campaign 24V684, Ford's own number 24S59: 144,516 Mavericks whose Connected Touch Radio could develop a memory leak, the kind of software rot where a program never throws anything away until it runs out of room and simply stops doing its job, which in this case was showing drivers what was behind their trucks.[3]

Ford issued that recall with no fix in hand.

Owners were told their cameras might freeze, then sent home to wait, because no software patch existed in September 2024; the remedy, an Audio Control Module update called G2.2, did not arrive until March 2025, half a year of backing up with a camera the manufacturer had already admitted it could not trust, and Ford now says it has been unable to replicate the problem on the new software.[2]

Five owners disagree.

Five Vehicle Owner Questionnaires now sit in NHTSA's files alleging the camera froze after the remedy was performed, and this is not the usual pile of unverified gripes: ODI investigators read them and then verified several complete failures on their own. One of them, filed as complaint 11662294, deserves to be read aloud: a 2024 Maverick in upstate New York, recall repair completed at the dealer, camera frozen again weeks later at 8,000 miles, no warning light, no re-diagnosis, the owner simply living with it.[5]

A blank screen is an honest failure. It announces the camera's death at full brightness. A frozen frame does the opposite: it shows the driveway as it was three seconds ago and lets you reverse into the present at full confidence, a false representation, to borrow the exact phrase from Ford's own recall filing. FMVSS 111 requires a rear view when you shift into reverse. A frozen image satisfies the letter of that standard while defeating its entire purpose, which is the kind of compliance only a lawyer could love.[7]

The query lands harder against Ford's camera history, which by now reads like a serialized novel: campaign 25V-315 recalled 1,075,299 vehicles for a SYNC 4 bug that made cameras lag, freeze, or go black; 25S72 covered hundreds of thousands more where short ignition cycles could freeze the system on its last image; and behind those sit 25V-270, 25V-273, and 20V-575, each one another chapter in the same story of a company spending years debugging the concept of showing drivers a picture of behind.[6]

Fairness demands the other half of the ledger: the original campaign recorded two crashes and zero injuries, the new query lists no crashes at all, and five complaints across 144,516 repaired trucks is a small numerator by any measure. A recall query is a question, not a verdict.[1]

But look at the denominator. The recall covered 144,516 trucks; the query covers 273,301, nearly double, which means NHTSA believes the defect reaches further than the recall did, or the remedy coverage was incomplete, or both, and a regulator that doubles your population is a regulator that did not find your first answer convincing.

What this means for you

If you drive a 2022–2024 Maverick with the base Connected Touch Radio, the kind without SYNC, go check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and confirm that recall 24S59 was performed, not merely scheduled, because the whole point of RQ26004 is that performed might not mean fixed. A frozen image throws no warning light, so treat a suspiciously static frame as a fault and use the mirrors, the old technology that has never suffered a memory leak in its life. If yours froze after the fix, file it at nhtsa.gov, because queries like RQ26004 do not begin in a lab; they begin with five people who refused to accept that the repair worked.[7]
