# Research: #964 — NHTSA Closes Honda Ridgeline Rear-View Camera Probe, Remedy Passes

## News peg
Reuters, **September 22, 2026** (today): NHTSA closed its engineering analysis into rear-view camera failures in 129,092 Honda Ridgeline pickups — the 2017–2019 models recalled in 2022 — **without further action**. The probe was checking whether the 2022 recall's remedy (a replacement wire harness) actually held up.

## Timeline
- **Nov 23, 2022** — Honda files recall **22V867000** (Honda #6RW): 117,445 MY 2017–2019 Ridgelines. Opening/closing the tailgate fatigues the rear-view camera wire harness (corrugated tubing too short to prevent bending wear + zip ties too loose to hold it), killing the camera image. Remedy: replacement harness with longer protective corrugated tubing and properly tightened zip ties. Honda's Part 573: estimated 7% of involved vehicles had the defect; 3,437 warranty claims (Dec 2017–Oct 2022); zero injuries/fatalities reported.
- **Jun 26, 2024** — NHTSA opens **Recall Query RQ24-011**, explicitly "Prompted By: Recall Remedy Review." Investigator Caroline Woody. Estimated population 129,266. Failure report summary: 0 crashes, 0 fires, 0 injuries, 0 fatalities.
- **Feb 13, 2025** — Upgraded to **Engineering Analysis** after Honda acknowledged **14 reports of remedy failures** — trucks repaired under the 2022 recall whose cameras failed again.
- **Sep 22, 2026** — EA closed. NHTSA's Vehicle Research and Test Center surveyed owners, inspected vehicles, and durability-tested the repaired harness. Finding: no camera failures caused by wire-harness damage; the part "not expected to fail during a reasonable vehicle lifespan." Most incoming reports were other failure modes, unlinked to the harness durability question.

## The angle (Rex Driverton, Investigation)
The two-year federal investigation that ended with "never mind" — and why that is the system working, not failing. Nobody writes about investigations that close without action. The novel frame: NHTSA audits its own recalls. A "Recall Remedy Review" is the agency checking Honda's homework — and Honda passed.

## Novel calculation
- Pre-recall complaint intensity: 3,437 warranty claims / 117,445 vehicles ≈ **2.9%** cumulative (Dec 2017–Oct 2022).
- Post-remedy: 14 remedy-failure reports / 129,092 ≈ **0.011%** — roughly a **270-fold drop** in the complaint rate after the fix. (Caveat: warranty claims and ODI complaints are different reporting channels; treat the ratio as directional, not precise.)
- Timeline: Nov 2022 recall → Sep 2026 closure = **46 months** from recall to "the fix works."

## Strongest counterargument
This is ODI doing exactly what it is supposed to do. Fourteen post-remedy failures on a back-over-prevention camera — a system whose entire job is keeping drivers from backing over children — is a legitimate reason to open an engineering analysis. The probe produced real durability data. Closing it without action is evidence-based regulation, not bureaucratic theater. Also: "most reports involved other failure modes" means some owners still have dead cameras — the closure vindicates the harness, not their trucks.

## Limitations
- NHTSA does not publish a clean annual count of investigations closed without action vs. leading to recalls in a single citable table; do NOT fabricate a funnel statistic. The article's numbers are limited to this case.
- Warranty claims ≠ ODI complaints; the 270x ratio is directional.
- Population figures differ slightly across documents (117,445 recalled vs. 129,266 RQ population vs. 129,092 at EA/closure) — report each as its source states it.
- We do not know the VRTC's test protocol details; only the outcome as reported by Reuters.

## Actionable takeaways
- Own a 2017–2019 Ridgeline? Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for 22V867000 / Honda 6RW. If never remedied, the harness replacement is free.
- Camera died *after* the remedy? File a complaint at nhtsa.gov — ODI's Recall Remedy Reviews are literally triggered by owner reports. Fourteen reports opened a 20-month federal investigation.
- Lesson for every used-car buyer: a completed recall is not the end of the story. NHTSA keeps watching the fix.
