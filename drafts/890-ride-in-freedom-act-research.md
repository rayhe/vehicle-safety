# Article #890 — Research Notes

## Angle
Two congressmen introduced the "Ride in Freedom Act" (H.R. 10067 / S. 5274) in August 2026 to prohibit Washington, D.C. from requiring motorcycle helmets, explicitly as a beachhead for rolling back helmet mandates nationwide. Meanwhile Missouri is legislating to RESTORE its repealed universal helmet law and Hawaii is moving to ENACT one, because the repeal experiment already ran and the results are on tombstones: Florida +35%, Kentucky +50%, Louisiana +100% motorcyclist fatality rate after repeal; Nebraska's 2024 repeal produced +32% fatalities vs the five-year average; Missouri's 2020 repeal brought a +47% rise and its deadliest year on record (174 deaths, 2023). The data is moving one way; the politics the other.

## Kill test
- **Newsworthy?** Yes. Bill introduced ~Aug 11, 2026; Van Orden in a toss-up re-election race (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, Sep 4); D.C. motorcycle deaths tripled YTD (2 → 6).
- **Novel?** Yes. Zero site coverage of helmet laws, helmet-law repeals, or this bill. Original contribution: framing the national repeal natural experiment as a table (state → repeal year → fatality change), juxtaposed against the two states currently legislating TOWARD helmets (MO SB 990, HI SB2226) and Congress legislating AWAY. Nobody has run this as "the experiment is over, the states are reversing, and Congress didn't get the memo."
- **Verdict:** PROCEED.

## Core data (primary sources)

### 1. The bill (H.R. 10067, S. 5274 — introduced early Aug 2026)
- Rep. Derrick Van Orden (R-Wis.), Harley rider, co-chair of Congressional Motorcycle Caucus; Sen. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.).
- Prohibits D.C. from requiring helmets for motorcycle operators/passengers 18+. D.C.'s current law covers ALL riders regardless of age.
- Spokeswoman Grace Kim (per WaPo/Carscoops, Aug 2026): "D.C. is an important place to start. If Congress can change the policy here, it opens the door to having a larger conversation about the federal government's role in helmet mandates across the country."
- Van Orden (Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, Sep 4, 2026): "It's nothing kooky. You should have the choice to wear a helmet or not."
- MRF president Kirk "Hardtail" Willard supports; Motorcycle Riders Foundation lobbying.
- No House cosponsors as of Sep 4 (jsonline).
- Context: D.C. has no voting representation in Congress; Maryland and Virginia (its neighbors) both have universal helmet laws — repeal would create a helmet donut: law on, law off, law on across three jurisdictions.
- 47 states have helmet laws of some kind; 17 have universal laws like D.C.'s (GHSA via ResetEra/WaPo).

### 2. D.C. motorcycle deaths (Fox5 DC / Carscoops, Aug 2026)
- 6 motorcyclist deaths in D.C. in 2026 YTD, vs 2 in all of 2025, vs 10 in 2024.
- 67+ D.C. motorcycle deaths since 2014. City data doesn't record helmet use at crash.

### 3. NHTSA Motorcycles: 2024 Data (rosap.ntl.bts.gov, DOT/92321)
- Motorcyclists killed: 2023: 6,366 (peak); 2024: 6,228 (first drop); 2022: 6,251; 2021: 6,144; 2020: 5,620.
- Fatality rate 2024: 28.00 per 100M VMT.
- Per VMT, motorcyclists ~27x more likely than car occupants to die in crashes (NHTSA 2024, via NOTUS), and 5x more likely to be injured.
- NHTSA: "If you're ever in a serious motorcycle crash, the best hope you have for protecting your brain is a motorcycle helmet."
- NHTSA national trend: 13 consecutive quarterly declines in overall traffic fatalities; Q1 2026 rate 0.99, near-record. Motorcyclist deaths fell 2023→2024 but remain at historic highs.

### 4. The repeal natural experiment (Advocates for Highway and Auto Safety, Feb 4, 2026 letter supporting MO SB 990; Hawaii SB2226 findings)
- Missouri: repealed universal law 2020 → fatalities up 47%; deadliest year on record, 174 deaths, 2023. SB 990 would restore all-rider law (Feb 2026, pending).
- Florida: repeal → motorcyclist fatality rate +35%.
- Kentucky: repeal → +50%.
- Louisiana: repeal → +100% (doubled).
- Michigan: repealed 2012; estimated 26 fewer deaths (21% reduction) in the repeal year had the law stayed.
- Nebraska: repeal took effect 2024; preliminary data show +32% motorcyclist fatalities vs five-year average.
- Hawaii (SB2226, Jan 2026): 27 motorcycle deaths in 2025; 61% unhelmeted. Bill would require all operators and passengers to wear helmets.
- IIHS statistical director Eric Teoh (WaPo, Aug 2026): universal helmet laws are "one of the most effective, common-sense rules of the road for improving motorcycle safety"; "Weakening that will result in people dying."

### 5. The other side (for counterargument)
- American Motorcyclist Association: helmet use shouldn't be mandated; "helmet use alone is insufficient to ensure a motorcyclist's safety." (via NOTUS)
- MRF VP Jay Jackson: freedom of choice for riders visiting the capital (Fox5).
- Philosophical counterargument: helmets reduce head-injury severity, not crash probability; rider training, conspicuity, and impaired-riding prevention save more lives per dollar; mandates are paternalism.

## Original contribution
Cross-jurisdiction timeline table: the repeal experiment's before/after fatality deltas (FL +35, KY +50, LA +100, NE +32, MO +47) against the current legislative moment (MO and HI moving toward helmets; Congress moving D.C. away). Plus the arithmetic of the beachhead strategy: D.C.'s 6 YTD deaths happened WITH a universal law; the question is the counterfactual delta repeal would add, which the state experiments answer at +35–100%.

## Limitations
- D.C. numbers are tiny (6 vs 2): noise dominates; can't attribute any individual death to law status. The bill's effect can't be measured prospectively; inference is from other states' experiments.
- Repeal-state comparisons have confounders: registration growth, ridership trends, VMT, enforcement, demographics. The +35–100% figures are cited from Advocates; underlying methodologies not independently verified this run.
- Helmets address head injury only — the deadliest single mode, but not the whole crash. A rider can do everything right and still die; a helmet doesn't fix the 27x exposure.
- Nebraska's +32% is preliminary state data. Missouri SB 990 and Hawaii SB2226 are pending, not passed.

## Strongest counterargument (full strength)
The freedom argument is real, not cosmetic: a majority of states (30+) already allow adult riders to choose, and D.C. would merely join them. Helmets don't prevent crashes; they mitigate one injury mode. If the goal is saving riders' lives, crash-avoidance (ABS, traction control, rider training, cracking down on left-turn drivers) arguably deserves the political capital. And there's a representation problem in reverse too: D.C. riders' home-state laws differ; MRF's point that visiting riders face a patchwork has force. Also: correlation across repeal states isn't a controlled trial, and some of the fatality growth tracks national ridership growth. But: the size and consistency of the repeal-state deltas (+35% to +100%) dwarfs plausible confounders, the head-injury mechanism is biomechanically direct, and the states that ran the experiment are now legislating to undo it — revealed preference beats rhetoric.
