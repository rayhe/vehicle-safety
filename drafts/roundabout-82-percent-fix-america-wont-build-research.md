# Research Notes: Roundabouts — The 82% Fix America Won't Build

## Angle
The US has over 11,000 roundabouts and ~328,000 signalized intersections. Roundabouts reduce fatal and injury crashes by 82% (FHWA Proven Safety Countermeasure). Intersections account for 23% of US fatal crashes (~9,400 deaths/year at 2023 FARS levels). Converting just 10% of signalized intersections to roundabouts would prevent ~290 fatal crashes per year (IIHS estimate). Yet from 2000 to 2026, the US went from 356 to ~11,000 roundabouts — a 3.4% penetration rate after 26 years. At current pace, converting all suitable intersections would take centuries. Meanwhile, ~9,400 Americans die at intersections annually.

The story isn't "roundabouts are good" — that's known. The story is the PACE. America is building a proven 82% fatality reducer slower than it's killing people at the intersections it refuses to convert.

## Primary Sources

### 1. FHWA (Federal Highway Administration)
- Roundabouts are an FHWA **Proven Safety Countermeasure**
- Reduce fatal and injury crashes by **82%** on average
- Intersections account for up to **23% of fatal** and **50% of serious injury** crashes in the US
- US now home to **over 11,000 roundabouts** (National Roundabouts Week page, 2026)
- Source: https://highways.dot.gov/safety/intersection-safety/nrw

### 2. IIHS (Insurance Institute for Highway Safety)
- Studies of US intersections converted to roundabouts: **90% reduction** in fatal/incapacitating injury crashes (Retting et al., 2001)
- **76% reduction** in all injury crashes
- **38% reduction** in all crash severities combined
- Eisenman et al. (2004): Converting **10% of signalized intersections** → prevent ~50,000 crashes/year including **~290 fatal crashes** and 32,000+ injury crashes (updated to 2023 data)
- Pedestrian crashes reduced by ~**75%** (European studies, Brilon et al. 1993, Schoon & van Minnen 1994)
- Two-lane roundabout safety **improves over time** as drivers get familiar (Hu & Cicchino, 2019 — Washington state study: crashes decreased 9%/year, injury odds decreased ~33%/year)
- Source: https://www.iihs.org/research-areas/roundabouts

### 3. Retting et al. (2001) — IIHS/PMC Study
- Full title: "Crash and injury reduction following installation of roundabouts in the United States"
- Published in PMC (PMC1446639)
- 24 intersections converted from stop sign/traffic signal to modern roundabouts
- Results: 38% reduction all severities, 76% injury crashes, ~90% fatal/incapacitating
- Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1446639/

### 4. ITE (Institute of Transportation Engineers) Estimate
- 327,860 signalized intersections in the US (2018 estimate)
- Source: Traffic Signal Benchmarking and State of Practice Report, cited by INRIX

### 5. FARS Data (2023)
- ~40,990 traffic fatalities in 2023 (NHTSA)
- 23% at intersections = ~9,428 intersection deaths
- Source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/fatality-analysis-reporting-system-fars

### 6. Fast Company / University of Dayton
- US had **356 roundabouts in 2000**
- Grew to over **10,000** by 2024 (11,000+ by 2026)
- Some states have "roundabout first" policies: New York, Virginia
- 750 roundabouts in Florida; fewer than 50 in ND/SD/WY combined
- Source: Fast Company (Deogratias Eustace, University of Dayton)

### 7. California LTAP Data
- Converting four-way traffic signal → roundabout: **78% reduction** in severe crashes, **48% reduction** in overall crashes
- Converting two-way stop → roundabout: **82% reduction** in severe crashes, **44%** overall
- Source: California LTAP, cited by Sally Morin Law analysis

## Key Calculations

### Penetration Rate
- 11,000 roundabouts / 327,860 signalized intersections = **3.4%** of signal-controlled intersections
- But roundabouts also replace stop-sign intersections — still minuscule vs. total intersection count

### Annual Growth Rate
- 356 (2000) → 11,000 (2026) = 10,644 added in 26 years = ~409/year average
- At 409/year, converting 327,860 signalized intersections would take **~800 years**

### Lives Saved Projection
- Converting 10% of signalized intersections = ~32,786 conversions
- IIHS estimates this would prevent **290 fatal crashes/year**
- At 409/year pace, converting 10% would take **~80 years**
- Meanwhile, over those 80 years at 290 preventable deaths/year = **23,200 preventable deaths** during the wait

### Cost
- A roundabout costs $250,000–$500,000 to build (varies by complexity)
- A traffic signal costs $250,000–$500,000 to install PLUS $3,000–$8,000/year maintenance
- Over 20 years, roundabouts are often **cheaper** than signals (lower maintenance, no electricity)
- Source: FHWA, various state DOT estimates

## Counterarguments (Must Present at Full Strength)
1. **Not all intersections are suitable for roundabouts.** High-volume multi-lane intersections, constrained urban sites, and areas with heavy freight traffic may be poor candidates.
2. **Unfamiliar drivers can cause crashes initially.** Two-lane roundabouts show higher crash rates initially before declining as familiarity grows (IIHS Hu & Cicchino 2019).
3. **Pedestrian accessibility concerns.** Blind and visually impaired pedestrians have difficulty navigating roundabouts without audible signals.
4. **Community opposition is real.** Surveys consistently show public resistance before construction but high approval after — the familiarity gap.

## Limitations
- The IIHS 10% conversion estimate (Eisenman et al. 2004) is 22 years old. Intersection volumes and vehicle fleet composition have changed.
- FARS intersection fatality percentage (23%) includes all intersection types, not just signalized.
- Not all signalized intersections would benefit equally from roundabout conversion.
- The 82% figure is an average; individual intersection results vary widely (25%–90%).
