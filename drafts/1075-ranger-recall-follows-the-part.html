Ford Recalled 11,405 Rangers. Then It Recalled the Replacement Screens Too.

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
October 4, 2026

Before you check your VIN, check your repair receipt. Ford's Ranger screen recall is actually two recalls, and the second one is the one nobody told you about. First: 11,405 brand-new 2026 Rangers whose backup cameras can go black without warning. Second: 46 replacement screens sold through dealer service departments, screens that may already be sitting in trucks from model years the recall supposedly doesn't touch.[1][2] One recall follows the truck; the other follows the part. If you only check the first, you can miss the second.

11,405
Factory 2026 Rangers in the vehicle recall, NHTSA campaign 26V605000

The vehicle campaign is the one that made the news. Certain 2026 Rangers with the 10.1-inch center stack screen can lose power to the display, killing the rearview camera image and putting the truck in violation of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111, the rear-visibility rule that has required backup cameras on new vehicles for years.[1] The root cause, per Ford's safety recall report, is a cracked multilayer ceramic capacitor on the display's circuit board, a component Ford suspects is counterfeit.[3] A fake two-cent part shorts, pops a fuse, and takes down a federally mandated safety system. Ford identified 15 warranty claims and says it knows of no accidents or injuries. Remedy still under development; interim owner letters go out October 5.[4]

Now the part nobody reported: alongside the vehicle campaign, Ford filed NHTSA campaign 26E065000, an equipment recall covering 46 of the same 10.1-inch screens sold as service replacement parts for 2024 through 2026 Rangers, produced between December 2025 and August 2026.[2] Read that again: those screens were sold to fix Rangers. If your 2024 or 2025 Ranger had its center screen replaced at a dealership in the last year for any reason at all, a screen that died of old age, a glitch, a cracked display, the replacement bolted into your dash could be one of the 46, and the defective part found you anyway.

46
Service replacement screens in the equipment recall, NHTSA campaign 26E065000

This is the gap the recall system doesn't advertise. Vehicle recalls are VIN-scoped: you type 17 characters into nhtsa.gov and get a yes or no. Equipment recalls are part-scoped: they chase components through dealer service records, and the consumer-facing lookup was built for the first kind, not the second. Ford did quarantine suspect service inventory on September 2, which stops further parts from leaving the network, but a quarantine only helps the screens still on the shelf.[5] It does nothing for the ones already installed in someone's dashboard.

Give Ford its due, because the timeline here is genuinely competent. On August 14, the Michigan Assembly Plant team flagged a warranty trend of black screens at low mileage. Six teardowns confirmed the cracked capacitor by August 20. By late September the population was sized and both campaigns were filed, about five weeks from first signal to federal filing.[5] That is a quality system doing its job, and the equipment campaign exists precisely because the system tracks service parts instead of shrugging at them. Forty-six units is also tiny, so your individual risk here is minuscule, and no crash or injury has been tied to either campaign.

But minuscule is not zero, and the fix for it costs you a phone call. If you own a 2026 Ranger with the 10.1-inch screen, run your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls under campaign 26V605000 and watch for the interim letter arriving this week. If you own any 2024 through 2026 Ranger that had its center screen replaced at a dealer between December 2025 and August 2026, skip the VIN lookup and call the servicing dealer directly: reference equipment campaign 26E065000 and ask for the replacement part's production date. And if any Ranger's screen goes black in reverse before a fix arrives, drive it like the backup camera doesn't exist, because it doesn't: mirrors, a walk-around, a spotter. FMVSS 111 exists because backover crashes kill people, and a dead screen is the same as no camera at all.

Honest limits: the installed-versus-quarantined split of the 46 service screens is not public, and some may never have left dealer shelves. One caveat on sourcing: the September 2 quarantine detail comes from a single secondary report, so treat the date as reported, not Ford-confirmed. Whether NHTSA's VIN tool surfaces equipment-campaign vehicles is ambiguous, which is exactly why the article's advice routes around the VIN tool rather than through it. And to be clear about scale: 46 parts against roughly 11,500 factory trucks means the service-part population is a rounding error, but rounding errors still have owners.
