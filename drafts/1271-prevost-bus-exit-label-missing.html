7,509 Prevost Buses Are Missing the Label That Tells Passengers How to Escape

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
January 4, 2028

Prevost is recalling 7,509 buses because the entrance door is missing the label that tells passengers it doubles as an emergency exit, according to NHTSA campaign 26V675000, announced October 15, 2026.[1] Federal safety standard FMVSS 217 requires emergency exits to carry operating instructions, and these coaches fail that requirement on the door every passenger walks through.

7,509
Buses recalled under NHTSA campaign 26V675000 (Prevost campaign SR26-19), with no do-not-drive warning attached
102
Associated models listed on the NHTSA page for this recall, spanning Prevost H3 and X3 coaches

In a fire or a rollover, nobody reads a manual. Passengers look for a lit sign, a handle, a picture showing which way to push. On these buses, that instruction is missing or unclear, according to both the US recall and a parallel Transport Canada recall issued the same day, which warns that unclear exit instructions could confuse occupants and delay escape.[2] Prevost will mail a replacement label free of charge, with owner letters going out December 7, 2026, and owners can also have an authorized service center install it.

No crash, injury, or death has been tied to this defect, and NHTSA issued no do-not-drive warning. That absence cuts both ways: nothing has gone wrong yet, and nothing about a missing label announces itself before it matters. A cracked windshield gets noticed. A bald tire gets noticed. A blank rectangle above a door handle looks like nothing at all, which is exactly why the standard requires the label in the first place.

Consider what an evacuation actually looks like. Fifty strangers, low light, smoke, luggage in the aisle, one driver shouting over an alarm. Engineers design exits for that scene, then trust a printed label to do the last ten feet of communication. Remove the label and the exit still exists, but the crowd no longer knows it. An exit nobody recognizes is a wall with a handle.

Volvo Group should know this failure mode by heart. Prevost is a Volvo Group brand, and Volvo Bus 9700 coaches were recalled in December 2024 after emergency-exit labels that were not secured properly could detach, with Prevost itself handling the replacement labels.[3] A separate Volvo Bus recall covered exits designated without operating instructions. Same parent, same standard, same little rectangle, three different ways to get it wrong. Quality control keeps missing something a passenger could spot in three seconds, if any passenger knew to look.

Now the case for calm, stated fairly. A sticker is not a structural defect. Doors on these coaches still open, windows still release, and drivers still run the vehicle. Nobody should refuse a bus ticket over this recall, and no operator needs to park a fleet. Risk here is measured in seconds of delay during an evacuation that may never happen, not in crash probability on a normal run. Overstating that would be its own kind of mislabeling.

Still, seconds are the unit evacuations are measured in. Smoke fills a coach fast, panic fills it faster, and the difference between an orderly exit and a crush at one door is often one clear instruction read by the first person to reach it. Prevost is betting a mailed sticker closes that gap. Probably it does, for operators who install it promptly. For the rest, the gap stays open until December letters turn into January installations, or until a driver notices the blank space on a walkaround and asks why it is blank.

So check the label before the next run if you operate a coach, and check it on the entrance door specifically, not just the windows and roof hatches. Owners can call Prevost at 1-866-870-2046 or check a VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. If you ride a coach, count the exits before departure, and treat the front door as an exit whether or not a label says so. Labels fail. Doors, fortunately, do not need permission to open.
