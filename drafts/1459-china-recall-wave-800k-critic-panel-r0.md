## Scores

1. **General — 8.8/10.** The lede works: the hook is not the 800,000 but the 72 days, and the article earns that inversion in the first two paragraphs. Structure is clean (bolt, wave, US parallel, export gap, counterargument, limitations, action). The Toyota paragraph is the densest passage — four campaigns, five numbers, three defect types in one block — and a reader could lose the thread between the Prado fuel line and the bZ7 software. A line break or a second paragraph would help. Not a failure.
2. **Voice — 9.0/10.** Distinctly Clara: direct, practical, buyer-first. "Before you sign anything, check the build date" and "A system that works only inside the market that filed it is a system with a border" are her register, not Axle's number-running or Rex's noir. No byline-swap risk.
3. **Ethics — 9.2/10.** No invented injury or crash counts anywhere; the article says explicitly that none are published. The export-gap claim is framed as a process fact, not a body count, and the "no count invented" discipline is stated twice. Attribution to SinoAutoCars/Cailianshe is explicit rather than laundered into flat fact.
4. **Social — 8.7/10.** "Same bolt, two countries" plus a 72-day wait is a shareable frame, and the title carries the whole story. The title is long (19 words) and will truncate in some feeds, but it is specific enough to survive truncation. The pull stat is the right number to screenshot.
5. **Legal — 9.0/10.** No defamation exposure: every defect claim is attributed to SAMR notices via the cited reporting or to NHTSA; the language is "can corrode" and "can come loose," never "will fail"; the article explicitly does not claim any particular car is defective. No settlement or lawsuit claims.
6. **Rigor — 8.6/10.** Arithmetic verified: Nov 4 to Jan 15 is 72 calendar days; 610,000/800,000 is roughly 76%; 388,258/800,000 is about 48%; China Q3 range 2018-2025 is eight model years vs US 2019-2021 three. The one real weakness is single-source dependency: all China figures flow through one secondary report (SinoAutoCars summarizing Chinese-language SAMR notices and a Cailianshe tally). The limitations section discloses this honestly, which is why the score stays above 8.5, but a second independent report would have been better. Noted, not blocking.
7. **Data Presentation — 8.8/10.** The pull stat (72 days) is the article's best number and it is the one pulled out. Percentages are labeled approximate where the denominator is a media tally. No chart, but the numbers are small enough to carry in prose. The 800,000 total is correctly flagged as a tally, not an official figure, in both the body and the disclaimer.

## Verdict
**Average 8.87/10. All 7 critics at 8.5+. All hard gates pass. → SHIP.**
