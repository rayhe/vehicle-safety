The Gap

Stellantis Built Two Cars on One Platform. Both Are Broken Five Different Ways.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
August 13, 2026
48,777
Vehicles recalled August 8 for rear seatbelt retractors that twist and stop retracting. That figure exceeds the number of Dodge Hornets sold in the U.S. in any calendar year.

The Dodge Hornet and the Alfa Romeo Tonale are the same car. Same platform. Same assembly line at Stellantis's Pomigliano d'Arco plant outside Naples. Same 2.0-liter turbo four. One wears a crosshair grille, the other a triangular shield. Both have now been recalled for brakes that collapse, seatbelts that twist, and cameras that go blind.

On August 8, NHTSA published the latest: recall covering 48,777 Hornets (2023-2025) and Tonales (2023-2026) because their rear seatbelt retractors can twist internally, preventing the belt from retracting properly.[1] Stellantis also recalled Mopar replacement seatbelt assemblies sold through its dealer network, meaning even the fix parts needed fixing.

That 48,777 figure is interesting because Dodge sold 20,559 Hornets in the entire U.S. market in 2024.[2] Alfa Romeo moved 3,383 Tonales.[3] The seatbelt recall alone covers more vehicles than either model sold in its best year.

But the seatbelts are recall number five. Or four. Or six, depending on how you count the expansions. In October 2024, Stellantis recalled 21,069 PHEV variants because the brake pedal arm could snap during normal braking.[4] One accident, no injuries. The recall expanded twice: 5,974 more vehicles in December 2025, then again in early 2026. A separate recall addressed a reverse camera that could go dark when the vehicle was in reverse. Then the seatbelts.

The combined recall exposure across all campaigns approaches 80,000 vehicle-recall touchpoints. Total combined US sales since launch: roughly 35,000 units. Simple math. Your average Hornet or Tonale owner has been recalled more than twice.

Mechanically, this is what shared-platform economics looks like when the platform has problems. Every engineering shortcut, every supplier quality gap, multiplies across both brands simultaneously. The brake pedal arm that fails in a Hornet fails identically in a Tonale because it is the same part, sourced from the same supplier, bolted to the same subframe. Stellantis didn't build two unreliable vehicles. It built one unreliable vehicle and put two badges on it.

Production tells the rest of the story. The Pomigliano plant assembled 82,300 Hornets and Tonales combined in 2023.[5] Output collapsed to 36,980 in 2024. First quarter 2025 ran at a pace of roughly 20,000 annualized. Then Stellantis halted Hornet production entirely in July 2025, citing U.S. import tariffs on Italian-built vehicles.[6] The Tonale continues in limited production, selling 1,156 units in the first half of 2025, down 28% from the same period in 2024.

So the platform is effectively dead. But the recalls are not. The August 2026 seatbelt recall covers 2023-2026 Tonales, meaning vehicles that rolled off the line months ago are still accumulating safety defects discovered after the fact. Owners of a discontinued Hornet will still be getting recall letters for a car Stellantis no longer makes.

If you own a 2023-2025 Dodge Hornet or 2023-2026 Alfa Romeo Tonale: Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. You likely have at least one open recall. Given the platform's track record, assume your rear seatbelts need inspection even if you haven't received a letter yet. If you own a PHEV variant, the brake pedal recall is separate and critical: the pedal arm can physically collapse under your foot during braking. Get that fixed before you worry about the seatbelts.
