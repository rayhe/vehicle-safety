Every Model Year of the Mustang Mach-E Has Been Recalled. Every Single One.

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
August 15, 2026

Ford sold roughly 215,000 Mustang Mach-Es in the United States between 2021 and mid-2026.[1] In that span, the company has issued at least eight separate recall campaigns touching every model year the vehicle has existed. Pick any Mach-E ever manufactured, from the first 2021 rolling off the Cuautitlán line to the latest 2025 on a dealer lot, and it carries at least three open safety actions. Pick a 2023, and you inherit four.

8
Recall campaigns across five model years

July 2026 alone delivered two new ones. On July 7, Ford recalled 42,784 Mach-Es from 2021 through 2023 because the rear differential pinion shaft, supplied by BorgWarner, can fracture while the vehicle is moving.[2] Fracture the shaft and you either lose drive power on the highway or roll out of a parking space with nobody behind the wheel, depending on whether you remembered to set the parking brake that morning. Ford says the remedy will not be ready until December 2026. Twenty-one days later, Ford recalled another 86,543 Mach-Es from 2023 through 2025 because the rear quarter window glass trim can detach at speed, turning a decorative strip into a projectile for whoever is tailgating you.[3] That one traces to Saint-Gobain México, where a new assembly jig sped up the trim installation process but shorted the primer's cure time, producing tens of thousands of window assemblies with bonds that never fully formed.

Those two campaigns alone cover 129,327 vehicles, but the pile underneath them runs deeper than most owners realize. A December 2025 recall flagged the integrated park module on 2024–2026 Mach-Es for failing to lock into park.[4] A separate campaign covered electronic door latches across all 2021–2025 models that can trap a child in the back seat if the battery charge drops too low. Add wiper motors that go dead in rain on 2023–2024 models, an over-the-air update that accidentally killed ABS and stability control on 2021 units, and a re-recall for a botched battery-contactor repair, and the 2023 Mach-E sits under four simultaneous recall populations: pinion shaft, glass trim, wiper motor, and door latches, while every other model year carries at least three.

Nobody died in any of these eight campaigns, and the two July recalls report zero injuries between them. Ford finds the problems, files the paperwork, and sends the letters, which deserves credit in an industry where some manufacturers quietly let complaints accumulate until regulators force action. The ICE Mustang kills people at a FARS rate of roughly 6 per 100 million vehicle miles traveled; the Mach-E's recall problems are embarrassing, not lethal.[5]

But eight campaigns across five model years, with two different suppliers in two different countries failing for two different reasons in the same month, paints a picture Ford's marketing team would rather you not see. Ford pitched the Mach-E as a clean-sheet design unburdened by a century of internal-combustion baggage, and what it actually built was a vehicle that has never completed a single model year of production without generating a recall. If you own one, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Then check it again next month.
