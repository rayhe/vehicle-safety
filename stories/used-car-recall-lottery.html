One in Four Used Cars Comes With a Defect the Manufacturer Already Knows About

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
August 5, 2026

A three-year-old used vehicle costs $31,548 on average this quarter.[1] For that, you might expect everything to work. You'd be wrong roughly 25% of the time.

14,000,000
Recalled vehicles still unrepaired on US roads as of February 2026

Carfax estimates one in four registered vehicles carries at least one open safety recall.[2] Approximately 72 million cars, trucks, and SUVs with defects serious enough that the manufacturer filed federal paperwork, NHTSA assigned a campaign number, and a fix exists or is being developed. Everyone in the chain knows. Everyone except, possibly, the person about to buy it.

No federal law requires a used car dealer to repair an open recall before completing a sale.[3] New car franchise dealers are contractually bound by manufacturer agreements to fix recalls on new inventory. Used car operations face zero equivalent obligation. CarMax, Carvana, your neighborhood lot: all can hand you keys to a vehicle with a known seatbelt defect, a fire-prone harness, or an airbag that misfires.

NHTSA's 2025 Annual Recalls Report documented 670 campaigns covering 28 million vehicles. Repair completion by February 2026: 50.3%.[4] Coin-flip odds. Not every open recall is a fire risk; many are software patches or minor component swaps. But 87 powertrain campaigns and multiple "park outside" fire advisories sit in the same queue as the low-severity fixes, and the VIN search doesn't sort them by severity for you.

Tariffs worsen the wait. With 47% of US repair parts coming from Mexico and duties adding 5% to 6% across repair operations, components for free recall fixes are backordered at inflated costs.[5] Your vehicle is covered by the recall. It's waiting for a part stuck in customs.

FARS data adds context. Vehicles dominating used lots overlap with FARS's deadliest models. Honda Accord: 3.07 deaths per 100M VMT, double the sedan average. Ford Explorer: 1.54, triple the SUV average.[6] Popular used cars. High-risk used cars. Same vehicles.

Before you buy, search the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Ten seconds. If an open campaign appears, negotiate, walk, or buy and schedule the repair yourself. FTC rules require dealers to display a Buyers Guide. Not open recalls. Until that changes, the check is on you.
