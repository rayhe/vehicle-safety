The Ford Bronco Has Been Recalled 21 Times in Five Years. The Latest One Is a Piece of Plastic.

Mia Crumplezone • Clautomotive Desk
July 27, 2026

A wiring harness in the engine bay of the Ford Bronco rubs against surrounding metal parts. Given enough miles, the insulation wears through. The exposed copper shorts. The short creates heat. The heat creates fire. 565,691 Broncos just got recalled for this, covering every model year the vehicle has existed.[1]

21
Recalls issued for the 2021 Ford Bronco in 58 months of production

Ford's dealers will perform a fix so simple it's almost insulting: install sheathing over the wiring. A plastic sleeve. The kind of thing a first-year automotive engineer specifies on day one of a wiring harness routing exercise. Ford shipped half a million trucks without it.

Fifteen fires. Seven in Bronco Raptors, eight in base models. Zero injuries, zero deaths. NHTSA received the reports between October 2024 and June 2026, meaning the wires were chafing for years before anyone flagged the pattern. Ford's internal investigation identified a "lack of protection from abrasion" as the root cause.[1] In engineering terms, that means no one specified a grommet, a clip, or a $0.40 corrugated loom tube between the harness and the block.

This is the Bronco's 21st recall since production began in September 2020.[2] Twenty-one defects in 58 months works out to one new recall every 83 days. A vehicle that starts at $38,000 and climbs past $80,000 for the Raptor accumulates fresh safety defects faster than most cars accumulate oil changes.

What makes the Bronco recall list unsettling isn't the volume. It's the variety. Roof panels that crack and separate at highway speed. A windshield that can detach in a crash. A transmission-to-transfer-case joint that wears its own splines down. Engine intake valves that fracture and drop into the cylinder. A block heater that shorts when you plug it in. Seat frame pivot bolts that work loose. And now, a wire that rubs against metal until something ignites.[2][3] This isn't one recurring flaw. These are eight different systems failing in eight different ways on the same vehicle.

Ford's broader recall math makes the Bronco look almost routine. The automaker issued 153 recalls in 2025, affecting 12.9 million vehicles. Through May 2026, it had already launched 36 recall campaigns covering nearly 10 million more. In July alone, Ford recalled close to 2 million vehicles, including 388,000 Explorers with seats that unlatch while driving and these 565,691 Broncos with wires that spark.[4] All of this happened while Ford was running a national ad campaign with the tagline: "We put quality first. Now Ford is first in quality."[5]

JD Power's Initial Quality Study measures problems reported in the first 90 days of ownership. A wiring harness that chafes over years of driving does not show up in a 90-day survey. Neither does an intake valve that cracks at 20,000 miles, or a roof panel that delaminates after prolonged sun exposure. JD Power measures whether the infotainment screen froze during your first road trip. NHTSA measures whether the engine bay caught fire during your third year. Ford topped one list while the other kept growing.

Counterpoint: a high recall count can mean better defect surveillance, not worse engineering. Ford may be catching problems faster than competitors who let the same issues fester under warranty claims. And the results support this: zero deaths, zero injuries across 15 fires. The Bronco has the lowest fatality rate of any Ford vehicle in FARS at 0.05 per 100 million VMT, though the modern model is too new for that number to mean much.[6] The recall system is catching problems before they kill anyone.

But 21 recalls in five years is not a surveillance success story. It is a manufacturing discipline problem. A windshield adhesion failure, a roof delamination, and an unprotected wiring harness are not subtle, late-emerging failure modes that only appear after millions of miles of fleet data. They are basic process control and design review items that should be caught before the first unit rolls off the line. Ford's quality audit is functioning. Its engineering gate review is not.

If you own a 2021-2026 Bronco or Bronco Raptor: check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. If you smell anything hot or see smoke from the engine compartment, stop driving immediately and contact Ford at 866-436-7332. The sheathing repair is free. It should have been there in the first place.
