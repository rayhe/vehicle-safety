Evenflo's Clean Slate Failed in 15 Months

Clara Rollover • Clautomotive Desk
July 29, 2026

Evenflo launched the REO by Revolve360 as the next generation of its rotational car seat line. A fresh design. New model numbers. A product that was supposed to leave four previous recalls behind. On June 15, NHTSA strapped a 3-year-old crash test dummy into a REO seat, raised the headrest to its highest positions, and ran a frontal impact test[1]. The headrest shifted during the crash.

59,661
REO seats recalled after 15 months of production

Every unit manufactured between March 2025 and June 2026 is affected. Evenflo announced the recall on July 12[2] after NHTSA flagged the violation of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 213[4], the baseline standard for child restraint systems. The headrest latch can disengage at positions 10 and 11 in forward-facing mode and position 11 in rear-facing mode. Those are the settings your child grows into. The seat works fine until they need it most.

This is the fifth recall across the Revolve360 product family. The original Revolve360 accumulated three recalls for various defects. The Revolve360 Slim added a fourth when NHTSA discovered children could dislodge foam from the headrest, creating a choking hazard. Now the REO, marketed as a distinct product with its own model number prefix (CS26021), has failed the same agency's crash testing within its first 15 months on the market. Three product names. Five recalls. One engineering culture.

0
Injuries reported. NHTSA found this in testing, not the field.

Credit where it matters: nobody got hurt. NHTSA caught this through proactive crash testing, not through a parent discovering it in a real collision. But that distinction cuts both ways. Evenflo's own quality assurance should have caught a headrest latch failure in a standard FMVSS 213 test before shipping 59,661 units over 15 months. The fact that NHTSA found it first means Evenflo either did not run the test at the affected headrest positions or ran it and missed the failure.

Meanwhile, the remedy is "under development." Parents whose children need positions 10 or 11 have no fix. They can use lower positions or call Evenflo's consumer care line and wait. For a seat that retails north of $300, "we'll figure it out" is not a safety plan.

What Parents Should Actually Do

If you own a REO by Revolve360 (model numbers starting with CS26021), keep the headrest at position 9 or below in forward-facing mode and position 10 or below in rear-facing mode. If your child has outgrown those settings, stop using the seat and contact Evenflo at 1-800-233-5921. Affected models include the Brookline, Lenox, Holden, Nantucket, Danvers, and Chelsea colorways. The Revolve180, original Revolve360, Revolve360 Extend, and Revolve360 Slim are not part of this specific recall, though the Slim has its own active recall for the choking hazard.

If you are shopping for a rotational seat today, the pattern across five recalls should factor into your decision. Competing rotational seats from Nuna (REVV), Britax (Boulevard ClickTight), and Chicco (Fit360 ClearTex) have not accumulated comparable recall histories. That is not a guarantee of perfection. It is a data point.
