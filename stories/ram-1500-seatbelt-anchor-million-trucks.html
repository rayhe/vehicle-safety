Stellantis Built 1.2 Million Trucks Where the Seat Belt Might Not Be Bolted Down

Vin Wreckage • Clautomotive Desk
August 3, 2026

The seat belt is the oldest continually effective safety device in the automobile, one that requires no software, no sensors, and no over-the-air updates. A strap, a buckle, and a bolt holding both of them to the body of the vehicle. Stellantis has now recalled 1,271,294 Ram 1500 pickups spanning model years 2019 through 2026 because some of them may have left the factory with that bolt, the second-row seat belt buckle anchor, never properly attached to the truck’s body structure.[1]

1,271,294
Ram 1500s recalled over seat belt anchors that may not be attached to the truck

Seven model years of production, and Stellantis told the Detroit Free Press that affected trucks “may have been built without the seat belt buckle anchor properly attached to the vehicle’s body structure,” with the defect in either the driver-side or center seat of the second row.[2] In a crash, those belts could fail to restrain rear passengers entirely, which makes the recall language about “increased risk of injury” a spectacular understatement for a device whose purpose is preventing ejection.

The cosmic joke: the Ram 1500 is, by the numbers, one of the safest trucks on American roads. FARS fatal crash data from 2014 through 2023 shows it posting a lethality ratio of 0.34 deaths per fatal-crash involvement and a fatality rate of 0.13 per 100 million VMT, the third-lowest lethality of any vehicle in the dataset with 500-plus crash involvements.[3] Only the Ram 2500 performs better among full-size pickups. Mass, ride height, structural rigidity: the truck protects its occupants, unless they are sitting in the second row of one of the 1.27 million units where the seat belt anchor was never tightened down.

The strongest counterargument: not every recalled unit has the defect, and Stellantis is voluntarily recalling before any reported injuries, which is fair on both counts. But the system also allowed a manufacturing process to run seven continuous years without catching a failure in the most basic restraint a vehicle carries. NHTSA estimated seat belts saved 14,955 lives in 2017 alone.[4] That technology requires one thing from the assembly line: bolt it to the frame.

A necessary limitation: FARS does not disaggregate fatalities by seating position or restraint hardware failure, so we cannot tell from crash data whether any rear-seat Ram 1500 deaths involved defective anchors. We are not claiming they did, but the single most proven lifesaving device in automotive history, one that predates electronic stability control by forty years, is only as good as the bolt holding it to the truck—and over a million buyers had to trust that the bolt was there.

What to do: If you own a 2019–2026 Ram 1500, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or call Stellantis at 800-853-1403. Dealers will inspect the second-row seat belt system and reattach the buckle anchor if needed, free of charge. Until then, consider whether your rear passengers should be riding in the front row instead.
