Ford Is Half of Every U.S. Recall in 2026. It Sells One in Eight Cars.

Axle McScatter • Clautomotive Desk
August 6, 2026

Through July 28, Ford Motor Company recalled 13,518,991 vehicles across 61 campaigns, which is more than every other automaker in the country combined.[1] The rest of the U.S. auto industry managed 13,221,234 from 165 campaigns, and sometime around mid-July Ford quietly crossed the 50 percent threshold and kept climbing.

4.0×
Ford's recall intensity ratio: its share of recalls divided by its share of sales

I built a metric I'm calling the recall intensity ratio, which divides a manufacturer's share of total U.S. recall volume by its share of new-vehicle sales. Ford's number landed at 4.0 because it holds 50.6 percent of all recalled vehicles on approximately 12.6 percent market share, per Cox Automotive's first-half estimates.[2] The rest of the industry scores 0.57, producing a gap of roughly seven to one.

Ford's average recall campaign covers 221,622 vehicles, nearly triple the non-Ford average of 80,129, partly because the F-Series has been America's best-selling truck for 49 straight years and any defect on that platform cascades across a staggering population.[3] Toyota and GM sell durable, high-volume trucks too, and neither is within shouting distance of half the industry's recall total.

FARS fatality data adds a dimension Ford probably doesn't want examined. Over the 2014 to 2023 reporting window, Ford vehicles recorded 34,954 occupant deaths across 29 tracked models, representing 18.3 percent of all FARS-tracked fatalities on that same roughly 12.6 percent market share.[4] Ford recalls at 4.0 times its weight but dies at only 1.45 times, and the gap between those two numbers raises an uncomfortable question about whether the recall machine is even aimed at the right defects.

The fairest counterargument is fleet age, because Ford's installed base skews older than its new-sales share implies: F-150s and Explorers survive for decades, and a recall spanning 2019 through 2026 model years pulls in vehicles that predate current market-share math. Severity matters too, since a software patch pushed overnight and a do-not-drive order both count as one recalled vehicle, and nearly half of Ford's Q1 volume was eligible for over-the-air fixes.[5] Strip the OTA campaigns and Ford's share drops, but it does not drop below everyone else combined.

Ford sold roughly 993,852 vehicles in the first half and recalled 13.5 million, which works out to 13.6 recalled vehicles for every one it sold.[2] At the current pace, Ford will finish 2026 north of 20 million recalls on about 2 million in sales.

What to do

If you own any Ford built between 2019 and 2026, check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls because statistically you have at least one open campaign waiting. Explorer and Aviator owners: two separate seat-related recalls hit the same platform in July alone.
